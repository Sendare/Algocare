Complications of Moulding

Introduction and Anatomical Adaptations of Fetal Skull Moulding

During labour, the fetal head undergoes moulding, which involves the overlapping of cranial bones to reduce the bispinous diameter and allow passage through the birth canal. The fetal skull is uniquely suited for this mechanical stress because the bones are not yet fused. Instead, flexible membranous intervals known as sutures separate the flat bones of the vault, while wide spaces called fontanelles mark the junctions where multiple sutures meet. As uterine contractions press the presenting part against the rigid maternal pelvis, the parietal bones commonly slide over the occipital bone posteriorly and beneath the frontal bone anteriorly. This overlapping is a normal, protective mechanism that prevents sudden intracranial pressure spikes, provided the process occurs gradually within a normal duration of labour. When evaluating a newborn in Nigerian clinical settings, midwives and nurses routinely palpate these suture lines during the immediate postnatal head-to-toe examination to determine the extent of bone displacement and ensure normal physiological recovery.

Pathological Moulding and Excessive Mechanical Stress

While moderate moulding is entirely normal, excessive or rapid mechanical force results in pathological moulding. This typically arises during prolonged second-stage labour, cephalopelvic disproportion, or malpositions such as occipitoposterior presentations. When the fetal head meets unyielding resistance without adequate time to adapt, severe shearing forces develop within the cranium. The tentorium cerebellum and falx cerebri, which are rigid dural folds supporting the brain, experience extreme tension as the bones override each other beyond physiological limits. Such severe stress can tear bridging veins and dural membranes, precipitating serious intracranial injuries. In busy maternity units across Nigeria, recognizing prolonged second stage labour early prevents the cascade of events leading to severe pathological moulding and subsequent neonatal compromise.

Soft Tissue Scalp Swelling: Caput Succedaneum vs. Cephalhematoma

Scalp swellings frequently accompany difficult deliveries and must be differentiated from bony moulding complications.

  • Caput Succedaneum: Serosanguineous subcutaneous fluid accumulation above the periosteum. It crosses suture lines, feels soft and pitting, and often resolves spontaneously within a few days.
  • Cephalhematoma: Bleeding between the skull bone and its periosteum. It is bounded by suture lines, feels firm and tense, and carries a risk of hyperbilirubinemia as the extravasated blood breaks down.

Vascular Injuries and Intracranial Haemorrhage

Severe mechanical trauma from extreme moulding can rupture fragile intracranial vessels. Subdural haemorrhage occurs when bridging veins or the dural sinuses tear, frequently associated with difficult forceps deliveries or severe cephalopelvic disproportion. This condition can lead to rapid neurological deterioration, seizures, and signs of increased intracranial pressure. Subarachnoid haemorrhage results from bleeding into the subarachnoid space, often seen in preterm infants or following traumatic deliveries, sometimes presenting with apnea or irritability. While mild bleeds may be asymptomatic and resolve over time, massive haemorrhages require urgent pediatric intervention, intensive monitoring, and sometimes surgical evacuation. Nurses must be vigilant for subtle changes in neonatal tone, feeding tolerance, and fontanelle tension, as these may be the earliest warning signs of hidden intracranial bleeding.

Neurological Complications and Brain Tissue Stress

Beyond overt bleeding, severe moulding places direct mechanical stress on cerebral tissue and cranial nerves. As the cranial bones compress underlying structures, focal ischemia and transient neurological dysfunction can ensue. Infants may exhibit high-pitched crying, lethargy, poor suck reflexes, or hypotonia. Cranial nerve compression—particularly the facial and abducens nerves—can result from prolonged pressure against the sacral promontory or pelvic sidewalls during obstructed labour. This manifests as asymmetrical facial movements or abnormal eye-gaze alignment. Although many of these neurological signs are transient and resolve as cerebral edema subsides, distinguishing them from hypoxic-ischaemic encephalopathy requires careful, systematic neurological assessment by the attending nurse.

Clinical Assessment and Documentation of Newborn Skull Status

Systematic evaluation of the newborn head is a core competency in neonatal nursing. The nurse examines the head shape, identifies overlapping sutures, and assesses fontanelle size and tension. Moulding is typically graded from zero, where bones are separated with soft sutures, to grade three, where bones are severely overlapped, fixed, and cannot be easily displaced. It is vital to document whether sutures can be easily reduced by gentle digital pressure or if they are locked in place. In busy Nigerian clinical practice, clear documentation of head circumference, presence of caput, cephalhematoma, or abnormal moulding provides an essential baseline for detecting delayed complications during the first few days of life.

Nursing Management and Supportive Care

Comprehensive nursing care for an infant with complications of moulding centers on minimizing metabolic stress, monitoring neurological status, and supporting parental coping. The newborn should be handled gently to avoid aggravating potential intracranial pressure or pain. Vital signs, especially temperature, respiration, and color, require frequent monitoring. If hyperbilirubinemia develops secondary to the resorption of a large cephalhematoma, phototherapy protocols must be initiated promptly. Furthermore, parents often experience significant anxiety when they observe swelling, bruising, or an oddly shaped head on their newborn; nurses play a crucial role in explaining that physiological moulding and caput succedaneum typically resolve within a few days without long-term consequences.

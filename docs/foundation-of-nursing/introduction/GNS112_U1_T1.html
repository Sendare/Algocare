Definition of Nursing/Nurse

Introduction to Nursing as Both an Art and a Science

Nursing stands at a unique intersection between compassionate human care and rigorous biological science. As a science, it relies on anatomy, physiology, pharmacology, and evidence-based research to assess patients and safely administer treatments. As an art, it demands empathy, active listening, and the intuition required to connect with a vulnerable human being during their darkest hours. You cannot separate these two halves. A nurse who possesses great scientific knowledge without empathy lacks the human touch required for true healing, while pure compassion without clinical competence can lead to dangerous errors at the bedside.

Historical Evolution from Caregiving to Profession

For centuries, nursing was viewed as an informal domestic duty performed by family members or religious orders out of charity. Florence Nightingale fundamentally shifted this perception during the Crimean War by introducing sanitary reforms, statistical tracking of mortality, and structured training for women. Her work proved that a clean environment and organized care directly saved lives. Over time, nursing moved away from subservient obedience toward autonomous critical thinking, higher education, and formal licensure, establishing itself as a distinct regulated profession.

Global Definitions of Nursing

Major international bodies define nursing to capture its broad scope across different cultures and healthcare systems. The International Council of Nurses describes nursing as autonomous and collaborative care of individuals of all ages, families, groups, and communities, whether sick or well, in all settings. The World Health Organization emphasizes health promotion, prevention of illness, and the care of ill, disabled, and dying people. These definitions highlight that nursing is not merely carrying out doctors' orders inside a hospital ward, but encompasses proactive health promotion and advocacy everywhere people live.

The Four Metaparadigm Concepts of Nursing

Nursing theory is built around four central concepts that define the entire worldview of the profession. These four pillars guide how a nurse approaches every clinical encounter.

  • Person: The recipient of nursing care, including individuals, families, and communities with unique physical, emotional, and spiritual needs.
  • Environment: The internal and external surroundings that affect a person's health, encompassing living conditions, family support, and physical surroundings.
  • Health: A dynamic state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being, rather than merely the absence of disease.
  • Nursing: The actions, characteristics, and attributes of the nurse providing care on behalf of or in partnership with the person.

Core Roles and Responsibilities of the Nurse

Nurses wear many hats depending on the needs of their patients and healthcare institutions. As direct caregivers, they manage wounds, administer medications, and monitor vital signs. As educators, they explain discharge instructions and disease management to patients and families so they can care for themselves at home. As advocates, nurses protect patient rights, ensure informed consent, and speak up when a patient's safety is compromised by the care team. Additional roles include functioning as a counselor, leader, collaborator, and researcher within the multidisciplinary team.

Characteristics of a Professional Nurse

Professionalism in nursing goes beyond wearing a neat uniform and holding a university degree. It requires unwavering accountability for one's clinical actions, accepting responsibility for mistakes, and engaging in lifelong learning to keep skills sharp. Compassion and emotional intelligence allow a nurse to build therapeutic trust with strangers during high-stress moments. Furthermore, adherence to a strict code of ethics ensures patient confidentiality, dignity, and justice are maintained in every healthcare setting regardless of personal biases.

The Scope of Nursing Practice in Nigeria

In Nigeria, nursing practice is strictly regulated by the Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria. This body sets the curriculum for nursing education, conducts professional licensing examinations, and enforces discipline and scope of practice rules. Nigerian nurses work across primary healthcare centers, mission hospitals, secondary clinics, and tertiary teaching hospitals. The scope includes independent nursing actions like health promotion and basic bedside care, as well as interdependent actions such as executing medical treatment plans prescribed by physicians, all aimed at reducing maternal and child mortality across the nation.

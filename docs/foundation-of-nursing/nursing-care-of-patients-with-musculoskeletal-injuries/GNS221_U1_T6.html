Stockinette

Introduction and Definition of Stockinette

A stockinette is a seamless, tubular, knitted fabric used extensively in clinical nursing and orthopedic practice. It acts as a primary, protective layer placed directly against a patient's skin before the application of orthopedic casts, splints, or compression bandages. Because it is manufactured using a knit stitch rather than a woven process, the material stretches easily in both width and length, allowing it to conform smoothly to various body contours without gathering or forming hard folds that could press into the skin. Nurses encounter stockinettes daily in emergency rooms, fracture clinics, and surgical wards. Understanding its basic structure is the first step in preventing skin breakdown under rigid immobilization devices. The fabric is typically supplied in continuous rolls of varying widths, enabling the healthcare provider to cut custom lengths tailored to an individual patient's arm, leg, or trunk.

Purposes and Clinical Indications in Orthopedic Care

The primary purpose of a stockinette is to form a protective barrier between fragile human skin and the rigid, abrasive materials used in casting, such as plaster of Paris or fiberglass. Without this underlying layer, hardening casting materials can cause friction burns, severe itching, and pressure ulcers.

  • Skin Protection: Guards against direct contact with rough casting resins.
  • Moisture Management: Absorbs minor sweat to keep the underlying skin relatively dry.
  • Padding Support: Holds underlying cotton cast padding (such as Sofban) securely in place.
  • Edge Comfort: Allows the nurse to fold the edges over the top and bottom of a finished cast to create a smooth, non-irritating border against the patient's skin.

Types and Materials: Cotton vs. Synthetic

Stockinettes are manufactured from different fibers, and selecting the correct type depends on the patient's clinical needs and the treatment plan.

Material TypeKey CharacteristicsCommon Clinical Use
1. 100% CottonHighly breathable, absorbent, soft against sensitive skinStandard orthopedic casts, pediatric care
2. Synthetic (Polyester/Rayon)Water-resistant, quick-drying, synthetic blendWaterproof casting systems, hydro-cast applications
3. ElasticizedContains spandex or rubber threads for high compressionVaricose vein support, stump wrapping for amputees

Nurses must check hospital stock and patient allergies before selecting a material, particularly ensuring that synthetic blends do not cause contact dermatitis in sensitive individuals.

Patient Preparation and Size Selection

Before applying any stockinette, the nurse must prepare both the patient and the environment. First, inspect the skin integrity of the affected limb. Wash and dry the skin thoroughly, and cover any open wounds or surgical incisions with sterile dressings, because a stockinette is clean but rarely sterile unless explicitly packaged as such.

Selecting the correct width is critical. If a stockinette is too small, it will squeeze the limb, restrict blood flow, and cause severe pain. If it is too large, it will wrinkle and bunch up underneath the cast, creating localized pressure spots that lead to skin breakdown. As a general rule, the flat width of the tubular stockinette should be slightly wider than the widest part of the limb it will cover, allowing it to glide on smoothly without excessive stretching or loose folds.

Step-by-Step Application Technique

Applying a stockinette correctly requires practice to ensure a smooth, wrinkle-free fit. Gather the tubular fabric onto your hands like a doughnut or accordion before pulling it over the limb.

  1. Measure and Cut: Cut a length of stockinette that extends well beyond both proximal and distal ends of the planned cast (usually 10 to 15 centimeters extra at each end).
  2. Gather the Fabric: Bunch the entire length of the tube onto your thumbs and index fingers.
  3. Slide Onto Limb: Gently glide the gathered fabric over the patient's fingers or toes and pull it smoothly up the limb.
  4. Smooth Wrinkles: Pull both ends firmly to remove any twists, creases, or folds in the fabric.
  5. Leave Extremities Free: Ensure that fingers or toes remain completely uncovered at the distal end so the nurse can easily check capillary refill and pulse later.

Post-Application Care and Monitoring for Complications

Once the cast or splint is in place over the stockinette, the nursing job shifts to vigilant monitoring. Complications often arise if the stockinette shifts, wrinkles, or becomes wet underneath a non-waterproof cast. Moisture trapped against the skin inside a cotton stockinette creates an ideal environment for fungal and bacterial growth, leading to foul odors and skin breakdown.

Nurses must perform regular neurovascular assessments using the 5 Ps: Pain (unrelieved by medication), Pallor (pale skin), Pulselessness, Paresthesia (tingling or numbness), and Paralysis. If a patient complains of a burning sensation or a localized hot spot underneath the cast, it often signals a wrinkle in the stockinette causing a pressure ulcer. Never insert foreign objects like coat hangers or knitting needles down the stockinette to scratch an itch, as this easily breaks the fragile skin and introduces infection.

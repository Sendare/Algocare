Physiotherapy in Nursing Care

Introduction to Physiotherapy in Nursing

Physiotherapy in nursing focuses on restoring physical function, maintaining mobility, and preventing complications related to immobility. Nurses work closely with physical therapists to execute daily care plans that support patient recovery. Bedrest and prolonged hospital stays weaken muscle mass and reduce joint flexibility. Early physical intervention preserves functional independence and shortens hospital admission periods. Ward nurses observe baseline movement abilities during admission assessments to spot early signs of physical decline.

Goals and Objectives of Therapeutic Exercises

  • Active exercises: Performed independently by the patient to build muscle strength and endurance.
  • Passive exercises: Carried out entirely by the nurse or therapist to maintain joint range of motion when the patient cannot move.
  • Active-assistive exercises: Patient performs movement with external help from the nurse to support weak limbs.
  • Isometric exercises: Muscle contraction without joint movement, useful for bedridden patients to prevent atrophy.

Physiotherapy Modalities in Ward Care

Various physical modalities assist nurses in managing pain, reducing inflammation, and promoting tissue healing.

ModalityPrimary Clinical UseNursing Caution
Cryotherapy (Cold)Reduces acute swelling and numbs sharp painAvoid direct skin contact to prevent tissue damage
Thermotherapy (Heat)Relieves chronic muscle spasms and joint stiffnessNever use over fresh bleeding or acute inflammation
Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation (TENS)Blocks pain signals traveling to the spinal cordDo not place electrodes over broken skin or pacemakers

Nurses must inspect the treatment site before and after applying any physical modality to catch skin irritation early.

The Nurse's Role in Mobility and Ambulation

Getting a patient out of bed requires careful planning and proper body mechanics. Nurses assess vital signs, dizziness, and overall strength before attempting any transfer. Dangling the legs at the bedside prevents orthostatic hypotension by allowing blood pressure to stabilize. Assistive devices like crutches, walkers, and canes must be checked for rubber tips and structural integrity before use. During ambulation, the nurse walks slightly behind and to the side of the patient, holding a gait belt for security.

Positioning and Preventing Musculoskeletal Complications

Immobile patients face severe risks including pressure ulcers, foot drop, and joint contractures. Repositioning every two hours relieves sustained capillary pressure over bony prominences. Footboards or specialized splints maintain neutral ankle alignment to prevent permanent plantar flexion contractures, commonly known as foot drop. Trochanter rolls keep the hips from externally rotating while the patient lies supine. Nurses incorporate these alignment measures into routine turn schedules across all hospital shifts.

Collaborative Care with Physical Therapists

Interprofessional teamwork ensures patients receive safe, continuous physical rehabilitation. Physical therapists evaluate complex movement deficits and design specialized exercise regimens. Nurses implement these routines throughout the day, reinforcing proper form and encouraging patient participation. Clear communication between nursing and therapy staff identifies barriers to progress, such as unmanaged pain or fatigue. When both disciplines align their schedules, patients experience less exhaustion and faster recovery milestones.

Patient Education and Safe Discharge Planning

Preparing a patient to return home requires thorough education on safe physical activity and environmental modifications. Nurses teach family members how to assist with transfers and home exercises without injuring their own backs. Home assessments cover removing loose rugs, installing bathroom grab bars, and ensuring adequate lighting to prevent falls. Written instructions reinforce verbal teaching so recovering patients remember their movement restrictions and follow-up appointment dates.

Gallows Traction

Introduction and Definition of Gallows Traction

Gallows traction, also known as **Bryant's traction**, is a specialised form of skin traction primarily used in orthopaedics for infants and young children. The technique involves suspending both lower extremities vertically at a ninety-degree angle to the trunk, while the child's buttocks are slightly elevated off the bed mattress. The child's own body weight acts as the natural counter-traction force, pulling the fractured femur into proper alignment. Because of anatomical considerations and weight limits, this method is strictly reserved for infants under the age of two or children weighing less than twelve kilograms. If used on a heavier or older child, the body weight is insufficient for counter-traction, or the child may pull themselves up and dislodge the apparatus, defeating the therapeutic purpose.

Indications for Paediatric Femoral Fractures

The primary indication for applying gallows traction is the management of a **femoral shaft fracture** in a small child. Unlike adults, young children rarely require complex surgical fixation for simple closed femur fractures because their bones possess rapid and robust healing capabilities. Gallows traction provides a non-invasive method to reduce the fracture fragments and maintain proper limb length while callus formation occurs. Occasionally, it may also be utilised to correct severe congenital hip dislocations or severe soft tissue contractures prior to definitive surgical intervention, though femur fractures remain the most common clinical scenario encountered in paediatric wards.

Equipment Setup and Application Procedure

Setting up gallows traction requires precision to ensure safety and therapeutic efficacy. Adhesive or non-adhesive traction strips are applied longitudinally to both of the child's lower limbs and secured with elastic bandages wrapped in a spiral fashion from distal to proximal. Ropes are attached to the footplates or spreader bars, threaded through overhead pulleys on a rigid frame, and connected to weights. The correct amount of weight is suspended freely, ensuring the weights never rest on the floor or the bed frame. The most critical positional requirement is that the infant's **buttocks must clear the mattress** by a small margin, allowing the torso weight to act as the counter-traction force.

Nursing Assessment and Neurovascular Monitoring

- **Peripheral Pulses**: Check dorsalis pedis and posterior tibial pulses regularly to ensure adequate arterial blood flow to the feet. - **Capillary Refill**: Press gently on the toenails and observe the return of pink colour; sluggish refill indicates compromised circulation. - **Skin Temperature**: Compare both feet; a cold extremity points towards vascular restriction caused by tight bandages. - **Sensation and Movement**: Observe spontaneous wigglings of the toes and note the infant's response to tactile touch. - **Bandage Inspection**: Ensure adhesive wraps are not slipping, twisting, or cutting into the fragile skin folds of the infant.

Daily Care, Hygiene, and Skin Integrity Management

Maintaining skin integrity is exceptionally challenging because the infant's buttocks hover just above the bed, creating a tight space for diaper changes and routine hygiene. Faeces and urine can easily track down toward the traction tapes, causing skin maceration and breakdown. Nurses must perform meticulous perineal care, sliding waterproof materials or small bedpans carefully beneath the suspended pelvis without altering the traction angle. The skin beneath the bandages must be monitored for redness, blisters, or pressure sores during scheduled checks. Creams or lotions should not be applied under adhesive tapes, as moisture combined with lubricants increases the risk of severe skin slippage and breakdown.

Potential Complications and Nursing Interventions

Several complications can arise during the course of gallows traction treatment. **Circulatory impairment** and compartment syndrome can develop if the bandaging is too restrictive or if severe swelling occurs. **Skin breakdown** is another frequent issue, especially around the sacrum and heels. Furthermore, respiratory complications can occur because the upward vertical position restricts full diaphragmatic expansion to some degree, making the child prone to hypostatic congestion. Nurses must monitor respiratory rate, encourage deep breathing through play, perform frequent position shifts of the upper body, and immediately report any signs of neurovascular deficits or compromised skin.

Patient and Family Education and Psychosocial Support

Hospitalisation and strict immobilisation are extremely distressing for both the infant and their parents. An infant in gallows traction cannot crawl, sit up, or explore their environment freely, which can lead to sensory deprivation and developmental regression. Parents often feel helpless, guilty, or frightened by the complex array of ropes and pulleys. Nurses play a vital role in providing emotional reassurance, explaining every procedure clearly, and encouraging parents to participate in feeding, playing, and comforting the child. Incorporating toys that dangle within the child's reach helps maintain sensory stimulation and encourages positive coping during a difficult therapeutic journey.

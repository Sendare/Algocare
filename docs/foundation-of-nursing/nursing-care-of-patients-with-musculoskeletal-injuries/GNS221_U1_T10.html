Gallows Traction

Introduction, Indications, and Principles

Gallows traction, also known as Bryant's traction, is a specialised form of skin traction used exclusively in infants under the age of two years or weighing less than roughly 12 to 14 kilograms. It is primarily indicated for the closed reduction and stabilisation of femoral shaft fractures or for the correction of severe congenital hip conditions in very young children. Heavier or older children cannot be safely managed with this method because their body weight is too great to act as a proper counter-traction force.

The core physiological principle relies on gravity to help reduce the fracture alignment. Both lower extremities are suspended vertically at a 90-degree angle to the body's horizontal plane. Straps or adhesive tape are applied to the child's skin along both legs, connected to ropes and weights. The unique mechanism of Gallows traction involves lifting the infant's buttocks just slightly off the mattress. The weight of the baby's upper torso provides the necessary counter-traction to keep the fractured femur aligned properly while bone healing begins.

Equipment Setup and Application Procedure

Setting up Gallows traction requires careful preparation and strict adherence to safety protocols. Nursing staff work alongside the medical team to ensure all components are assembled correctly before the infant is placed in the bed.

  1. Skin Preparation: Wash and dry the child's lower limbs completely, applying a skin barrier tincture if ordered to protect delicate infant skin from adhesive irritation.
  2. Traction Strips: Apply foam traction strips or adhesive moleskin along the lateral and medial aspects of both legs, leaving the heel exposed.
  3. Securing the Wraps: Wrap the legs gently with elastic bandages in a spiral motion from distal to proximal, ensuring the wraps are firm but never constrictive.
  4. Attachment to Ropes: Connect the traction strips to the spreader bars, ropes, and pulleys attached to the overhead frame.
  5. Weight Application: Hang the prescribed weights, ensuring they hang freely in the air without touching the floor or the bed frame at any point.
  6. Positioning the Child: Adjust the ropes so the infant's pelvis is lifted just barely off the mattress, allowing a hand to slide underneath the buttocks.

Nursing Assessment and Neurovascular Monitoring

Continuous neurovascular assessment is critical for any child in skeletal or skin traction. Because adhesive wraps and elastic bandages encase the infant's legs, the risk of compromised circulation or nerve damage is remarkably high.

Perform neurovascular checks every two to four hours. Evaluate the five Ps of circulation: pain, pallor, pulselessness, paresthesia, and paralysis. Since infants cannot verbally describe numbness or tingling, observe their behaviour. Persistent, inconsolable crying often serves as the primary indicator of severe pain or compartment syndrome.

Inspect the exposed toes for capillary refill time, colour, and skin temperature. Compare the affected limb against the unaffected limb. A cool toe or delayed capillary refill exceeding three seconds demands immediate reporting to the physician, as it may signal that the bandage is wrapped too tightly and acting as a tourniquet.

Daily Care, Hygiene, and Skin Integrity Management

Maintaining skin integrity and personal hygiene is exceptionally challenging when an infant is suspended in Gallows traction. Faeces and urine can easily seep upward into the skin tapes and perineal region, leading to severe skin breakdown or infection.

  • Perineal Care: Clean and dry the diaper area frequently, slipping a small waterproof nappy pad underneath the hips without disturbing the traction ropes.
  • Skin Inspection: Check the skin around the edges of the adhesive wraps daily for redness, blistering, or signs of pressure sores.
  • Linen Changes: Change bed linens using a top-to-bottom rolling technique while keeping the child's body alignment stable and supported by an assistant.
  • Friction Reduction: Ensure crumbs, food particles, or wrinkled sheets do not gather beneath the infant's back and legs.

Potential Complications and Nursing Interventions

Several complications can arise during Gallows traction therapy, requiring vigilant monitoring and prompt nursing interventions. Recognising early warning signs prevents long-term orthopedic or systemic issues.

  • Circulatory Compromise: Caused by overly tight bandages or swelling. Intervene by notifying the doctor and preparing to loosen or reapply wraps.
  • Skin Breakdown: Caused by moisture, friction, or adhesive allergy. Treat by providing meticulous hygiene and barrier creams on undamaged skin.
  • Respiratory Restriction: The elevated leg position and abdominal compression can sometimes restrict deep lung expansion. Encourage frequent position shifts of the upper torso and monitor breath sounds.
  • Contractures: Stiffness in the knees or hips. Ensure gentle range-of-motion exercises are performed on unaffected joints as ordered.

Patient and Family Education and Psychosocial Support

Hospitalisation and immobilisation impose significant emotional strain on both the infant and their family. Parents often experience guilt, fear, and helplessness seeing their baby suspended by the legs.

Nurses must explain the purpose of the equipment clearly, reassuring parents that the traction is safe and necessary for proper bone healing. Teach family members how to interact with the child without altering the traction setup. Encourage play therapy, such as dangling soft toys within the infant's visual field, reading stories, and providing gentle tactile comfort like stroking the face and hands since holding the baby is not possible.

Address parental anxiety proactively by explaining every nursing procedure before touching the child. Involve social workers or child life specialists early to provide holistic family support throughout the treatment duration.

Weaning, Removal, and Post-Traction Care

Once radiological evidence confirms adequate callus formation and bone healing, the physician will order the discontinuation of Gallows traction. The weaning and removal process must be handled cautiously to prevent sudden stress on the newly healed femur.

The removal procedure typically involves cutting the adhesive skin tapes, releasing the weights gradually if ordered, and lowering the limbs flat onto the mattress. Nurses should anticipate muscle stiffness, joint immobility, and skin sensitivity once the apparatus is removed.

Post-traction care focuses on gentle skin cleansing to remove adhesive residue without tearing fragile skin. Assist with gentle, passive range-of-motion exercises as prescribed to restore flexibility to the hips and knees. Monitor the infant closely as they readjust to unrestricted movement.

Urinary Retention

Definition and Types of Urinary Retention

Urinary retention is the inability to completely empty the bladder during urination. It can present suddenly as an painful medical emergency or develop slowly over time without immediate discomfort.

  • Acute urinary retention: A sudden, painful inability to pass urine despite a full bladder. It requires immediate drainage.
  • Chronic urinary retention: A painless, gradual accumulation of urine in the bladder where the patient can still void small amounts, retaining high residual volumes.

Underlying Causes and Risk Factors

The inability to empty the bladder typically stems from either a physical blockage in the urinary tract or a failure of the bladder muscle to contract properly.

  • Obstructive causes: Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) in older men, urethral strictures, bladder stones, and severe pelvic organ prolapse in women.
  • Non-obstructive causes: Neurological damage from spinal cord injury, diabetic neuropathy, stroke, and certain medications like anticholinergics or opioids that weaken detrusor muscle tone.

Common misconception: Urinary retention is not only a male health issue; women frequently experience it following childbirth or severe pelvic floor dysfunction.

Clinical Manifestations and Assessment

Spotting urinary retention early prevents severe complications. Nurses must recognize both subjective complaints and objective physical signs.

  • Subjective signs: Lower abdominal discomfort, a feeling of incomplete bladder emptying, and urinary hesitancy or straining.
  • Objective signs: A firm, distended lower abdomen that is dull to percussion, suprapubic tenderness, and overflow incontinence where small amounts leak out involuntarily when pressure gets too high.

Always ask patients when they last urinated and how much they passed.

Diagnostic Evaluation

Confirming urinary retention requires a mix of bedside clinical checks and diagnostic imaging.

  • Bladder scanner: A non-invasive ultrasound tool used at the bedside to measure post-void residual (PVR) volume quickly.
  • Catheterization: Passing a small tube if a scanner is unavailable to measure residual urine directly.
  • Laboratory tests: Urinalysis to rule out infection, and serum creatinine to check if the backup of urine has started harming the kidneys.

Medical and Interventional Management

Managing urinary retention focuses on immediate relief followed by treating the root cause.

  • Urgent decompression: Prompt placement of a urethral or suprapubic catheter to drain the accumulated urine.
  • Gradual drainage: For massive retention, healthcare teams sometimes clamp the catheter intermittently to prevent sudden bladder collapse and bleeding from rapid decompression.
  • Pharmacotherapy: Alpha-blockers like tamsulosin to relax prostate and bladder neck smooth muscle, or 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors to shrink an enlarged prostate over time.

Nursing Care and Catheterization Procedures

Providing hands-on care for patients with urinary retention requires strict sterile technique and careful monitoring.

  • Aseptic technique: Maintain absolute sterility during catheter insertion to prevent hospital-acquired urinary tract infections (CAUTI).
  • Securement: Anchor the catheter tubing securely to the patient's thigh or lower abdomen to prevent urethral traction.
  • Output tracking: Monitor and record urine output hourly in acute settings, watching for large post-obstructive diuresis where the kidneys temporarily flood out massive volumes of dilute urine.

Complications and Patient Education

Unmanaged urinary retention leads to serious downstream health issues that demand vigilance and clear teaching.

  • Infection risks: Stagnant urine acts as a breeding ground for bacteria, frequently leading to cystitis or severe pyelonephritis.
  • Permanent damage: Chronic high pressure can cause hydronephrosis, ureteral dilation, and irreversible kidney failure.
  • Patient education: Teach patients how to care for their catheters at home, recognize signs of infection such as fever or foul-smelling urine, and report any lack of drainage immediately.

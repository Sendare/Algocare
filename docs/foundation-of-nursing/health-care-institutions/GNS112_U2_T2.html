Structure of Health Facilities and Social Services

Introduction to Healthcare Delivery Systems in Nigeria

The healthcare delivery system in Nigeria is structured in a tiered or hierarchical manner to ensure services reach both urban and rural populations. This framework is divided into three main levels: primary, secondary, and tertiary. Each tier has distinct responsibilities, staffing patterns, and resource allocations designed to handle specific health needs.

  • Primary Level: The foundation of the system, focusing on basic community care and prevention.
  • Secondary Level: General hospitals providing specialized care for patients referred from primary centers.
  • Tertiary Level: Highly specialized teaching hospitals and medical centers managing complex cases.

Understanding this structure helps nursing students see how patient care flows across different facilities. When a community clinic cannot manage a complicated illness, the system provides a clear path to move the patient up to a higher level of care.

Primary Health Care Facilities

Primary health care (PHC) represents the first point of contact individuals and families have with the national health system. These facilities are located closest to the people, usually within rural communities and local government areas. They focus heavily on health promotion, disease prevention, routine immunization, and managing common, uncomplicated ailments.

Staffing at this level is often led by community health extension workers, primary health care nurses, and midwives. Common services provided include antenatal care, normal vaginal delivery, growth monitoring for children, and basic health education. A common misconception is that primary facilities only treat minor injuries; in reality, their primary mission is community-wide wellness and preventing diseases before they require hospital admission.

Secondary Health Facilities

Secondary health facilities consist of general hospitals, comprehensive health centers, and state specialist hospitals. Patients typically arrive here through referrals from primary health centers, though emergency walk-ins also occur. This level provides specialized care that requires more advanced diagnostic equipment and professional expertise than a primary clinic can offer.

Services at secondary facilities are departmentalized into core areas:

  • Internal Medicine: Managing non-surgical adult illnesses.
  • General Surgery: Performing routine operations such as appendectomies and herniorrhaphies.
  • Paediatrics: Caring for sick children and newborns needing hospital admission.
  • Obstetrics and Gynaecology: Handling complicated deliveries, caesarean sections, and women's health issues.

General hospitals bridge the gap between community-level clinics and high-level teaching hospitals, easing the burden on tertiary centers.

Tertiary Health Facilities

Tertiary health facilities are the apex of the healthcare delivery system. They include teaching hospitals, federal medical centres, and highly specialized institutes. These institutions provide sophisticated diagnostic, therapeutic, and rehabilitative services for complex medical and surgical conditions.

Beyond patient care, tertiary facilities serve as primary centers for medical, nursing, and allied health training, as well as biomedical research. They feature advanced technology such as magnetic resonance imaging, intensive care units, and specialized sub-specialists like cardiologists, neurosurgeons, and oncologists. Because they manage the most severe cases, patients usually gain access only through formal referral from secondary facilities, except during acute life-threatening emergencies.

Integration of Social Services in Health

Health is not merely the absence of disease, but a state of complete well-being. Social services work hand-in-hand with clinical healthcare to address the non-medical determinants of health, such as poverty, homelessness, abuse, and family breakdown. Medical social workers play a vital role in hospitals by assessing a patient's social and economic background to ensure treatment plans are realistic and sustainable.

Social services support vulnerable populations, including orphans, abandoned elderly individuals, patients with chronic stigmatizing illnesses like tuberculosis or HIV, and victims of gender-based violence. Nurses often collaborate with social workers to coordinate discharge planning, connect families with community financial aid, and arrange shelter or rehabilitation. Without social support, many patients experience repeated hospital readmissions due to harsh living conditions outside the hospital walls.

Inter-Referral Pathways and Continuity of Care

An inter-referral pathway is a structured system that guides the movement of a patient between different levels of care. When a condition exceeds the capability of a primary clinic, a formal referral note is written, and the patient is transferred to a general hospital. If further specialized intervention is needed, the general hospital refers the patient up to a teaching hospital.

Continuity of care ensures that healthcare delivery remains uninterrupted as the patient transitions between home, clinics, and hospitals. Proper documentation, clear communication, and accurate handover notes are essential tools for nurses to prevent medical errors during these transitions. A breakdown in the referral pathway often leads to delayed treatment, worsening conditions, and increased mortality.

The Role of Nurses Across Different Facility Levels

Nurses form the backbone of the healthcare workforce, and their responsibilities shift depending on the facility level where they practice. At primary health centers, nurses often act as independent managers, providing immunization, health education, and maternal care with minimal doctor oversight. In secondary hospitals, nurses take on more structured ward duties, assisting with general surgical care, medical nursing, and emergency triage.

At the tertiary level, nursing roles become highly specialized. Nurses work in intensive care units, dialysis centers, and operating theatres, requiring advanced clinical skills and critical thinking. Across all these settings, the core nursing duties remain: advocating for patients, administering medications safely, monitoring vital signs, and coordinating holistic care.

Vulva Swabbing

Clinical Indications and Anatomy of the Vulvar Region

A vulvar swab is a diagnostic procedure performed to collect microbial samples from the external female genitalia. Nurses carry out this procedure when patients present with symptoms such as abnormal discharge, persistent itching, burning, unexplained lesions, or suspected sexually transmitted infections. Understanding the target anatomy ensures accurate sample collection. The vulva includes the mons pubis, labia majora, labia minora, clitoris, urethral meatus, and vaginal opening. Each of these structures has unique tissue characteristics and moisture levels that can harbor different pathogens, making precise swabbing essential for a reliable laboratory result.

Patient Communication, Consent, and Positioning

Because vulvar swabbing involves an intimate area of the body, clear communication and informed consent are paramount. The nurse must explain the procedure in simple, reassuring language, detailing why it is necessary and what the patient will feel. Privacy must be strictly maintained throughout. Position the patient in the dorsal lithotomy position, with feet supported in stirrups and knees apart, ensuring she is draped appropriately to minimize exposure and anxiety. Always allow the patient a moment to ask questions before proceeding.

Equipment Preparation and Infection Control

Gathering the correct supplies beforehand prevents delays and maintains a sterile field. Standard items include:

  • Clean examination gloves (sterile gloves may be required if palpating internal structures)
  • Pre-packaged sterile swab sticks with synthetic tips (such as dacron or rayon, as cotton can inhibit certain tests)
  • Transport medium tubes appropriate for the suspected pathogen
  • Disposable underpad to protect the examination table
  • Antiseptic wipes if cleaning gross debris is necessary

Strict infection control practices, including proper hand hygiene and universal precautions, prevent cross-contamination between patients and protect healthcare workers.

Step-by-Step Procedure for Specimen Collection

  1. Perform hand hygiene and put on clean examination gloves.
  2. Inspect the area gently, parting the labia majora with your non-dominant hand to visualize lesions, discharge, or inflamed mucosa.
  3. Avoid touching surrounding skin with the swab to prevent contaminating the sample with normal skin flora.
  4. Rotate the swab firmly across the lesion, ulcer base, or area of heavy discharge for several seconds to collect adequate cellular material.
  5. Withdraw the swab carefully without touching the patient's outer clothing or the examination table.
  6. Insert the swab immediately into the designated transport container.

Specimen Handling, Labeling, and Transport

Once the specimen is secured inside the transport tube, immediate and accurate processing is required. Label the specimen container directly at the bedside with the patient's full name, hospital identification number, date, time of collection, and anatomical site. Never label only the outer bag. Complete the laboratory requisition form with clinical details, including suspected diagnoses and any current antibiotic therapy. Transport the specimen to the laboratory promptly according to facility protocol to maintain specimen viability and ensure accurate diagnostic results.

Documentation and Post-Procedure Care

After completing the procedure, ensure the patient is comfortable, clean, and dressed. Dispose of all contaminated supplies safely in biohazard containers and perform hand hygiene. Document the procedure thoroughly in the patient's medical record. Include the date and time, anatomical site swabbed, patient tolerance, characteristics of any discharge or lesions observed, and when the specimen was sent to the laboratory. Proper documentation provides a clear clinical record and ensures continuity of care.

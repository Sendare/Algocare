Use of Bed Accessories

Introduction to Hospital Bed Accessories

Hospital bed accessories are specialized attachments and devices designed to modify standard hospital beds to meet specific therapeutic needs. In Nigerian clinical practice, where resource limitations often mean managing patients with diverse conditions on standard ward beds, understanding these accessories is vital for basic nursing care. These tools transform a simple resting surface into an active component of patient recovery, preventing complications such as pressure injuries, foot drop, and respiratory distress.

Purpose and Principles of Patient Comfort and Safety

The fundamental goal of utilizing any bed accessory is to maintain physiological alignment, promote comfort, and prevent iatrogenic injuries. Nurses must evaluate each patient individually before applying any device. The basic mechanical principles involve distributing body weight evenly, reducing shear and friction during movement, and maintaining safe joint angles. Improper application can worsen the patient's condition rather than alleviate it.

Using the Bed Cradle for Pressure and Weight Relief

A bed cradle is a rigid, arched framework placed over a patient's body to keep bedclothes from making direct contact with the skin. It is frequently indicated for clients with severe burns, painful skin lesions, open wounds, or recovering from lower extremity fractures where the weight of heavy blankets causes intolerable pain or impedes healing.

Utilizing the Air Ring for Pressure Ulcer Prevention and Management

An air ring, often referred to as a donut cushion, is a circular inflatable device placed under the buttocks to relieve pressure on the sacral and coccygeal regions. However, modern nursing consensus warns against the routine use of conventional ring cushions because they can restrict venous return and cause localized edema by creating a high-pressure ring around the targeted tissue. When utilized, they must be inflated moderately and used with strict repositioning schedules.

Supportive Devices: Backrests, Footboards, and Bed Blocks

  • Backrest: Mechanical or manual frames used to elevate the upper body, promoting lung expansion and facilitating feeding or communication.
  • Footboard: Placed perpendicular to the mattress to maintain the feet in a dorsiflexed position, actively preventing foot drop in immobile clients.
  • Bed Blocks: Wooden or plastic blocks placed beneath the bed frame legs to elevate either the head or the foot of the bed for specific postural drainage or shock management.

Safety Precautions and Maintenance of Bed Accessories

Ensuring the safety of hospital bed accessories requires routine inspection, proper cleaning, and correct installation. Nurses must check metallic frames for rust, tears in inflatable materials, and secure locking mechanisms before every shift. In Nigerian wards, where equipment may undergo frequent reuse, thorough disinfection protocols prevent cross-contamination between patients.

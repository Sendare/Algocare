Use of Bed Accessories

Introduction to Hospital Bed Accessories

Hospital bed accessories are specialized devices attached to or placed on standard hospital beds to improve patient care, safety, and clinical outcomes. These tools transform a basic resting platform into an adaptable therapeutic environment. Nurses use them daily to prevent complications of immobility, manage posture, and ease physical discomfort during long hospital stays. Understanding how each accessory functions helps you select the right tool for the right patient condition.

Purpose and Principles of Patient Comfort and Safety

Patient comfort in bed relies heavily on maintaining proper body alignment and reducing pressure points. When a person stays in bed for weeks, gravity and immobility combine to threaten skin integrity and joint mobility. Accessories act as protective buffers between the body and the hard mattress surface.

  • Alignment: Keeping the spine, hips, and limbs in a neutral, unsupported posture.
  • Pressure Redistribution: Spreading body weight across a larger surface area to protect delicate skin.
  • Friction Reduction: Minimizing shearing forces when moving clients up or down in bed.

Using the Bed Cradle for Pressure and Weight Relief

A bed cradle is a rigid metal frame placed over a patient's body under the top bedclothes. Its main job is to lift the heavy weight of sheets and blankets away from sensitive skin, toes, or surgical wounds. Nurses frequently use bed cradles for clients with severe burns, open ulcers, fragile skin grafts, or painful gout flare-ups.

When setting up a bed cradle, ensure the frame is securely anchored to the bed rails or mattress base so it cannot slip and injure the client. Check that the top covers drape loosely over the frame without creating hidden pressure points on the lower extremities.

Precautions for Utilizing Air Rings and Doughnut Cushions

Air rings, sometimes called rubber doughnuts, are circular inflatable cushions used to relieve pressure on the buttocks and sacral area. However, modern nursing practice approaches air rings with extreme caution.

Traditional circular ring cushions can actually worsen skin breakdown. When a patient sits on a ring, the inner hole creates a localized pressure drop, but the hard ring rim compresses the surrounding tissue tightly. This cuts off local blood flow and causes venous congestion in the very area you are trying to protect. If an air ring must be used, inflate it only slightly, ensure it is covered with a soft drawsheet, and limit sitting times strictly.

Supportive Devices: Backrests, Footboards, and Bed Blocks

Different postural devices help maintain body alignment while the patient rests or sits in bed.

  • Backrests: Mechanical or triangular foam supports used to elevate the upper body for eating, reading, or easing breathing difficulties.
  • Footboards: Rigid panels placed at a 90-degree angle to the soles of the feet to prevent foot drop (plantar flexion contracture).
  • Bed Blocks: Wooden or rubber blocks placed under the bed frame legs to either elevate the entire head of the bed or raise the foot of the bed for specific drainage goals.

Safety Precautions and Maintenance of Bed Accessories

Using bed accessories safely requires ongoing vigilance from the nursing team. Always inspect plastic, metal, and wooden components for cracks, sharp edges, or loose parts before placing them with a patient. Faulty equipment can cause sudden injuries or falls.

Keep all accessories clean by following standard hospital infection control protocols. Wash removable fabric covers regularly, and wipe down rigid frames with hospital-grade disinfectant between patients. Never use an accessory that appears damaged or unstable until maintenance staff clears it for use.

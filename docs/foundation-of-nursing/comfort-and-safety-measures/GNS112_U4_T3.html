Special Bed Making

Principles of Special Bed Making

Bed making is a fundamental nursing procedure designed to provide a clean, safe, and comfortable environment for clients. In hospital wards across Nigeria, mastering bed making goes beyond mere aesthetics; it directly influences patient recovery and prevents hospital-acquired complications. - **Asepsis**: Prevent cross-infection by keeping soiled linen away from the uniform and never shaking sheets in the air. - **Body Mechanics**: Maintain a wide base of support and adjust the bed height to waist level to protect the lumbar spine during the procedure. - **Wrinkle-Free Surfaces**: Smooth all sheets tightly to eliminate friction points that lead to pressure ulcers, particularly in immobile clients. - **Efficiency**: Gather all necessary equipment before starting to minimize trips and reduce energy expenditure.

General Equipment and Infection Control

A well-organized trolley or bedside chair speeds up the bed-making process and maintains strict infection control standards. Standard items include a mattress protector, bottom sheet, draw sheet, top sheet, blanket, and pillowcase. A laundry hamper or water-resistant bag must be placed nearby. Never place removed linen on the floor, as this spreads dust and pathogens across the ward environment. Always sanitize hands before handling clean linen and wear utility gloves if the previous occupant was on isolation precautions or if body fluids contaminate the bed.

Admission Bed

The admission bed, often called an unoccupied or open bed, is prepared to receive a new ambulatory client from the admitting department or outpatient clinic. The top covers are typically fan-folded to the foot or side of the bed to make it effortless for the incoming client to get in. Preparing this bed involves placing a clean mattress pad, bottom sheet, draw sheet, and top linens, ensuring the unit looks welcoming and tidy. In busy Nigerian tertiary hospitals, having admission beds ready ensures a smooth transition from the reception area to the ward.

Operation Bed

The operation bed, or post-anesthetic recovery bed, is designed for safe and swift transfer of a client returning from surgery. The primary objective is to facilitate easy transfer from a trolley and provide warmth, while protecting the bed from potential drainage or emesis. - **Top Linens**: Fan-folded to the side or bottom of the mattress opposite the entrance door. - **Protection**: A waterproof sheet and draw sheet are placed at the head and upper body area. - **Additional Items**: A kidney dish, tissues, and an IV pole are kept ready, along with extra blankets for warmth since post-operative clients often experience hypothermia.

Cardiac Bed

A cardiac bed is arranged specifically for clients experiencing severe respiratory distress or heart failure, particularly those with orthopnea. The design allows the client to sit upright comfortably without sliding down. - **Backrest**: Raised to a high Fowler's position using a mechanical crank or cardiac table. - **Support**: Several pillows are arranged behind the back and under the arms to support the shoulder girdle and expand the thorax. - **Foot Rest**: A foot board or rolled blanket is placed at the bottom to prevent the client from sliding downward.

Patient Safety, Comfort, and Ergonomics

Ensuring client safety during bed making involves raising side rails when working on the opposite side and locking all bed wheels before starting. Ergonomic principles protect the nurse from chronic back injuries caused by repeated bending. Adjust the bed to a comfortable working height whenever available. When the task is complete, return the bed to its lowest position and ensure the call bell is within the client's reach. Never compromise on these safety checks, as falls in hospital wards remain a major adverse incident.

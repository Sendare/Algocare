Interviewing and Counselling

Introduction to Interviewing and Counselling in Nursing

Interviewing and counselling are foundational skills for professional nursing practice in Nigeria, bridging the gap between clinical science and human-centered care. In nursing, an interview is a purposeful, goal-directed conversation used to gather health data, identify patient needs, and understand the client's perspective of their illness. Counselling, on the other hand, is a supportive process that helps patients cope with emotional distress, make informed health decisions, and adopt healthier lifestyle behaviors. Unlike casual conversations, professional nursing communication is strictly goal-oriented, prioritizing the patient's well-being and autonomy. Nigerian nursing students must recognize that effective interviewing and counselling require cultural competence, empathy, and a deep understanding of local healthcare contexts, including beliefs, language diversity, and socioeconomic factors prevalent in Nigeria. By mastering these competencies, nurses can foster trust, reduce patient anxiety, enhance treatment adherence, and promote holistic health outcomes across diverse clinical settings, ranging from bustling urban teaching hospitals to rural primary health care centres.

Core Communication Skills and Therapeutic Use of Self

Core communication skills and the therapeutic use of self are vital tools for every nursing student. The therapeutic use of self involves the nurse consciously using their personality, insights, perceptions, and experiences to establish a constructive relationship and structure nursing interventions. Core communication skills encompass both verbal and non-verbal techniques. Active listening is paramount; it requires paying full attention to what the patient says and implies, observing non-verbal cues such as body posture, facial expressions, and tone of voice. Open-ended questions encourage patients to elaborate on their thoughts and feelings rather than offering simple 'yes' or 'no' answers, which often limit information gathering. Clarifying, reflecting, and paraphrasing demonstrate to the patient that they are truly heard and understood, thereby validating their emotional experiences. Non-verbal communication, such as maintaining appropriate eye contact (while respecting cultural norms in Nigeria), open body posture, and a calm demeanor, reinforces reassurance and empathy. By consciously refining these skills, nurses create an environment of safety and psychological comfort, encouraging patients to share sensitive health information openly.

The Nursing Interview Process: Phases and Techniques

The nursing interview process is systematically structured into three distinct phases: the introductory/orientation phase, the working phase, and the termination phase. During the introductory phase, the nurse establishes rapport, introduces themselves, explains the purpose of the interview, and ensures patient confidentiality and comfort. This initial step is crucial for reducing patient anxiety and building trust. The working phase is the core of the interview where the nurse gathers comprehensive data, explores health concerns, and engages the patient in collaborative problem-solving. Active listening, skillful questioning, and observation are heavily utilized here. The termination phase marks the end of the interview. The nurse summarizes key findings, thanks the patient, outlines the next steps in their care or treatment plan, and provides an opportunity for final questions. Smooth transitions between these phases ensure that the patient feels respected, informed, and actively involved in their healthcare journey. Understanding these structured phases helps Nigerian nursing students conduct organized and thorough clinical assessments.

Patient Assessment and Gathering Health Histories

Patient assessment and gathering health histories are foundational responsibilities that dictate the trajectory of nursing care. The health history provides a comprehensive narrative of the patient's past and present health status, encompassing biographical data, chief complaints, history of present illness, past medical history, family history, and a review of systems. Conducting a thorough health history requires a blend of systematic inquiry and compassionate engagement. Nurses must be adept at gathering subjective data—what the patient feels, experiences, and reports—while remaining objective and non-judgmental. In the Nigerian healthcare context, it is vital to explore traditional or alternative treatments the patient may have tried before presenting to the hospital, as this impacts medication concordance and clinical management. Furthermore, assessing psychosocial and environmental factors, such as housing, economic stability, and social support systems, gives the nurse a holistic view of the patient's health determinants. Accurate health history collection ensures that subsequent nursing diagnoses and interventions are tailored, safe, and effective.

Principles and Theories of Counselling in Healthcare

Principles and theories of counselling provide the theoretical foundation that guides nurses in supporting patients through emotional and psychological challenges. Key counselling theories include Carl Rogers' Person-Centred Theory, which emphasizes unconditional positive regard, empathy, and genuineness, empowering the patient to find their own solutions. Another important framework is the Health Belief Model, which helps nurses understand why patients adopt or fail to adopt health-promoting behaviors based on perceived susceptibility, severity, benefits, and barriers. Core counselling principles mandate that the counselling session remains confidential, non-directive (guiding rather than dictating), and focused on the client's empowerment. Nurses must avoid imposing personal values or judgments on patients, especially when discussing sensitive topics such as reproductive health, chronic illnesses, or terminal diagnoses. By applying these principles, Nigerian nurses can facilitate meaningful behavior modification, encourage adherence to therapeutic regimens, and support patients through grief, lifestyle adjustments, and mental health challenges with professional competence.

Establishing a Therapeutic Nurse-Patient Relationship

Establishing a therapeutic nurse-patient relationship is the cornerstone of effective nursing practice. This specialized relationship differs from social relationships because it is goal-directed, time-limited, and centered exclusively on the patient's health needs. Peplau's Theory of Interpersonal Relations outlines four sequential phases of this relationship: orientation, identification, exploitation, and resolution. During the orientation phase, the patient seeks help, and the nurse-patient contract is established. In the identification phase, the patient begins to express feelings and respond to the healthcare team. The exploitation phase involves the patient making full use of available nursing services and active problem-solving. Finally, the resolution phase marks the successful termination of the relationship as health goals are met. Trust, respect, empathy, and professional boundaries are critical throughout these phases. Boundary maintenance ensures the nurse remains objective and protects both the patient and the nurse from emotional exploitation. Cultivating this relationship enables Nigerian nursing students to deliver compassionate, individualized care that profoundly impacts patient recovery and satisfaction.

Addressing Barriers to Effective Communication

Addressing barriers to effective communication is essential for ensuring accurate clinical assessments and successful counselling outcomes. Communication barriers can be categorized into environmental, physiological, psychological, and sociocultural factors. Environmental barriers include excessive noise, lack of privacy, and uncomfortable room temperatures in busy healthcare facilities. Physiological barriers involve sensory deficits such as hearing loss, visual impairment, aphasia, or severe pain that impairs cognitive focus. Psychological barriers encompass anxiety, fear, depression, and distrust, which can cause patients to withhold crucial information or misinterpret nursing instructions. Sociocultural barriers include language differences, differing health beliefs, stigma associated with certain diseases (such as HIV/AIDS or mental illness in Nigeria), and differing generational or gender norms. Nurses must proactively identify and mitigate these barriers by securing private interviewing spaces, utilizing interpreters when language differences exist, speaking clearly at an appropriate pace, employing assistive devices for sensory impairments, and practicing patience and cultural sensitivity. Overcoming these hurdles ensures clear, empathetic, and effective healthcare interactions.

Ethical and Legal Considerations in Counselling

Ethical and legal considerations are paramount during interviewing and counselling in nursing practice. Nurses are bound by professional codes of ethics and legal frameworks that dictate patient rights, confidentiality, and informed consent. Confidentiality means that all personal health information shared by the patient must be protected and only disclosed to members of the healthcare team directly involved in their care, or when legally mandated (such as reporting gunshot wounds or infectious disease outbreaks as required by Nigerian law). Informed consent ensures that patients understand the risks, benefits, and alternatives of any proposed intervention before agreeing to it. Nurses must also respect patient autonomy, ensuring clients have the freedom to make their own healthcare decisions without coercion. Ethical dilemmas frequently arise, such as balancing patient confidentiality with the duty to protect third parties, or respecting a patient's refusal of life-saving treatment due to cultural or religious beliefs. Nigerian nursing students must navigate these complex situations by consulting institutional policies, adhering to the Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria (NMCN) code of ethics, and maintaining unwavering professional integrity.

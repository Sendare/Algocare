Home Delivery and Family Planning Services

Introduction to Community Midwifery and Home Deliveries

Home delivery remains a significant part of maternal healthcare delivery across Nigeria, particularly in rural and semi-urban communities where access to health facilities can be limited. Midwives and community health nurses often support expectant mothers who choose or end up having planned or unplanned births outside of a formal hospital setting. While modern obstetric care strongly favors facility-based deliveries to manage unforeseen complications, community nursing requires preparedness to ensure safe deliveries wherever they occur. Nurses working in these settings bridge the gap between traditional practices and evidence-based clinical care, promoting maternal and newborn survival.

Preparation and Antenatal Risk Assessment

A successful home birth depends heavily on thorough antenatal assessment and careful planning. Nurses must identify high-risk pregnancies early—such as women with severe hypertension, multiple gestations, malpresentations, or a history of previous cesarean sections—and counsel them to deliver in a fully equipped health facility. For low-risk women planning a home birth, the nurse must ensure a clean delivery kit is prepared.

  • Clean surface: A waterproof material and clean cloths for the newborn.
  • Cord care: Clean, boiled razor blade or cord scissors and thread.
  • Infection control: Soap and clean running water for handwashing.
  • Emergency plan: Clear transportation strategy and emergency contact numbers.

Intrapartum and Immediate Newborn Care at Home

During labor at home, the nurse monitors contractions, fetal heart rate, and maternal vital signs closely using a partograph when possible. Active management of the third stage of labor is vital to prevent postpartum hemorrhage.

  1. Oxytocin administration: Give an intramuscular dose of oxytocin immediately after the baby is born and the second twin is ruled out.
  2. Controlled cord traction: Gently pull the umbilical cord while counter-pressure is applied to the uterus.
  3. Uterine massage: Massage the fundus to encourage contraction after the placenta is delivered.
  4. Newborn assessment: Clear the airway gently if needed, dry the baby thoroughly to prevent hypothermia, and initiate early skin-to-skin contact.

Managing Obstetric Emergencies in the Community

Emergencies can arise without warning during home births. The nurse must recognize danger signs early and initiate prompt stabilization and emergency transfer. Common emergencies include postpartum hemorrhage, shoulder dystocia, cord prolapse, and severe eclampsia. Delay in seeking referral is a major contributor to maternal mortality. The nurse must never hesitate to activate emergency transport while providing life-saving first aid, such as bimanual uterine compression for heavy bleeding or positioning the mother to relieve cord pressure.

Postnatal Maternal and Newborn Care

Postnatal visits in the home are essential for monitoring recovery and identifying hidden complications. The nurse assesses lochia color and amount, uterine involution, perineal healing, and vital signs. For the infant, feeding establishment, umbilical cord healing, and temperature regulation are checked. Exclusive breastfeeding for the first six months is strongly encouraged and supported. Nurses also provide emotional support, screening the mother for postpartum blues or depression, which can affect bonding and infant care.

Principles of Family Planning and Postpartum Counseling

Family planning counseling should begin during antenatal care and be reinforced during immediate postpartum home visits. Spacing pregnancies allows the mother's body to recover fully, reducing maternal and infant mortality rates. Nurses must provide unbiased, comprehensive information, respecting the couple's values and cultural background. Effective counseling involves active listening, addressing myths and misconceptions about modern contraceptives, and helping clients choose the most suitable method for their lifestyle and health status.

Contraceptive Methods Available for Community Delivery

Community health nurses can distribute several safe and effective contraceptive methods directly to homes and local clinics.

  • Barrier methods: Male and female condoms protect against both pregnancy and sexually transmitted infections.
  • Injectables: Depot medroxyprogesterone acetate provides long-acting progestin-only protection administered every three months.
  • Oral contraceptive pills: Daily hormone pills suitable for women who prefer short-acting, reversible methods.
  • Lactational Amenorrhea Method: A temporary natural method based on exclusive breastfeeding during the first six months postpartum while menses has not returned.

Ethical, Cultural, and Legal Considerations in Home Healthcare

Providing maternal and family planning services in homes requires navigating complex cultural beliefs and ethical duties. Nurses must respect patient autonomy, ensuring that women give informed consent without coercion from partners or family members, which is common in patriarchal settings. Confidentiality is paramount, especially regarding contraceptive use in communities where privacy is limited. Furthermore, nurses must practice within their legal scope of practice defined by the Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria, knowing when a case exceeds community capabilities and requires formal medical referral.

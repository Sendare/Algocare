Concept of Health Care Financing

Introduction and Core Functions of Health Care Financing

Health care financing is the process through which money is raised, pooled, and allocated to pay for health services. Without a steady flow of funds, even the best-equipped hospitals cannot function. The system ensures that health care is available and affordable when people fall sick.

There are three core functions that make up any financing system:

  1. Revenue collection: Gathering money from various sources like taxes, out-of-pocket payments, or insurance premiums.
  2. Risk pooling: Combining funds so that the financial risk of illness is shared across the whole population rather than carried by the sick individual alone.
  3. Purchasing: Using the collected funds to buy health services from public and private providers, ensuring good quality and value for money.

Sources of Health Care Funds

Money for health care comes from different pockets, and knowing these sources helps us understand how a country sustains its health sector.

  • Out-of-pocket payments: Direct cash payments made by patients to health providers at the point of service. This is often inefficient and pushes vulnerable families into poverty.
  • General government revenue: Money collected through national and state taxes, which is then budgeted for public health facilities and programs.
  • Health insurance contributions: Mandatory or voluntary payments made by individuals, employers, or both into a shared fund.
  • Donor funding and external aid: Grants and financial assistance provided by international organizations, foreign governments, and philanthropic foundations to support specific health interventions.

Mechanisms of Risk Pooling and Purchasing

Risk pooling protects individuals from catastrophic health expenditures. When healthy people contribute alongside those who are chronically ill, the pool accumulates enough capital to cover expensive treatments when needed.

Strategic purchasing changes how money is spent. Instead of just giving hospitals a blank check based on past budgets, purchasing agencies buy specific services based on quality, need, and cost-effectiveness. This encourages health facilities to improve their services, manage drugs efficiently, and treat patients with respect.

Health Insurance Systems in Nigeria

Nigeria operates a mixed health insurance system overseen at the federal level by the National Health Insurance Authority (NHIA). The goal is to reduce reliance on out-of-pocket spending and make care accessible.

The system includes formal sector programs for government and organized private sector workers, informal sector programs for market traders and artisans, and state-level health insurance schemes designed to capture vulnerable groups. Despite these structures, coverage remains low due to informal employment dominance and limited awareness.

Challenges of Health Care Financing in Developing Countries

Developing nations face structural hurdles that cripple their health financing systems. A major barrier is the large informal economy, which makes tax collection and mandatory payroll deductions difficult.

Strategies for Achieving Universal Health Coverage

Universal Health Coverage (UHC) means all people can get the quality health services they need without facing financial hardship. To reach this goal, countries must restructure how funds are raised and spent.

Key strategies include expanding mandatory health insurance to cover informal workers, increasing government budgetary allocations to meet international benchmarks like the Abuja Declaration, and subsidizing care for vulnerable groups such as pregnant women, children under five, and the elderly. Efficient resource management and plugging financial leakages are equally crucial.

The Role of Nurses in Health Care Financing and Resource Allocation

Nurses are often seen only at the bedside, but they play a strategic role in health care financing. Because they manage ward supplies, administer medications, and supervise daily unit activities, nurses directly influence hospital expenditures.

Nurses help prevent financial waste by reducing hospital-acquired infections, minimizing waste of consumables, and accurately documenting services rendered so hospitals can bill correctly. Furthermore, nurses advocate for patients by pointing out financial barriers to treatment and participating in institutional resource planning.

Types of Health Care Costs

Introduction to Health Care Costs in Nursing

Health care costs represent the total economic burden associated with the prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of disease. For nurses practicing in Nigeria, understanding these financial components is vital because resource scarcity directly impacts patient care outcomes. Financial constraints in primary, secondary, and tertiary health facilities often mean nurses must manage care with limited supplies. Recognizing how costs accumulate helps nurses advocate effectively for patients facing catastrophic out-of-pocket expenses.

Direct Medical and Non-Medical Costs

Direct costs are expenses that are explicitly traceable to a specific health condition or patient encounter. They are further divided into medical and non-medical categories.

  • Direct Medical Costs: Expenses for services delivered by health professionals, diagnostic investigations, surgeries, and pharmaceutical products.
  • Direct Non-Medical Costs: Expenses incurred by patients and families to access care, such as transportation fares to the hospital, lodging for relatives, and special nutritional items required during admission.

Indirect Costs and Productivity Losses

Indirect costs measure the economic value lost when a person cannot work or perform normal daily activities due to illness, disability, or premature death. These are hidden losses that do not involve an immediate financial transaction like paying a hospital bill. For instance, when a breadwinner is admitted to a ward for weeks, the family loses daily income, and the broader economy loses labor output. Nurses witness this impact when families struggle to purchase prescribed medications because illness has halted their livelihood.

Intangible Costs and Patient Quality of Life

Intangible costs refer to the non-financial human suffering caused by illness. They include physical pain, emotional distress, anxiety, grief, and a diminished quality of life. Unlike direct or indirect costs, intangible costs cannot be easily measured in monetary terms or assigned a standard market price. Despite this difficulty, nurses address these costs daily through compassionate bedside care, pain management, and emotional support, recognizing that suffering is just as real as financial debt.

Fixed versus Variable Costs in Facility Management

Hospital administration requires a clear understanding of operating expenses, categorized by how they respond to patient volume.

  • Fixed Costs: Expenses that remain constant regardless of the number of patients admitted, such as building rent, administrative salaries, and facility depreciation.
  • Variable Costs: Expenses that fluctuate directly with patient numbers, such as disposable syringes, intravenous fluids, gauze, and specific medications used during treatment.

Capital Costs and Operational Expenses

Capital costs involve heavy investments in long-term assets such as hospital buildings, surgical suites, and advanced diagnostic machinery like MRI scanners. These expenses are amortized over many years. In contrast, operational expenses cover day-to-day running costs, including utility bills, routine equipment maintenance, and nursing staff wages. Balancing these two financial demands is essential for maintaining hospital solvency and ensuring continuous patient service delivery.

Implications of Health Care Costs for Nursing Practice and Patient Advocacy

High health care costs directly affect clinical decision-making and patient compliance in Nigeria. When patients face steep out-of-pocket payments, they may delay seeking care or request early discharge before full recovery. Nurses serve as vital advocates by identifying cost-effective alternatives, connecting vulnerable patients to social welfare services, and minimizing waste of medical supplies. Understanding the economic realities of care empowers nurses to promote health equity and safeguard patient well-being.

Roles of Primary Health Care Workers

Introduction to the Primary Health Care Workforce in Nigeria

Primary Health Care (PHC) forms the bedrock of the health system in Nigeria, serving as the first point of contact for individuals, families, and communities with the national health system. The success of this tier relies heavily on a multi-disciplinary team of health workers operating primarily at the local government level through designated health posts, primary health clinics, and comprehensive health centres. Unlike tertiary facilities dominated by specialist physicians and advanced technologies, the PHC workforce focuses on preventive, promotive, curative, and rehabilitative services tailored to grassroots needs. Workforce composition varies widely across the federation, influenced by federal policies, state-level implementation, and rural-urban migration patterns that often leave remote settlements underserved.

Community Health Officers and Public Health Nurses

Community Health Officers (CHOs) and Public Health Nurses (PHNs) occupy supervisory and management roles within Nigerian primary health facilities. CHOs act as the clinical and administrative heads of many primary health centres, coordinating routine service delivery, managing facility logistics, and bridging the gap between community needs and local government health departments. Public Health Nurses focus intensely on population-level health indices, managing immunization schedules, conducting maternal and child welfare clinics, and spearheading epidemiological surveillance for preventable childhood diseases. Both cadres are essential for translating national health policies into operational reality at the facility level.

Community Health Extension Workers

  • Senior Community Health Extension Workers (SCHEWs): Completed higher-level training enabling them to diagnose and treat common minor ailments, conduct normal deliveries, provide family planning services, and supervise junior cadres.
  • Junior Community Health Extension Workers (JCHEWs): Focus on home visiting, basic health education, growth monitoring, recording vital health statistics, and assisting senior colleagues during clinic days.
  • Clinical Scope: Both cadres form the largest numerical block of primary health workers in Nigeria, bearing the heaviest burden of direct community outreach and basic curative care.

Midwives and Maternal Care Providers

Midwives in the primary health care system specialize in antenatal care, intrapartum monitoring, normal spontaneous vaginal deliveries, and postnatal follow-up. Programs such as the Midwife Service Scheme (MSS) were historically deployed to bridge severe maternal mortality gaps in rural Nigerian communities. Beyond delivering babies, midwives identify high-risk pregnancies that exceed the clinical capacity of a primary health clinic, ensuring timely referral to secondary facilities. They also provide essential family planning counselling and neonatal resuscitation services immediately after birth.

Community-Level Actors: Village Health Workers and Traditional Birth Attendants

Community-level actors bridge the cultural and physical distance between formal health institutions and rural populations. Village Health Workers (VHWs) are chosen by their own communities to receive basic training in hygiene promotion, simple first aid, and reporting infectious disease outbreaks. Traditional Birth Attendants (TBAs) are indigenous practitioners who have historically attended deliveries within communities. Modern primary health strategies focus on training and integrating TBAs to recognize danger signs during pregnancy and collaborate with formal health facilities rather than attempting to prohibit their practice entirely.

Interprofessional Collaboration and Referral Systems

No single cadre can deliver comprehensive health care in isolation. Effective primary health care requires seamless communication and role clarity among physicians, nurses, CHOs, extension workers, and community volunteers. When a patient presents with conditions surpassing primary clinic capabilities—such as severe pre-eclampsia, obstructed labor, or severe dehydration unresponsive to oral rehydration therapy—the referral system is activated. A functional referral protocol ensures clear documentation, stabilization before transport, and feedback loops between primary health clinics and secondary general hospitals.

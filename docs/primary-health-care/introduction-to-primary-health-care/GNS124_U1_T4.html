Elements of Primary Health Care

Introduction to Primary Health Care and the Alma-Ata Declaration

Primary Health Care (PHC) is the bedrock of the health system in Nigeria, designed to bring healthcare as close as possible to where people live and work. The formal framework was established during the international conference in Alma-Ata in 1978. PHC shifts the focus from expensive hospital-based treatment to community-driven wellness and prevention. It relies on full community participation and intersectoral collaboration, meaning agriculture, education, and housing ministries all play a role in health outcomes. In the Nigerian context, PHC is delivered primarily through designated primary health centers in rural and urban-slum communities.

Education Concerning Prevailing Health Problems and Methods of Prevention

Health education is the first essential element of PHC because informed communities can better protect themselves. Nurses translate complex medical data into clear, actionable messages for local populations.

  • Targeted Messaging: Focusing health talks on seasonal outbreaks like cholera or Lassa fever.
  • Community Outreach: Utilizing village squares, markets, and churches to reach vulnerable groups.
  • Behavioral Change: Teaching handwashing, safe waste disposal, and mosquito net usage.

Promotion of Food Supply and Proper Nutrition

Nutrition underpins physical growth, cognitive development, and immune defense, particularly in infants and pregnant women. In many Nigerian communities, food insecurity and cultural food taboos contribute heavily to malnutrition.

An Adequate Supply of Safe Water and Basic Sanitation

Waterborne diseases like cholera, typhoid, and diarrhea thrive where safe drinking water and proper excreta disposal are absent. Nurses promote environmental health by inspecting water sources and teaching household water purification methods such as boiling or chlorination. Basic sanitation involves constructing VIP latrines, preventing open defecation, and maintaining clean drainage systems to discourage breeding sites for disease vectors.

Maternal and Child Health Care, Including Family Planning

Maternal and child health (MCH) targets the most vulnerable demographics in any population. Antenatal care (ANC) allows nurses to monitor pregnancies, detect complications early, and administer tetanus toxoid vaccines and intermittent preventive treatment for malaria. Family planning services help space births, reducing maternal exhaustion and infant mortality. Postnatal care ensures proper recovery for the mother and tracks developmental milestones for the newborn.

Immunization Against Major Infectious Diseases

Immunization is one of the most cost-effective public health interventions. The Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) protects children against vaccine-preventable diseases such as tuberculosis, poliomyelitis, diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, measles, and yellow fever. Maintaining the cold chain is critical in tropical settings like Nigeria to preserve vaccine potency from the central medical store down to remote rural health posts.

Prevention and Control of Locally Endemic Diseases

Endemic diseases like malaria, tuberculosis, and schistosomiasis impose a constant burden on Nigerian communities. PHC strategies focus on vector control, early case detection, and proper treatment compliance. Nurses conduct active surveillance in communities, ensuring that suspected outbreaks are reported promptly to local health authorities to mount a swift response.

Appropriate Treatment of Common Diseases and Injuries

Primary health centers must be equipped to manage everyday medical complaints and minor trauma effectively. This includes treating uncomplicated malaria, acute respiratory infections, simple gastroenteritis, and lacerations. Nurses utilize standardized standing orders and protocol guidelines to assess patients, provide first-line treatment, and recognize warning signs that require urgent referral to secondary or tertiary facilities.

Provision of Essential Drugs and Supplies

A primary health facility cannot function without a reliable stock of essential medicines. These include antimalarials, oral rehydration salts, antibiotics, analgesics, and oxytocics. The Essential Drugs List ensures that limited resources are spent on medicines of proven safety, efficacy, and cost-effectiveness. In Nigeria, revolving drug funds are often used to maintain continuous stock availability and prevent out-of-stock syndromes.

The Role of the Nurse in Implementing PHC Elements in Nigeria

Community health nurses and midwives are the frontline workforce driving PHC implementation across Nigeria. Their roles span clinical care, health advocacy, home visiting, supervision of community health extension workers (CHEWs), and liaising with local leaders. By working directly with village health committees, nurses bridge the gap between formal health policy and grassroots realities, ensuring that healthcare remains accessible, acceptable, and affordable to all.

Elements of Primary Health Care

Introduction to Primary Health Care and the Alma-Ata Declaration

Primary Health Care (PHC) is the bedrock of community health services. It brings essential care as close as possible to where people live and work. The journey of modern PHC formally began at the historic Alma-Ata Declaration in 1978. Representatives from across the globe gathered in Kazakhstan to declare that health is a fundamental human right. They agreed that governments must protect the health of all citizens, especially the vulnerable. PHC is not just about treating the sick. It focuses on disease prevention, health promotion, and community participation. In Nigeria, the PHC system operates primarily at the local government level through primary health centers. This approach ensures that even remote villages have access to basic, affordable medical services without traveling miles to a tertiary hospital.

Education Concerning Prevailing Health Problems and Methods of Prevention

Knowledge is a powerful tool in public health. When communities understand why diseases happen, they can take active steps to stop them. Nurses play a major role in teaching people about common local illnesses like malaria, cholera, and Lassa fever. Effective health education avoids complicated medical jargon. Instead, it uses simple local languages, pictures, and community meetings.

  • Malaria Education: Teaching families to clear stagnant water and sleep under treated nets.
  • Hygiene Education: Demonstrating proper handwashing with soap before eating and after using the toilet.
  • Diarrhea Prevention: Showing mothers how to prepare oral rehydration salt solutions at home.

Promotion of Food Supply and Proper Nutrition

Good health starts with proper nourishment. Malnutrition weakens the immune system, making children easy targets for infections like measles and diarrhea. PHC addresses nutrition by teaching families how to make the best use of locally available food crops. Nurses do not just tell people to eat well; they show mothers how to combine local grains and legumes to create balanced meals for growing children. Community agricultural programs and backyard farming initiatives are also encouraged to boost food security. In local clinics, nurses monitor children's growth using weight charts to catch early signs of faltering nutrition before severe wasting occurs.

An Adequate Supply of Safe Water and Basic Sanitation

Many tropical diseases are water-borne, spreading rapidly when communities lack clean drinking water and proper waste disposal. Providing safe water and basic sanitation is a cornerstone of preventive medicine. Nurses educate communities on simple water purification methods such as boiling, filtration, and adding chemical purifiers when clean tap water is unavailable. Proper sanitation involves constructing and using safe pit latrines away from water sources, as well as managing household refuse correctly to keep disease-carrying vectors like flies and mosquitoes away from living areas.

Maternal and Child Health Care, Including Family Planning

Maternal and child health services protect the most vulnerable members of society. Pregnancy and childbirth should be safe journeys, not life-threatening events. PHC ensures that pregnant women receive regular antenatal care, tetanus toxoid injections, and routine screening for infections like malaria and HIV. Child care involves monitoring growth, providing micronutrient supplements, and managing childhood illnesses promptly. Family planning is integrated into this element to help parents space their children properly, allowing mothers to recover their physical strength and ensuring adequate resources for every child born.

Immunization Against Major Infectious Diseases

Immunization is one of the most cost-effective public health interventions ever created. Vaccines train the human immune system to fight off dangerous pathogens without causing the actual disease. The Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) provides protection against childhood killers such as polio, measles, tuberculosis, pertussis, and diphtheria. Nurses are central to this effort, managing vaccine cold chains, conducting outreach sessions in remote villages, and educating parents on the importance of completing the full vaccination schedule for their infants.

Prevention and Control of Locally Endemic Diseases

Endemic diseases are infections that remain constantly present within a specific geographic region or population. In Nigeria and much of West Africa, malaria, tuberculosis, and neglected tropical diseases require constant community-level vigilance. PHC focuses on active surveillance, early diagnosis, and vector control. Nurses work with community volunteers to distribute long-lasting insecticidal nets, track tuberculosis treatment compliance through Directly Observed Therapy, and report unusual outbreaks of diseases like cholera to public health authorities immediately.

Appropriate Treatment of Common Diseases and Injuries

Not every health issue requires a trip to a large teaching hospital. Primary health centers are equipped to provide first-line treatment for everyday illnesses and minor injuries. Nurses assess patients, treat simple fevers, manage minor cuts and burns, and recognize danger signs that require urgent referral to a doctor or hospital. Providing prompt, effective care at the grassroots level reduces suffering, prevents complications, and builds community trust in the local health system.

Provision of Essential Drugs and Supplies

A health clinic cannot function effectively without the necessary tools and medications. The essential drugs concept ensures that life-saving medicines are available at all times in adequate amounts, in appropriate dosage forms, and at a price the community can afford. Common essential medicines include anti-malarials, antibiotics, analgesics, oral rehydration salts, and vaccines. Nurses manage clinic inventory, prevent stock-outs, ensure proper storage conditions, and check expiration dates regularly to guarantee patient safety.

The Role of the Nurse in Implementing PHC Elements in Nigeria

Nurses are the backbone of Primary Health Care delivery in Nigeria. Operating in rural and urban primary health centers, nurses wear many hats: clinician, educator, manager, and community advocate. They coordinate immunization campaigns, conduct antenatal clinics, manage disease outbreaks, and train local community health extension workers. Beyond clinical tasks, nurses build bridges between formal health systems and traditional community structures. By working closely with local leaders and families, nurses ensure that health programs are culturally accepted, sustainable, and truly responsive to the needs of the Nigerian population.

Elements of Primary Health Care

Foundation and Principles of Primary Health Care

Primary Health Care represents the core of a country's health system, serving as the first level of contact individuals, families, and communities have with national health services. Formally launched at the international conference in Kazakhstan, the landmark Alma-Ata Declaration of 1978 defined health not merely as the absence of disease, but as a complete state of physical, mental, and social well-being. This historic document established health as a fundamental human right and positioned social justice at the center of public health planning. Universal accessibility, community participation, intersectoral collaboration, and the use of appropriate technology form the four foundational pillars that operationalize these ideals in everyday clinical and community settings.

Health Education and Prevention Strategies

Health education serves as the initial, vital element of Primary Health Care, empowering communities to take charge of their own wellbeing. By focusing on prevailing local health issues and practical prevention methods, health workers bridge the gap between clinical knowledge and community practice.

  • Communicable Disease Awareness: Teaching families how infections spread and how simple hygiene habits break transmission chains.
  • Lifestyle Modification: Promoting physical activity, balanced diets, and smoking cessation to lower non-communicable disease risks.
  • Environmental Health Education: Guiding households on safe garbage disposal, vector control, and protecting water sources.

In Nigerian rural postings, nurses often utilize market squares, village square meetings, and church or mosque gatherings to deliver these messages in local languages.

Food Supply and Nutritional Promotion

Adequate nutrition is inextricably linked to survival, growth, and national development. Malnutrition weakens immune responses, turning minor childhood infections into fatal conditions. Primary Health Care addresses this by promoting sustainable agricultural practices, homestead gardening, and proper dietary habits tailored to locally available food crops.

Nurses teach mothers how to combine staple cereals with legumes to achieve a balanced amino acid profile, particularly in communities where animal protein is scarce or expensive. Growth monitoring sessions at primary health clinics help track infant development, allowing early detection of protein-energy malnutrition like kwashiorkor and marasmus before irreversible physical and cognitive stunting occurs.

Water, Sanitation, and Environmental Health

Waterborne pathogens are responsible for a massive burden of morbidity and mortality globally, particularly among children under five years of age. An adequate supply of safe drinking water and basic sanitation forms the bedrock of preventive medicine. Simple interventions—such as constructing VIP (ventilated improved pit) latrines away from water tables, boiling drinking water, and practicing rigorous hand hygiene—drastically cut down the transmission of cholera, typhoid, and diarrheal illnesses. Community health nurses frequently lead sanitation campaigns to inspect residential surroundings, clear stagnant drainage ditches that breed mosquitoes, and ensure proper refuse disposal sites are maintained.

Maternal, Child Health, and Family Planning

Maternal and child health services safeguard the most vulnerable segments of the population. Pregnancy and childbirth should be safe passages rather than life-threatening events. Comprehensive PHC delivery includes antenatal care, safe intrapartum delivery attended by skilled birth attendants, postnatal monitoring, and comprehensive immunization schedules.

Family planning is integrated into this framework to allow couples to space pregnancies adequately, reducing maternal exhaustion, high-risk parity, and infant mortality rates. Midwives and nurses at primary care centers provide education on modern contraceptive choices, screen for obstetric complications, and ensure timely referral to secondary facilities when obstructed labor or severe pre-eclampsia arises.

Immunization Against Major Infectious Diseases

Immunization remains one of the most cost-effective public health interventions ever devised. By stimulating active immunity against vaccine-preventable diseases—such as poliomyelitis, measles, diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, and tuberculosis—health systems protect both individuals and the wider community through herd immunity. The Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) forms a core pillar of routine primary healthcare delivery across Nigeria. Cold chain maintenance is an absolute clinical priority; vaccines must be stored within strict temperature ranges from the central depot down to the solar-powered refrigerator at the rural health post, otherwise their antigenic potency is irreversibly lost.

Prevention and Control of Locally Endemic Diseases

Endemic diseases—such as malaria, onchocerciasis, schistosomiasis, and tuberculosis—place a crushing socioeconomic and health burden on tropical populations. Primary Health Care tackles these conditions through localized control measures rather than waiting for patients to reach advanced, complicated stages. Vector control strategies, such as distributing insecticide-treated nets (ITNs) and clearing brush near homesteads, drastically reduce malaria transmission. Active case finding, Directly Observed Treatment Short-course (DOTS) for tuberculosis, and mass drug administration for neglected tropical diseases ensure that community-level surveillance keeps local outbreaks under control.

Treatment of Common Diseases and Injuries

Even with robust prevention, illnesses and accidental injuries occur daily in every community. Primary Health Care ensures that first-line diagnosis and treatment for common ailments—such as uncomplicated malaria, acute respiratory infections, minor wounds, and simple gastroenteritis—are readily available at the grassroots level. This element relies on standard treatment guidelines, basic diagnostic tools like malaria rapid diagnostic tests (RDTs), and prompt referral protocols. Managing minor traumas and common infections locally prevents trivial health problems from deteriorating into catastrophic emergencies that overwhelm secondary and tertiary health institutions.

Provision of Essential Drugs and Supplies

A primary health clinic is ineffective if its shelves are empty of basic therapeutics. The essential drugs concept focuses on making a carefully selected list of life-saving medications available at all times, in adequate quantities, and at an affordable cost. These medicines—including oral rehydration salts, antimalarials, oxytocin, basic analgesics, and essential antibiotics—address the vast majority of public health needs. Effective supply chain management, transparent inventory control, and avoiding stock-outs are critical administrative responsibilities for nurses managing primary health facilities in Nigeria.

The Role of the Nurse in Implementing PHC in Nigeria

Nurses and midwives form the backbone of primary health care implementation across Nigeria's 774 Local Government Areas. Operating within primary health care centers (PHCCs) and community outreach posts, the nurse acts as a clinician, educator, administrator, and community advocate. Responsibilities range from conducting antenatal clinics and immunizing infants to supervising Community Health Extension Workers (CHEWs) and mobilizing village health committees. Navigating cultural beliefs, overcoming logistical hurdles in hard-to-reach riverine or nomadic settlements, and bridging the gap between western medicine and community traditions require exceptional leadership and resilience from Nigerian nurses.

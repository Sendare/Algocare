Women Empowerment and Decision Making

Understanding Women Empowerment in Healthcare

Women empowerment is the process where women gain power, control over their own lives, and the ability to make strategic life choices. In nursing and public health, this means giving women the knowledge, confidence, and authority to decide about their own bodies, healthcare needs, and family welfare. When a woman is empowered, she does not just wait for instructions; she actively participates in wellness planning, disease prevention, and treatment adherence. Health systems cannot achieve universal coverage if half the population lacks the autonomy to seek care without seeking permission first. True empowerment bridges the gap between available health facilities and the women who actually need to use them.

Socio-Cultural and Economic Determinants of Autonomy

A woman's ability to make health decisions is heavily shaped by her surrounding society and financial standing. Cultural traditions often dictate that men hold absolute authority over household resources, leaving women financially dependent and unable to pay for transportation to a clinic or prescribed medications. Education acts as a powerful equalizer. Girls who complete secondary school marry later, earn their own income, and understand basic hygiene and nutrition. Conversely, poverty and harmful traditional practices create layers of dependency that suppress a woman's voice during medical emergencies.

Maternal and Child Health Decision Making

Maternal survival often hinges on who holds the power to make quick choices during labor complications. If a pregnant woman recognizes danger signs like severe bleeding or blurred vision, but must wait for her husband or mother-in-law to authorize a hospital trip, precious time is lost. This delay contributes heavily to maternal mortality rates. Empowering women means ensuring they recognize obstetric emergencies and have a voice in planning their place of delivery, antenatal attendance, and childhood immunizations.

Barriers to Women Empowerment in Local Communities

Deep-seated patriarchal norms remain a stubborn obstacle to gender equality and health autonomy across many Nigerian communities. Belief systems often frame submission as a virtue, discouraging women from questioning medical advice given by elders or husbands. Fear of domestic violence or social ostracism keeps many women silent. Additionally, religious misinterpretations sometimes restrict women from utilizing modern family planning methods or seeking care from male healthcare providers, compounding their health vulnerabilities.

Community-Based Strategies and the Role of the Nurse

  • Health Education: Nurses must conduct grassroots awareness campaigns that educate both men and women on the benefits of shared household decision-making.
  • Women Support Groups: Establishing local cooperatives helps women pool financial resources, fostering economic independence and mutual health support.
  • Involving Male Champions: Engaging community leaders, husbands, and religious heads helps dismantle harmful traditions from within.
  • Inpatient Advocacy: Nurses must deliberately direct health counseling toward the primary caregiver—often the mother—while respecting cultural contexts.

Guidance and Counselling

Introduction and Definition of Guidance and Counselling

Guidance and counselling form an integral pillar of community health and primary healthcare delivery in Nigeria. Guidance is a broad, continuous process centered on helping individuals understand themselves, navigate educational and vocational choices, and make wise life decisions. It is largely preventative and developmental, often involving the provision of information and directional advice.

Counselling, conversely, is a more specialized, interpersonal, and confidential relationship between a trained professional and a client. It dives deeper into emotional, psychological, or behavioral challenges that impede a person's well-being. While guidance tells or points the way, counselling helps the client explore their inner thoughts to find their own solutions. Both processes share the ultimate goal of fostering self-reliance, emotional resilience, and holistic health among individuals, families, and communities.

Core Principles and Ethics in Counselling

Ethical practice ensures trust, safety, and respect for human dignity during healthcare interactions. Community nurses must anchor every counselling session in established bioethical and professional principles.

  • Confidentiality: Safeguarding all client disclosures. Information shared remains private unless there is a clear legal or safety mandate to breach it, such as active child abuse or imminent harm to self or others.
  • Autonomy: Respecting the client's right to self-determination. The nurse facilitates decision-making without coercion or imposing personal beliefs.
  • Beneficence: Acting in the best interest of the client, ensuring that interventions promote healing and overall well-being.
  • Non-maleficence: Doing no harm. Avoiding dual relationships, judgmental attitudes, or sharing unverified advice that could mislead the patient.

The Counselling Process and Stages

Effective counselling follows a structured yet flexible progression to ensure the client moves from distress to resolution.

  1. Building the Relationship: Establishing rapport, creating a welcoming environment, and assuring confidentiality to reduce initial anxiety.
  2. Assessment and Exploration: Encouraging the client to articulate their concerns, gathering relevant history, and identifying core issues.
  3. Goal Setting: Collaboratively defining what the client hopes to achieve by the end of the counselling sessions.
  4. Intervention and Action: Exploring coping mechanisms, discussing behavior changes, and implementing practical strategies.
  5. Termination and Follow-up: Reviewing progress made, consolidating gains, and scheduling a follow-up check to prevent relapse.

Communication Skills and Techniques

Mastering therapeutic communication is essential for extracting accurate health histories and fostering emotional healing. Nurses must utilize both verbal and non-verbal tools.

  • Active Listening: Concentrating fully on what the client says, acknowledging feelings, and observing body language without interrupting.
  • Open-Ended Questions: Posing inquiries that require more than a simple "yes" or "no" answer, such as "How has this illness affected your daily routine?"
  • Reflecting and Paraphrasing: Restating the client's core message in the nurse's own words to verify understanding and show empathy.
  • Silence: Allowing strategic pauses for the client to process complex emotions and gather their thoughts.

Role of the Primary Health Care Nurse in Counselling

Primary Health Care (PHC) nurses in Nigeria operate at the grassroots level, making them frontline agents for health promotion and preventive counseling. Their scope encompasses maternal and child health, routine immunization advocacy, nutritional education, family planning, and mental health support within local communities.

Beyond clinical duties, PHC nurses act as bridges between vulnerable populations and specialized health resources. They identify psychosocial distress early during routine antenatal care or home visits, providing immediate basic counseling and executing timely referrals for specialized psychiatric or social welfare services when complex needs exceed primary care capabilities.

Challenges and Barriers to Effective Counselling

Counselling within resource-constrained environments faces numerous practical hurdles that can compromise clinical effectiveness.

  • Cultural Beliefs and Stigma: Deeply ingrained cultural misconceptions surrounding mental health, witchcraft, or personal weakness often prevent individuals from seeking professional help openly.
  • Language Barriers: Nigeria's linguistic diversity can hinder clear communication if the nurse and client do not share a fluent common language, risking misunderstandings.
  • Time Constraints and Workload: Overburdened primary health facilities with high patient-to-nurse ratios leave very little time for in-depth, unhurried counseling sessions.
  • Lack of Privacy: Open-plan or overcrowded clinic spaces make true confidentiality difficult to maintain, eroding client trust.

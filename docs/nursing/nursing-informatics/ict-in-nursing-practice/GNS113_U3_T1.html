Technology Trends Applied to Nursing

Introduction to Nursing Informatics in Nigeria

Nursing informatics merges nursing science, computer science, and information science to manage and communicate data, information, knowledge, and wisdom in nursing practice. Across Nigerian healthcare facilities, the shift from bulky paper records to digital platforms is slowly gaining momentum. This digital transformation aims to reduce human error, streamline patient handovers, and secure vital health data against physical loss or damage. Nurses serve as the primary bridge between clinical care and these emerging technological tools, making baseline computer literacy a necessary skill for modern practice.

Mobile Health (mHealth) Applications

Mobile health utilizes smartphones, tablets, and personal digital assistants to support clinical workflows and public health interventions. In resource-limited settings across Nigeria, mHealth bridges geographic gaps between urban specialists and rural community health workers. Nurses use mobile apps for rapid drug calculations, accessing clinical guidelines, and tracking immunization schedules in remote villages. Text message reminders sent to patients improve appointment adherence and reduce missed antenatal care visits.

Wearable Health Devices and Remote Monitoring

  • Smartwatches and Fitness Bands: Track daily physical activity, sleep patterns, and continuous heart rates.
  • Continuous Glucose Monitors: Small sensors placed under the skin that transmit real-time blood sugar readings to a mobile device.
  • Smart Blood Pressure Cuffs: Bluetooth-enabled cuffs allowing hypertensive patients to log and share blood pressure trends with clinic staff.

These gadgets empower patients to take charge of chronic disease management while giving nurses access to continuous physiological data rather than isolated spot-checks during hospital visits.

Telehealth and Telemedicine in Clinical Practice

Telehealth encompasses remote clinical services, health education, and administrative meetings facilitated by telecommunications technology. Telemedicine specifically refers to remote clinical diagnoses and treatments. In Nigeria, telehealth connects patients in rural northern or riverine southern communities with specialists located in tertiary teaching hospitals. Nurses play a crucial role during telehealth sessions by preparing the patient, operating remote diagnostic peripherals, and ensuring accurate vital signs are transmitted before the virtual consultation begins.

Artificial Intelligence and Clinical Decision Support

Artificial intelligence (AI) systems process massive volumes of healthcare data to identify patterns, predict patient deterioration, and suggest evidence-based interventions. Clinical Decision Support Systems (CDSS) embedded within electronic health records alert nurses to potential medication allergies, abnormal lab values, or impending pressure ulcer risks. While these tools enhance patient safety, nurses must apply critical thinking to AI recommendations rather than following automated alerts blindly, as algorithms can occasionally generate false positives.

Electronic Health Records and Interoperability

Electronic Health Records (EHRs) replace paper charts with comprehensive digital profiles containing patient history, diagnostics, and medication administration records. Interoperability refers to the ability of different software systems and hardware devices to communicate and exchange data seamlessly. In many Nigerian hospitals, standalone computer systems cannot share information with one another, creating digital silos. Achieving true interoperability ensures that a patient's treatment history follows them safely from an emergency room to the intensive care unit without data loss.

Challenges and Ethical Considerations in Nursing Informatics

Integrating technology into nursing practice introduces significant challenges, including data privacy breaches, high implementation costs, and resistance to change among older staff. Patient confidentiality remains paramount; nurses must safeguard digital passwords and never leave patient terminals unlocked in public view. Furthermore, erratic electricity supply and poor internet connectivity across parts of Nigeria pose daily operational hurdles. Addressing these barriers requires ongoing staff training, robust cybersecurity protocols, and reliable power backup systems in healthcare facilities.

Definition of Conflict

Introduction to Conflict in Healthcare Settings

Conflict is an inevitable reality in busy healthcare environments like Nigerian hospitals and clinics. It arises whenever two or more people interact and their goals, values, or opinions clash. In nursing practice, daily pressures, heavy workloads, and resource shortages frequently trigger friction among healthcare professionals. Recognizing that tension exists is the first step toward effective resolution.

Defining Conflict: Core Concepts and Terminology

At its core, conflict represents a dynamic state of disagreement or opposition between individuals or groups. It involves perceived threats to an individual's needs, interests, or concerns. Nurses must understand that conflict is not always destructive; constructive disagreement often stimulates creative problem-solving and improves team performance.

Intrapersonal versus Interpersonal Conflict

  • Intrapersonal conflict: Occurs within an individual. A nurse might experience this when facing moral distress, such as caring for a patient when resources are too scarce to provide optimal care.
  • Interpersonal conflict: Occurs between two or more people. This is common when a nurse and a physician disagree on a patient's care plan or when two colleagues clash over shift rosters.

Intragroup and Intergroup Conflict in Nursing Practice

  • Intragroup conflict: Happens among members of the same group, such as nursing staff within a specific ward disagreeing on how to delegate tasks.
  • Intergroup conflict: Occurs between different departments or teams, such as friction between the emergency department and the intensive care unit over patient bed admissions.

Stages of the Conflict Process

  1. Latent conflict: Conditions are ripe for disagreement, even if no open dispute has started yet, such as chronic understaffing.
  2. Perceived conflict: Staff members recognize that a problem or opposing viewpoint exists.
  3. Felt conflict: Emotional involvement begins, bringing feelings of anger, frustration, or hostility.
  4. Manifest conflict: Behavior emerges, visible as arguments, uncooperative actions, or verbal disputes.
  5. Conflict aftermath: The final resolution leaves lasting positive or negative effects on relationships.

The Impact of Conflict on Patient Care Outcomes

Unresolved workplace conflict directly compromises patient safety. When nurses spend energy fighting or ignoring colleagues, communication breakdowns occur. Vital handover details get missed, medication errors increase, and patient recovery slows down. Cultivating a collaborative environment is therefore a clinical necessity, not just an administrative preference.

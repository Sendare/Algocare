Assertive Behaviour

Introduction to Assertive Behaviour in Nursing

Assertive behaviour in nursing involves clearly expressing one's professional needs, thoughts, and clinical judgments while maintaining respect for the rights and opinions of patients, doctors, and fellow nurses. Unlike aggressive communication that overrides others, or passive communication that suppresses personal views, assertiveness establishes a balanced, confident dialogue. In busy Nigerian clinical environments where hierarchical structures can sometimes silence junior staff, clear and professional self-expression safeguards patient safety and promotes professional accountability.

Comparing Assertiveness with Passivity, Aggression, and Passive-Aggression

  • Assertiveness: Direct, honest, and respectful expression of ideas and boundaries.
  • Passivity: Ignoring one's own needs, failing to speak up, and constantly yielding to others to avoid conflict.
  • Aggression: Violating the boundaries of others through shouting, belittling, or demanding compliance without regard for their feelings.
  • Passive-Aggression: Expressing negative feelings indirectly through sarcasm, stubbornness, or silent treatment rather than open dialogue.

Professional Benefits and Clinical Impact of Assertiveness

When nurses communicate assertively, patient outcomes improve significantly because critical safety concerns are communicated without delay. Professional confidence grows, reducing burnout and emotional exhaustion linked to bottled-up frustration. Clear communication also minimizes medication administration errors and misinterpretations of medical orders. Collaborative teamwork strengthens when every multidisciplinary team member feels valued and heard during shift handovers and patient reviews.

Barriers to Assertive Communication in Healthcare Settings

Cultural norms in many Nigerian communities heavily emphasize absolute obedience to seniority and age, which can inadvertently spill over into clinical practice and discourage junior nurses from questioning unsafe orders from senior doctors or nursing officers. Fear of victimization, intimidation, and poor institutional support for whistleblowers further reinforce silence. Additionally, a lack of formal training in conflict resolution leaves many clinicians unsure of how to voice concerns professionally.

Core Techniques for Practicing Assertive Behaviour

  1. I-Statements: Focus on personal perspective using 'I feel' or 'I need' rather than accusatory 'You' statements.
  2. Broken Record Technique: Calmly repeating a valid clinical request or boundary multiple times without becoming aggressive.
  3. Fogging: Acknowledging a critic's truth partially while keeping your own calm stance without defensive argument.
  4. DEAL Formula: Describe the situation, Express feelings, Ask for changes, and List consequences.

Managing Conflict and Resistance Professionally

Conflict is inevitable in fast-paced healthcare environments involving diverse professionals. Assertive conflict management involves addressing issues early before minor disagreements escalate into hostile disputes. Nurses must stick to objective clinical facts rather than personal attacks during arguments. Listening actively to opposing viewpoints demonstrates professional maturity and helps find common ground that prioritizes patient well-being.

Delegation of Responsibility

Introduction to Delegation in Nursing

Delegation allows a registered nurse to transfer the performance of a specific nursing task to competent unlicensed or licensed personnel while retaining professional accountability for the overall outcome. With rising patient loads in Nigerian healthcare facilities, effective task sharing ensures efficient utilization of skilled nursing manpower. The registered nurse remains responsible for assessing the patient, deciding to delegate, evaluating the outcome, and ensuring the task was completed safely.

The Five Rights of Delegation

Safe task transfer relies on adherence to five standard parameters that guide clinical decision-making. Ignoring any single right compromises patient safety and breaches professional nursing standards.

  • Right Task: Activities that are non-complex and carry minimal risk for the specific patient.
  • Right Circumstance: Appropriate patient setting, available resources, and relevant clinical stability.
  • Right Person: Verifying that the designated worker possesses the requisite training and proven competence.
  • Right Direction/Communication: Providing a clear, concise description of the task, including expected results and timelines.
  • Right Supervision/Evaluation: Monitoring the worker, intervening if necessary, and assessing the completed work.

Roles and Responsibilities in the Delegation Process

Every member of the healthcare team carries distinct duties during task allocation. The registered nurse must perform initial patient assessments, match tasks to personnel capabilities, and provide ongoing guidance. Conversely, the delegatee accepts the responsibility within their scope of practice, performs the task diligently, and reports abnormal findings promptly back to the nurse. Mutual communication sustains patient safety and professional trust on the ward.

Factors Affecting Delegation Decisions

Clinical judgment dictates whether an assignment is appropriate. Nurses must evaluate patient acuity, environmental stability, staff skill mix, and the predictability of the outcome. A newly admitted patient experiencing acute breathlessness requires direct professional intervention, whereas a stable patient awaiting discharge can safely receive routine support care from auxiliary staff.

Barriers and Challenges to Effective Delegation

Several obstacles frequently disrupt smooth task transfer in clinical settings. Registered nurses often struggle with over-supervision due to perfectionism, or conversely, engage in under-supervision out of negligence. Subordinate staff may exhibit reluctance or fear due to inadequate training or lack of clear institutional guidelines. Overcoming these barriers requires open communication, clear job descriptions, and supportive ward leadership.

Supervision and Accountability in Nursing

Supervision bridges the gap between task assignment and outcome evaluation. It involves monitoring performance, guiding the worker, and verifying that standards are met. Accountability remains with the nurse who made the delegation decision, while responsibility for the actual performance of the act is shared by the person who accepted it. Clear documentation of supervisory checks protects both the patient and the nursing license.

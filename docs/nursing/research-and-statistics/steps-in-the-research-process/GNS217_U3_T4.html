Criteria for Selection of Research Problems

Introduction to Nursing Research Problems

A nursing research problem is a specific gap in knowledge, a troublesome clinical situation, or a theoretical puzzle that requires systematic investigation to improve patient care or nursing education. Selecting a good problem is the foundation of any valid study. Without a clear problem, the research process lacks direction and fails to produce actionable findings for clinical practice in Nigerian healthcare settings.

Significance and Relevance to Clinical Practice

A selected problem must address a genuine clinical need. In Nigerian hospitals and community clinics, resources are often limited, so research must target high-priority issues such as maternal mortality, nurse burnout, infection control, or management of chronic diseases like hypertension. If solving the problem does not improve patient outcomes or nursing workflows, the study lacks practical justification.

Feasibility and Resource Availability

Even a brilliant research idea is useless if it cannot be carried out. Researchers must evaluate time constraints, financial backing, equipment access, and cooperation from institutional authorities. Many student projects in resource-constrained environments fail because the scope exceeds available funds or transport logistics. Practical planning prevents abandoned studies.

Ethical Considerations and Patient Safety

  • Vulnerable Populations: Special care is needed when studying groups like pregnant women, children, or psychiatric patients.
  • Informed Consent: Participants must understand the study and agree freely without coercion.
  • Confidentiality: Patient records and identities must remain secure and anonymous.
  • Harm Minimization: The risk of physical, psychological, or social harm must not outweigh potential benefits.

Researcher Expertise and Interest

Motivation drives completion. If a nurse researcher has no genuine passion for the topic, fatigue sets in during data collection and analysis. However, interest alone is insufficient; the investigator must possess or develop the methodological skills required to execute the study properly, whether through quantitative statistics or qualitative interviewing.

Novelty and Contribution to Knowledge

Replicating past studies endlessly wastes time unless confirming previous anomalous results. A good research problem should offer novelty—either by exploring a new phenomenon, testing an intervention in a different cultural setting like Nigeria, or updating outdated clinical guidelines. It must add a fresh piece to the body of nursing science.

Measurability and Availability of Data

Concepts in nursing must be capable of being observed and measured. Vague philosophical ideas are hard to study empirically. The researcher must confirm that valid data collection tools—such as questionnaires, biometric scales, or observation checklists—exist or can be developed and tested to measure the variables accurately.

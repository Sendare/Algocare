Classification of Research

Introduction to Research Classification

Research classification provides a systematic way to categorize studies based on specific characteristics, objectives, and methodologies. By organizing research into structured groups, nurses and healthcare professionals can quickly identify study designs, evaluate evidence quality, and apply findings to clinical practice. Understanding these categories is foundational for evidence-based nursing in Nigerian healthcare settings.

Classification Based on Purpose and Goal

Studies are often grouped by what the researcher aims to achieve. The two main categories here are basic research and applied research.

  • Basic Research: Focuses on expanding general scientific knowledge and understanding fundamental principles without immediate clinical application.
  • Applied Research: Designed to solve practical, real-world problems, such as testing a new nursing protocol in a Lagos hospital.
  • Exploratory Research: Investigates a new or under-researched phenomenon where little previous data exists.
  • Descriptive Research: Observes and documents characteristics of a population or situation without manipulating variables.

Classification Based on Data Collection and Analysis

The approach to data handling splits research into quantitative, qualitative, and mixed methods paradigms.

QuantitativeNumerical, statisticalQuestionnaires, vital signs

Quantitative research tests hypotheses through measurable variables, while qualitative research explores human experiences and perspectives in depth.

Classification Based on Time Dimension

Time plays a crucial role in how a study is structured. Cross-sectional studies collect data at a single point in time, providing a snapshot of a population. Longitudinal studies follow participants over an extended period to observe changes or outcomes as they develop.

Retrospective studies look backward in time at existing records to examine past factors related to a current condition. Conversely, prospective studies start in the present and follow subjects forward into the future to see what events occur.

Classification Based on Research Environment

Research settings influence how data is gathered and how well results can be generalized. Laboratory research takes place in a controlled, artificial environment where the researcher manipulates variables with high precision. Field research occurs in natural settings, such as community health centers, patient homes, or hospital wards, capturing real-world behaviors but with less control over external factors.

Clinical and Nursing Research Classifications

Nursing research focuses specifically on phenomena relevant to patient care, nurse education, and healthcare administration. Experimental nursing studies involve active interventions, such as testing a new wound care technique against standard care. Non-experimental nursing studies observe phenomena without intervention, such as surveying nurses on workplace stress or assessing patient satisfaction after discharge.

Significance of Classifying Research in Nursing Practice

Recognizing how research is classified enables nurses to appraise literature critically. When nurses understand whether a study is qualitative or quantitative, clinical or laboratory-based, they can judge its applicability to Nigerian clinical settings. This ensures that nursing interventions remain safe, effective, and grounded in sound scientific evidence.

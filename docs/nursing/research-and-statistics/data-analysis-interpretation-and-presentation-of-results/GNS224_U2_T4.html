Presentation of Results

Introduction to Data Presentation in Nursing Research

Data presentation translates raw numbers and field notes from nursing research into accessible evidence. When data remains unorganized, spotting clinical trends or evaluating patient outcomes becomes impossible. Effective presentation bridges the gap between complex statistical analysis and practical bedside application. Researchers must decide whether narrative text, tabular grids, or visual charts best communicate their specific findings to clinical colleagues and policy makers.

Choosing Between Tables, Graphs, and Textual Descriptions

  • Textual descriptions: Best used for highlighting one or two isolated figures, or explaining complex narrative details that numbers cannot capture.
  • Tables: Ideal for presenting exact numerical values, demographic summaries, and multi-variable comparisons where readers need to look up specific data points.
  • Graphs and charts: Superior for showing trends over time, frequency distributions, and visual comparisons that emphasize the overall shape of the data at a glance.

Effective Design and Formatting of Statistical Tables

A well-designed statistical table relies on simplicity and clear organization rather than decorative gridlines. Every table requires a concise title, clearly labeled column headers, and defined units of measurement. Researchers should avoid vertical internal borders and minimize horizontal lines to keep the layout clean. Totals and percentages must be clearly distinguished from raw counts to prevent misinterpretation by clinical readers.

Creating Clear and Accurate Charts and Graphs

Charts must faithfully represent data without distorting the underlying scale. A bar chart compares distinct categories, a line graph illustrates continuous changes over time, and a pie chart shows proportional parts of a whole. Axis labels must be explicit, including the exact variable and unit of measure. Misleading graphs—such as those with truncated axes—exaggerate small differences and violate scientific integrity.

Synthesizing Qualitative Findings and Narrative Data

Qualitative results rely on thematic analysis rather than statistical calculations. Researchers present these findings using direct participant quotes, thematic categories, and narrative summaries that capture the lived experience of patients or nurses. Quotes must be carefully selected to represent the broader consensus rather than just outlier opinions. Integrating thematic tables alongside narrative text helps organize complex qualitative insights for readers.

Common Pitfalls and Ethical Considerations in Results Presentation

Ethical presentation demands absolute honesty and transparency regarding study outcomes. Researchers must report all pre-planned analyses, even when the results fail to show statistical significance. Cherry-picking favorable data points while ignoring contradictory findings misleads clinical practice. Additionally, protecting patient anonymity in qualitative case descriptions requires thorough removal of identifiable demographic details.

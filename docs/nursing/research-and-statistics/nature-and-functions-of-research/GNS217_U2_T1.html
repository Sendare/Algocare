Characteristics and Features of Research

Introduction and Empirical Nature of Research

Research is a systematic process of collecting, analyzing, and interpreting information to answer questions or solve problems. At its core, the empirical nature of research means that findings are rooted in direct observation and real-world experience rather than mere personal belief or speculation. In nursing, this translates to gathering hard data from patient assessments, clinical trials, or observational studies to inform bedside care. Without empirical evidence, clinical practices remain based on outdated tradition rather than verifiable truth.

Systematic and Logical Principles

  • Systematic: Research follows a structured, step-by-step procedure. Investigators do not skip steps; they move from defining the problem to reviewing literature, gathering data, and drawing conclusions in an orderly sequence.
  • Logical: Every step in a study is guided by valid reasoning. Inductive and deductive logic help researchers connect premises to conclusions without falling into emotional or biased traps.
  • Verifiable: Other independent investigators must be able to replicate the study using the same methods to check if the findings hold true.

Controlled and Analytical Approach

Control is vital in research, especially in experimental designs, to isolate variables. By minimizing the influence of extraneous variables, researchers can establish a clear cause-and-effect relationship between the independent and dependent variables. Alongside control, an analytical approach is necessary to break down complex data sets into smaller components. This deep examination prevents superficial conclusions and ensures that patterns hidden within clinical data are correctly identified.

Cyclical and Recursive Process

Research is rarely a one-off event that ends neatly with a final report. Instead, it is a cyclical and recursive journey. Every completed study invariably raises new questions, gaps, or unexpected findings that trigger fresh inquiries. When a nurse researcher finishes a project on wound care management, the limitations and new questions discovered during the study immediately pave the way for the next investigation.

Originality and Contribution to Knowledge

Good research must add value to the existing body of knowledge. Originality does not always mean discovering something entirely brand-new; it can involve replicating an older study in a new setting, such as testing a Western nursing intervention within a local Nigerian healthcare facility. The ultimate goal is to expand professional boundaries, improve patient outcomes, and guide evidence-based health policies.

Ethical Considerations in Research

Integrity forms the backbone of all credible scientific investigations. Researchers must protect human subjects through informed consent, maintain confidentiality, and prevent physical or psychological harm. Falsifying data, plagiarizing text, or coercing vulnerable patients into participation violates core ethical standards. Ethical oversight boards review every proposal to guarantee that the rights and dignity of participants remain safeguarded throughout the study.

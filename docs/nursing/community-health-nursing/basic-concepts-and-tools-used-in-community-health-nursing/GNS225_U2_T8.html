Weighing Scale

Introduction to the Weighing Scale in Community Health

Body weight is a primary indicator of nutritional status, fluid balance, and overall physical health. In Nigerian community health nursing, accurate weight measurement guides clinical diagnosis, medication dosing, and nutritional rehabilitation programs. Routine weight checks help identify early signs of faltering growth in under-five children and flag sudden weight changes linked to chronic illnesses like hypertension or heart failure in adults. Without precise measurement, health interventions lose their baseline and clinicians cannot accurately monitor patient progress over time.

Types of Weighing Scales Used in Clinical and Field Settings

Different clinical environments require specific scale designs to ensure safety and precision.

  • Bathroom Scale: Spring-loaded mechanical scales common in homes and outpatient clinics; prone to losing calibration over time.
  • Beam Balance Scale: Uses sliding weights on metal bars; highly accurate and does not require electricity, making it ideal for rural health posts.
  • Digital Scale: Electronic display units running on batteries or mains power; provides rapid readings but requires steady ground and regular power checks.
  • Infant Salter Scale: Spring scale with a hanging sling or trousers used primarily for weighing babies during immunisation outreach clinics.
  • Bed/Chair Scale: Specialized mechanical or digital equipment designed for immobile patients admitted to hospital wards.

Preparation and Calibration of the Weighing Scale

Before taking any measurement, the scale must be checked for accuracy. Place the device on a hard, flat surface rather than soft linoleum, earth, or carpeting, which absorbs pressure and skews the reading. For mechanical scales, adjust the calibration screw until the pointer rests perfectly at zero. Digital scales should be switched on and allowed to cycle to zero before a patient steps on. Neglecting this preparatory step introduces systematic errors that misinform clinical judgment.

Step-by-Step Procedure for Weighing Infants and Children

Weighing infants requires patience and strict safety measures due to sudden movements. Undress the infant down to light underwear or a dry diaper, remembering to zero out the weight of the diaper if it cannot be removed. Place the baby gently into the infant weighing pan or hang them securely in a clean Salter scale sling, keeping hands hovering close without touching to prevent falls. Read the measurement at eye level once the needle or digital numbers stabilize. Record the value immediately to the nearest 0.1 kilogram.

Step-by-Step Procedure for Weighing Adolescents and Adults

Instruct the patient to remove heavy outer garments, shoes, caps, and empty their pockets of heavy items like phones and keys. Ask them to step onto the center of the scale platform, standing straight and looking forward with arms relaxed at their sides. Ensure their weight is distributed evenly on both feet without leaning against the wall or equipment. Read the measurement once the mechanism stops moving and document the finding clearly in the patient record.

Interpreting Anthropometric Measurements and Growth Charts

Raw weight figures gain clinical meaning when plotted against standardized reference charts, such as WHO growth standards for children or Body Mass Index (BMI) tables for adults. A single weight point shows current status, but serial measurements over weeks or months reveal growth velocity and nutritional trends. A downward crossing of weight percentiles on a child's chart signals potential malnutrition or acute illness requiring prompt nursing intervention.

Maintenance, Cleaning, and Storage of Weighing Scales

Weighing scales require routine care to maintain mechanical integrity and infection control standards. Wipe down platforms and infant pans with a disinfectant wipe between patients to prevent cross-contamination. Keep mechanical pivots and springs free from dust, moisture, and rust. Store scales in a dry, secure area where they will not be knocked over or exposed to extreme temperatures that warp internal components.

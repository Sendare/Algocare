Delivery Kit

Introduction to the Delivery Kit in Community Health

A clean delivery kit is a pre-packaged collection of essential items designed to promote hygienic practices during childbirth, particularly in resource-limited or home-delivery settings. In many Nigerian rural communities where access to a fully equipped health facility is delayed by distance or transport, births often occur outside formal hospitals. The primary goal of this intervention is to reduce the incidence of puerperal sepsis and neonatal tetanus, which remain major contributors to maternal and newborn mortality. By providing sterile barriers and clean cutting tools, the kit bridges the safety gap during domiciliary deliveries managed by traditional birth attendants or community health extension workers.

Standard Components and Contents of a Delivery Kit

A standard clean delivery kit contains specific items carefully chosen to manage the birth environment and the immediate needs of the mother and newborn. While packaging may vary slightly among manufacturers, the contents generally include:

  • Soap: For washing the birth attendant's hands and the mother's perineum.
  • Plastic sheet: To provide a clean, moisture-barrier surface for the delivery.
  • Cord tie or thread: To securely ligate the umbilical cord.
  • Blade or razor: To cleanly cut the umbilical cord.
  • Clean cloth or gauze: To dry the newborn and wipe the mother.
  • Pictorial instructions: For guidance in low-literacy environments.

Aseptic Techniques and Infection Prevention

Maintaining strict asepsis during childbirth protects both parturient women and newborns from pathogenic invasion. The birth attendant must thoroughly wash hands with soap and water before opening any sterile components. Touching the inner surfaces of sterile wrappers or the active parts of the cutting tool must be strictly avoided. The perineal area should be cleansed gently from front to back to prevent introducing faecal organisms into the birth canal. Every action during the procedure focuses on creating a barrier between environmental germs and vulnerable tissues.

Preparation and Inspection of the Kit Before Use

Before relying on a delivery kit during labor, the health worker must perform a thorough physical inspection. Packaging must be checked for tears, punctures, or moisture damage that could compromise sterility. Expiration dates stamped on the packaging or individual components like blades and thread must be verified. All required items must be accounted for and laid out on a clean surface within easy reach before active pushing begins, preventing frantic searching during a critical moment.

Step-by-Step Utilization During Childbirth

Using the delivery kit follows a logical sequence aligned with the stages of labor. First, the clean plastic sheet is placed under the mother's buttocks as delivery approaches. The birth attendant prepares the soap and washes hands. As the baby is born, it is received onto a clean cloth from the kit and dried immediately to prevent heat loss. Once the umbilical cord stops pulsating, it is tied tightly in two places using the provided string and cut cleanly between the ties using the sterile blade.

Immediate Postpartum Care and Newborn Management

Following the delivery of the baby and placenta, attention shifts to stabilizing both mother and infant. The newborn is kept warm skin-to-skin on the mother's chest, wrapped in clean cloths from the kit or extra blankets. The birth attendant assesses the mother's uterus to ensure it remains firm and contracted, which prevents excessive bleeding. The umbilical stump is inspected for any immediate oozing of blood. Early initiation of breastfeeding is encouraged while documenting the time of birth and monitoring vital signs.

Maintenance, Replenishment, and Disposal Guidelines

Proper management of kit remnants protects the community from accidental injuries and infection. Used razor blades must be placed in a puncture-proof sharps container or rigid box rather than left loose in household waste. Contaminated plastics, soiled cloths, and packaging materials should be disposed of according to local safe waste protocols, such as burning or deep burial. Unused kits must be stored in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight and chemicals to preserve their integrity until needed.

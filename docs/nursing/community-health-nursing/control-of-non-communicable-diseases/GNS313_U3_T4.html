Substance Abuse

Introduction and Definition of Substance Abuse

Substance abuse refers to the harmful or hazardous use of psychoactive substances, including alcohol and illicit drugs. This pattern of use typically leads to significant impairment or distress, such as health problems, social consequences, or failure to fulfill major role obligations at work, school, or home. When working in Nigerian healthcare settings, nurses must distinguish between casual use, misuse, dependence, and addiction. Misuse implies using a substance for a purpose other than intended, while dependence involves physiological and psychological adaptation where stopping the substance triggers clear withdrawal symptoms.

Commonly Abused Substances and Their Classifications

Psychoactive substances are grouped based on how they alter central nervous system activity. Understanding these categories helps nurses predict overdose symptoms and withdrawal syndromes.

  • Depressants: Slow down brain function, lower blood pressure, and reduce anxiety. Examples include alcohol, barbiturates, and benzodiazepines.
  • Stimulants: Speed up central nervous system activity, increasing alertness, heart rate, and energy. Examples include cocaine, amphetamines, and locally abused caffeine or ephedrine products.
  • Cannabinoids: Alter perception, mood, and cognition. Cannabis (marijuana or 'igbo') is the most widely consumed illicit substance in Nigeria.
  • Opioids: Relieve pain and induce euphoria, carrying a high risk of respiratory depression. Examples include tramadol, codeine-containing cough syrups, and heroin.

Etiology and Risk Factors in Community Settings

Substance abuse rarely stems from a single cause. It results from a complex interaction of biological, psychological, and environmental factors. In Nigerian communities, key drivers include youth unemployment, peer pressure, economic hardship, and easy access to cheap pharmaceutical opioids like tramadol. Family history of substance use disorders also increases genetic vulnerability, while trauma and untreated mental health conditions often lead to self-medication.

Pathophysiology and Mechanisms of Dependence

Chronic substance use alters brain chemistry, particularly within the mesolimbic dopamine pathway, often referred to as the brain's reward center. Repeated drug exposure floods this pathway with dopamine, reinforcing the behavior. Over time, the brain reduces its natural dopamine production and receptor sensitivity, creating tolerance. The user now needs larger amounts of the substance just to feel normal. When use stops abruptly, a chemical deficit triggers intense cravings and unpleasant withdrawal symptoms.

Clinical Manifestations and Behavioral Assessment

Nurses must observe both physical and behavioral signs during assessments. Physical indicators include unsteadiness, needle tracks, bloodshot eyes, poor personal hygiene, and sudden weight changes. Behavioral cues involve sudden mood swings, withdrawal from family activities, secretiveness, and financial distress. In clinical practice, clients may deny their problem or minimize their intake, making objective assessment tools essential.

Screening Tools and Community Health Nursing Diagnosis

Early detection improves treatment outcomes. Nurses use validated screening instruments such as the CAGE questionnaire or AUDIT (Alcohol Use Disorders Identification Test) during routine community outreaches.

Common nursing diagnoses related to substance abuse include:

  • Ineffective Coping: Related to inadequate stress management strategies, manifested by drug use.
  • Risk for Injury: Related to central nervous system depression or intoxication effects.
  • Imbalanced Nutrition: Less than body requirements, related to poor dietary habits during active addiction.

Management, Detoxification, and Rehabilitation Strategies

Management of substance use disorders involves a multi-step continuum of care. The first phase is detoxification, which safely manages acute withdrawal symptoms under medical supervision. Certain drugs, particularly alcohol and sedatives, require tapering schedules to prevent life-threatening seizures. Following detoxification, rehabilitation focuses on psychological and social recovery through counseling, cognitive behavioral therapy, and support groups like Narcotics Anonymous to prevent relapse.

Prevention, Health Education, and Policy in Nigeria

Community health nurses play a vital role in preventing substance abuse through targeted health education in schools, churches, mosques, and youth centers. Nurses debunk myths about drugs and highlight the risks of self-medication. At the national level, agencies like the NDLEA (National Drug Law Enforcement Agency) enforce drug control policies, while the NAFDAC regulates pharmaceutical distribution to curb the illegal sale of prescription opioids.

Standard Precautions

Introduction and Core Principles of Standard Precautions

Standard precautions serve as the foundational infection control practices designed to reduce the risk of transmission of bloodborne and other pathogens in healthcare settings. These measures apply to all patients receiving care, regardless of their diagnosis or presumed infection status. Treating every patient interaction with the same level of caution protects both healthcare personnel and the patients themselves. The core premise relies on the assumption that any body fluid, non-intact skin, and mucous membrane may harbor infectious agents. Implementing these practices consistently forms the bedrock of patient safety across all tiers of the Nigerian healthcare delivery system.

Hand Hygiene Practices and Techniques

Hand hygiene is widely recognized as the single most effective intervention for preventing healthcare-associated infections.

  • Handwashing: Using soap and water when hands are visibly soiled with blood or other body fluids, or after caring for patients with spore-forming organisms like Clostridioides difficile.
  • Hand Rubbing: Using an alcohol-based hand rub for routinely decontaminating hands when they are not visibly dirty.
  • Key Moments: Performing hand hygiene before touching a patient, before clean or aseptic procedures, after body fluid exposure risk, after touching a patient, and after touching patient surroundings.

Selection and Proper Use of Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

Personal protective equipment acts as a physical barrier between the healthcare worker and potential infectious materials. Selecting the appropriate gear depends on the anticipated type of exposure.

  • Gloves: Wear when touching blood, body fluids, mucous membranes, and non-intact skin. Change gloves between tasks on the same patient if moving from a contaminated to a clean body site.
  • Gowns: Wear to protect skin and prevent soiling of clothing during procedures that are likely to generate splashes of body fluids.
  • Masks and Eye Protection: Use masks along with goggles or face shields during procedures that generate splashes or sprays of blood, body fluids, secretions, or excretions.

Safe Injection Practices and Sharps Management

Injuries from needles and other sharp instruments pose a severe occupational hazard in clinical environments. Safe practices protect both staff and patients from bloodborne viral transmissions such as Hepatitis B, Hepatitis C, and HIV.

  1. Never Recap Needles: Avoid recapping used needles using two hands. If recapping is unavoidable due to specific procedural requirements, use a one-handed scoop technique or a mechanical device.
  2. Immediate Disposal: Directly discard used needles, syringes, and lancets into a puncture-resistant safety box immediately after use.
  3. Box Capacity: Never overfill safety boxes; seal and replace them once they reach the three-quarters full mark.

Respiratory Hygiene and Cough Etiquette

Respiratory hygiene targets the containment of respiratory pathogens spread through droplet and airborne routes. Patients, visitors, and staff must incorporate these habits at the very first point of contact with a healthcare facility.

  • Cover the mouth and nose with a tissue when coughing or sneezing.
  • Dispose of used tissues promptly in the nearest waste receptacle.
  • Perform hand hygiene after contact with respiratory secretions.
  • Wear a surgical mask if coughing, or provide masks to symptomatic individuals at reception areas.

Environmental Cleaning and Equipment Management

Patient care environment surfaces and shared equipment can act as reservoirs for microbial transmission if not managed properly. Routine cleaning schedules must focus on high-touch surfaces such as bed rails, bedside tables, and door handles using hospital-approved disinfectants. Reusable equipment, including blood pressure cuffs and thermometers, must be cleaned and disinfected between every patient use. Linen soiled with blood or body fluids should be handled with minimum agitation and placed in leak-proof bags to prevent environmental contamination.

Management of Occupational Exposures

Despite strict precautions, accidental exposures to blood and body fluids still occur in clinical settings. Immediate action minimizes the risk of seroconversion for bloodborne infections.

  1. First Aid: Wash the affected skin area immediately with soap and water. If mucous membranes are splashed, flush thoroughly with clean water.
  2. Report: Immediately report the incident to the designated supervisor or infection control officer.
  3. Evaluate: Undergo prompt medical evaluation for post-exposure prophylaxis, particularly regarding HIV and Hepatitis B status.

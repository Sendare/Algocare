Obstetric Emergencies in the Community

Introduction and Triage of Community Obstetric Emergencies

Obstetric emergencies in community settings demand rapid recognition because skilled surgical care is often miles away. Midwives and community health extension workers must instantly separate routine discomforts from life-threatening crises. Triage focuses on preserving maternal perfusion and fetal oxygenation using basic, portable assessment tools.

  • Primary Survey: Assess airway, breathing, circulation, and level of consciousness immediately.
  • Secondary Survey: Rapidly check vital signs, abdominal tenderness, and bleeding status.
  • Danger Signs: Severe headache, blurring vision, heavy vaginal bleeding, and convulsions.

Management of Severe Antepartum and Postpartum Hemorrhage

Bleeding remains a leading cause of maternal mortality across rural and urban Nigerian communities. Antepartum hemorrhage stems from conditions like placenta previa or abruptio placentae, while postpartum hemorrhage usually results from uterine atony. Community providers must initiate physical interventions while preparing urgent transport.

  1. Uterine Massage: Firmly massage the fundus to stimulate contractions and control postpartum bleeding.
  2. Bimanual Compression: Compress the uterus between a fist inside the vagina and an abdominal hand if massage fails.
  3. Uterotonic Administration: Give oxytocin or misoprostol promptly if available in the community health post guideline.
  4. Fluid Resuscitation: Establish large-bore intravenous access and infuse normal saline to maintain blood pressure.

Handling Hypertensive Disorders and Eclamptic Seizures

Hypertensive crises can escalate rapidly into generalized seizures, threatening both mother and baby. When an eclamptic fit occurs outside a hospital, protecting the airway and preventing maternal injury are the immediate priorities. Magnesium sulfate is the gold-standard anticonvulsant for managing and preventing recurrent eclamptic seizures.

  • Position the patient on her side to prevent aspiration of saliva or vomitus.
  • Clear the airway gently without forcing hard objects between clenched teeth.
  • Administer magnesium sulfate according to local standing orders.
  • Monitor respiratory rate closely to detect early magnesium toxicity.

Addressing Shoulder Dystocia and Breech Presentation

Unexpected malpresentations and mechanical obstructions during home deliveries require calm, systematic maneuvers. Shoulder dystocia occurs when the baby's shoulder impacts against the maternal pubic bone after the head emerges. Breech presentation involves the buttocks or feet presenting first, raising the risk of head entrapment.

  • McRoberts Maneuver: Flex the mother's thighs sharply toward her abdomen to straighten the pelvis.
  • Suprapubic Pressure: Apply gentle downward pressure just above the pubic bone to dislodge the shoulder.
  • Breech Hand-Off: Allow the baby to hang passively until the scapula appears, avoiding early pulling.
  • Lovset's Maneuver: Rotate the baby to deliver the arms safely during a breech birth.

Emergency Management of Cord Prolapse and Uterine Rupture

Catastrophic events like umbilical cord prolapse and uterine rupture require instant diversion of resources toward emergency surgical transfer. Cord prolapse cuts off fetal oxygen when the cord is compressed by the presenting part. Uterine rupture presents with sudden, severe abdominal pain, cessation of contractions, and signs of internal bleeding.

  • Cord Elevation: Insert a sterile gloved hand into the vagina to lift the presenting part off the cord.
  • Knee-Chest Position: Place the mother in a knee-chest or exaggerated Sims position to reduce gravity pressure.
  • Oxygen Therapy: Administer high-flow oxygen to maximize fetal oxygen saturation during transit.
  • Emergency Laparotomy: Prepare the family for urgent transport because definitive repair requires surgery.

Neonatal Resuscitation and Maternal Stabilization

When a baby is born limp and not breathing, the golden minute of life begins immediately. Community providers must dry, stimulate, and clear airways efficiently. Simultaneously, the mother requires continuous monitoring for shock, hypovolemia, and perineal trauma.

  • Assess breathing and heart rate within thirty seconds of birth.
  • Clear the airway with suction if meconium or thick secretions block breathing.
  • Initiate positive pressure ventilation with a bag-valve-mask if apnea persists.
  • Monitor maternal vital signs every fifteen minutes during stabilization.

Community Referral Systems and Post-Crisis Care

Successful emergency management extends beyond initial resuscitation to safe transport and structured community debriefing. Communication gaps often delay definitive hospital care in remote Nigerian settings. Establishing clear referral links ensures patients reach comprehensive emergency obstetric care without administrative delays.

  • Contact the receiving facility before departure to confirm bed space and readiness.
  • Accompany the patient with a trained health worker carrying emergency supplies.
  • Document all interventions, vital signs, and medications given during the crisis.
  • Conduct psychological debriefing for family members and birth attendants.

Antiretroviral Drugs

Introduction to HIV Pathophysiology and the Role of Antiretroviral Therapy

Human Immunodeficiency Virus targets the immune system, specifically destroying CD4+ T-lymphocytes. As the virus replicates, the immune system gradually weakens, eventually leading to Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome if left untreated. Antiretroviral therapy does not cure the infection, but it suppresses viral replication to undetectable levels, preserves immune function, and prevents opportunistic infections.

Classes of Antiretroviral Drugs and Their Mechanisms of Action

Antiretroviral medications are grouped into different classes based on the specific stage of the viral life cycle they block. Using a combination of these drugs stops the virus from mutating and multiplying effectively.

  • Nucleoside Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors: Block the enzyme reverse transcriptase by acting as faulty building blocks, stopping viral DNA creation.
  • Non-Nucleoside Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors: Bind directly to the reverse transcriptase enzyme to disable its working mechanism.
  • Protease Inhibitors: Block the protease enzyme, preventing the virus from assembling into mature, infectious particles.
  • Integrase Strand Transfer Inhibitors: Stop the viral enzyme integrase from inserting HIV genetic material into host human DNA.

First-Line and Second-Line Antiretroviral Regimen Guidelines in Nigeria

National guidelines in Nigeria dictate standardized regimens to ensure effective treatment access and manage resistance patterns across healthcare facilities. Treatment typically combines two Nucleoside Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors with an Integrase Inhibitor or Non-Nucleoside Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitor.

  • Preferred First-Line Regimen: Tenofovir plus Lamivudine plus Dolutegravir for adults and adolescents.
  • Alternative First-Line Regimen: Tenofovir plus Lamivudine plus Efavirenz when dolutegravir is contraindicated.
  • Second-Line Regimen: Zidovudine plus Lamivudine combined with a boosted Protease Inhibitor like Atazanavir/ritonavir or Lopinavir/ritonavir following first-line treatment failure.

Pharmacokinetics, Administration, and Patient Adherence Strategies

Consistent drug levels in the blood are necessary to keep the virus suppressed. Missing doses allows viral replication to resume and breeds drug-resistant strains. Nurses must educate patients on exact dosing schedules, food requirements for specific drugs, and practical reminder tools like pill boxes or treatment supporters to foster lifelong adherence.

Adverse Drug Reactions, Toxicity, and Safety Monitoring

Antiretroviral medications can cause short-term side effects and long-term toxicities that require careful clinical observation. Routine laboratory monitoring, including renal function tests, liver function tests, and full blood counts, helps catch organ damage early.

  • Tenofovir: Can cause renal impairment and bone mineral density loss.
  • Efavirenz: Frequently associated with central nervous system effects such as vivid dreams, dizziness, and depression.
  • Zidovudine: Known to cause bone marrow suppression leading to severe anemia and neutropenia.
  • Protease Inhibitors: Often linked to metabolic changes including dyslipidemia and insulin resistance.

Management of Antiretroviral Drug Interactions and Resistance

Antiretroviral drugs interact with many other medications, including tuberculosis treatments, herbal preparations, and contraceptives, often through liver enzyme pathways. These interactions can either lower antiretroviral levels, causing treatment failure, or raise them, increasing toxicity. Nurses must review all concurrent substances the patient takes to maintain therapeutic safety.

Special Considerations: Pregnancy, Pediatric Patients, and Co-infections

Managing HIV requires tailored approaches for specific populations. Pregnant women receive specific regimens to prevent mother-to-child transmission. Pediatric patients require weight-based dosing and palatable liquid formulations. Co-infections like tuberculosis or hepatitis B complicate care due to overlapping drug toxicities and timing of treatment initiation.

Nursing Responsibilities in Patient Education and Care

Nurses play a central role in supporting patients initiated on antiretroviral therapy. Beyond dispensing medications, nursing duties involve assessing readiness, managing side effects, offering psychosocial support, and fighting stigma. Empowering patients through clear education ensures long-term retention in care and healthier clinical outcomes.

Nurse's Role in Prevention of Neoplastic Diseases

Introduction to Neoplastic Diseases and Prevention

Neoplastic diseases, commonly known as cancers, involve abnormal and uncontrolled cell division that can invade surrounding tissues and spread to distant organs. In clinical practice across Nigeria, nurses encounter these conditions at various stages, making a solid understanding of prevention vital for reducing morbidity and mortality. Prevention strategies aim to either stop the disease from developing or catch it early enough for curative treatment.

Levels of Prevention in Oncology

Healthcare professionals divide prevention into three distinct tiers to target patients at different health stages:

  • Primary prevention: Interventions that prevent the initial occurrence of a disease, such as vaccination and lifestyle changes.
  • Secondary prevention: Screening and early detection methods designed to identify pre-cancerous lesions or early-stage malignancies.
  • Tertiary prevention: Care and rehabilitation provided after a diagnosis to limit complications, manage symptoms, and prevent recurrence.

Primary Prevention and Risk Reduction

Primary prevention focuses on keeping carcinogens away from vulnerable populations and boosting host resistance. Nurses educate individuals on modifying known behavioral risk factors, including tobacco use, excessive alcohol consumption, poor diet, and physical inactivity. Additionally, administering vaccinations like the human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccine and the hepatitis B vaccine protects against viral infections strongly linked to cervical and liver cancers.

Secondary Prevention through Early Detection

Secondary prevention relies on diagnostic screening tools that catch malignancies while they remain localized and highly treatable. Common clinical modalities include mammography for breast cancer, visual inspection with acetic acid for cervical lesions, and fecal occult blood tests for colorectal cancer. Nurses play a frontline role by organizing community screening camps, teaching correct self-assessment techniques, and ensuring clients with abnormal results receive prompt diagnostic follow-up.

Tertiary Prevention, Rehabilitation, and Recurrence

Tertiary prevention begins after a definitive cancer diagnosis and successful primary treatment. The focus shifts toward preventing disability, managing chronic treatment side effects, restoring physical function, and monitoring for disease recurrence or secondary malignancies. Nurses coordinate multidisciplinary care plans, monitor lymphedema, encourage proper nutrition, and offer psychological support to help survivors reintegrate into their daily lives and communities.

Patient Education and Genetic Counseling

Empowering patients requires clear, accessible health education tailored to local cultural contexts and literacy levels. Nurses explain complex screening guidelines, clarify misconceptions about cancer transmission, and discuss hereditary risk factors. When family histories suggest inherited cancer syndromes, such as BRCA gene mutations linked to breast and ovarian cancers, nurses facilitate referrals for professional genetic counseling so individuals can make informed family planning and surveillance decisions.

Community Oncology Advocacy and Policy

Nurses extend their impact beyond individual bedside care by advocating for systemic health policies that protect populations from carcinogens. This includes supporting tobacco control legislation, promoting clean cooking fuels to reduce indoor air pollution, and lobbying for better funding for public cancer screening centers. Community oncology advocacy addresses the social determinants of health, ensuring that marginalized populations gain equitable access to preventive services and care.

Neoplastic Diseases

Introduction to Neoplastic Diseases

Neoplasm literally means 'new growth'. It refers to an abnormal mass of tissue that forms when cells grow and divide at an excessive rate without normal bodily control mechanisms. Unlike physiological growth like healing or pregnancy, neoplastic growth persists even after the original trigger is removed. In Nigerian clinical practice, nurses encounter neoplastic diseases across all age groups, ranging from Burkitt lymphoma in children to cervical and breast cancers in adults. Understanding these conditions requires looking at how normal cellular regulation fails, leading to uncontrolled proliferation.

Characteristics of Benign and Malignant Cells

Neoplasms are broadly categorized into benign and malignant based on their cellular behavior and growth patterns.

  • Benign Tumors: Localized, slow-growing, encapsulated masses composed of well-differentiated cells that closely resemble the tissue of origin. They do not invade surrounding tissues or spread to distant sites.
  • Malignant Tumors: Rapidly growing, non-encapsulated masses composed of poorly differentiated or anaplastic cells. They infiltrate surrounding structures and frequently metastasize through blood or lymphatic channels.

Classification of Neoplasms

Classification systems help healthcare teams determine treatment pathways and predict outcomes. Tumors are categorized based on their tissue of origin and biological behavior. Carcinomas arise from epithelial tissues, sarcomas from connective or supportive tissues, and leukemias or lymphomas from blood-forming and lymphatic tissues. In clinical settings, using standardized naming conventions ensures clear communication among multidisciplinary teams managing oncology cases.

Staging and Grading of Cancers

Staging and grading are distinct processes used to evaluate the extent of cancer and plan effective interventions. Grading assesses the cellular appearance under a microscope to determine how abnormal and aggressive the cells look. Staging evaluates how far the cancer has spread anatomically from its original site. The TNM system is widely used for staging, where T represents primary tumor size, N indicates regional lymph node involvement, and M signifies distant metastasis.

Nursing Management in Oncology Care

Caring for patients with neoplastic diseases requires holistic nursing interventions that address physical, psychological, and social needs. Nurses manage side effects of treatments like chemotherapy and radiation, which often include myelosuppression, nausea, alopecia, and fatigue. Monitoring laboratory values, such as white blood cell and platelet counts, helps prevent life-threatening complications like infection and hemorrhage. Beyond clinical tasks, nurses provide continuous support to help patients and families navigate the emotional burden of a cancer diagnosis.

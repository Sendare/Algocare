Male Circumcision

Introduction and Definition of Male Circumcision

Male circumcision is the surgical removal of the prepuce, commonly known as the foreskin, which covers the glans penis. This practice has deep cultural, religious, and medical roots across various communities in Nigeria and globally. Historically performed as a rite of passage, modern healthcare settings also utilize the procedure for hygiene, cultural conformity, and targeted disease prevention. The operation exposes the glans, permanently altering the physical appearance and local anatomy of the penis.

Indications, Contraindications, and Clinical Considerations

  • Phimosis: A condition where the foreskin is too tight to retract over the glans, causing pain or difficulty urinating.
  • Paraphimosis: An emergency state where a retracted foreskin gets trapped behind the glans and restricts blood flow.
  • Recurrent Balanitis: Chronic inflammation or infection of the glans penis linked to a tight foreskin.
  • Hypospadias: A major structural malformation where the urethral opening is on the underside of the penis, serving as a strict contraindication for routine circumcision because the foreskin may be needed for future surgical reconstruction.

Preoperative Nursing Assessment and Preparation

Before the procedure, nurses must conduct a thorough physical and history assessment. Verify the infant or patient's identity, review consent forms signed by the parents or legal guardian, and check for any family history of bleeding disorders like hemophilia. Assess the local genital area for active rashes, diaper dermatitis, or structural anomalies. Ensure the patient has fasted according to age-specific guidelines if general anesthesia or sedation is planned, minimizing aspiration risks during the operation.

Surgical Techniques and Equipment

Several established techniques exist for male circumcision, varying by age and practitioner preference.

  • Plastibell Device: A plastic ring tied over the foreskin with a suture, cutting off blood supply so the foreskin falls off naturally within 5 to 8 days.
  • Gomco Clamp: A metal bell and plate system that crushes the foreskin before surgical excision, providing hemostasis.
  • Mogen Clamp: A flat metal clamp slid across the dorsal slit of the foreskin for rapid excision, often used in religious or neonatal settings.

Pain Management and Anesthesia

Circumcision causes significant acute pain, making effective analgesia a standard requirement of nursing and medical care. Neonates receive local anesthetic blocks, such as a dorsal penile nerve block or a ring block using lidocaine without epinephrine. Epinephrine must be avoided in local anesthetics for the penis to prevent severe vasoconstriction and ischemic necrosis of the tissue. Sweet-tasting oral sucrose solutions combined with non-nutritive sucking provide supplemental comfort for newborns during the procedure.

Postoperative Nursing Care and Wound Monitoring

Following the procedure, the nurse monitors the surgical site closely for hemorrhage, swelling, and signs of urinary retention. Apply sterile petroleum jelly gauze dressings gently to the wound if required, preventing the raw glans from sticking to the diaper. Observe for the first post-procedure voiding to ensure the urethra remains patent and undamaged. Instruct caregivers on gentle cleaning using warm water without harsh soaps while the wound heals.

Potential Complications and Emergency Management

Although generally safe, male circumcision carries risks of acute complications that require prompt nursing and medical intervention. Excessive bleeding from the frenular artery or incision margin is the most common immediate hazard. Infection, manifested by purulent discharge, foul odor, and spreading erythema, requires antibiotic therapy. If a Plastibell device slips proximally onto the shaft, it can cause severe strangulation and ischemia, requiring urgent surgical removal.

Patient Education and Discharge Instructions

Providing clear home care instructions to parents or adult patients ensures safe recovery and reduces anxiety. Explain that a yellowish exudate or crust forming on the glans is part of normal healing, not necessarily an infection. Advise caregivers to change diapers frequently and avoid pulling or forcing any remaining Plastibell ring off prematurely. Emphasize warning signs that require immediate hospital return, such as persistent bleeding, inability to urinate, or high fever.

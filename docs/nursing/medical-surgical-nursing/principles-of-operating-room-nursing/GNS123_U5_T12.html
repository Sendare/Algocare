Hazards of Operating Room Nursing

Introduction to Operating Room Nursing Hazards

Operating room nursing is a specialized and demanding career path within the Nigerian healthcare system. While providing vital care to surgical patients, perioperative nurses are continuously exposed to a unique combination of workplace dangers. Recognizing these hazards is the first step toward building a safer working environment in any hospital surgical suite.

Physical Hazards in the Surgical Suite

Physical hazards in the theatre environment stem from equipment, prolonged postures, and environmental factors. Nurses stand for hours during long procedures, leading to musculoskeletal strain and chronic back pain.

  • Radiation exposure: Portable X-ray machines and C-arm fluoroscopy units expose staff to ionizing radiation if lead aprons are not worn.
  • Electrical hazards: High-voltage electrosurgical units present risks of thermal burns and electrical shock.
  • Noise pollution: Constant alarms, suction machines, and heavy surgical tools contribute to hearing fatigue and communication errors.

Biological Hazards and Infection Risks

Surgical environments are high-risk zones for bloodborne pathogen transmission. Accidental sharps injuries from suture needles, scalpel blades, and hollow-bore needles remain a daily threat.

  • Bloodborne pathogens: Contact with patient blood and body fluids exposes staff to Hepatitis B, Hepatitis C, and HIV.
  • Surgical smoke plume: Diathermy and lasers vaporize human tissue, releasing bio-aerosols, viral particles, and toxic gases into the theatre air when smoke evacuators are absent.

Chemical Hazards and Hazardous Substances

Operating rooms utilize numerous chemical agents that pose respiratory and dermal risks. Inhalation of waste anesthetic gases like isoflurane or nitrous oxide occurs due to scavenging system leaks or uncuffed tubes. Additionally, toxic disinfectants such as glutaraldehyde, used for instrument sterilization, can trigger severe contact dermatitis and asthma attacks among nursing personnel if ventilation is poor.

Psychological and Emotional Hazards

The high-stakes nature of surgery creates intense psychological pressure for theatre nurses. Dealing with critical emergencies, massive patient hemorrhages, and unexpected surgical complications leads to chronic stress and burnout. Emotional exhaustion is compounded by irregular work shifts, on-call demands, and occasional traumatic patient outcomes that challenge the coping mechanisms of healthcare professionals.

Legal, Ethical, and Interpersonal Workplace Challenges

Operating room nurses navigate complex interpersonal dynamics and legal boundaries. Hierarchical friction among multidisciplinary teams can silence junior nurses who spot safety errors. Legally, nurses face liabilities regarding correct sponge and instrument counts, patient identification, and maintaining sterile boundaries. Failure in these duties can result in professional negligence claims and severe ethical dilemmas.

Prevention, Safety Protocols, and Occupational Health

Mitigating theatre hazards requires strict adherence to institutional safety frameworks. Standard precautions, proper use of personal protective equipment, and functional scavenging systems form the basis of physical protection.

  • Surgical safety checklists: The WHO surgical safety checklist reduces communication gaps and prevents wrong-site surgery.
  • Sharps safety devices: Implementing engineered safety needles minimizes accidental punctures.
  • Vaccinations: Mandatory Hepatitis B immunization protects personnel against biological transmission.

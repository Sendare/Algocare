Roles of the Nurse in Peri-operative Care

Introduction to Peri-operative Nursing

Peri-operative nursing encompasses the care provided to patients before, during, and after surgical procedures. This specialized field requires a seamless transition of responsibilities across three distinct phases: the pre-operative phase, the intra-operative phase, and the post-operative phase. Nurses in this field act as the central coordinator between the surgical team, the patient, and the patient's family. Understanding the entire surgical continuum allows the nurse to anticipate complications, reduce patient anxiety, and ensure a smooth recovery journey in busy Nigerian healthcare environments where resources may sometimes be constrained.

Pre-operative Nursing Responsibilities

The pre-operative phase begins when the decision for surgery is made and ends when the patient is transferred to the operating theatre. Nursing duties here focus heavily on physical preparation and psychological support.

  • Assessment: Conducting a thorough baseline health history, checking known allergies, and reviewing baseline laboratory investigations.
  • Consent Verification: Ensuring that the surgical and anesthetic consent forms are signed, dated, and properly understood by the patient.
  • Physical Prep: Administering pre-anesthetic medications, ensuring skin hygiene, and confirming fasting status (nil per os).
  • Psychological Support: Addressing patient and family fears regarding anesthesia and the outcome of the surgery.

Intra-operative Nursing Roles

Inside the operating theatre, nurses play vital roles divided into two main categories to maintain a sterile and organized environment.

  • Scrub Nurse: Works within the sterile field, performing surgical hand scrubs, donning sterile gowns and gloves, handling sterile instruments, and anticipating the surgeon's needs.
  • Circulating Nurse: Remains in the non-sterile zone, manages overall room safety, coordinates the surgical team, monitors the patient, maintains accurate documentation, and manages surgical counts.

Surgical counts of sponges, needles, and instruments are a critical intra-operative duty performed jointly by the scrub and circulating nurses to prevent retained foreign objects.

Post-operative Nursing Care

Post-operative care begins immediately after surgery when the patient is transferred to the Post-Anesthesia Care Unit (PACU) or recovery room. The immediate priority is maintaining a patent airway, supporting adequate breathing, and monitoring hemodynamic stability. Nurses frequently assess vital signs, check surgical dressings for excessive bleeding, evaluate pain levels, and monitor urine output. As the patient regains full consciousness, nursing focus shifts toward preventing common complications such as deep vein thrombosis, atelectasis, and wound infections through early mobilization and deep breathing exercises.

Patient Safety and Advocacy Across the Continuum

Patient safety is the bedrock of peri-operative nursing. Nurses act as unyielding patient advocates, especially when patients are vulnerable under anesthesia. Universal safety protocols, such as the World Health Organization (WHO) Surgical Safety Checklist (Sign In, Time Out, and Sign Out), must be rigorously implemented before every procedure. The nurse speaks up to verify patient identity, correct surgical site marking, and confirm allergy status. Advocacy also extends to protecting patient dignity, ensuring informed consent is truly voluntary, and preventing positional injuries during long surgical operations.

Documentation and Legal Ethical Considerations

Accurate, timely documentation in peri-operative nursing serves as a legal record of care and a communication tool for the healthcare team. Every medication given, sponge count verified, and vital sign recorded must be documented objectively. Ethically, nurses must uphold patient autonomy, beneficence, and confidentiality. A common pitfall is delayed documentation, which can compromise patient safety and lead to legal vulnerabilities in malpractice claims. The nurse ensures that all entries reflect the actual care rendered without generalized or biased remarks.

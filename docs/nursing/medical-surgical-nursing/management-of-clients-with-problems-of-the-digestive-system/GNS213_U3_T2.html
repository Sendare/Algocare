Assessment of Clients with Digestive Disorders

Introduction to Gastrointestinal Assessment in Nursing

Assessing the gastrointestinal system requires a structured approach to uncover hidden pathologies that may present with vague symptoms. Digestive disorders are common in clinical practice across Nigerian health facilities, ranging from acute infections like typhoid to chronic conditions such as peptic ulcer disease. Nurses serve as the primary observers who notice subtle changes in patient comfort, elimination patterns, and nutritional intake. Understanding normal gastrointestinal anatomy and physiology provides the baseline needed to identify abnormalities early before complications develop.

Comprehensive Health History

Gathering a thorough health history uncovers clues that physical examinations might miss. The interview should cover specific parameters:

  • Pain characteristics: Location, duration, intensity, and factors that worsen or relieve the discomfort.
  • Dietary habits: Typical daily intake, fluid consumption, and recent changes in appetite.
  • Elimination patterns: Frequency, consistency, and color of stools, noting any presence of blood, mucus, or melena.
  • Medical history: Previous abdominal surgeries, chronic illnesses, and current medications like NSAIDs that irritate the gastric mucosa.

Abdominal Physical Examination Techniques

The physical examination of the abdomen follows a strict sequence that differs from other body systems. You must always inspect, auscultate, percussion, and then palpate. Auscultation comes before percussion and palpation because pressing on the abdomen can alter bowel motility and artificially change bowel sounds.

  • Inspection: Observe for symmetry, distension, scars, visible peristalsis, and skin lesions.
  • Auscultation: Listen in all four quadrants using the diaphragm of the stethoscope for bowel sounds over one to five minutes.
  • Percussion: Assess the density of abdominal contents, identifying areas of tympany (air) and dullness (fluid or solid organs).
  • Palpation: Begin with light palpation to detect tenderness or muscle guarding, followed by deep palpation for organomegaly or masses.

Nutritional and Functional Status Evaluation

Gastrointestinal disorders frequently compromise nutrition due to malabsorption, pain, or early satiety. Evaluating nutritional status involves tracking body weight changes, calculating body mass index, and assessing dietary intake records. Functional status evaluations look at how digestive symptoms impact the patient's daily activities, such as shopping for food, preparing meals, and maintaining social interactions. Early identification of malnutrition allows nurses to collaborate with dietitians to initiate appropriate nutritional support.

Patient Preparation and Documentation

Proper preparation reduces patient anxiety and ensures accurate diagnostic results. Nurses must explain upcoming procedures clearly, verify informed consent, and adhere to fasting instructions when required. Accurate documentation of assessment findings provides a legal record and ensures continuity of care across shifts. Every documentation entry should be objective, timely, and descriptive, avoiding vague terms like 'normal abdomen' when specific descriptors such as 'soft, non-tender, active bowel sounds' are more appropriate.

Stock Holding Conventions

Introduction to Stock Holding Conventions

Stock holding conventions refer to the standardized rules and practices used to manage, store, and rotate supplies in a healthcare facility. In Nigerian hospitals, whether federal teaching centres or primary health clinics, proper stock management prevents waste of scarce medical resources. Nurses often act as ward storekeepers, making familiarity with these conventions a daily clinical necessity. Poor stock organization leads to expired medications, missing surgical consumables, and compromised patient safety.

Purpose and Importance of Inventory Control

Inventory control ensures that essential drugs, dressings, and equipment are always available when needed for patient care. It prevents both overstocking, which ties up capital and risks spoilage, and stockouts, which can halt emergency resuscitations. In resource-constrained settings, maintaining a reliable inventory balances clinical readiness with financial prudence.

Classification of Medical and Surgical Supplies

Medical inventory is grouped into categories to simplify storage and tracking.

  • Consumables: Single-use items like syringes, gauze, and gloves that are replenished frequently.
  • Non-consumables: Reusable instruments such as forceps, scissors, and basin bowls requiring sterilization.
  • Pharmaceuticals: Drugs, vaccines, and intravenous fluids subject to strict regulatory controls.

FIFO, LIFO, and Expiry Tracking

Stock rotation rules dictate the order in which items are issued for use.

  • FIFO (First-In, First-Out): Older stock is used first to prevent expiration on the shelf. This is the gold standard for nursing stores.
  • LIFO (Last-In, First-Out): Newer stock is used first, rarely used in healthcare except for specific non-perishable bulk goods.
  • Expiry Tracking: Regular physical checks of expiration dates recorded on stock cards or digital ledgers.

Stock Levels: Minimum, Maximum, and Reorder Points

Managing quantities effectively prevents shortages and surpluses.

  • Minimum Stock Level: The safety threshold; stock must never fall below this without triggering an emergency order.
  • Maximum Stock Level: The upper limit of inventory storage capacity to prevent wastage and overcrowding.
  • Reorder Level: The specific quantity trigger point that signals the nurse manager to place a new purchase order before supplies run out.

Storage Conditions and Maintenance of Stock Integrity

Environmental factors can degrade medical supplies long before their printed expiration dates. Pharmaceuticals and vaccines require strict temperature monitoring, often using cold chains. Supplies must be kept off the floor on pallets or shelves to prevent moisture damage from damp Nigerian floors, and protected from direct sunlight and pests.

Auditing, Stock-Taking, and Record Keeping

Regular stock-taking involves physically counting every item in the ward inventory and reconciling the count with ledger balances. Stock cards or electronic inventory sheets must be updated immediately upon receipt or issuance of any item. Discrepancies between physical counts and records highlight potential pilferage, misplacement, or recording errors that require immediate investigation.

Managing Waste, Discrepancies, and Expired Items

When items expire or break, nurses must follow institutional protocols for disposal rather than clandestine dumping. Expired pharmaceuticals must be quarantined in a separate, labeled container away from active stock to prevent accidental administration. Incident reports should be documented for high-value losses, and waste must be segregated according to hospital waste management guidelines.

Requisition of Injection Safety Commodities

Introduction to Injection Safety and Commodities

Injection safety forms the bedrock of infection prevention and control in Nigerian health facilities. Every injection delivered requires a reliable chain of specific materials to protect both the healthcare worker and the patient. Requisitioning these items requires an understanding of what constitutes a safe injection system, moving beyond just the syringe to include safety boxes and skin cleansing agents. A breakdown at any point in the supply chain exposes communities to bloodborne pathogens like Hepatitis B, Hepatitis C, and HIV. Nurses play a frontline role in identifying shortages before they result in unsafe reuse or improvised practices.

Essential Types of Injection Safety Commodities

A complete injection safety commodity package includes several distinct items designed to work together seamlessly:

  • Auto-Disable (AD) Syringes: These syringes lock or break after a single use, physically preventing accidental or intentional reuse in clinical settings.
  • Reconstitution Syringes: Standard disposable syringes utilized specifically for mixing lyophilized vaccines or medications with diluents prior to administration.
  • Sharps Containers: Puncture-resistant, leak-proof boxes designated for the safe collection of used needles and syringes.
  • Alcohol Swabs: Single-use antiseptic pads used to cleanse the skin surface prior to needle insertion.

Principles of Inventory Management and Stock Estimation

Accurate estimation prevents both oversupply and stockouts. Nurses calculate needs based on consumption data, patient attendance records, and wastage factors. The consumption rate reflects how many commodities are used over a specific timeframe, usually a month. Facilities must maintain a buffer stock—an extra quantity kept in reserve to cover unexpected delays in delivery or sudden surges in patient turnout. Over-ordering ties up scarce storage space and risks expiration of sterile packaging seals, while under-ordering compromises clinical operations.

The Requisition Process and Ordering Workflow

The requisition workflow follows a structured chain of command within the health system. It begins with a physical inventory count in the immunization room or ward dispensary. The nurse in charge fills out a standard requisition and issue form, detailing the quantities consumed, current balance, and quantities requested. This form requires authorization from the facility head or medical officer before submission to the local government area or central medical store. Adhering to scheduled ordering cycles ensures timely review and dispatch by logisticians.

Documentation and Logistics Management Information Systems

Good record keeping turns raw numbers into actionable supply chain intelligence. Stock cards must be updated immediately whenever commodities enter or leave the storage room. The Logistics Management Information System (LMIS) relies on these paper registers or electronic tools to track consumption trends across the country. Discrepancies between physical counts and stock card balances signal potential pilferage, poor record keeping, or unrecorded waste. Regular auditing of these logs protects public health resources.

Storage, Handling, and Quality Control

Proper storage preserves the integrity of injection commodities. Packaging must remain dry, clean, and intact to ensure sterility at the point of care. Cartons should be stacked off the floor on pallets and away from direct sunlight or damp walls. Nurses must inspect incoming shipments for damaged seals, crushed boxes, or water marks. Items showing compromised sterile barriers must be quarantined and reported immediately rather than used on patients.

Preventing Wastage and Managing Stockouts

Wastage drains facility budgets and depletes national stores. Common causes of loss include over-allocation, poor stock rotation where older items are left behind to expire, and careless handling. Facilities implement the First-Expired, First-Out (FEFO) rule to ensure older inventory is dispensed before newer arrivals. When a stockout occurs despite planning, nurses must communicate early with neighboring clinics or central authorities for emergency redistribution instead of improvising unsafe administration methods.

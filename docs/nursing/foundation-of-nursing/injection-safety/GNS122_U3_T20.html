United Nations Packaging Requirements

Introduction to Dangerous Goods Transport in Healthcare

Hospitals and laboratories routinely send infectious specimens, chemicals, and radioactive diagnostic agents between facilities. Transporting these materials carries inherent biological and chemical risks to postal workers, courier staff, and the general public. United Nations packaging requirements establish strict international baselines to prevent leaks, punctures, and exposure during transit. Adhering to these protocols ensures that diagnostic samples arrive intact without compromising community health or environmental safety.

Classification of Infectious Substances

Biological substances transported for medical analysis are split into two primary hazard tiers based on their severity and form.

  • Category A: Infectious substances transported in a form that, when exposure occurs, is capable of permanent disability, life-threatening, or fatal disease in otherwise healthy humans or animals. Assigned the proper shipping name UN 2814 or UN 2900.
  • Category B: Infectious substances that do not meet the criteria for inclusion in Category A. These are assigned the proper shipping name UN 3373 and cover most routine diagnostic specimens sent from clinics to reference labs.

The Triple Packaging System

Safe transport of infectious materials relies on a concentric three-layer containment design known as the triple packaging system. Each layer serves a distinct safety function to ensure zero leakage escapes into the environment.

  1. Primary Receptacle: A leak-proof glass, metal, or plastic primary container that holds the liquid or solid specimen directly.
  2. Secondary Packaging: A durable, leak-proof secondary container that encloses one or multiple primary receptacles, cushioned with enough absorbent material to soak up the entire liquid contents if breakage occurs.
  3. Outer Packaging: A rigid outer box of adequate strength for its capacity, mass, and intended use, bearing all mandatory UN markings and hazard labels.

The UN Packaging Code and Certification

Certified outer packaging displays a standardized alphanumeric code stamped or printed directly onto the material. This code proves that the packaging prototype passed rigorous drop, stack, and pressure resistance tests.

A typical UN code starts with the UN packaging symbol (the letters 'UN'), followed by a series of numbers and letters indicating the container type (such as 4G for a fiberboard box), the material subgroup, the performance standard (such as X, Y, or Z), the solid or liquid designation, the gross mass or vapor pressure, and the manufacturing year and country code.

Labeling, Marking, and Documentation Protocols

Proper exterior labeling informs handlers about the biological hazard inside and guides emergency responders during an incident. Outer packages containing Category B specimens must display the square-on-point diamond mark enclosing the UN 3373 code. In addition, the proper shipping name 'Biological Substance, Category B' must appear adjacent to the mark in letters at least 6 millimeters high. Contact information for a responsible person knowledgeable about the shipment must also be clearly visible on the exterior documentation.

Nursing Responsibilities in Specimen Transport

Nurses frequently collect and prepare diagnostic specimens, making them accountable for initial containment integrity. Personnel must verify that lids on primary tubes are tightly sealed to prevent leakage caused by atmospheric pressure changes during transport. Staff should never improvise packaging using standard garbage bags or uncertified food containers when shipping diagnostic samples off-site. Maintaining compliance safeguards both clinical coworkers and transport personnel from accidental biological exposure.

Paracentesis Abdominis

Introduction and Definition of Paracentesis Abdominis

Paracentesis abdominis is an invasive bedside or theater procedure where a sterile needle or catheter is introduced into the peritoneal cavity to withdraw ascetic fluid. This procedure serves both diagnostic purposes to identify the cause of new-onset ascites or spontaneous bacterial peritonitis, and therapeutic goals to relieve severe abdominal pressure and respiratory distress caused by massive fluid accumulation.

Indications and Therapeutic Goals

The clinical situations requiring fluid removal are distinct. Diagnostic paracentesis is indicated for any patient presenting with new-onset ascites of unknown origin or suspected infection. Therapeutic paracentesis is indicated when tense ascites causes pain, early satiety, or compromises lung expansion. The main goal is symptom relief and diagnostic clarity without inducing hemodynamic instability.

Contraindications and Risk Assessment

  • Absolute contraindications: Acute surgical abdomen requiring immediate exploratory laparotomy.
  • Relative contraindications: Severe bleeding disorders, uncooperative patient, severe bowel distension, and previous abdominal surgery with suspected bowel adhesions.

Nurses must review coagulation profiles, specifically platelet counts and International Normalized Ratio (INR), before the procedure begins to minimize bleeding risks.

Pre-Procedure Nursing Responsibilities and Patient Preparation

Preparation involves obtaining informed consent, explaining the steps to reduce anxiety, and instructing the patient to empty their bladder immediately before the procedure to prevent accidental puncture. Baseline vital signs and abdominal girth measurements are documented. Positioning the patient in a semi-Fowler's position or sitting upright allows fluid to pool dependently in the lower abdomen.

Equipment Needed and Sterile Technique

Gathering the correct equipment ensures a smooth procedure. Essential items include a paracentesis tray, local anesthetic, antiseptic solution, collection bags or vacuum bottles, specimen tubes, and sterile gloves. Strict aseptic technique is maintained throughout preparation and draping to prevent introduction of pathogens into the sterile peritoneal cavity.

Intra-Procedure Nursing Assistance and Patient Monitoring

During the procedure, the nurse supports the patient, monitors vital signs every fifteen minutes, and observes for signs of circulatory collapse or vasovagal episodes. Rapid fluid removal can cause sudden shifts in intravascular volume, leading to hypotension. The nurse watches for dizziness, pallor, sweating, or shortness of breath, and communicates any changes to the doctor immediately.

Post-Procedure Care, Specimen Handling, and Documentation

Following needle removal, apply a sterile dressing and monitor the puncture site for persistent leakage or bleeding. Record the total volume, color, and clarity of the fluid drained, along with post-procedure vital signs. Label collected specimen tubes correctly and send them to the laboratory promptly for cell count, gram stain, and culture.

Potential Complications and Emergency Nursing Interventions

Complications can arise despite careful technique. Persistent fluid leakage from the puncture site, localized hematoma, bowel perforation, and hypovolemic shock are primary concerns. Nurses must inspect the dressing frequently, monitor for severe abdominal pain or rebound tenderness indicating peritonitis, and report signs of shock immediately to initiate fluid resuscitation or albumin replacement.

Providing Means of Elimination

Introduction to Elimination Needs

Elimination is a basic human physiological need that involves the removal of metabolic waste products from the body. When patients are bedridden, weak, or recovering from surgery, they cannot use the standard bathroom facilities independently. Nurses must step in to provide alternative means of elimination such as bedpans, urinals, and specialized pads. Meeting this need promptly preserves skin integrity, prevents urinary tract infections, and maintains the psychological dignity of the patient. Delays in addressing elimination requests often lead to involuntary incontinence, patient distress, and unnecessary physical strain trying to reach a toilet unaided.

Using Bedpans Safely

A bedpan is used for elimination by patients who are confined to bed and unable to walk to the toilet. There are two main types: the standard fracture bedpan, which has a shallow end for patients with hip or spinal fractures, and the regular deep bedpan.

  • Fracture bedpan: Features a flat, low profile designed to minimize patient movement during placement.
  • Regular bedpan: Has a deep bowl suitable for patients with good upper body strength who can lift their hips.

Always warm cold metal bedpans before use and ensure privacy using screens. Elevating the head of the bed slightly mimics a natural sitting posture, which aids gravity during bowel elimination.

Managing Urinals for Male Patients

Urinals are portable containers designed primarily for male patients to void urine while lying in bed or sitting on a chair. Because anatomy allows for easy directional flow, male urinals reduce the need to lift the pelvis.

  • Plastic urinals: Lightweight, durable, and commonly used in modern wards.
  • Glass urinals: Older type, easy to inspect for urine color and sediment, but breakable.

Always empty, rinse, and wash urinals immediately after use to control ward odors and prevent bacterial growth. Hang the clean urinal securely on the side rail of the bed within the patient's reach if they are able to manage it independently.

Adult Diapers and Incontinence Pads

Adult diapers and absorbent pads are essential for managing urinary or fecal incontinence when bedpans and urinals are impractical. They protect the skin from moisture damage and preserve environmental hygiene. However, prolonged use without regular changing traps heat and moisture, creating a breeding ground for microorganisms. Nurses must assess skin integrity during every change, looking for redness, maceration, or skin breakdown, particularly over bony prominences like the sacrum and buttocks.

Perineal and Nappy Care

Perineal hygiene during elimination care prevents the spread of pathogens from the anal region to the urethra. When cleaning a female patient, the nurse must always wipe from the urinary meatus toward the anus to prevent introducing faecal bacteria into the urinary tract. Use warm water and mild soap, then pat the skin completely dry before applying a fresh diaper or pad. Avoid harsh rubbing, which strips the natural lipid barrier of vulnerable geriatric or bedridden skin.

Infection Control and Precautionary Measures

Providing means of elimination exposes healthcare workers and patients to bodily fluids containing infectious agents like hepatitis viruses and enteric pathogens. Standard precautions must be strictly observed during every elimination procedure.

  1. Hand hygiene: Wash hands before and after patient contact and remove gloves.
  2. Personal Protective Equipment: Wear clean non-sterile gloves and aprons when handling bedpans or urinals.
  3. Equipment sanitation: Empty waste into designated sluice sinks and sanitize reusable pans in thermal washers or disinfectant solutions.

Never place contaminated bedpans directly on bedside tables or chairs, as this spreads microorganisms across the ward environment.

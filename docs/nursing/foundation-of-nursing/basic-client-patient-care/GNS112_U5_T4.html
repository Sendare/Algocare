Care of the Nails

Introduction to Nail Anatomy and Physiology

The nail is an accessory structure of the integumentary system composed mainly of hard, keratinized epidermal cells. Understanding basic nail anatomy guides safe nursing interventions, especially during hygiene and clinical assessments.

  • Nail Plate: The visible, hard part of the nail resting on the nail bed, providing protection to the underlying sensitive fingertips.
  • Nail Bed: The skin beneath the nail plate containing rich blood vessels that give the nail its pink color.
  • Cuticle: A fold of skin at the base of the nail plate acting as a waterproof barrier to protect the matrix from infection.
  • Nail Matrix: The tissue under the cuticle containing active cells that generate new nail growth.
  • Lunula: The whitish, half-moon shaped area at the base of the nail visible above the cuticle.

Purposes and Importance of Nail Care in Nursing

Nail care is an essential component of basic nursing care aimed at promoting hygiene, comfort, and safety. Long or neglected nails harbor pathogenic microorganisms, which patients easily transfer to vulnerable mucous membranes or surgical wounds during scratching. Routine cleaning reduces cross-infection risks in hospital wards. Furthermore, systematic inspection of the nail unit serves as a non-invasive window into the patient's systemic health, revealing signs of respiratory distress, nutritional deficiencies, and circulatory issues.

Routine Nail Assessment and Common Abnormalities

Nurses must inspect the fingernails and toenails during admission and daily bed baths. Observation focuses on color, shape, texture, and the angle of the nail bed.

Equipment and Supplies for Basic Nail Hygiene

Performing safe nail care requires proper instruments to avoid accidental skin tears or tissue trauma. Gather a wash basin, warm water, mild soap, a soft washcloth, an orange stick or wooden cuticle pusher, and an emery board or nail file. Nail clippers should be used cautiously and only when permitted by facility policy or specific patient assessment. Avoid sharp metal instruments for cleaning underneath the nail plate, as they easily puncture the delicate nail bed and introduce bacteria.

Step-by-Step Procedure for Routine Nail Care

  1. Preparation: Explain the procedure to the patient, gather supplies, and wash hands.
  2. Soaking: Immerse the patient's hands or feet in warm, soapy water for 10 to 15 minutes to soften the nail plates and surrounding cuticles.
  3. Cleaning: Gently wash the hands or feet, using a soft washcloth and an orange stick to clean beneath the nail plates.
  4. Filing: File the nails straight across to prevent ingrown edges. Avoid filing down into the corners.
  5. Moisturizing: Apply lotion to the hands or feet, massaging gently while avoiding the spaces between the toes to prevent moisture buildup.

Special Considerations for Diabetic and High-Risk Patients

Patients with diabetes mellitus, peripheral vascular disease, or immunosuppression require extreme caution during nail care. Reduced peripheral sensation means these patients may not feel cuts or pain if injured, leading to silent ulcerations and severe infections like gangrene. Nurses must never use sharp scissors or clippers on diabetic patients without a direct physician or podiatrist order. File nails gently instead, and immediately refer any signs of ingrown toenails, fungal infections, or breaks in the skin to a specialist.

Prevention and Management of Common Nail Complications

Nail disorders such as paronychia, fungal infections (onychomycosis), and ingrown toenails cause significant discomfort and require prompt nursing intervention. Paronychia is an infection of the fold surrounding the nail, often presenting with redness, swelling, and pus, which requires warm soaks and topical or systemic antimicrobials. Instruct patients to wear properly fitted shoes to prevent pressure-induced nail trauma. Never attempt to surgically correct severe ingrown toenails at the bedside; always refer the patient to a qualified podiatrist or physician.

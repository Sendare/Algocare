Structure of Health Facilities and Social Services

Introduction to Healthcare Delivery Systems in Nigeria

The Nigerian healthcare delivery system operates on a tiered structure designed to provide comprehensive care from the grassroots up to specialized national centres. This three-tier arrangement divides responsibilities among the three arms of government: local, state, and federal. At the base lies the primary level, managed mainly by local government councils with support from state agencies. The secondary level falls under state government purview, catering to referrals from primary care. The tertiary level represents the apex of specialized medical care, managed primarily by the federal government through teaching hospitals and federal medical centres. Private practitioners, faith-based organizations, and non-governmental bodies also contribute significantly across all tiers, supplementing public sector efforts in service delivery.

Primary Health Care Facilities

Primary health care represents the first point of contact for individuals, families, and communities with the national health system. These facilities are situated close to where people live and work, making basic health services readily accessible.

  • Health Posts: The most basic unit providing rudimentary health education and minor treatments in deep rural areas.
  • Primary Health Clinics: Staffed by community health workers and nurses, offering outpatient care, routine immunization, and antenatal services.
  • Comprehensive Health Centres: Larger facilities capable of handling minor emergencies, normal deliveries, and basic laboratory investigations.

Services at this level focus heavily on disease prevention, health promotion, and management of common endemic ailments like malaria.

Secondary Health Facilities

Secondary health facilities provide specialized care for patients referred from primary health centres. These establishments typically take the form of general hospitals, comprehensive health centres managed at the state level, or private specialist clinics. They possess diagnostic equipment like X-ray machines and basic laboratory services that allow for deeper investigation of diseases.

Doctors, general practitioners, and specialized nurses operate within these walls to manage medical emergencies, surgical interventions, and inpatient admissions. A common misconception is that patients can walk into a secondary facility for routine immunization; however, these centres concentrate on curative inpatient services, obstetric emergencies, and pediatric care requiring admission.

Tertiary Health Facilities

Tertiary health facilities constitute the apex of healthcare delivery, consisting of teaching hospitals, federal medical centres, and highly specialized institutes. These institutions provide highly specialized medical, surgical, and diagnostic services that require advanced technology and expert sub-specialists. Beyond direct patient care, tertiary facilities serve as training grounds for doctors, nurses, and other allied health professionals, while driving clinical research to address national health challenges. Patients arrive here almost exclusively through formal referral channels from secondary facilities due to complex conditions like chronic renal failure, complicated neurosurgery, or advanced oncology care.

Social Services and Community Welfare

Healthcare extends beyond biological healing to address social determinants of health, poverty, and vulnerability. Social services operate within and alongside health facilities to support patients facing financial distress, domestic abuse, disability, or abandonment. Medical social workers assess psychosocial needs, link indigent patients to charity funds, arrange foster care for abandoned infants, and coordinate rehabilitation for individuals recovering from substance abuse. Integrating social welfare into clinical settings ensures holistic care, acknowledging that environmental and economic stressors directly impact physical recovery and disease transmission.

Inter-Referral Pathways and Continuity of Care

A functioning health system relies on seamless communication and structured referral pathways between different levels of care. When a primary clinic encounters a condition beyond its capacity, such as obstructed labor, staff initiate a formal referral letter detailing patient history, vital signs, and interventions attempted. The receiving secondary or tertiary facility evaluates the patient and eventually discharges them back to the primary facility for follow-up care and community reintegration. This bidirectional flow prevents hospital congestion, optimizes resource allocation, and ensures that patients experience uninterrupted medical oversight across their healing journey.

The Role of Nurses Across Different Facility Levels

Nursing responsibilities shift dynamically depending on the tier of the health facility. In primary health centres, community health nurses focus on public health, home visits, immunizations, and health education. Secondary facilities demand nurses skilled in general ward management, perioperative care, and acute medical-surgical interventions. Tertiary institutions employ specialized nurses—such as intensive care, renal, and cardiometabolic nurse specialists—who manage complex life support technologies and mentor student nurses. Despite these varied settings, nurses remain the central pillar coordinating patient care, advocating for vulnerable populations, and bridging communication gaps between patients and medical doctors.

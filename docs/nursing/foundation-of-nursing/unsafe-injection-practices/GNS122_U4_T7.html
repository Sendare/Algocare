Reasons Patients Do Not Insist on Safe Injections

Introduction to Safe Injection Practices

Safe injection practices protect both patients and healthcare workers from bloodborne pathogens and preventable infections. In many Nigerian clinical settings, parenteral therapy is culturally preferred, leading to a high volume of daily injections. When patients accept reused syringes or unsterile equipment without question, the risk of transmitting infections like Hepatitis B, Hepatitis C, and HIV increases significantly. Understanding why patients stay silent helps nurses bridge the communication gap and enforce safer clinical routines.

Power Dynamics and Doctor-Patient Hierarchy

A rigid hierarchical gap often exists between healthcare providers and patients. Many individuals view doctors and nurses as infallible authorities whose actions should never be questioned. This traditional view makes patients reluctant to ask about sterile techniques or needle safety, fearing they might be seen as disrespectful or disruptive to hospital workflow.

Health Literacy and Lack of Awareness

Many patients simply do not know what constitutes a safe injection. They might notice a needle being unwrapped, but they lack the technical knowledge to spot expired supplies, multi-dose vial contamination, or improper disposal habits. Without basic health literacy regarding transmission routes, patients cannot recognize dangerous shortcuts taken by busy staff.

Cultural Preference for Injections

  • Perceived potency: Many communities firmly believe that medications delivered via skin-piercing routes work faster and stronger than tablets.
  • Symbol of recovery: Receiving an injection is often viewed as a tangible sign of receiving serious, effective medical attention.
  • Desire for quick relief: Patients push for fast cures to return to daily economic activities, overlooking safety protocols.

Fear of Retaliation and Compromised Care

Patients often worry that questioning a nurse or doctor will label them as troublesome. In resource-constrained settings, patients fear that speaking up about hygiene or needle safety might cause staff to withhold proper attention, delay treatments, or treat them with hostility during their stay.

Institutional and Systemic Barriers

Systemic challenges within healthcare facilities create environments where safe injection practices are difficult to maintain. Chronic shortages of disposable syringes, absence of puncture-proof safety boxes, and overburdened shift schedules push personnel to cut corners. When supplies are scarce, patients may even be asked to buy their own injection kits from outside vendors, introducing variable quality and further reducing patient confidence in the system.

The Role of the Nurse in Patient Advocacy

Nurses occupy a frontline position to dismantle these barriers through active advocacy and patient education. By welcoming questions, explaining procedures in simple terms, and demonstrating infection control steps, nurses build trust. Creating an open ward environment reassures patients that their safety is a shared priority rather than an insult to professional competence.

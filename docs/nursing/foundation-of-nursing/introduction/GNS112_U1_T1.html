Definition of Nursing/Nurse

Introduction to Nursing as a Profession

Nursing is both an art and a science that focuses on the protection, promotion, and optimization of health and human abilities. It involves the prevention of illness and injury, the alleviation of suffering through the diagnosis and treatment of human response, and advocacy in the care of individuals, families, communities, and populations. As an autonomous profession, nursing blends compassionate patient-centered care with rigorous scientific knowledge.

Historical Evolution of Modern Nursing

  • Florence Nightingale Era: Shifted nursing from informal domestic help to a structured, sanitary, and disciplined vocation during the Crimean War.
  • Post-War Expansion: The mid-20th century saw nursing move into formal academic settings, establishing research-backed clinical guidelines.
  • Contemporary Era: Modern nursing demands advanced critical thinking, specialized certifications, and independent practice capabilities across diverse healthcare settings.

Core Definitions by Global Nursing Organizations

Global bodies shape how nursing is practiced and understood worldwide. The International Council of Nurses (ICN) defines nursing as encompassing autonomous and collaborative care of individuals of all ages, families, groups, and communities, whether sick or well, in all settings. Similarly, the World Health Organization (WHO) highlights the vital contribution of nurses to universal health coverage and primary healthcare delivery systems.

Key Concepts and Metaparadigm of Nursing

The nursing metaparadigm defines the four core phenomena that anchor all theoretical nursing frameworks and clinical practice:

  1. Person: The recipient of nursing care, including individuals, families, and communities.
  2. Environment: The internal and external surroundings that affect a person's health and recovery.
  3. Health: The dynamic, subjective state of well-being experienced by the individual.
  4. Nursing: The attributes, actions, and interventions executed by the nurse on behalf of the person.

Characteristics and Roles of a Professional Nurse

A professional nurse integrates specialized knowledge, ethical integrity, and empathy into daily patient interactions. Key roles include serving as a care provider, patient advocate, educator, and communicator. Professional accountability means taking responsibility for one's actions, maintaining competence through continuous learning, and adhering strictly to established codes of ethics.

The Scope of Nursing Practice in Nigeria

In Nigeria, nursing practice is regulated by the Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria (NMCN). The scope defines what qualified nurses are educated, authorized, and legally permitted to do. It covers preventive, curative, and rehabilitative care in primary health centers, teaching hospitals, and private clinics. Adhering to the NMCN guidelines safeguards the public against quackery and ensures high standards of professional conduct.

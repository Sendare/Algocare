Stockinette

Introduction and Definition of Stockinette

A stockinette is a seamless, knitted, tube-like fabric primarily used in orthopedic nursing to protect a patient's skin before the application of a plaster of Paris or synthetic cast. Because it stretches easily in the circumferential direction, it contours smoothly to various limb shapes without forming restrictive ridges or pressure points. Cotton stockinette allows for breathability, reducing moisture accumulation against the skin beneath rigid immobilization materials.

Clinical Purposes and Indications

Stockinettes serve multiple critical functions in surgical and emergency settings:

  • Skin Protection: Acts as a physical barrier between fragile skin and abrasive casting materials.
  • Friction Reduction: Prevents shear forces when rigid casts rub against bony prominences during limb movement.
  • Exudate Management: Absorbs minor perspiration, though it is never a substitute for proper wound dressings.
  • Padding Retention: Holds underlying orthopedic wool or padding securely in place.

Types of Stockinettes: Cotton vs. Synthetic

Clinical stockinettes are manufactured from different fibers depending on the specific procedural requirements. Cotton stockinettes are standard for routine casting because they are gentle and absorbent. Synthetic stockinettes, often made from polyester or polypropylene, are sometimes utilized with waterproof cast padding systems because they do not retain liquid and dry rapidly after washing or bathing.

Measurement and Application Technique

Proper sizing ensures the stockinette functions effectively without causing circulatory compromise. The nurse selects a tube width that matches the circumference of the limb—too large creates uncomfortable folds, while too small acts like a tourniquet.

  1. Measure the limb length, ensuring the stockinette extends several inches beyond both the proximal and distal ends of the planned cast.
  2. Gently slide the tube over the extremity without pulling forcefully on fragile skin.
  3. Smooth out all wrinkles completely to prevent localized pressure ulcers.
  4. Fold the extra material back over the cast edges once the hard layer is applied.

Post-Application Monitoring and Complications

Following cast or splint application over a stockinette, continuous monitoring is vital. Nurses assess neurovascular status regularly—checking pulse, pallor, pain, paresthesia, and paralysis. A common clinical error is assuming a stockinette prevents all pressure sores; if applied too tightly or allowed to bunch up, it contributes directly to compartment syndrome or skin necrosis.

Nursing Responsibilities and Patient Education

Nurses must educate patients to keep the stockinette and cast clean and dry. Patients should be instructed never to insert foreign objects like coat hangers or knitting needles down the stockinette to scratch an itch, as this easily breaks the skin and introduces bacteria. Any foul odor, unusual burning sensation, or wetness emanating from beneath the stockinette must be reported immediately.

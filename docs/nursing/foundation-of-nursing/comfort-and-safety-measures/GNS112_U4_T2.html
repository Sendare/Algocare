Simple Bed Making

Introduction and Clinical Purpose of Bed Making

Bed making is a fundamental nursing procedure designed to promote patient comfort, prevent skin breakdown, and maintain a clean healthcare environment. A neatly made bed reduces physical irritation from wrinkles, which is a primary trigger for pressure ulcer formation in immobile clients. Beyond physical comfort, managing the patient's immediate surroundings contributes significantly to their psychological well-being during hospital stays in Nigerian health facilities. Nurses perform various types of bed arrangements depending on the client's mobility status, ranging from completely empty frames to beds occupied by fragile individuals.

Infection Control Principles During Bed Making

Infection prevention is paramount when handling hospital linen. Microorganisms from patient skin and body fluids can contaminate sheets, creating a vector for cross-infection across the ward.

  • No shaking: Never shake used linen in the air, as this disperses dust and infectious particles into the ward atmosphere.
  • Keep away from uniform: Hold soiled linen away from your scrub suit or uniform to prevent contaminating your clothing.
  • Proper disposal: Place removed linen directly into a designated laundry hamper or waterproof bag rather than tossing it on the floor.
  • Hand hygiene: Perform hand washing and wear clean non-sterile gloves when handling soiled sheets.

Essential Equipment and Linen Preparation

Organising your supplies before starting saves time and prevents unnecessary trips across the clinical unit. Gathering all required items in the correct sequence ensures smooth workflow.

  • Bottom sheet: Used as the foundational layer, either flat or fitted.
  • Draw sheet: Placed across the middle of the mattress to help reposition the patient later.
  • Mackintosh: A waterproof sheet placed under the draw sheet to protect the mattress from bodily fluids.
  • Top sheet: Covers the patient, placed with the hem facing upwards initially.
  • Blanket and spread: Added for warmth and aesthetic finish depending on ward climate.
  • Pillowcases: Clean covers for each pillow used.
  • Laundry bag: For collecting used linens.

Making an Unoccupied Bed

An unoccupied bed is prepared when the client is ambulatory, in the bathroom, or away for diagnostic investigations. The primary goal is to provide a clean, smooth base for when they return. Begin by stripping the old linen one layer at a time, checking for forgotten patient items like dentures or glasses. Wipe the mattress if necessary and let it dry. Place the bottom sheet, mackintosh, and draw sheet, tucking them securely using mitered corners to prevent slipping. Move to the opposite side of the bed to pull and tuck the remaining sides tightly. Finish by adding the top sheet, blanket, and placing the neatly covered pillow at the head of the bed.

Making an Occupied Bed

Bed making with a patient in place requires careful coordination, communication, and body mechanics. This technique is reserved for clients who are strictly on bed rest or too weak to get up. Explain each step to the patient before touching them to reduce anxiety and secure cooperation. Roll the patient gently to one side of the bed facing away from you, then push the soiled linen toward the patient's back. Lay down the clean bottom sheet on the cleared half of the mattress, miter the corners, and roll the excess clean linen close to the patient's spine. Roll the patient back over the hump of clean linen, remove the dirty sheets from the other side, pull the clean sheets taut, and tuck them securely. Ensure proper body alignment and side rail usage throughout the process.

Post-Procedure Care and Documentation

The nursing procedure is incomplete until the environment is restored and safety checks are finalized. Lower the bed to its lowest position and lock the wheels to prevent falls when the client attempts to stand. Raise the appropriate side rails as dictated by the patient's care plan. Dispose of all waste and soiled linen according to standard hospital infection control protocols. Wash your hands thoroughly to remove any transient pathogens. Finally, document the procedure in the patient's nursing notes, noting the skin condition observed during the linen change and the client's tolerance of the activity.

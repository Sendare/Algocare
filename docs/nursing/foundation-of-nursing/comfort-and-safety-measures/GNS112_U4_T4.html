Use of Bed Accessories

Introduction and Purpose of Hospital Bed Accessories

Hospital bed accessories are specialized attachments used in clinical settings to modify standard hospital beds. These tools help maintain correct body alignment, prevent complications of prolonged bed rest, and enhance patient comfort. Nurses select these items based on individual patient needs, diagnosis, and mobility status.

Using the Bed Cradle for Pressure and Weight Relief

A bed cradle is a metal or plastic frame placed over the patient's body to lift the weight of top bedclothes off the lower extremities. It is particularly useful for clients with severe burns, open wounds, painful joint conditions, or advanced peripheral neuropathy.

  • Cylinder Cradle: A semi-cylindrical frame that keeps heavy blankets away from the legs.
  • Hoop Cradle: Features arched bars that create a tunnel over the affected body part.

When positioning a bed cradle, ensure the linens are tucked securely under the mattress edges so cold air does not draft onto the patient's skin.

Utilizing Air Rings and Pressure Reduction Cushions

An air ring is an inflatable doughnut-shaped cushion placed beneath a patient to relieve pressure on the sacrum or buttocks.

*Clinical Observation*: Traditional circular rubber air rings are largely discouraged in modern practice because they can actually restrict blood flow to the central tissue area by creating a localized edge effect, increasing ischemia risk. Modern flat-profile fluidized or alternating-pressure pads are preferred.

Supportive Devices: Backrests, Footboards, and Bed Blocks

Different structural accessories help maintain posture and prevent skeletal deformities during prolonged bed rest.

  • Backrest: Elevates the upper torso for feeding, reading, or easing dyspnea.
  • Footboard: Placed perpendicular to the mattress to prevent foot drop by keeping ankles at a ninety-degree angle.
  • Bed Blocks: Wooden or rubber blocks placed under the bed legs to elevate the entire frame for postural drainage or shock management.

Safety Precautions and Maintenance of Bed Accessories

Proper upkeep of bed accessories ensures patient safety and extends equipment longevity. Nurses must routinely inspect metal frames for sharp edges, check inflatable items for slow air leaks, and clean all plastic or rubber surfaces with hospital-approved disinfectants between patients to prevent cross-infection. Bed blocks must be stable and locked securely to prevent structural collapse.

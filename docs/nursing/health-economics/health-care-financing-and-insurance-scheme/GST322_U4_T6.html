Efficiency Effectiveness and Equity in Health Financing

Introduction to Health Financing Core Concepts

Health financing is the function of a health system that deals with the mobilization, accumulation, and allocation of money to cover the health needs of the population. The ultimate objective of any financing system is to improve health outcomes while protecting households from financial catastrophe due to out-of-pocket spending. In Nigeria, financing comes from various sources including government allocations, donor funding, out-of-pocket payments, and health insurance schemes.

  • Revenue collection: The process of raising money from health insurance contributions, taxes, and out-of-pocket spending.
  • Pooling: The accumulation and management of financial resources so that health risks are shared across the population.
  • Purchasing: The process of paying health care providers to deliver services to the insured or general population.

Defining Efficiency in Health Care Financing

Efficiency in health financing means getting the maximum possible health gain from the available financial and physical resources. Resources in the Nigerian health sector are often scarce, making it vital to minimize waste and optimize every naira spent. When a health system is efficient, it produces more outputs, such as immunizations or consultations, for the same or lower cost.

Allocative Versus Technical Efficiency

Efficiency is divided into two major categories that guide how health managers distribute and use funds.

  • Allocative efficiency: Occurs when resources are distributed among different health programs or interventions to maximize the health of the entire population. For example, shifting funds from expensive urban tertiary care to preventive primary health care.
  • Technical efficiency: Occurs when a specific health intervention or service is produced at the lowest possible cost, avoiding waste in labor, time, or medical supplies.

Understanding Effectiveness in Health Interventions

Effectiveness measures whether a health intervention actually produces the intended beneficial result in real-world clinical practice. While efficacy is measured under ideal laboratory conditions, effectiveness evaluates performance in everyday settings like a busy Nigerian general hospital. A financing system may be efficient by keeping costs low, but if the treatment does not improve patient health, it lacks effectiveness.

Ensuring Equity in Health Care Access and Financing

Equity refers to fairness in the distribution of health care services and the burden of financing them. Equity has two core dimensions: equal access for equal need, and financing according to the ability to pay. Out-of-pocket payments violate equity because a poor farmer pays the same flat price for a drug as a wealthy merchant, representing a larger share of the farmer's income.

Horizontal Versus Vertical Equity in Resource Allocation

  • Horizontal equity: Requires that people with the same health needs receive the same level of care and make equal financial contributions. For example, two pregnant women with normal, uncomplicated pregnancies should access standard antenatal care without discrimination.
  • Vertical equity: Requires that people with different health needs or different abilities to pay are treated differently to account for those differences. For example, wealthy citizens should pay higher health insurance premiums, or individuals with severe chronic illnesses should receive more subsidized care.

Balancing Efficiency, Effectiveness, and Equity in Nigeria's Health System

Health policy makers face the challenge of balancing efficiency, effectiveness, and equity, which can sometimes conflict. For instance, focusing solely on technical efficiency might lead to closing rural clinics because they serve fewer patients, which destroys equity and geographic access. Achieving Universal Health Coverage in Nigeria requires strategic purchasing, transparent resource allocation, and pro-poor financing reforms that harmonize all three principles.

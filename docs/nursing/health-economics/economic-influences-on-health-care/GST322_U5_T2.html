Importance of Proper Funding at All Levels of Health Care

Introduction to Health Care Financing

Health care financing involves mobilizing, pooling, and allocating financial resources to meet the health needs of a population. Without a structured financial framework, health systems struggle to maintain basic operations, procure essential medicines, or retain skilled personnel. In Nigeria, financing comes from a mix of government allocations, out-of-pocket payments, donor assistance, and health insurance schemes. Proper funding determines whether a health facility remains functional or collapses under the weight of community demand. Nurses often experience the direct fallout of poor financing when basic consumables are missing at the bedside.

Levels of Health Care Delivery in Nigeria

Nigeria operates a three-tier health care delivery system designed to ensure accessibility from the grassroots to specialized centers.

  • Primary Health Care (PHC): Managed at the local government level, serving as the first point of contact for basic community health, immunizations, and antenatal care.
  • Secondary Health Care: Managed by state governments, providing general hospital care, minor surgeries, and specialized nursing care for referred cases.
  • Tertiary Health Care: Managed predominantly by the federal government, consisting of teaching hospitals and federal medical centers offering highly specialized treatment and training.

Impact of Adequate Funding on Primary Health Care

Primary health care forms the bedrock of disease prevention and health promotion. When primary centers receive proper funding, routine immunizations remain consistent, maternal mortality drops, and infectious disease outbreaks are contained early. Adequate funds allow local clinics to maintain solar-powered refrigerators for vaccines, run functional laboratories, and hire community health workers. Conversely, underfunded primary centers force rural dwellers to travel long distances for simple ailments, placing an unnecessary burden on higher-level facilities.

Resource Allocation in Secondary and Tertiary Health Care

Secondary and tertiary institutions require massive capital investments to procure diagnostic machinery, maintain intensive care units, and support specialized training. Proper allocation ensures that teaching hospitals can conduct complex surgeries and biomedical research. However, budget delays and bureaucratic bottlenecks often stall infrastructural upgrades. Effective resource distribution means balancing the high costs of advanced technology with the everyday needs of ward nursing care and patient nutrition.

Consequences of Underfunding on Nursing Practice and Patient Outcomes

When health budgets shrink, nurses bear the brunt of operational deficits. Shortages of syringes, gloves, and disinfectants compromise infection control protocols and increase nosocomial infection rates. Nurses are frequently forced to improvise or ask impoverished patients to purchase basic supplies before treatment begins. This delays critical interventions and erodes public trust in the healthcare system. High workloads driven by staff shortages lead directly to burnout, mental exhaustion, and professional migration among nursing personnel.

Strategies for Improving Health Sector Funding in Nigeria

Revitalizing Nigeria's health sector requires deliberate policy actions beyond traditional budget allocations. Strengthening the implementation of the National Health Insurance Authority (NHIA) Act helps reduce catastrophic out-of-pocket spending. Governments must also increase budgetary allocations to align with regional commitments like the Abuja Declaration. Furthermore, plugging revenue leakages, engaging in public-private partnerships, and ensuring transparent fund management will maximize the impact of available resources.

Methods of Funding Primary Secondary and Tertiary Health Care

Introduction to Healthcare Financing and the Nigerian Health System

Healthcare financing determines how money is raised, pooled, and allocated to pay for health services in a population. Within the Nigerian three-tier health system, funding responsibilities are shared among federal, state, and local governments, alongside private entities. The primary tier focuses on basic health services at the community level, the secondary tier handles general hospital care, and the tertiary tier provides specialized medical interventions. Adequate resource mobilization remains essential for achieving universal health coverage, yet systemic underfunding often compromises service delivery across all levels.

Primary Healthcare Funding Mechanisms

Primary healthcare (PHC) forms the bedrock of disease prevention and basic curative services in Nigeria. Funding for this tier primarily flows through local government councils, supplemented by state allocations and federal interventions like the Basic Health Care Provision Fund (BHCPF).

  • Local Government Allocations: Statutory federal allocations directed to grassroots administration to run dispensaries and health posts.
  • BHCPF Support: Dedicated funds designed to revamp primary facilities, ensuring minimum a package of healthcare services.
  • Community Financing: Local contributions through community-based health committees to support minor facility maintenance and drug supplies.

Secondary Healthcare Funding Structures

Secondary healthcare centers operate primarily at the general hospital and zonal hospital levels, managing cases referred from primary facilities. State governments bear the primary financial responsibility for establishing, staffing, and maintaining these general hospitals. Funding mechanisms rely heavily on state budgetary allocations, internally generated revenue (IGR) from user fees, and state-level health insurance schemes. Because state budgets face competing demands from other sectors, general hospitals frequently experience delays in equipment maintenance and drug procurement.

Tertiary Healthcare Financing and Specialized Care

Tertiary institutions, including teaching hospitals and federal medical centers, deliver highly specialized care, medical education, and research. The federal government shoulders the financial burden of these apex institutions through direct budgetary allocations managed by the Federal Ministry of Health. Due to the high cost of advanced medical technology and specialized training, tertiary hospitals also generate revenue through specialized clinics, diagnostic services, and private-ward charges. Despite federal backing, capital expenditure for state-of-the-art equipment remains a continuous financial hurdle.

Health Insurance Schemes: NHIA and State Initiatives

Health insurance pools financial risks to protect individuals from catastrophic health expenditures. The National Health Insurance Authority (NHIA) regulates health insurance across Nigeria, covering formal sector employees and extending programs to vulnerable groups. Concurrently, various state governments have established State Health Insurance Agencies (SHIAs) to capture informal sector workers and rural populations. Premium contributions, government subsidies, and donor grants form the core revenue streams for these insurance pools, aiming to reduce dependence on direct cash payments at hospital counters.

Out-of-Pocket Payments and Their Impact on Access

Out-of-pocket (OOP) expenditure refers to direct payments made by individuals at the point of service where no third-party payer is involved. In Nigeria, OOP remains the dominant mode of health financing, creating severe barriers to healthcare access. When patients must pay cash for consultations, diagnostics, and medicines immediately, many delay seeking care or forego treatment entirely. This financial vulnerability often drives low-income households into poverty when confronted with sudden illnesses or chronic disease management.

Donor Funding, NGOs, and Public-Private Partnerships

External aid, non-governmental organizations (NGOs), and public-private partnerships (PPPs) supplement domestic health financing in Nigeria. International donors channel funds into specific disease control programs, such as malaria, HIV/AIDS, and routine immunization campaigns. While external grants provide vital commodities and technical support, they can create sustainability challenges when donor priorities shift or funding cycles end. Public-private partnerships leverage private sector capital and management efficiency to upgrade diagnostic laboratories and hospital infrastructure.

Challenges and Sustainable Solutions in Healthcare Financing

Nigeria's healthcare financing landscape faces persistent hurdles, including poor budgetary releases, corruption, inefficient resource allocation, and a massive informal sector that evades tax and insurance nets. To achieve sustainable financing, stakeholders advocate for increased domestic resource mobilization, strict enforcement of mandatory health insurance laws, and transparent financial management systems. Nurses and healthcare managers must understand these financial structures to advocate for better resource allocation and deliver cost-effective patient care within constrained environments.

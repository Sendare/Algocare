Model for Improvement

Introduction to the Model for Improvement

The Model for Improvement is a simple yet powerful framework designed to guide rapid, meaningful change in healthcare settings. Developed by Associates in Process Improvement, it helps nursing teams shift from merely talking about problems to taking structured action. Unlike traditional research that proves a theory over years, this model accelerates local quality improvement by answering three fundamental questions and testing small changes using structured cycles.

The Three Fundamental Questions

Every improvement project built on this model rests on three core questions that must be answered before any testing begins:

  1. What are we trying to accomplish?: Defines the precise aim or goal of the project.
  2. How will we know that a change is an improvement?: Establishes the specific measures to track progress.
  3. What change can we make that will result in improvement?: Identifies actionable ideas and interventions to test.

Setting Clear and Measurable Aims

An aim statement answers the first fundamental question by being specific, measurable, time-bound, and relevant to patient care. Vague goals like 'we want to reduce patient falls' fail because they lack direction. A strong aim statement sets a concrete target, such as 'reduce patient falls on the surgical ward by 30% within the next three months.' This clarity unites the nursing team and establishes a firm baseline for tracking success.

Establishing Effective Nursing Measures

To answer the second fundamental question, nurse leaders track three distinct types of data:

  • Outcome Measures: Assess the overall impact on patient health, such as pressure ulcer rates.
  • Process Measures: Track whether the steps of the intervention are actually being performed correctly, like documenting skin assessments every two hours.
  • Balancing Measures: Monitor for unintended side effects in other parts of the system, such as checking if increased repositioning leads to severe nurse fatigue.

Developing Change Ideas

Finding the right change idea requires brainstorming grounded in clinical reality. Nurses can draw ideas from evidence-based guidelines, examining current workflow bottlenecks, or benchmarking against successful practices in other wards. The goal is not to adopt every idea at once, but to select high-leverage changes that are easy to test and hold strong potential to improve patient care.

The Plan-Do-Study-Act (PDSA) Cycle

The PDSA cycle is the testing engine of the Model for Improvement.

  • Plan: State the objective, formulate a prediction, and outline the test steps.
  • Do: Carry out the small-scale test on the ward and document unexpected observations.
  • Study: Analyze the collected data and compare the results against the original prediction.
  • Act: Decide whether to adapt the change, adopt it fully, or abandon the idea.

Testing and Scaling Improvements

Improvement starts small. Nurses should always test a change on a single patient, with one nursing shift, or in one specific room before expanding. This miniature scale limits risk. Once a PDSA cycle shows success, the team scales up the test by running it across multiple shifts or expanding to the entire ward. Only after consistent success across broader tests should the change become standard hospital policy.

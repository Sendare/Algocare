Functions and Responsibilities of the Nurse in the Primary Health Care Setting

Introduction to Primary Health Care and the Nurse's Role

Primary Health Care represents the first level of contact individuals, families, and communities have with the national health system. It brings health care as close as possible to where people live and work. Within this setting, the nurse functions as the frontline provider, care coordinator, and community health leader. Nigerian nurses in primary care bridge the gap between complex hospital systems and rural or underserved communities. Their responsibilities extend beyond treating immediate sicknesses to promoting wellness and preventing diseases at the grassroots level.

Core Principles of PHC Guiding Nursing Practice

  • Equity: Ensuring that health services are accessible to all community members regardless of wealth or social status.
  • Community Participation: Involving local leaders and residents in planning and implementing health interventions.
  • Intersectoral Collaboration: Partnering with agriculture, education, and water supply sectors to address the social determinants of health.
  • Appropriate Technology: Utilizing medical tools and methods that are scientifically sound, culturally acceptable, and affordable for the local population.

Clinical and Curative Nursing Duties in the PHC Clinic

Daily clinical routines in a primary health care clinic involve diagnosing and treating common ailments. Nurses manage minor injuries, administer prescribed medications, and treat endemic illnesses such as uncomplicated malaria and acute respiratory infections. They utilize standardized standing orders and protocol guidelines to initiate treatment safely. When patient conditions exceed the scope of primary care, nurses execute timely emergency referrals to general hospitals.

Maternal, Newborn, and Child Health Services

Maternal and child health forms the bedrock of primary care nursing in Nigeria. Nurses conduct antenatal care sessions to monitor fetal development and detect pregnancy complications early. They provide safe delivery services where facilities permit, followed by postnatal assessments for both mother and newborn. Child health clinics focus on growth monitoring, nutritional counseling, and routine developmental screenings to reduce infant mortality rates.

Disease Surveillance, Immunization, and Preventive Care

Preventive services protect entire communities from outbreaks of vaccine-preventable diseases. Nurses manage cold chain storage systems to maintain vaccine potency before administration. They conduct routine immunizations against polio, measles, tetanus, and other childhood scourges. Additionally, PHC nurses act as epidemiological sentinels by reporting unusual disease clusters or symptoms of potential epidemic-prone diseases to local health authorities.

Community Mobilization, Health Education, and Advocacy

Empowering communities requires active health education and local mobilization. Nurses translate complex medical concepts into accessible local languages during health talks and home visits. They address harmful traditional practices through respectful dialogue rather than confrontation. By advocating for clean water, sanitation facilities, and vector control, nurses tackle the root environmental causes of endemic diseases.

Leadership, Management, and Interprofessional Collaboration

Running a primary health care facility demands sound administrative and leadership skills. Nurses supervise community health extension workers, manage medical inventory, and maintain accurate health records. They collaborate daily with environmental health officers, laboratory technicians, and traditional birth attendants. Effective team management ensures that limited resources are maximized to deliver comprehensive care to every visiting patient.

Challenges and Ethical Considerations for the PHC Nurse

Primary care nurses in Nigeria often practice under difficult conditions characterized by infrastructural deficits, erratic power supply, and drug stockouts. Despite these hurdles, ethical standards must be upheld. Nurses must respect patient confidentiality, secure informed consent before procedures, and provide care without prejudice. Balancing personal safety with professional duty remains a constant reality when managing infectious outbreaks in under-resourced settings.

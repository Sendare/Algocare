Family Patterns in Primary Health Care

The Family as a Unit of Health Care

Viewing the family as the client rather than focusing solely on an individual patient is a core principle of primary health care in Nigeria. A health issue experienced by one member invariably impacts the physical, emotional, and economic stability of the entire household. Nurses examine family dynamics to understand how illness alters daily routines, caregiving burdens, and resource allocation. This approach shifts clinical practice from reactive disease treatment to comprehensive community-oriented health promotion.

Types and Structures of Nigerian Families

Family structures in Nigeria are diverse, influenced by ethnic traditions, religious beliefs, and urbanization. Understanding these structures helps nurses identify support systems and decision-makers during health crises.

  • Nuclear Family: Consists of a father, mother, and children living in the same household.
  • Polygamous Family: Involves one man married to multiple wives with their respective children, common in many Nigerian settings.
  • Extended Family: Includes grandparents, aunts, uncles, and cousins living together or maintaining close interdependent ties.
  • Single-Parent Family: Headed by one parent due to divorce, separation, death, or choice.

Family Life Cycle Stages

Families pass through predictable developmental stages, each presenting unique health challenges and tasks. Recognizing these stages allows nurses to anticipate health education needs and preventative interventions.

  1. Beginning Family: Newly married couple establishing their home without children.
  2. Childbearing Family: Infants are added, requiring immunization and child care adjustments.
  3. Family with School-Age Children: Managing school schedules, peer influences, and socialization.
  4. Launching Center Children: Adolescents and young adults leaving home for work or higher education.
  5. Aging Family: Retirement, declining physical strength, and coping with chronic illnesses.

Socio-Cultural and Economic Determinants

Health behaviors within Nigerian families are heavily shaped by cultural norms, religious practices, and financial capabilities. Economic constraints can delay healthcare-seeking, forcing families to rely on traditional remedies before visiting a primary health center. Cultural beliefs surrounding disease causation may dictate whether a family seeks orthodox medicine, spiritual healing, or herbal preparations. Nurses must navigate these factors respectfully without compromising patient safety.

Tools for Family Assessment

Primary health care nurses use specialized tools to map family structures, relationships, and community interactions. The genogram acts as a family tree that records medical history across at least three generations, helping to identify hereditary conditions like hypertension and sickle cell disease. The ecogram maps the family's interactions with external systems such as work, school, church, and extended relatives, revealing sources of support or stress.

Common Family Health Problems and Dysfunction

Dysfunctional family patterns can manifest as poor communication, domestic violence, substance abuse, or neglect of vulnerable members like children and the elderly. In primary care, nurses frequently encounter families struggling with role overload, where a single caregiver bears the entire physical and financial burden of chronic illness. Financial stress often precipitates marital conflict, which indirectly affects the nutritional and psychological well-being of the children.

The Role of the Nurse in Family-Centered Care

The community health nurse acts as an advocate, educator, and counselor within family-centered care. By establishing trust, the nurse facilitates open dialogue during home visits and clinic consultations. Nurses empower families by teaching basic disease prevention, promoting healthy nutrition, and linking households to social welfare services. Successful intervention relies on respecting family values while guiding them toward healthier lifestyle choices.

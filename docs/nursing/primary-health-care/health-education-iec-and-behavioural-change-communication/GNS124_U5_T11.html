Guidance and Counselling

Introduction and Definition of Guidance and Counselling

Guidance and counselling are two supportive processes aimed at helping individuals navigate life challenges, make informed decisions, and achieve personal growth. While often used interchangeably, they have distinct scopes in healthcare and educational settings. Guidance is broader, often involving the provision of information, educational pathways, and directional advice to help a person choose a path. Counselling is a more intimate, deeper, and structured relationship between a professional and a client, focusing on emotional exploration, psychological support, and behavioral change.

In Nigerian healthcare practice, nurses encounter patients dealing with chronic illnesses, terminal diagnoses, or social burdens who require more than just medical prescriptions. Guidance helps patients understand their disease options, while counselling helps them process the emotional shock and adhere to treatment regimens.

Differences Between Guidance and Counselling

Although both concepts aim to assist individuals, comparing their key operational attributes clarifies their unique applications in nursing practice.

AttributeGuidanceCounselling
ScopeBroader; deals with external choices and informationNarrower, deeper; deals with personal and emotional issues
ApproachPrescriptive; often involves telling or showing optionsNon-prescriptive; helps clients find their own solutions
SettingSchools, career centers, public health campaignsClinical rooms, private offices, specialized clinics
ExpertiseCan be provided by teachers, nurses, or mentorsRequires specialized training in psychological techniques

Core Principles and Ethics in Counselling

Ethical guidelines protect vulnerable clients seeking help from healthcare professionals. Violating these principles erodes trust and can cause psychological harm.

  • Confidentiality: Keeping all shared information private, except when a client threatens self-harm or harm to others.
  • Autonomy: Respecting the client's right to make their own independent decisions regarding their life and health.
  • Beneficence: Acting always in the best interest of the client to promote their welfare.
  • Non-maleficence: Ensuring that the counselling intervention causes no physical or psychological harm.

Types and Areas of Guidance and Counselling

Counselling addresses various dimensions of human life. Nurses frequently encounter specific categories within community and hospital settings.

  1. Educational Counselling: Helps students or young adults overcome learning difficulties, choose subjects, and manage academic stress.
  2. Vocational/Career Counselling: Assists individuals in aligning their skills with job opportunities or managing workplace stress.
  3. Health and Lifestyle Counselling: Focuses on managing chronic conditions, nutrition, adherence to antiretroviral therapy, and family planning.
  4. Psychological/Personal Counselling: Deals with grief, anxiety, trauma, depression, and marital conflicts.

The Counselling Process and Stages

A successful counselling relationship follows a structured pathway. Rushing through these phases limits effectiveness.

  • Building Rapport: Establishing trust, empathy, and a safe environment so the client feels comfortable.
  • Assessment and Exploration: Gathering information, identifying the core problem, and listening actively to the client's story.
  • Goal Setting: Collaboratively agreeing on what the client wants to achieve by the end of the sessions.
  • Intervention/Action: Exploring coping strategies, implementing solutions, and teaching new behavioral skills.
  • Termination and Follow-up: Reviewing progress, gradually ending the sessions, and scheduling follow-up checks.

Communication Skills and Techniques for Community Nurses

Effective communication forms the bedrock of nursing counselling. Without it, messages are easily misunderstood.

Active listening involves paying full attention, nodding, and avoiding interruptions. Non-verbal cues such as maintaining an open posture, keeping eye contact, and showing a calm facial expression demonstrate empathy. Asking open-ended questions encourages clients to express their true feelings rather than giving short yes-or-no answers. Reflection and paraphrasing help clarify statements and show the client that the nurse truly understands their perspective.

Role of the Primary Health Care Nurse in Counselling

Primary Health Care (PHC) nurses in Nigeria serve as frontline educators and counsellors. They bridge the gap between biomedical treatments and community realities.

PHC nurses provide maternal and child health counselling, immunization education, nutritional guidance, and home-based care advice. They identify mental health warning signs early during routine clinic visits and refer complicated psychological cases to appropriate psychiatric facilities. By educating families, nurses reduce social stigma associated with diseases like tuberculosis and HIV.

Challenges and Barriers to Effective Counselling in Primary Care

Several obstacles hinder nurses from delivering optimal counselling services in Nigerian primary health care centers.

  • Heavy Workload: High patient-to-nurse ratios leave very little time for lengthy individual counselling sessions.
  • Lack of Privacy: Open-plan clinics make confidential conversations difficult to conduct without interruption.
  • Cultural Barriers: Traditional beliefs and stigmatizing attitudes can make clients reluctant to open up.
  • Language Differences: Communication gaps between the nurse and patients from diverse ethnic backgrounds hinder deep understanding.

Development Committees in Primary Health Care Services in Nigeria

Structure of Development Committees in Nigerian Primary Health Care

Primary Health Care in Nigeria relies heavily on community participation, which is operationalized through a tiered system of development committees. These structures bridge the gap between healthcare providers and the communities they serve, ensuring that local voices shape health service delivery. The committees operate at different administrative levels to mobilize resources, identify community needs, and promote health awareness.

  • Village Development Committee (VDC): Operates at the grassroots or settlement level to mobilize community members for health action.
  • Health Facility Development Committee (HFDC): Works directly around a specific primary health care facility to manage local resources and facility maintenance.
  • District Development Committee (DDC): Coordinates primary health care activities across several villages or wards within a district.
  • Local Government Area Primary Health Care Management Committee (LGPHCMA): Oversees health service delivery and resource allocation across the entire local government area.

Village Development Committee

The Village Development Committee (VDC) represents the foundation of community-based health governance in Nigeria. Comprising traditional leaders, women's leaders, youth representatives, and local opinion formers, the VDC identifies pressing health needs within the immediate settlement. Members act as change agents, encouraging local acceptance of immunization campaigns, antenatal care attendance, and environmental sanitation exercises. Operating without formal bureaucratic constraints, this committee relies heavily on communal trust and voluntary participation to drive health-seeking behavior.

Health Facility Development Committee

The Health Facility Development Committee (HFDC) is established for a specific primary health care clinic or health post. Unlike the broader grassroots focus of the VDC, the HFDC focuses on the operational readiness of the health facility itself. Responsibilities include maintaining facility infrastructure, managing user fees or community health insurance funds, ensuring security for health workers, and resolving minor grievances between patients and staff. Nurses working in primary health care facilities often serve as key secretaries or technical advisors to this committee, guiding them on urgent medical supply needs.

District and Local Government Area Committees

Moving up the administrative ladder, the District Development Committee (DDC) coordinates activities across multiple village clusters and health facility catchments. It consolidates reports, pools local resources, and resolves inter-community conflicts regarding health project locations.

At the tier above, the Local Government Area Primary Health Care Management Committee (LGPHCMA) holds statutory responsibility for primary health care implementation at the local government level. This committee is chaired by the Local Government Chairman and includes the PHC Coordinator, representatives from health facility committees, and key sectoral heads such as education and water sanitation officers. They oversee budgeting, staff deployment, and cold-chain maintenance across all clinics within the local government area.

State and National Policy Implementation Structures

While grassroots committees handle community mobilization, higher-level bodies ensure policy alignment and funding flow. The State Primary Health Care Development Board (SPHCDB) provides overarching technical and financial supervision for primary health care across all local government areas in a state. At the apex, the National Primary Health Care Development Agency (NPHCDA) formulates national policies, sets standards, and coordinates international donor support. These higher tiers depend on data and feedback generated by lower-level development committees to evaluate whether national health interventions actually reach rural Nigerian populations.

Challenges and Strategies for Effective Committee Operations

Despite their strategic importance, development committees in Nigeria face persistent operational hurdles. Common issues include paucity of funds, apathy from community members, political interference, and illiteracy among local leaders which hinders effective record-keeping and participation.

To overcome these bottlenecks, specific strengthening strategies must be applied:

  • Capacity Building: Regular training workshops for committee members on project monitoring and basic financial management.
  • Transparent Governance: Open reporting of facility income and expenditure to build community trust.
  • Inclusive Representation: Ensuring marginalized groups, including women and nomadic populations, have a voice in committee decisions.
  • Government Commitment: Prompt release of counterpart funding by local and state governments to sustain committee morale.

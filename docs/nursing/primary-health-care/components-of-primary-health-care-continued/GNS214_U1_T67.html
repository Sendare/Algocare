Review of Aseptic Techniques in Immunization

Introduction to Aseptic Techniques in Immunization

Aseptic technique refers to the practices and procedures used to prevent contamination from pathogens. In immunization clinics across Nigeria, maintaining sterility protects both clients and health workers. Immunization introduces antigens directly into body tissues, bypassing natural mucosal and skin barriers. If equipment or vaccine vials are contaminated, microbes enter directly into the bloodstream or muscle, causing severe local abscesses or systemic infections. Every step of the immunization process, from vaccine reconstitution to injection, requires strict adherence to these sterility protocols.

Standard Precautions and Hand Hygiene Protocols

Hand hygiene is the single most effective way to halt the spread of healthcare-associated infections. Nurses in busy primary health centers must perform hand hygiene before and after every client interaction, even when gloves are worn.

  • Alcohol-based rub: Preferred when hands are not visibly soiled, taking about twenty to thirty seconds.
  • Soap and water: Mandatory when hands are visibly dirty, contaminated with blood or body fluids, or after restroom use.

Safe Vaccine Preparation and Cold Chain Maintenance

Preparing vaccines requires meticulous attention to detail to preserve potency and sterility. Nurses must inspect vaccine vial monitors and expiry dates before drawing up doses. Reconstitution must be done using only the specific diluent supplied by the manufacturer for that exact vaccine. Mixing errors or using contaminated diluents can neutralize the vaccine or introduce bacteria. Maintaining the cold chain ensures antigens do not degrade before reaching the recipient's immune system.

Administration Techniques and Injection Safety

Administering vaccines safely involves selecting the correct anatomical site, using the appropriate needle length, and following aseptic skin preparation.

  1. Skin Cleansing: Wipe the injection site with an alcohol swab using a spiral motion from the center outward, and allow it to dry completely.
  2. Needle Insertion: Insert the needle swiftly at the correct angle specified for the vaccine type, such as intramuscular or subcutaneous.
  3. Post-Injection Care: Apply light pressure with a clean cotton swab if bleeding occurs, without massaging the site.

Prevention of Needle-Stick Injuries and Sharps Management

Needle-stick injuries expose healthcare workers to bloodborne pathogens like Hepatitis B, Hepatitis C, and HIV. Safety rules dictate that needles must never be recapped after use. Recapping is a primary cause of accidental punctures. Used syringes and needles must be dropped immediately into a puncture-resistant safety box located within arm's reach of the immunization station. Safety boxes should never be filled beyond the designated fill line.

Maternal and Child Health Services

Introduction and Objectives of Maternal and Child Health Services

Maternal and Child Health (MCH) services form the bedrock of primary health care delivery in Nigeria. The primary goal is to reduce morbidity and mortality rates among women of reproductive age and children under five years old. These services encompass preventive, promotive, curative, and rehabilitative interventions tailored to the unique physiological needs of mothers and growing children. By focusing on early detection of risk factors, health education, and timely referrals, MCH programs aim to secure optimal health outcomes at the community level.

Antenatal Care: Monitoring Pregnancy and Preventing Complications

Antenatal care (ANC) provides regular medical supervision throughout pregnancy to ensure the well-being of both mother and fetus. Current global standards recommend a minimum of eight contacts to allow for comprehensive risk assessment and prompt interventions. Routine ANC activities include:

  • Blood pressure screening: Monitoring for gestational hypertension and pre-eclampsia.
  • Tetanus toxoid vaccination: Protecting the newborn from neonatal tetanus.
  • Intermittent Preventive Treatment in Pregnancy (IPTp): Administering sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine to prevent malaria.
  • Nutritional supplementation: Providing iron and folic acid to prevent maternal anemia.

Intrapartum and Postnatal Care for Mother and Newborn

The intrapartum period spans from the onset of true labor through the delivery of the placenta. Skilled birth attendants monitor uterine contractions, fetal heart rates, and maternal vitals using the partograph to catch obstructed labor or fetal distress early. Immediate postnatal care begins right after delivery, focusing on the prevention of postpartum hemorrhage through active management of the third stage of labor, drying and warming the newborn, and initiating early breastfeeding within the first hour of life.

Child Health Services: Growth Monitoring and Developmental Milestones

Growth monitoring and promotion (GMP) tracks a child’s physical growth over time using weight-for-age charts found on health cards. Regular weighing helps detect faltering growth before clinical malnutrition becomes severe. Alongside physical growth, nurses assess developmental milestones such as sitting unsupported, walking, and vocalizing, ensuring that neurological and physical delays receive early specialist evaluation.

Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) in Nigeria

The Expanded Programme on Immunization protects infants and young children against vaccine-preventable diseases like tuberculosis, poliomyelitis, diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, measles, and yellow fever. Maintaining the cold chain is critical, as vaccines lose potency when exposed to improper temperatures during storage and transport. Nurses play a frontline role in administering vaccines, educating caregivers on side effects, and tracking defaulters to maintain high community coverage.

Integrated Management of Childhood Illnesses (IMCI)

The Integrated Management of Childhood Illnesses strategy addresses the major causes of childhood mortality, including pneumonia, diarrhea, measles, malaria, and malnutrition. Instead of treating single symptoms in isolation, IMCI uses an integrated case management approach to assess the sick child completely. Health workers check for danger signs, evaluate nutritional status, assess immunization records, and provide clear guidance on home care and treatment administration.

Nutrition and Family Planning Integration within MCH

Nutritional counseling and family planning services are tightly intertwined with maternal and child health. Adequate maternal nutrition during pregnancy and lactation prevents low birth weight and stunting. Family planning allows women to space pregnancies adequately, reducing health risks associated with closely spaced births. Integrating these services at primary health centers ensures that mothers receive holistic counseling on child spacing and nutrition during every clinical contact.

Challenges and Community Strategies in Primary Health Care Delivery

Primary health care delivery in Nigeria faces persistent hurdles, including infrastructural deficits, drug stockouts, cultural barriers, and a shortage of skilled personnel in rural areas. Overcoming these gaps requires active community engagement, such as partnering with local leaders, mobilizing traditional birth attendants, and utilizing community health extension workers for outreach. Strengthening community participation ensures that health interventions remain culturally acceptable and sustainable.

Prevention of Maternal Newborn and Infant Morbidity and Mortality

Introduction and Key Statistical Indicators in Nigeria

Nigeria carries a heavy burden of maternal, newborn, and infant mortality. Understanding the basic metrics helps nurses grasp the scale of the public health challenge.

  • Maternal Mortality Ratio (MMR): The number of maternal deaths per 100,000 live births, resulting from pregnancy or management complications.
  • Neonatal Mortality Rate (NMR): The number of deaths during the first 28 completed days of life per 1,000 live births.
  • Infant Mortality Rate (IMR): The number of deaths in children under one year of age per 1,000 live births.
  • Under-Five Mortality Rate: The probability of dying between birth and exactly five years of age per 1,000 live births.

Major Direct Causes of Maternal and Newborn Deaths

Most maternal and newborn deaths stem from preventable complications. Recognizing these direct causes allows clinical nurses to prioritize rapid, life-saving interventions.

  • Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH): Excessive bleeding after delivery, often due to uterine atony.
  • Eclampsia: Severe pregnancy-induced hypertension leading to seizures, which can cause cerebral hemorrhage or multi-organ failure.
  • Puerperal Sepsis: Bacterial infections of the reproductive tract following childbirth or miscarriage.
  • Birth Asphyxia: The inability of the newborn to initiate and sustain adequate breathing at birth.
  • Prematurity: Complications arising from babies born alive before 37 weeks of pregnancy.

Antenatal and Intrapartum Care Interventions

Quality care during pregnancy and labor forms the bedrock of risk reduction. Antenatal care (ANC) visits provide opportunities for screening, health promotion, and disease prophylaxis such as intermittent preventive treatment for malaria and tetanus toxoid vaccination.

During the intrapartum period, skilled birth attendants utilize the partograph to monitor labor progress and maternal-fetal well-being. Active management of the third stage of labor (AMTSL) is a proven clinical practice that significantly lowers the incidence of postpartum hemorrhage by administering uterotonics, performing controlled cord traction, and massaging the uterine fundus.

Essential Newborn Care Practices

Immediate care given to a newborn directly influences survival rates. Every baby requires a clean delivery, thermal protection, early initiation of breastfeeding, and resuscitation if breathing is absent.

  • Thermal Protection: Drying the baby immediately and practicing skin-to-skin contact prevents hypothermia.
  • Cord Care: Applying dry cord care or chlorhexidine gel reduces the risk of omphalitis and systemic infection.
  • Eye Prophylaxis: Administering tetracycline eye ointment prevents gonococcal ophthalmia neonatorum.
  • Exclusive Breastfeeding: Initiating breastfeeding within the first hour of life provides vital colostrum and immune protection.

Immunization and Nutrition Programs

Prevention extends beyond the delivery room through routine immunization and nutritional support. The national immunization schedule protects infants against vaccine-preventable diseases such as tuberculosis, poliomyelitis, measles, and diphtheria.

Nutritional counseling focuses on exclusive breastfeeding for the first six months, followed by the introduction of safe, nutrient-dense complementary foods while continuing breastfeeding up to two years or beyond. Micronutrient supplementation, including vitamin A and iron, guards against deficiency-related morbidity.

Community Strategies and Referral Systems

Many maternal and infant deaths happen outside health facilities due to the 'three delays model': delay in deciding to seek care, delay in reaching a care facility, and delay in receiving adequate treatment.

Community health extension workers and traditional birth attendants, when properly trained, help bridge the gap by identifying danger signs early and facilitating prompt referrals. Establishing functional emergency transport systems and community awareness networks ensures that women experiencing obstetric emergencies reach secondary or tertiary hospitals without fatal delays.

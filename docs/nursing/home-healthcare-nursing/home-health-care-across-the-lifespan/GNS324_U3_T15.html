Home Delivery and Family Planning Services

Context of Home Delivery and Community Family Planning in Nigeria

Many pregnant women in Nigeria deliver outside formal health facilities due to cultural preferences, financial constraints, or geographic distance. Community health nurses and midwives play a critical role in bridging this gap by providing safe domiciliary care and bringing family planning services directly to grassroots populations. Understanding the local context helps practitioners respect traditional beliefs while introducing evidence-based maternal and child health interventions.

Preparation and Antenatal Risk Assessment

Conducting a thorough risk assessment during antenatal visits determines whether a home delivery is relatively safe or high risk. Nurses must identify pre-existing conditions like severe hypertension, previous cesarean sections, or malpresentations that demand facility-based delivery. Essential items for a clean home delivery kit include a clean blade, sterile cord ties, clean towels, waterproof sheets, and personal protective equipment.

Managing Intrapartum Care and Emergencies at Home

During the second and third stages of labor at home, the nurse monitors uterine contractions, fetal heart rate, and maternal vital signs closely. Immediate postpartum hemorrhage is the most frequent and dangerous complication. Nurses must be prepared to administer uterotonics like oxytocin or misoprostol and perform active management of the third stage of labor.

Postnatal Maternal and Newborn Care

  • Uterine involution: Assessing fundal height and firmness to ensure the uterus contracts properly.
  • Lochia evaluation: Monitoring the color, amount, and odor of vaginal discharge to detect early infection.
  • Thermal regulation: Drying the newborn immediately and promoting skin-to-skin contact to prevent hypothermia.
  • Cord care: Keeping the umbilical stump clean and dry without applying harmful substances like saliva or dung.

Principles of Postpartum Family Planning Counseling

Family planning counseling should begin during antenatal visits and continue in the postpartum period. Nurses use the method information index to help clients select contraceptives that suit their lifestyle, health status, and reproductive goals. Spacing births by at least two years improves maternal recovery and reduces infant mortality.

Legal, Ethical, and Cultural Realities in Nigerian Home Healthcare

Providing care in homes requires navigating complex ethical and legal boundaries. Nurses must respect patient autonomy while ensuring safety, particularly when a client refuses a necessary hospital transfer. Cultural norms regarding gender roles, family decision-making, and privacy heavily influence healthcare uptake, requiring nurses to engage supportive family members respectfully.

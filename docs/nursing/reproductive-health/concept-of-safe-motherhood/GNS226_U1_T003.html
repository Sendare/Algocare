Components of Safe Motherhood

Introduction to Safe Motherhood

Safe motherhood is a global strategy aimed at reducing maternal mortality and morbidity while ensuring that all women receive the necessary care during pregnancy, childbirth, and the postnatal period. In Nigeria, maternal health indicators remain a significant public health challenge due to poverty, delayed referrals, and limited access to functional health facilities. The initiative rests on the premise that pregnancy should not be a life-threatening event. Nurses act as frontline advocates, educators, and care providers who bridge the gap between rural communities and clinical services, translating policy into practical bedside interventions.

Preconception Care and Family Planning

Preconception care addresses health risks before pregnancy occurs. It involves nutritional counseling, screening for chronic conditions like hypertension and diabetes, and updating immunizations such as the tetanus toxoid vaccine.

  • Folic Acid Supplementation: Administered early to prevent neural tube defects.
  • Family Planning: Empowers couples to space pregnancies adequately, reducing risks associated with high parity and short birth intervals.
  • Screening for Infections: Identifies sexually transmitted infections and HIV early to prevent vertical transmission.

Antenatal Care and Maternal Monitoring

Antenatal care (ANC) offers a vital window for continuous risk assessment and health promotion. Focused ANC models recommend at least eight contacts with a skilled provider throughout gestation to monitor fetal growth and maternal well-being.

Clean and Safe Delivery Practices

The majority of maternal deaths occur during labor, delivery, and the immediate postpartum period. Clean delivery practices focus on infection prevention through the strict application of the clean technique, often summarized as the clean hands, clean surface, clean cord, and clean blade principles.

Skilled birth attendance ensures active management of the third stage of labor, which involves the administration of uterotonics like oxytocin immediately after baby delivery. This simple practice dramatically reduces the incidence of postpartum hemorrhage, the leading cause of maternal mortality in Nigeria.

Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care

Emergency obstetric and newborn care (EmONC) services are life-saving interventions required when sudden, unpredictable complications arise during labor or birth. Basic EmONC facilities provide parenteral antibiotics, anticonvulsants, uterotonics, manual vacuum aspiration, and newborn resuscitation. Comprehensive EmONC facilities add blood transfusion services and safe surgical capabilities like caesarean sections.

Nurses working in these settings must be proficient in rapid triage, recognizing shock early, and initiating intravenous access before clinical deterioration becomes irreversible.

Postnatal Care for Mother and Infant

The postnatal period is a critical window of vulnerability often neglected by mothers and health systems alike. The first 24 hours require intense monitoring for hemorrhage, sepsis, and eclampsia. Routine postnatal visits occur at 48 to 72 hours, 7 to 14 days, and at six weeks postpartum.

For the newborn, care centers on maintaining thermal stability, promoting early initiation of breastfeeding within the first hour of life, and checking the umbilical stump for signs of infection. Nurses assess maternal psychological adjustment to detect early signs of postpartum blues or depression.

Post-Abortion Care Services

Post-abortion care (PAC) is an essential emergency component of safe motherhood, addressing incomplete abortions, spontaneous miscarriages, and unsafe terminations. PAC focuses on treating life-threatening complications, offering compassionate counseling, and providing immediate contraceptive services to prevent repeat unintended pregnancies.

Nurses must maintain a non-judgmental, confidential attitude, ensuring that care remains objective and centered strictly on patient survival and recovery regardless of the legal or social context.

Role of the Nurse in Promoting Safe Motherhood in Nigeria

Nurses function as educators, care coordinators, researchers, and clinical experts within the Nigerian healthcare landscape. They conduct community outreach to dispel cultural myths, encourage facility-based deliveries, and train traditional birth attendants on hygiene and referral triggers.

By advocating for resource allocation and maternal health policy enforcement at local levels, nurses directly influence systemic change. Their vigilance at the bedside ensures that minor deviations from normal physiological parameters are caught and corrected before escalating into catastrophic maternal or neonatal emergencies.

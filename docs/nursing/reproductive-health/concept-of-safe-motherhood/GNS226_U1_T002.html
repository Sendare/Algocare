Principles of Safe Motherhood

Introduction and Definition of Safe Motherhood

Safe motherhood refers to the strategies, activities, and interventions designed to ensure that women experience normal pregnancies, childbirth, and the postnatal period without facing life-threatening complications. In Nigeria, maternal mortality remains a significant public health challenge, making these principles vital for every student nurse and midwife to grasp. The initiative is not merely about preventing maternal death; it encompasses the overall physical, mental, and social well-being of the mother and her child. Ensuring safe motherhood requires a functional healthcare system that addresses socio-cultural barriers, poverty, and delays in seeking medical attention.

Core Pillars and Principles of Safe Motherhood

The framework of safe motherhood stands on four main pillars that guide healthcare delivery at all levels. Nurses must understand how these pillars interact to reduce maternal morbidity and mortality.

  • Family Planning: Empowers couples to choose the number and spacing of their children, preventing high-risk pregnancies.
  • Antenatal Care (ANC): Detects and manages preexisting medical conditions and pregnancy-related complications early.
  • Obstetric Care: Ensures access to skilled attendance during labor and emergency services when complications arise.
  • Postnatal Care (PNC): Monitors the health of both mother and newborn during the critical weeks following delivery.

Antenatal Care and Maternal Health Promotion

Antenatal care provides a vital touchpoint between pregnant women and the health system. Current World Health Organization guidelines recommend a minimum of eight contacts throughout pregnancy to reduce perinatal mortality and complications. During these visits, nurses perform risk assessments, administer tetanus toxoid immunizations, provide intermittent preventive treatment for malaria in pregnancy (IPTp), and distribute insecticide-treated nets. Health education forms a cornerstone of these visits, preparing the mother for labor signs, proper nutrition, and exclusive breastfeeding. A common misconception is that ANC is only necessary when a pregnant woman feels sick; nurses must emphasize that routine visits are crucial even during a normal, asymptomatic pregnancy.

Essential Intrapartum Care and Skilled Attendance

Intrapartum care focuses on the management of labor and delivery. The presence of a skilled birth attendant—such as a midwife, nurse, or doctor—is the single most critical intervention for saving maternal lives. Utilizing the partograph is mandatory during labor to monitor cervical dilation, maternal vital signs, and fetal heart rate, allowing prompt identification of prolonged labor or fetal distress. Clean delivery practices, active management of the third stage of labor using uterotonics, and immediate newborn resuscitation skills drastically reduce postpartum hemorrhage and birth asphyxia.

Postnatal Care for Mother and Newborn

The postnatal period is a vulnerable window when the majority of maternal and neonatal deaths occur. Despite this risk, postnatal care is often the most neglected phase of maternity services. The first postnatal contact should occur within 24 hours of birth, followed by checks at day three, between weeks two and six, and at six weeks. Nurses assess uterine involution, lochia, perineal healing, and signs of postpartum depression in the mother. For the newborn, care includes evaluating cord healing, establishing breastfeeding, and monitoring temperature. Education on danger signs empowers families to seek prompt readmission if complications arise.

Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care (EmONC)

When unexpected complications arise, timely access to Emergency Obstetric and Newborn Care determines survival. Facilities are categorized into Basic EmONC (BEmONC) and Comprehensive EmONC (CEmONC) based on the life-saving signal functions they provide. BEmONC facilities handle administration of parenteral antibiotics, uterotonics, anticonvulsants, manual removal of placenta, and neonatal resuscitation. CEmONC facilities provide all basic functions plus blood transfusion services and surgical delivery via Caesarean section. Nurses working in primary health centers must recognize referral triggers early to prevent delays in reaching comprehensive care.

The Role of the Nurse and Midwife in Safe Motherhood

Nurses and midwives serve as the backbone of maternal and child health services across Nigeria. Their duties extend far beyond the labor ward; they act as educators, advocates, clinical care providers, and community leaders. By promoting positive health behaviors, conducting respectful maternity care, and challenging harmful traditional practices, nurses safeguard the dignity and safety of women. Furthermore, participating in maternal death surveillance and response helps health teams learn from adverse events and improve future service delivery.

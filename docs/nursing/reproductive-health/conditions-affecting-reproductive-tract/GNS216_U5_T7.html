Vulval Cyst

Introduction and Definition of Vulval Cysts

A vulval cyst is a closed sac-like pocket of tissue located on the external female genitalia, which can contain fluid, pus, or semi-solid material. These lesions develop within the labia majora, labia minora, or the vestibule. While many remain small and asymptomatic, others grow large enough to cause significant discomfort during daily activities. Recognizing these formations early prevents unnecessary alarm for patients presenting to gynecology clinics across Nigeria.

Anatomical Considerations and Types

Understanding vulval cysts requires knowledge of the external female genitalia, particularly the glands and ducts situated in the area. The most common types include:

  • Bartholin duct cysts: Formed when the main duct of the Bartholin gland becomes blocked, causing mucus accumulation.
  • Epidermal inclusion cysts: Develop from trapped epidermal tissue beneath the skin surface, often following trauma or episiotomy scars.
  • Mucinous cysts: Arise from misplaced embryonic remnants of the urogenital sinus or gastrointestinal tract.
  • Sebaceous cysts: Result from blocked oil-producing sebaceous glands within the hair-bearing labia majora.

Clinical Presentation and Assessment

Patients with vulval cysts frequently report feeling a painless or tender lump in the perineal region. When obtaining a nursing history, inquire about pain intensity during walking, sitting, or sexual intercourse. Physical inspection involves assessing the size, location, mobility, and overlying skin condition of the lesion. Look out for local erythema and warmth, which indicate secondary bacterial infection and potential abscess formation.

Diagnostic Evaluation and Differential Diagnosis

Diagnosing a vulval cyst relies primarily on clinical examination. However, healthcare providers must differentiate benign cysts from other pelvic floor pathologies.

ConditionKey Clinical Feature
Vulval CystDiscrete, mobile, fluid-filled subcutaneous swelling
Inguinal HerniaSwelling extending from the inguinal canal, reducible
LipomaSoft, doughy, non-fluid-filled benign fatty tumor
Malignant Vulvar TumorFirm, irregular, ulcerated lesion with fixation to deep tissue

Diagnostic tools like ultrasound may be used to confirm fluid content or rule out solid masses when presentation is atypical.

Medical and Surgical Nursing Management

Management depends entirely on symptom severity and cyst size. Asymptomatic small cysts often require only watchful waiting and good perineal hygiene. For symptomatic or infected cysts, medical therapy includes warm sitz baths to promote spontaneous drainage and prescribed analgesics. Surgical interventions involve incision and drainage, marsupialization for Bartholin cysts to create a permanent opening, or complete surgical excision of the cyst wall.

Holistic Nursing Care and Patient Education

Nursing care extends beyond physical treatment to address the emotional distress many women experience with genital lesions. Provide a private, non-judgmental environment for discussions, as cultural stigma around pelvic conditions is common in Nigeria. Teach patients proper perineal hygiene, such as wiping from front to back and wearing loose cotton underwear. Instruct them to report increasing pain, fever, or recurrent swelling immediately.

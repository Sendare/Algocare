STI Information and Counselling (Female)

Introduction to Sexually Transmitted Infections in Females

Sexually transmitted infections represent a major public health concern across Nigeria, heavily impacting reproductive health and maternal well-being. Women face a higher biological vulnerability to acquiring these infections compared to men due to a larger mucosal surface area in the reproductive tract and micro-tears that can occur during intercourse. Hormonal and anatomical differences also facilitate pathogen transmission from male to female partners more efficiently. Left untreated, common infections can ascend from the lower genital tract to cause pelvic inflammatory disease, leading to chronic pelvic pain, ectopic pregnancy, and tubal factor infertility. Addressing these conditions requires understanding both the biological risks and the social realities Nigerian women face, including gender power dynamics that often limit their ability to negotiate safe sex or demand barrier protection.

Common Pathogens and Clinical Features

  • Chlamydia trachomatis: A bacterial infection often presenting with a mucopurulent cervical discharge and intermenstrual bleeding, though frequently silent.
  • Neisseria gonorrhoeae: A bacterium causing purulent vaginal discharge, dysuria, and pelvic discomfort, contributing significantly to pelvic inflammatory disease.
  • Trichomonas vaginalis: A protozoan parasite characterized by a frothy, yellow-green vaginal discharge, strong odor, and intense vulvar pruritus.
  • Herpes Simplex Virus Type 2: A viral infection causing painful vesicular lesions and ulcerations on the vulva, perineum, or cervix.
  • Human Papillomavirus: A viral pathogen associated with genital warts and high-risk strains that drive cervical dysplasia and cancer.

The Challenge of Asymptomatic Presentations

A critical barrier in managing reproductive tract infections in women is the high prevalence of asymptomatic cases. Many bacterial infections, particularly chlamydia and early gonococcal infections, manifest with subtle or completely absent symptoms. Consequently, infected women continue their daily routines without seeking care, unknowingly facilitating ongoing transmission to sexual partners. This silent progression allows pathogens to silently damage upper reproductive structures over months or years. Routine screening during antenatal visits, family planning consultations, and general gynecological check-ups remain essential clinical tools for identifying these hidden reservoirs of infection before irreversible complications develop.

Diagnostic Workup and Screening Protocols

Accurate diagnosis guides targeted antimicrobial therapy and prevents unnecessary drug use. Clinicians utilize several diagnostic modalities depending on facility resources and clinical presentation:

  • Endocervical Swab: Used for nucleic acid amplification tests or bacterial cultures to detect chlamydia and gonorrhoea.
  • Wet Mount Microscopy: A quick bedside test where vaginal secretions mixed with saline or potassium hydroxide reveal trichomonads or clue cells indicating bacterial vaginosis.
  • Serological Assays: Blood tests utilized for screening systemic viral infections such as HIV and syphilis.
  • Visual Inspection with Acetic Acid: Used in resource-limited settings to identify abnormal cervical tissue changes indicative of human papillomavirus infection.

Principles of Effective Patient Counselling

Counselling a female patient diagnosed with a reproductive tract infection requires a non-judgmental, empathetic, and confidential approach. Nurses must create a safe environment where the patient feels respected and free from blame, as stigma often discourages disclosure and follow-up. The conversation should cover the nature of the infection, transmission routes, prescribed treatment regimens, and the necessity of abstaining from sexual intercourse until therapy is complete and symptoms resolve. Nurses must also explore potential barriers to treatment, assess the patient's emotional response, and provide clear, simple explanations to ensure full comprehension.

Partner Notification and Management Strategies

Treating the index patient alone is insufficient if sexual partners remain infected, leading to a continuous cycle of reinfection. Partner management involves tracing, testing, and treating all recent sexual contacts, typically covering the preceding sixty days depending on the specific organism. Nurses play a crucial role in facilitating partner notification, offering options such as patient referral or provider-assisted referral where confidentiality is maintained. In many resource-constrained settings, expedited partner therapy allows the index patient to deliver medications or prescriptions directly to their partner, significantly improving treatment coverage when clinic visits are unfeasible.

Medical Management and Treatment Adherence

Pharmacological treatment relies on targeted antimicrobial therapy based on clinical guidelines and sensitivity patterns. Single-dose oral regimens or multi-day antibiotic courses must be taken precisely as prescribed to ensure complete pathogen eradication. Nurses must emphasize the importance of finishing the full course of medication, even if symptoms subside within days. Premature cessation breeds antimicrobial resistance and allows residual organisms to multiply. Follow-up appointments are scheduled to confirm cure, particularly for certain bacterial infections and during pregnancy, ensuring that the therapeutic intervention was fully successful.

Prevention, Risk Reduction, and Health Education

Long-term control relies heavily on community education and primary prevention strategies. Nurses should actively promote correct and consistent barrier methods, such as male and female condoms, which offer dual protection against both unintended pregnancy and reproductive tract infections. Health education programs must debunk myths surrounding transmission and encourage open communication between partners regarding sexual history and health status. Empowering women through knowledge and accessible healthcare services creates a foundation for sustained reproductive wellness and lowers overall community infection rates.

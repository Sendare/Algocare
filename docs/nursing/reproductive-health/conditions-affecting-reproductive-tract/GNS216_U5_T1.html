Sexually Transmitted Infections (Male)

Introduction and Overview of Male Sexually Transmitted Infections

Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs) among men represent a major public health challenge across Nigeria, impacting reproductive health and overall well-being. Many men carrying these infections remain asymptomatic, unknowingly spreading pathogens to their partners. Stigma often prevents male patients from seeking early medical attention at primary health centres, leading to delayed diagnoses. Public health campaigns focus on encouraging routine screening and normalising open discussions about sexual health in clinical settings.

Common Bacterial and Viral Pathogens

Male STIs stem from diverse microbial agents categorized into bacterial and viral groups.

  • Neisseria gonorrhoeae: A gram-negative diplococcus responsible for gonorrhoea, marked by purulent urethral discharge.
  • Chlamydia trachomatis: An obligate intracellular bacterium causing nongonococcal urethritis, frequently co-existing with gonorrhoea.
  • Treponema pallidum: A spirochete bacterium causing syphilis, characterized by primary painless chancres.
  • Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV): A retrovirus targeting CD4+ T-lymphocytes, compromising cell-mediated immunity.
  • Herpes Simplex Virus Type 2 (HSV-2): A viral pathogen causing painful vesicular lesions on the genital mucosa.

Clinical Manifestations and Signs in Male Patients

Clinical presentations vary depending on the specific causative organism. Urethritis presents with dysuria and clear or purulent urethral discharge. Genital ulcers appear in conditions like syphilis or genital herpes, differing in their pain levels and base characteristics. Scrotal swelling and pain point toward epididymitis or orchitis, often resulting from ascending bacterial infections. Inguinal lymphadenopathy frequently accompanies localized genital infections as the immune system responds.

Diagnostic Evaluation and Screening Tests

Accurate diagnosis relies on a combination of physical assessment, specimen collection, and laboratory testing.

  • Urethral Swab: Collected using a fine swab inserted into the anterior urethra for gram staining and culture.
  • First-Void Urine Specimen: Utilized for Nucleic Acid Amplification Tests (NAAT) to detect chlamydia and gonorrhoea DNA.
  • Serological Tests: Blood tests such as VDRL or RPR used to screen for systemic antibodies against syphilis.
  • Rapid Diagnostic Tests (RDTs): Point-of-care finger-prick blood tests utilized for HIV and syphilis screening in community outreach settings.

Pharmacological Management and Nursing Interventions

Treatment protocols depend on confirmed or presumptive laboratory findings, requiring prompt antibiotic or antiviral administration. Gonorrhoea management typically requires dual therapy using ceftriaxone injection and azithromycin tablets to prevent resistance. Chlamydia responds well to oral doxycycline or azithromycin courses. Nurses play a crucial role in administering medications, educating patients on completing the full dose, and emphasizing complete sexual abstinence until treatment finishes. Contact tracing and partner notification represent essential nursing duties to break transmission chains.

Potential Complications of Untreated Infections

Ignoring STI symptoms in men leads to severe long-term health consequences affecting the genitourinary and systemic systems. Chronic urethral inflammation can result in urethral strictures, causing painful and obstructed urination. Untreated infections ascend into the reproductive tract, causing epididymitis, prostatitis, and subsequent male infertility. Systemic infections like untreated syphilis progress to cardiovascular and neurosyphilis stages. Additionally, genital ulcers and inflammation increase the mucosal susceptibility and transmission risk for acquiring HIV.

Patient Education and Prevention Strategies

Primary prevention remains the most effective strategy against male STIs in community health practice. Correct and consistent condom use provides a physical barrier against infectious bodily fluids. Regular screening for sexually active individuals helps catch asymptomatic infections early. Nurses conduct health education sessions addressing high-risk behaviours, substance use, and multiple sexual partnerships. Vaccination campaigns against Human Papillomavirus (HPV) and Hepatitis B also protect young males from preventable viral complications.

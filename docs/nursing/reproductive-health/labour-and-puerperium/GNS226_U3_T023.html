Referral to Family Planning Clinic

Introduction and Postpartum Reproductive Physiology

The puerperium is a vulnerable transition phase where maternal physiology returns to the pre-pregnant state. Ovulation can resume before the return of regular menstruation, particularly in women who practice partial breastfeeding or formula feeding. Without appropriate birth spacing, closely spaced pregnancies heighten the risk of maternal depletion, low birth weight, and premature delivery. Nurses play a crucial role during postnatal ward rounds and immunisation clinics to identify sexually active postpartum women and introduce timely contraceptive options.

Timing and Readiness for Postpartum Contraception

Deciding when to start contraception depends on breastfeeding status and the type of method chosen.

  • Exclusive Breastfeeding: Lactational Amenorrhea Method (LAM) offers reliable protection for the first six months if specific criteria are met.
  • Progestin-Only Methods: Implants, injectables, and pills can generally be started immediately postpartum without affecting milk supply.
  • Combined Hormonal Contraceptives: Oestrogen-containing methods are typically delayed for at least three weeks postpartum due to elevated thromboembolism risks, or six weeks if other risk factors are present.
  • Intrauterine Devices: Can be inserted immediately after placental delivery or delayed until four to six weeks postpartum.

Indications and Referral Criteria

Not every contraceptive method is suitable for every postpartum client. Nurses use medical eligibility criteria to screen patients before making a referral to the specialist family planning clinic. Absolute contraindications, severe postpartum hemorrhage, active deep vein thrombosis, or uncontrolled hypertension require tailored specialist evaluation. A formal referral is also indicated when a client expresses a desire for long-acting methods like intrauterine device insertion or permanent surgical sterilization that require specialized clinical skills.

Pre-Referral Counselling and Client Assessment

Effective counselling follows a client-centered approach, helping the woman select a method that fits her lifestyle, health status, and future reproductive goals. The nurse explores myths, cultural beliefs, and partner dynamics that might influence compliance. Providing balanced information about side effects ensures realistic expectations and minimizes discontinuation rates. Once the client makes an informed decision, the nurse prepares the formal referral document with all pertinent medical and obstetric history.

Writing and Facilitating the Formal Referral

A well-written referral letter bridges the communication gap between the postnatal ward and the family planning clinic. Essential components include the client's demographic data, parity, delivery date, relevant medical history, current infant feeding method, and the specific reason for referral. The nurse ensures the client understands where to go, what documents to bring, and what to expect during the clinic visit, thereby reducing anxiety and missed appointments.

Addressing Barriers and Ensuring Follow-Up

Many factors can hinder a woman from attending a referred family planning clinic, including spousal opposition, cultural stigma, transport costs, and fear of side effects. Nurses must proactively identify these barriers and involve partners in health education whenever feasible. Establishing a tracking system or utilizing community health extension workers helps trace clients who miss their appointments, ensuring continuous reproductive healthcare support.

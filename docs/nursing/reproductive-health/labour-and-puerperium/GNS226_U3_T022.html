Referral to Health Centre

Introduction and Rationale for Postnatal Referrals

The postnatal period is a critical phase where most maternal and newborn deaths occur. Despite routine care at primary health clinics, complications often exceed the facility's capacity. A referral system is a structured process designed to link lower-level health facilities with higher centers of specialized care. Effective referral ensures that women and newborns experiencing severe postpartum complications receive timely, life-saving interventions. Delays in reaching this higher level of care significantly contribute to preventable morbidity and mortality rates across Nigeria.

Maternal and Neonatal Indications for Escalation

Identifying complications early dictates whether a mother or baby survives the postnatal phase. Midwives must continuously assess clinical parameters to catch deviations from normal recovery.

  • Postpartum haemorrhage: Blood loss exceeding normal limits that fails to respond to initial uterotonics.
  • Puerperal sepsis: Persistent high fever, foul-smelling lochia, and lower abdominal tenderness.
  • Severe neonatal jaundice: Yellow discoloration of the skin appearing within the first twenty-four hours of life.
  • Birth asphyxia: Newborn failure to initiate and sustain spontaneous breathing at birth.

Pre-Referral Stabilization and Emergency Management

Before dispatching a patient, the midwife must stabilize vital functions to prevent sudden collapse during transit. Stabilization is not a reason to delay transfer; rather, life-saving measures occur simultaneously with referral preparations.

For a bleeding mother, secure large-bore intravenous access, infuse normal saline or Ringer's lactate rapidly, and ensure the bladder is empty. For a convulsing mother with eclampsia, administer magnesium sulfate according to national protocols. For a cold or breathing-troubled newborn, clear the airway, maintain warmth through skin-to-skin contact, and provide oxygen if available. Never transport an unstable patient without establishing basic physiological support.

Roles and Responsibilities of the Midwife

The midwife acts as the primary coordinator, clinical assessor, and communicator during any referral event. Clinical responsibilities extend beyond medical stabilization to include psychological reassurance for anxious family members. The midwife must designate a trained companion or accompany the patient if the condition is extremely critical. Coordinating with the receiving hospital beforehand via phone call prevents overcrowding and ensures the receiving team prepares the necessary blood products or equipment.

Communication and Referral Documentation

A poorly completed referral form breaks the continuum of care and wastes valuable time at the receiving hospital. The referral letter must be legible, concise, and contain specific clinical details. Essential elements include patient identification, vital signs upon departure, suspected diagnosis, emergency treatment administered, and the name of the referring midwife. Accompanying relatives must understand the reason for transfer so they can provide accurate history to the receiving clinicians.

Overcoming Systemic Barriers in Nigeria

Referrals in Nigeria frequently encounter severe obstacles including poor road networks, transportation costs, and inter-facility communication gaps. Financial constraints often delay a family's decision to accept a referral, leading to dangerous home management. Community engagement, functional emergency transport schemes, and emergency toll-free lines help mitigate these delays. Midwives must actively educate communities on recognizing danger signs early to bypass financial and logistical bottlenecks.

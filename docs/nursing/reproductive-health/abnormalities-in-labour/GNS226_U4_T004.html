High Risk Pregnancy in Labour

Introduction and Definition of High Risk Labour

A high-risk labour is one where the life or health of the mother, the unborn child, or both are at an increased jeopardy compared to a normal physiological birth. This vulnerability can stem from pre-existing medical disorders, pregnancy-induced complications, or unexpected events that arise suddenly during childbirth. In Nigerian clinical practice, identifying these cases early prevents maternal mortality and severe morbidity such as obstetric fistula or postpartum hemorrhage. The primary goal of nursing care shifts from routine monitoring to active anticipation, rapid detection, and prompt multidisciplinary intervention.

Pathophysiology and Underlying Risk Factors

Risk factors during labour can be categorized into pre-existing maternal conditions and acute intrapartum events. Chronic illnesses alter uteroplacental perfusion, while mechanical obstacles disrupt normal fetal descent.

  • Hypertensive disorders: Chronic hypertension or severe pre-eclampsia causes vasospasm, reducing blood flow to the placenta and risking placental abruption or fetal hypoxia.
  • Cephalopelvic disproportion: A mismatch between the fetal head size and the maternal pelvic capacity prevents normal engagement and descent.
  • Previous uterine scars: A history of prior classical or multiple lower segment caesarean sections increases the susceptibility to uterine rupture under labour contractions.
  • Grand multiparity: Uterine muscle fibers become overstretched and fatigued, predisposing the mother to weak contractions and postpartum hemorrhage.

Classification and Types of High Risk Conditions During Labour

High-risk labour conditions manifest across different stages of childbirth and demand distinct clinical responses. Fetal distress indicates inadequate oxygenation, often identifiable through abnormal heart rate patterns or thick meconium-stained liquor. Malpresentations, such as breech or transverse lie, render standard vaginal delivery hazardous or impossible without specialized surgical intervention. Prolonged or obstructed labour presents a grave danger, frequently resulting from neglected dystocia, leading to uterine exhaustion, infection, and potential rupture if oxytocin is mismanaged.

Maternal and Fetal Assessment Techniques

Continuous surveillance during high-risk labour utilizes both clinical examination and technological tools. Midwives monitor vital signs frequently to catch early signs of hemorrhage or infection. Fetal well-being is assessed using intermittent auscultation with a Pinard stethoscope or Doppler ultrasound, alongside electronic fetal monitoring when available. Assessing uterine contraction frequency, intensity, and duration helps evaluate labour progress and prevent hyperstimulation.

Intrapartum Monitoring and Partograph Utilization

The partograph is a graphical record of the progress of labour and salient conditions of mother and fetus. In high-risk scenarios, meticulous plotting on the partograph serves as an early warning system for prolonged labour and cephalopelvic disproportion. The alert line and action line guide clinical decision-making. When cervical dilation crosses the alert line into the action line, the nurse must initiate timely referrals or escalate care to higher medical personnel.

Nursing Management and Interventions

Comprehensive nursing care in high-risk labour combines clinical vigilance with supportive comfort measures. The nurse maintains intravenous access, administers prescribed medications such as antihypertensives or tocolytics, and ensures proper hydration and nutrition where permitted. Positioning the patient on her left lateral side improves venous return and enhances uteroplacental blood flow. Clear communication with the parturient woman and her family reduces anxiety and fosters cooperation during critical moments.

Emergency Obstetric Complications and Crisis Management

Obstetric emergencies demand swift, calm, and coordinated action from the entire healthcare team. Conditions like cord prolapse, postpartum hemorrhage, and eclamptic seizures can turn fatal within minutes. For a prolapsed cord, the nurse must immediately elevate the presenting part off the cord manually to relieve compression while preparing the patient for emergency delivery. In cases of severe eclamptic seizures, maintaining a patent airway, administering magnesium sulfate, and protecting the patient from physical trauma are top priorities.

Postpartum Care and Prevention of Long-term Sequelae in Nigeria

The danger does not end with the delivery of the baby; the immediate postpartum period carries high risks of hemorrhage, sepsis, and thromboembolism. Nurses must monitor lochia, uterine tone, and vital signs closely during the crucial first two hours after birth. In resource-constrained settings across Nigeria, preventive measures like active management of the third stage of labour significantly lower hemorrhage rates. Health education on danger signs, nutrition, and family planning ensures long-term maternal well-being and reduces recurrent risks in future pregnancies.

Diaphragm

Anatomy and Overview of the Diaphragm

The diaphragm is a dome-shaped, thin muscular and tendinous structure that separates the thoracic cavity from the abdominal cavity. In the context of reproductive health and family planning, a diaphragm is a barrier contraceptive device. It consists of a dome made of latex or silicone stretched over a flexible, circular rim made of metal or plastic. When inserted correctly into the vaginal canal, it sits snugly against the posterior vaginal fornix and behind the pubic bone, physically blocking sperm from entering the uterus and reaching an ovum. Unlike male condoms, this barrier method requires a healthcare provider to determine the correct size before use.

Mechanism of Action in Contraception

Contraceptive diaphragms function primarily through mechanical and chemical means. Mechanically, the latex or silicone dome covers the external cervical os, creating a physical roadblock that spermatozoa cannot bypass. Chemically, the diaphragm must always be used alongside a spermicide (usually nonoxynol-9) applied inside the dome and around the rim. The spermicide immobilizes and kills sperm upon contact, compensating for any minor gaps between the rim and the vaginal wall. This dual action significantly increases the overall effectiveness of the method compared to barrier protection alone.

Types of Rims and Sizing

Diaphragm rims come in different designs to accommodate varying pelvic floor muscle tone and anatomical shapes.

  • Coil spring: Features a fine wire coil, making the rim very soft and flexible; suitable for women with good pelvic tone.
  • Arcing spring: Bends in a straight line easily and is designed for women with a cystocele, a retroverted uterus, or general weak muscle support.
  • Flat spring: Offers a firmer rim containing a flat steel band, ideal for women with strong perineal muscles.

Sizing is measured in millimeters, typically ranging from 50mm to 95mm. A fitting session using a fitting ring set is mandatory to find the exact size that rests securely behind the pubic symphysis without causing pressure necrosis or discomfort.

Client Selection and Fitting Procedure

Fitting a diaphragm requires a bimanual pelvic examination to assess the distance between the posterior vaginal fornix and the inferior surface of the pubic bone. The nurse or clinician inserts fingers into the vagina to measure this space and selects the trial fitting ring that stays in place comfortably while the client bears down.

Clients must be reassessed for sizing after specific life events: vaginal delivery, pelvic surgery, or a weight fluctuation of five kilograms or more, because these changes alter vaginal dimensions.

Client Instructions on Insertion and Removal

Proper technique ensures the diaphragm provides adequate contraceptive protection. The user applies about a tablespoon of spermicidal cream or jelly inside the dome and around the rim before insertion. The device can be inserted up to six hours before intercourse. To place it, the user squeezes the opposite sides of the rim together and guides it deep into the vagina along the posterior wall until it tucks behind the pubic bone.

The diaphragm MUST remain in place for at least six hours following intercourse to ensure all sperm are neutralized, but it should not stay inside longer than twenty-four hours total to prevent toxic shock syndrome. To remove it, the user hooks a finger over the rim and pulls downward and outward.

Device Maintenance and Lifespan

Good hygiene and proper care prolong the lifespan of a diaphragm. After removal, the device should be washed with mild soap and warm water, rinsed thoroughly, and allowed to air dry completely. Storing it in its plastic case in a cool, dry place prevents degradation from heat or light. Clients must inspect the rubber regularly by holding it up to a light source or filling it with water to check for pinholes, cracks, or thinning spots. A well-maintained silicone or latex diaphragm typically lasts between one to two years before replacement is necessary.

Advantages and Limitations

The diaphragm offers specific benefits for women who prefer hormone-free birth control. It is user-controlled, portable, does not interrupt foreplay if inserted beforehand, and can be used during lactation without affecting breast milk supply.

However, limitations exist. It offers zero protection against sexually transmitted infections, and frequent use of spermicides containing nonoxynol-9 can cause vaginal irritation or disrupt normal flora. It requires a high level of motivation, adequate dexterity, and comfort with touching one's genitalia, which may deter some users.

Potential Complications and Troubleshooting

While generally safe, diaphragm use carries certain risks if care instructions are ignored. Increased frequency of urinary tract infections is a recognized complication because the rim can exert pressure on the urethra, obstructing complete bladder emptying. Additionally, leaving the device in place beyond twenty-four hours elevates the risk of toxic shock syndrome, a rare but dangerous bacterial illness.

Troubleshooting complaints usually involves refitting for a different size if the user experiences pelvic pain, expulsion during straining, or frequent urinary symptoms.

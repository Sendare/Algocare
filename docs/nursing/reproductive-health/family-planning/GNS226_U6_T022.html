Counselling in Family Planning

Introduction to Family Planning Counselling

Family planning counselling is an interactive process between a healthcare provider and a client to help them make voluntary, informed decisions about their reproductive health. It is not about pushing a specific method, but rather helping individuals or couples choose a contraceptive option that fits their life goals, health status, and reproductive intentions. In busy Nigerian clinics, where patient loads are high, rushing this step often leads to method dissatisfaction and early discontinuation.

Core Principles and Ethics in Reproductive Counselling

Effective reproductive counselling rests on ethical pillars that safeguard the client's rights and dignity.

  • Confidentiality: All personal and medical information shared during the session must remain private, which encourages honest disclosure.
  • Voluntarism: The client holds the absolute right to accept, reject, or switch methods without coercion.
  • Informed Choice: Clients must receive accurate, unbiased, and complete information regarding all available options.

The GATHER Approach in Family Planning

The GATHER acronym is a reliable step-by-step framework used to structure family planning counselling sessions systematically.

  • G - Greet: Welcome the client respectfully and establish rapport.
  • A - Ask: Inquire about their reproductive goals, medical history, and needs.
  • T - Tell: Provide clear information about available contraceptive choices.
  • H - Help: Assist the client in selecting the most suitable method.
  • E - Explain: Explain how to use the chosen method correctly and manage side effects.
  • R - Return: Schedule follow-up visits and encourage return for any concerns.

Communication Techniques and Active Listening

Good communication builds trust and ensures clarity between the nurse and the client. Active listening involves paying full attention, nodding, and avoiding interruptions while the client speaks. Using open-ended questions helps clients express their true feelings rather than just answering yes or no. Non-verbal cues, such as maintaining an open posture and a welcoming facial expression, are just as important as the spoken words in clinical settings.

Assessing Client Needs and Medical Eligibility

Before offering any contraceptive method, the nurse must assess the client's health status using established guidelines, such as the World Health Organization Medical Eligibility Criteria for Contraceptive Use (MEC). This screening ensures that a chosen method does not pose unnecessary health risks. For instance, prescribing combined hormonal contraceptives to a client with a history of severe hypertension or deep vein thrombosis can lead to severe cardiovascular complications.

Addressing Myths, Misconceptions, and Side Effects

Many clients reject modern contraceptives due to widespread community myths, such as beliefs that methods cause permanent infertility, cancer, or children born with deformities. Nurses must patiently debunk these myths with factual information while validating the client's concerns. Furthermore, discussing normal side effects—like changes in menstrual bleeding patterns with injectables or implants—prevents panic and reduces early abandonment of the method.

Special Considerations in Nigerian Clinical Settings

Providing family planning services in Nigeria requires sensitivity to cultural, religious, and socio-economic dynamics. Spousal consent is often a major factor, as many women feel they must seek their husband's approval before using contraceptives, despite having the right to personal health choices. Additionally, addressing youth-friendly services is critical to breaking down barriers for unmarried adolescents who need reproductive health information without judgment.

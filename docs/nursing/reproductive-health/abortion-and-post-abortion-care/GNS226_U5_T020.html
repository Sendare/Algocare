Establishing PAC Services

Introduction to Post-Abortion Care (PAC) Services

Post-Abortion Care is an emergency intervention designed to treat complications arising from spontaneous or induced abortions. In many Nigerian hospitals and primary health centers, PAC saves lives by managing severe hemorrhage, sepsis, and cervical injuries. The model addresses both the immediate clinical emergency and the broader psychological and reproductive needs of the client.

The Five Essential Elements of PAC

A comprehensive PAC program relies on five interconnected components to ensure holistic patient recovery:

  • Community engagement: Raising awareness and mobilizing resources to bridge the gap between rural populations and health facilities.
  • Counseling: Providing confidential, non-judgmental guidance addressing emotional well-being, contraceptive choices, and reproductive goals.
  • Treatment of complications: Managing incomplete abortion, hemorrhage, and sepsis using evidence-based medical and surgical methods.
  • Contraceptive and family planning services: Offering immediate access to a full range of modern methods before client discharge.
  • Reproductive and other health services: Connecting clients to broader primary healthcare services like STI screening and HIV testing.

Needs Assessment and Community Readiness

Before launching PAC services, nurses must evaluate local epidemiological data, cultural beliefs, and infrastructural capacities. Community readiness determines how services are received. If traditional birth attendants or community leaders view modern healthcare facilities with suspicion, utilization rates will drop. Nurse-led sensitization campaigns help demystify PAC and build local trust.

Facility Readiness, Infrastructure, and Supplies

A PAC-ready facility requires dedicated space for confidential counseling, a clean procedure room, and reliable infection prevention protocols. Essential supplies include Manual Vacuum Aspiration (MVA) instruments, misoprostol, antibiotics, intravenous fluids, and personal protective equipment. Stockouts of basic analgesics or MVA kits compromise safety and force dangerous clinical delays.

Training and Competency-Based Capacity Building

Effective PAC implementation relies on skilled personnel across the multidisciplinary team. Competency-based training moves away from purely theoretical lectures into practical skill stations, simulation, and supervised clinical practice. Healthcare providers must master physical assessment, MVA techniques, pain management, and empathetic communication.

Clinical Management of Abortion Complications

Management depends on the specific presentation: incomplete, inevitable, missed, or septic abortion. Initial stabilization focuses on airway, breathing, and circulation. Uterine evacuation is performed if retained products of conception are present. Broad-spectrum antibiotics are mandatory when signs of pelvic infection or sepsis appear.

Integrating Family Planning and Counseling Services

Integrating family planning into PAC is the most effective strategy to prevent repeat unintended pregnancies. Counseling must be voluntary, confidential, and tailored to the client's individual life plans. Nurses should present a wide range of contraceptive methods, including barrier methods, injectables, implants, and intrauterine devices, allowing clients to make informed choices before leaving the facility.

Monitoring, Evaluation, and Quality Improvement

Routine tracking of PAC service utilization, complication rates, and supply chains ensures sustainable program success. Quality improvement committees review clinical audits and near-miss cases to identify systemic gaps. By fostering a blame-free reporting culture, healthcare teams learn from errors and continuously elevate the standard of emergency reproductive care.

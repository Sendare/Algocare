Maternal Morbidity from Unsafe Abortion

Introduction to Maternal Morbidity and Unsafe Abortion in Nigeria

Unsafe abortion remains a leading contributor to maternal mortality and severe morbidity across Nigeria. The World Health Organization defines an unsafe abortion as a procedure for terminating an unintended pregnancy carried out either by persons lacking the necessary skills or in an environment that does not conform to minimal medical standards. In many Nigerian tertiary and secondary health facilities, post-abortion complications strain emergency obstetric care units. These complications stem largely from restrictive legal frameworks, societal stigma, and financial barriers that drive women toward clandestine, unqualified providers. Young women and adolescents in both rural and urban informal settlements bear a disproportionate burden of these preventable health crises.

Pathophysiology and Underlying Mechanisms of Abortion-Related Injury

Injuries from unsafe terminations typically arise from mechanical trauma, introduction of non-sterile instruments, or caustic chemical agents. When sharp objects penetrate the uterine wall, visceral perforation of the bowel or bladder often follows. Chemical agents, such as potassium permanganate or noxious plant extracts, cause localized tissue necrosis, severe chemical vaginitis, and rapid systemic absorption leading to toxicity. Once the mucosal barrier of the uterine cavity is breached by unsterile maneuvers, ascending polymicrobial infection rapidly establishes endometritis, progressing to parametritis, pelvic peritonitis, and fulminant septic shock.

Classification and Types of Complications

  • Hemorrhage: Caused by incomplete evacuation, uterine atony, or cervical lacerations, leading to acute hypovolemic shock.
  • Sepsis: Results from ascending infection introduced by unsterile equipment, presenting with fever, foul-smelling discharge, and pelvic pain.
  • Visceral Injury: Direct damage to the bladder, bowel, or ureters during blind instrumentation.
  • Chemical Toxicity: Systemic poisoning resulting from the insertion of noxious concoctions or toxic solutions into the vagina.

Clinical Assessment and Diagnostic Evaluation

A rapid, systematic clinical assessment is critical when a patient presents with suspected abortion complications. Nurses must prioritize stabilizing the airway, breathing, and circulation before undertaking a detailed history. Assess the volume of vaginal bleeding, measure vital signs for indicators of shock, and palpate the abdomen for guarding, tenderness, or rebound pain indicating peritonitis. Diagnostic evaluations typically include a hemoglobin estimation, blood grouping and cross-matching, a serum pregnancy test, and a pelvic ultrasound to identify retained products of conception or free fluid in the peritoneal cavity.

Emergency Nursing Management and Stabilization

Immediate nursing interventions focus on halting hemorrhage and fighting hypovolemic shock. Secure large-bore intravenous access instantly and infuse isotonic crystalloids rapidly. Administer prescribed uterotonics, such as oxytocin or misoprostol, to encourage uterine contraction if retained tissue or atony is present. Prepare the patient for surgical evacuation using Manual Vacuum Aspiration where appropriate. Monitor urinary output strictly via an indwelling catheter to evaluate renal perfusion. Ensure blood products are readily available if cross-matching indicates profound anemia.

Post-Abortion Care (PAC) and Holistic Rehabilitation

Comprehensive Post-Abortion Care extends far beyond emergency medical evacuation. Nurses play a pivotal role in delivering empathetic, non-judgmental care that addresses physical, emotional, and psychological recovery. Provide structured counseling on reproductive health, contraception, and danger signs before discharge. Address the psychological trauma associated with loss, stigma, or coercion by offering compassionate psychological support or referrals. Ensure linkage to ongoing community health services to support the patient's long-term well-being and prevent recurrent unwanted pregnancies.

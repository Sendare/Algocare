Pain Management in Post-Abortion Care

Introduction to Post-Abortion Pain Management

Pain following pregnancy termination is a normal physiological response that requires structured clinical attention. Post-abortion care (PAC) guidelines mandate that pain relief is integrated directly into routine recovery protocols. In many Nigerian health facilities, pain is underreported due to stoicism or fear of medical judgment, making proactive assessment essential. Effective management shortens recovery time, reduces anxiety, and prevents acute discomfort from escalating into chronic pelvic pain syndromes.

Pathophysiology and Sources of Post-Abortion Pain

  • Uterine Contractions: Prostaglandin release triggers rhythmic myometrial contractions to expel remaining tissue and achieve hemostasis, felt as sharp cramping.
  • Cervical Dilatation: Mechanical stretching of the internal and external cervical os stimulates local nociceptors, producing sharp or pressure-like lower abdominal discomfort.
  • Surgical Trauma: Instrument passage and suction curettage cause localized endometrial inflammation and micro-trauma to the uterine wall.

Comprehensive Pain Assessment and Monitoring

Accurate pain measurement guides analgesic administration and evaluates treatment efficacy. Nurses should combine standardized rating scales with behavioral observations, especially for patients who struggle with numerical tools. Pain scores must be documented alongside vital signs during the immediate post-procedure window.

Pharmacological Interventions and Analgesic Protocols

Pain relief follows a stepped pharmacological approach. Mild to moderate cramping responds well to Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drugs (NSAIDs) such as ibuprofen, which target the underlying prostaglandin production. For more intense pain, clinicians may combine NSAIDs with paracetamol. Severe or refractory pain requires short-acting opioids, though monitoring for respiratory depression and excessive sedation remains critical. Prophylactic administration before the procedure reduces overall analgesic consumption.

Non-Pharmacological Comfort Measures

Non-pharmacological techniques enhance medical analgesia and provide psychological reassurance. Applying a warm compress or hot water bottle to the lower abdomen relaxes myometrial fibers and eases cramping. Encouraging comfortable positioning, such as lateral recumbent posture with knees flexed, relieves tension on pelvic muscles. Guided breathing exercises lower sympathetic nervous system arousal, helping patients manage procedural and recovery anxiety.

Identifying Complications and Abnormal Pain Patterns

Normal post-abortion pain gradually decreases over several days. Nurses must remain vigilant for red flags that indicate serious complications such as uterine perforation, retained products of conception, or pelvic infection. Abnormal patterns include pain that steadily worsens instead of improving, severe unilateral pelvic pain, or pain accompanied by foul-smelling discharge, high fever, and heavy vaginal bleeding soaking more than two large pads per hour.

Patient Education and Discharge Counseling

Comprehensive discharge preparation ensures safe recovery at home. Nurses must teach patients what level of discomfort to expect and how to take prescribed analgesics on a schedule rather than waiting for pain to become severe. Clear instructions must be provided regarding warning signs that require immediate hospital return. Reviewing self-care measures, such as avoiding tampons and vaginal douches, prevents ascending infections during the healing phase.

Medication Abortion

Introduction to Medication Abortion

Medication abortion involves using pharmaceutical agents to terminate an early pregnancy as a non-surgical alternative. This approach is widely utilized in Nigerian healthcare settings for comprehensive reproductive health care within approved gestational age limits. It offers privacy and autonomy to patients while reducing the burden on surgical theatre resources. Understanding this method allows nurses to provide accurate information and dispel common myths surrounding medical termination.

Mechanism of Action of Common Regimens

The standard medical abortion regimen combines two distinct drugs: mifepristone and misoprostol.

  • Mifepristone: A synthetic steroid that acts as a progesterone receptor antagonist. It blocks progesterone, causing the decidua to break down and the cervix to soften.
  • Misoprostol: A prostaglandin analogue that binds to myometrial cells to cause strong uterine contractions, expelling the products of conception.

Indications and Eligibility Criteria

Eligibility for medication abortion depends primarily on gestational age and medical history. The combined regimen is typically approved for early pregnancies, generally up to nine to ten weeks of gestation, depending on local clinical guidelines.

Contraindications must be carefully screened:

  • Confirmed or suspected ectopic pregnancy
  • Chronic adrenal failure or long-term corticosteroid therapy
  • Known allergy to mifepristone or misoprostol
  • Presence of an intrauterine device in place

Pre-Procedure Assessment and Counseling

A thorough nursing assessment establishes accurate dating, confirms intrauterine location via ultrasound when available, and rules out contraindications. Baseline vital signs and a physical examination are mandatory. Counseling must cover what the patient will experience physically, expected bleeding timelines, and psychological support. Nurses ensure the patient understands when to seek emergency care.

Step-by-Step Clinical Protocol and Administration

The clinical protocol involves sequential administration of the chosen medications. First, mifepristone is swallowed orally in the clinic. Twenty-four to forty-eight hours later, misoprostol is administered, which can be taken buccally, sublingually, or vaginally. The nurse observes the patient for a brief period after administration and provides pain management medications, such as ibuprofen, to manage upcoming cramping.

Expected Side Effects and Normal Process

The process mimics a heavy spontaneous miscarriage. Patients experience severe cramping and vaginal bleeding that is typically heavier than a normal menstrual period, often accompanied by the passage of blood clots and tissue. Systemic side effects from misoprostol include transient nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, dizziness, and low-grade fever or chills within hours of taking the dose.

Identifying Complications and Warning Signs

While generally safe, nurses must be vigilant in identifying complications that require immediate medical intervention. Hemorrhage is a primary risk, defined as soaking through two large sanitary pads per hour for two consecutive hours. Other warning signs include persistent high fever, severe pelvic pain not relieved by analgesics, or foul-smelling vaginal discharge indicating possible infection or incomplete abortion.

Post-Abortion Care, Contraception, and Follow-Up

Post-procedure care ensures physical recovery and prevents rapid repeat unintended pregnancies. Fertility can return as early as two weeks following an abortion. Nurses provide immediate contraceptive counseling and initiate chosen methods—including injectables, implants, pills, or barrier methods—before the patient leaves the facility. A follow-up visit or telephone check is scheduled to confirm the complete termination of the pregnancy.

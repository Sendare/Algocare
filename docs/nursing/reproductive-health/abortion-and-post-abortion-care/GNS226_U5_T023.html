Termination of Unwanted Pregnancy

Legal Framework and Definitions

The legal status of pregnancy termination in Nigeria is strictly governed by the Criminal Code (Southern states) and the Penal Code (Northern states). Under these laws, an abortion is illegal unless it is performed strictly to save the pregnant woman's life. Performing or undergoing an illegal termination carries severe imprisonment terms for both the practitioner and the woman. Despite these restrictive laws, the clinical reality in many Nigerian healthcare facilities involves managing the severe complications of clandestine procedures. Nurses must understand these legal boundaries while prioritizing emergency lifesaving care for patients presenting with incomplete or septic complications.

Safe Versus Unsafe Termination

Pregnancy termination outcomes depend heavily on whether the procedure is classified as safe or unsafe. The World Health Organization defines safe abortion as a procedure using methods recommended by the WHO, appropriate to the pregnancy duration, and performed by a trained provider. Unsafe termination involves methods executed by unskilled individuals, hazardous substances, or unsanitary environments.

  • Safe Procedure: Performed using manual vacuum aspiration or approved medical regimens under sterile clinical conditions.
  • Unsafe Procedure: Involves insertion of foreign objects, ingestion of caustic chemicals, or massage by traditional practitioners.
  • Morbidity and Mortality: Unsafe practices drastically increase rates of severe hemorrhage, pelvic infection, and maternal death.

Etiology of Unwanted Pregnancies

Unwanted or unintended pregnancies arise from a complex interplay of social, economic, and educational factors. In many Nigerian communities, poverty limits access to modern contraceptive commodities, leading to inconsistent use or complete non-use. Cultural norms, gender inequality, and lack of comprehensive sex education among adolescents further compound the vulnerability to unplanned conception. Sexual violence and coercion also remain significant contributors, leaving victims with no control over their reproductive health.

Clinical Assessment and Pre-Termination Counseling

A thorough clinical assessment is mandatory before initiating any intervention for a terminated or terminating pregnancy. Nurses must evaluate vital signs, estimate gestational age through last menstrual period and physical examination, and check for signs of active bleeding or infection. Pre-termination counseling requires a non-judgmental, empathetic approach. The nurse must explore the patient's emotional state, confirm that the decision is voluntary, and provide clear information regarding the chosen procedure, expected discomfort, and potential warning signs.

Medical and Surgical Methods

Where legally permissible, pregnancy termination utilizes either medical regimens or surgical evacuation. Medical termination involves medications such as mifepristone followed by misoprostol to induce uterine contractions and expel the products of conception. Surgical methods primarily include Manual Vacuum Aspiration (MVA) or Dilatation and Evacuation (D&E), prized for their speed and effectiveness in early gestations. Nurses manage medication administration, monitor pain levels, observe for excessive bleeding, and offer continuous reassurance throughout the process.

Complications of Unsafe Termination

Unsafe terminations frequently result in catastrophic physical complications that demand immediate emergency nursing care. Retained products of conception cause persistent, heavy bleeding leading to hypovolemic shock. Perforations of the uterus can occur from sharp instruments wielded by untrained operators, causing internal hemorrhage and peritonitis. Septic abortion arises when unsterile objects introduce bacteria into the uterine cavity, progressing rapidly to septic shock and multi-organ failure if left untreated.

Comprehensive Post-Abortion Care Nursing

Comprehensive Post-Abortion Care (PAC) is a vital strategy for reducing maternal mortality and morbidity. The nursing care model rests on three pillars: emergency treatment of complications, contraceptive counseling, and emotional support. Nurses monitor vital signs closely, administer prescribed antibiotics and uterotonics, and manage pain. Providing empathetic, non-judgmental care is essential, as patients often experience stigma, guilt, or fear of legal repercussions when seeking help in Nigerian hospitals.

Contraceptive Counseling and Prevention

Preventing future unwanted pregnancies is the ultimate goal of post-abortion care. Ovulation can return as early as two weeks after a pregnancy termination, meaning patients are immediately at risk of conception if unprotected. Nurses must provide tailored contraceptive counseling before discharge. Offering a wide range of methods—including injectables, implants, intrauterine devices, and barrier methods—empowers women to take control of their reproductive health and avoids the cycle of repeat terminations.

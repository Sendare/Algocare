Unsafe Abortion in Nigeria

Definition and Scope of Unsafe Abortion in Nigeria

An unsafe abortion is defined by the World Health Organization as a procedure for terminating an unwanted pregnancy carried out either by persons lacking the necessary skills or in an environment that does not conform to minimal medical standards, or both. In Nigeria, this public health challenge contributes heavily to maternal morbidity and mortality statistics. Most procedures occur secretly, driven by the stigma surrounding premarital sex and unwanted pregnancies. Young girls and women in rural and semi-urban settings often resort to unqualified practitioners due to financial constraints or a lack of accessible reproductive health services. Consequently, complications from incomplete evacuations and sepsis overwhelm emergency wards across teaching hospitals and general clinics daily.

Legal and Socio-Religious Context in Nigeria

Nigeria's legal framework regarding pregnancy termination is highly restrictive, governed mainly by the Criminal Code in the Southern states and the Penal Code in the Northern states. Under these laws, an abortion is only legally permissible when it is performed strictly to save the pregnant woman's life. Performing or undergoing an illegal termination carries severe prison sentences for both the woman and the practitioner. Culturally and religiously, Nigerian society predominantly views abortion as a moral infraction. This religious conservatism, combined with punitive laws, creates a climate of silence, forcing patients to hide complications until they reach advanced, life-threatening stages.

Common Methods and Pathophysiology of Unsafe Termination

Methods used in unsafe abortions vary widely based on resource availability and the provider's level of training.

  • Mechanical instrumentation: Insertion of unsterile objects such as knitting needles, cassava stems, or plastic tubing directly into the uterus, causing perforation, hemorrhage, and bacterial introduction.
  • Chemical and herbal concoctions: Oral or vaginal administration of caustic substances, concentrated potash solutions, detergent mixtures, or unverified herbal infusions designed to induce uterine contractions.
  • Abuse of abortifacient drugs: Unsupervised use of medications like misoprostol or oxytocin, often obtained from patent medicine vendors without proper dosage guidance.

Pathophysiologically, these methods trigger severe uterine trauma, localized tissue necrosis, chemical peritonitis, and ascending pelvic infections that quickly spread into the systemic circulation.

Clinical Presentation and Complications

Patients presenting to emergency departments following an unsafe abortion exhibit a spectrum of acute symptoms. The clinical picture typically features lower abdominal pain, foul-smelling vaginal discharge, abnormal bleeding, and signs of systemic toxicity.

Nursing Assessment and Rapid Diagnosis

Initial nursing care requires a rapid, non-judgmental history and physical assessment to stabilize the patient. Nurses must prioritize assessing the ABCs—Airway, Breathing, and Circulation—because severe hemorrhage can induce rapid hypovolemic shock. Vital signs should be monitored continuously, noting any drop in blood pressure or spiking temperature. A thorough abdominal examination checks for tenderness, guarding, and bowel sounds. The nurse also assesses the amount and characteristics of vaginal bleeding, saving any passed tissue fragments for medical inspection. Establishing intravenous access with large-bore cannulas and collecting blood samples for cross-matching and full blood counts form immediate priorities.

Emergency Medical and Surgical Management

Medical management focuses on correcting fluid deficits, reversing shock, and fighting infection. Broad-spectrum intravenous antibiotics are administered immediately to cover polymicrobial flora introduced during the procedure. For patients with incomplete abortions and minimal bleeding, uterotonic agents may assist, but surgical intervention remains the cornerstone of care. Manual Vacuum Aspiration (MVA) or Dilatation and Evacuation (D&E) are performed under aseptic conditions to empty the uterine cavity completely and stop hemorrhage. If a uterine perforation with visceral injury is suspected, emergency exploratory laparotomy is mandatory to repair the damage and perform a peritoneal lavage.

Post-Abortion Care (PAC) and Counseling

Post-Abortion Care is a comprehensive strategy designed to reduce maternal death and complications. PAC encompasses emergency treatment of complications, contraceptive counseling, and linkage to broader reproductive health services. Nurses play a frontline role in delivering empathetic, confidential counseling before the patient is discharged. Because fertility returns very rapidly following an abortion—often within two weeks—discussing family planning options is critical to prevent rapid repeat unintended pregnancies. Contraceptive methods, ranging from barrier methods to long-acting reversible contraceptives, should be offered directly at the bedside based on the client's informed preference.

Preventive Strategies and the Role of the Nurse

Prevention of unsafe abortion requires multi-sectoral collaboration, with nurses acting as educators, advocates, and care providers. Community outreach programs led by nurses help demystify reproductive health, providing accurate information on safe sex and modern contraception to adolescents and young adults. Nurses must advocate for youth-friendly clinics that offer non-judgmental counseling and services without demanding parental consent or imposing moral barriers. Furthermore, engaging community and religious leaders in dialogues about maternal health helps dismantle the stigma surrounding family planning, ultimately reducing the incidence of clandestine terminations.

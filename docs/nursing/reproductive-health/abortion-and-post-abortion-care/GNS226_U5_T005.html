Classification of Abortion

Definition and Broad Classification Frameworks

Abortion refers to the termination of pregnancy before the fetus is viable outside the womb, usually defined in clinical practice as expulsion or removal of the products of conception before 22 to 24 weeks of gestation, or a fetal weight of less than 500 grams. In Nigerian healthcare settings, understanding this timeline is critical for legal, ethical, and clinical documentation. Broadly, abortion is divided into two primary categories based on how the termination is initiated:

  • Spontaneous Abortion: Commonly known as a miscarriage, this occurs naturally without external physical or chemical interference.
  • Induced Abortion: This is the intentional termination of a pregnancy through medical, surgical, or traditional methods.

Clinical Subtypes of Spontaneous Abortion

Spontaneous abortions present in various clinical stages, each requiring specific nursing interventions and medical management. Nurses must accurately differentiate these subtypes based on physical examination and ultrasound findings.

  • Threatened Abortion: Vaginal bleeding occurs with or without mild abdominal cramps, but the cervical os remains closed and the pregnancy continues.
  • Inevitable Abortion: Bleeding and cramping increase, and the internal cervical os is open, making the expulsion of products of conception certain.
  • Incomplete Abortion: Some, but not all, products of conception have been expelled from the uterus, leaving tissue inside that causes ongoing bleeding.
  • Complete Abortion: All products of conception have completely passed out of the uterus, and the cervix has closed.
  • Missed Abortion: The embryo or fetus has died in utero, but the products of conception are retained inside the uterus without bleeding or expulsion.
  • Septic Abortion: Any form of spontaneous or induced abortion complicated by intrauterine infection, common in unsafe or unsterile environments.

Classification of Induced Abortion

Induced abortions are categorized primarily by safety, legality, and the method used. In Nigeria, legal frameworks restrict abortion unless it is performed to save the pregnant woman's life.

Gestational Age and Anatomical Categorization

Classifying abortions by gestational age guides the choice of evacuation method and predicts potential complications. First-trimester abortions occur before 13 completed weeks of gestation, where medical options or simple suction techniques are usually effective. Second-trimester abortions occur between 13 and 24 weeks, requiring more complex medical induction protocols or surgical dilation and evacuation due to increased fetal skeletal development and placental attachment.

Anatomical classifications focus on the location of the pregnancy. While intrauterine pregnancies terminate within the uterine cavity, ectopic pregnancies represent an abnormal implantation outside the uterine cavity—most commonly in the fallopian tube. Though technically a distinct condition, ruptured ectopic pregnancies present with acute surgical emergencies that mimic severe spontaneous bleeding.

Nursing Assessment and Diagnosis

Nurses play a frontline role in evaluating patients presenting with bleeding and pelvic pain. A comprehensive assessment includes obtaining an accurate menstrual history, estimating gestational age, and assessing vital signs to detect hypovolemic shock. Nurses inspect perineal pads to quantify blood loss and monitor for tissue passage.

Diagnostic tools ordered by the physician and supported by nursing preparation include quantitative serum beta-hCG tests and transvaginal ultrasound scans. In emergency scenarios, stabilizing maternal hemodynamics takes precedence over definitive classification.

Post-Abortion Care and Complications Management

Post-abortion care encompasses physical recovery, infection prevention, psychological support, and contraceptive counseling. Nurses must monitor for immediate complications such as severe hemorrhage, uterine perforation, and pelvic infection. Patients treated for septic abortion require broad-spectrum intravenous antibiotics and close monitoring for septic shock.

Emotional support is vital, as grief can occur regardless of whether the abortion was spontaneous or induced. Nurses provide non-judgmental care and connect patients with family planning services to prevent rapid repeat unintended pregnancies.

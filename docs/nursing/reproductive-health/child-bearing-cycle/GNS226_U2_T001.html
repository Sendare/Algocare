Definition of Pregnancy

Biological Definition of Pregnancy

Pregnancy, medically termed gestation, is the period during which one or more offspring develops inside a woman's uterus. Biologically, it begins with the successful fertilization of an oocyte by a spermatozoon and culminates in birth. Throughout this span, the maternal body undergoes profound anatomical and endocrine adjustments to sustain the growing fetus. In clinical nursing practice in Nigeria, understanding this definition forms the baseline for calculating expected dates of delivery and providing holistic maternal care.

Fertilization and Conception

Fertilization typically occurs in the ampulla of the fallopian tube within hours of sexual intercourse. Millions of sperm are deposited in the vagina, but only a few hundred reach the oocyte. A single spermatozoon penetrates the outer layers of the secondary oocyte, leading to the fusion of male and female pronuclei. This union forms a single-celled zygote containing a complete set of 46 chromosomes, marking the official creation of a new genetic blueprint.

Implantation and Early Development

As the zygote travels down the fallopian tube toward the uterine cavity, it undergoes rapid mitotic cell division, transforming into a morula and then a blastocyst. Implantation occurs roughly six to ten days after fertilization when the blastocyst burrows into the vascular endometrium of the uterus. Once embedded, the outer layer of cells begins producing human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG), a vital hormone that signals the corpus luteum to continue producing progesterone and maintain the uterine lining.

Duration and Trimesters of Pregnancy

Normal human gestation lasts approximately 280 days, or 40 weeks, calculated from the first day of the last normal menstrual period (LNMP). Clinically, this timeline is divided into three distinct segments called trimesters:

  • First Trimester: Weeks 1 through 13, marked by organogenesis and high risk of spontaneous miscarriage.
  • Second Trimester: Weeks 14 through 27, a period of rapid fetal growth and maternal stabilization.
  • Third Trimester: Weeks 28 through 40, focusing on final weight gain, lung maturation, and preparation for birth.

Key Physiological Changes in the Mother

Maternal physiology alters dramatically to support fetal development. Blood volume increases by up to 40 to 50 percent to meet the metabolic demands of the uteroplacental unit. Cardiac output rises, respiratory rate increases slightly due to elevated progesterone levels, and the renal system filters an increased workload. Nurses must recognize these adaptations to distinguish normal variants from pathological conditions during antenatal visits.

Clinical Confirmation of Pregnancy

Signs of pregnancy are categorized into presumptive, probable, and positive indicators. Presumptive signs include subjective symptoms like amenorrhea, breast tenderness, and nausea. Probable signs are objective findings observed by a clinician, such as a positive urine pregnancy test, softening of the cervix (Goodell sign), or uterine enlargement. Positive signs provide absolute proof of fetal presence and include fetal heart tones, visualization of the fetus via ultrasound, and active fetal movements felt by the examiner.

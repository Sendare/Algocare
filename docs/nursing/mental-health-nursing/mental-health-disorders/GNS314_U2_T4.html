Causes of Mental Disorders

Introduction to Etiology in Mental Health-Psychiatric Nursing

Etiology refers to the study of the causes or origins of diseases, including psychiatric conditions. In psychiatric nursing, understanding why a mental disorder develops helps the nurse move past simply managing behavioural symptoms to targeting the root cause. Mental illnesses rarely stem from a single isolated source. Instead, they usually emerge from a complex interplay of multiple variables. Recognizing this complexity prevents nurses from reducing a patient's suffering to personal weakness or bad character.

The Biopsychosocial Framework

The biopsychosocial model integrates biological, psychological, and social factors to explain mental health conditions. No single domain operates in complete isolation.

  • Biological domain: Genetics, brain structure, and physical health.
  • Psychological domain: Coping styles, emotional regulation, and personality traits.
  • Social domain: Family dynamics, poverty, cultural background, and life stressors.

Biological and Genetic Influences

Genetics significantly influence vulnerability to psychiatric disorders. Family history studies show that conditions like schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, and major depressive disorder run in families. However, inheriting a genetic predisposition does not guarantee a person will develop the illness. Environmental triggers often act as the catalyst that turns a genetic vulnerability into an active disorder.

Neurochemical and Brain Alterations

Neurotransmitters are chemical messengers that transmit signals across synapses in the brain. Imbalances or dysregulation involving serotonin, dopamine, norepinephrine, and gamma-aminobutyric acid are strongly linked to various mental disorders. For instance, excess dopamine activity is associated with the psychotic symptoms seen in schizophrenia, while low serotonin levels often correlate with clinical depression.

Psychological and Personality Factors

Maladaptive personality traits and poor coping mechanisms increase vulnerability to mental health distress. Cognitive distortions, such as overgeneralizing or catastrophic thinking, maintain anxiety and depression. A person who struggles to regulate emotions or lacks resilience when facing minor setbacks is more likely to develop a mood or anxiety disorder following a life crisis.

Life Stressors, Trauma, and Adverse Experiences

Severe life events often trigger the onset of psychiatric episodes. Adverse childhood experiences, physical or sexual abuse, chronic poverty, and displacement create lasting neurological and emotional changes. In Nigeria, economic hardship, community violence, and security challenges serve as major environmental stressors that precipitate or worsen conditions like post-traumatic stress disorder and severe depressive episodes.

Substance Use and Dual Diagnosis

The relationship between substance use and mental disorders is complex and bidirectional. Psychoactive substances like cannabis, alcohol, and methamphetamine can induce psychiatric symptoms or trigger underlying latent psychotic disorders. Conversely, individuals with mental illnesses often use substances to self-medicate their distress. Managing dual diagnosis requires treating both the addiction and the psychiatric illness concurrently.

Cultural and Social Determinants in Nigeria

Cultural beliefs heavily influence how mental illness is perceived, diagnosed, and treated in Nigeria. Many communities attribute mental disorders to spiritual attacks, witchcraft, or breaking ancestral taboos rather than biological or psychological factors. This cultural attribution often delays professional psychiatric care, as families frequently seek help from traditional healers or spiritual houses first before visiting a formal mental health facility.

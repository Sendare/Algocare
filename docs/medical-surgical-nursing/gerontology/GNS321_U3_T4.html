Needs of the Elderly

Introduction to Gerontology and Aging

Gerontology is the scientific study of aging, covering physical, mental, and social changes in older adults. Geriatrics, on the other hand, focuses specifically on the medical care and treatment of diseases in old age. As people grow older, they experience normal age-related changes that differ from actual diseases. Recognizing this distinction helps nurses prevent unnecessary medical treatments for normal bodily shifts. Aging is a natural part of the human life span influenced by genetics, lifestyle, and environment.

Physiological and Biological Needs

Older adults experience gradual decline across all major body systems. The cardiovascular system stiffens, leading to higher blood pressure. Respiratory muscles weaken, reducing overall lung capacity. Skin loses its elasticity and protective fat layer, making it prone to tears and pressure ulcers. Sensory organs like eyes and ears also decline, resulting in presbyopia and hearing loss. These physical shifts require nurses to adapt care plans to protect fragile skin and support failing senses.

Psychological and Emotional Needs

Aging brings unique emotional challenges, including retirement, loss of spouses, and declining physical independence. These major life shifts can trigger depression, anxiety, and feelings of isolation. Nurses must actively assess mental health and provide compassionate presence. Encouraging social connections and validating their life experiences helps older adults maintain self-worth and emotional stability.

Nutritional and Dietary Needs

Nutritional requirements change in later life due to slower metabolism and reduced physical activity. Older adults need fewer total calories, but their need for quality proteins, vitamins, and minerals remains high.

  • Hydration: Decreased sense of thirst puts elders at high risk for dehydration.
  • Fiber: Essential to prevent chronic constipation caused by slow bowel motility.
  • Calcium and Vitamin D: Crucial for maintaining bone density and preventing fractures.
  • Texture Modification: Soft or minced diets help patients with missing teeth or swallowing difficulties.

Safety, Mobility, and Fall Prevention

Falls are a major cause of injury, disability, and loss of independence among older adults. Poor vision, unsteady gait, muscle weakness, and environmental hazards like loose rugs contribute to fall risks. Nurses play a key role in home and hospital safety assessments. Removing clutter, ensuring good lighting, and encouraging the use of assistive devices like walking frames greatly reduce injury rates.

Pharmacological Considerations and Medication Management

Older adults process medications differently because liver and kidney functions decline with age. This slowdown causes drugs to stay in the body longer, increasing the risk of toxic side effects. Polypharmacy, the use of multiple medications at once, is common and raises the chance of dangerous drug interactions. Nurses must perform regular medication reviews and educate patients on correct dosing schedules.

Holistic Nursing Care and Support Systems in Nigeria

In Nigeria, care for the elderly has traditionally rested heavily on the extended family system. However, urbanization and economic pressures are straining this cultural safety net. Holistic nursing care must involve family caregivers, community health workers, and spiritual leaders. Nurses bridge the gap by teaching families basic home nursing skills and connecting vulnerable elders with community resources.

Nursing Management of Eye, Ear, Nose and Throat Disorders

Comprehensive EENT Assessment and Diagnostic Techniques

Assessing the eyes, ears, nose, and throat requires a systematic approach, combining physical inspection, specialized tools, and client history. A thorough assessment pinpoints early sensory deficits and prevents minor conditions from escalating into permanent damage.

  • Eye Assessment: Inspect the external structures for symmetry, discharge, or lesions. Assess visual acuity using the Snellen chart for distance and a handheld card for near vision. Check pupillary reaction to light and accommodation.
  • Ear Assessment: Use an otoscope to view the tympanic membrane. In adults, gently pull the pinna up and back to straighten the ear canal; in young children, pull it down and back. Assess hearing using the whisper test, Weber test, and Rinne test.
  • Nose and Sinus Assessment: Inspect the nasal mucosa for color, swelling, and bleeding. Palpate the frontal and maxillary sinuses for tenderness, which indicates possible sinusitis.
  • Throat Assessment: Inspect the lips, gums, tongue, and pharynx using a penlight and tongue depressor. Grade tonsillar size and check the mucous membranes for hydration and exudate.

Nursing Care of Patients with Common Eye Disorders

Eye disorders range from mild irritation to sight-threatening conditions like glaucoma, cataracts, and macular degeneration. Nurses play a crucial role in monitoring vision changes, administering specialized eye drops, and ensuring safety.

Glaucoma involves a rise in intraocular pressure (IOP) that damages the optic nerve. It is often called the silent thief of sight because chronic open-angle glaucoma develops slowly without early pain. Nursing care focuses on teaching clients to take lifelong prescribed miotic or beta-blocker eye drops religiously to control pressure.

Catataracts cause a clouding of the natural eye lens, leading to blurred vision and faded colors. Postoperative care after cataract removal requires instructing the client to avoid bending over, heavy lifting, or straining during bowel movements, as these activities spike intraocular pressure and can rupture surgical incisions.

Nursing Management of Ear and Hearing Disorders

Disorders of the ear affect hearing, balance, and overall communication. Common conditions include otitis media, otitis externa, and Meniere's disease.

Otitis media is an infection of the middle ear, frequently seen in children due to the shorter, more horizontal Eustachian tube. Nursing interventions focus on pain relief, administering prescribed antibiotics, and monitoring for fever or drainage.

Meniere's disease affects the inner ear, causing vertigo, tinnitus, fluctuating hearing loss, and a feeling of fullness in the ear. Nursing management during an acute vertigo attack centers on safety: keeping the client in a quiet, dark room, maintaining bed rest, and minimizing sudden head movements. Educating clients on low-sodium diets helps reduce endolymph fluid buildup in the inner ear.

Care of Patients with Upper Respiratory and Nasal Conditions

Nasal and upper respiratory conditions such as allergic rhinitis, sinusitis, and epistaxis (nosebleeds) disrupt daily comfort and respiration. Nursing care emphasizes relieving congestion, preventing infection, and managing acute bleeding.

For epistaxis, position the client sitting upright and leaning forward to prevent blood from swallowing into the stomach, which causes nausea and vomiting. Apply direct firm pressure to the soft lower portion of the nose for at least ten to fifteen minutes. Instruct the client to breathe through the mouth during this time.

For chronic sinusitis, encourage adequate fluid intake to thin respiratory secretions. Teach clients how to use saline nasal sprays properly and warn against the overuse of over-the-counter decongestant nasal sprays, which can cause rebound congestion (rhinitis medicamentosa) if used for more than three to five consecutive days.

Throat Disorders and Airway Maintenance

Throat infections, tonsillitis, and post-surgical interventions require vigilant monitoring because the pharynx is shared by both the respiratory and digestive tracts. Maintaining a patent airway is always the primary nursing priority.

Following tonsillectomy, nurses must monitor closely for signs of hemorrhage. Frequent swallowing, restlessness, clearing the throat, and vomiting bright red blood are early warning signs that the surgical site is bleeding. Keep suction equipment and emergency airway supplies readily available at the bedside.

Provide cool fluids and soft foods once the client is fully alert and gag reflex has returned. Avoid red-colored or citrus beverages, as red fluids mimic blood if vomited, and citrus juices irritate the raw surgical wound.

Pharmacological Therapy and Administration of EENT Medications

Administering medications to the eyes, ears, and nose requires specific techniques to ensure the drug reaches the target site without causing contamination or tissue injury.

  • Eye Drops: Wash hands and wear gloves. Have the client look up, pull down the lower lid to form a pocket, and instill drops into the conjunctival sac. Never touch the dropper tip to the eye or eyelashes. Apply gentle pressure to the lacrimal duct for one minute to prevent systemic absorption.
  • Ear Drops: Warm drops to room temperature. For adults, pull the pinna up and back; for children, pull down and back. Instill drops along the side of the ear canal and have the client remain on their side for a few minutes.
  • Nasal Sprays: Have the client blow the nose gently. Close one nostril, insert the spray tip into the other nostril, point the tip away from the center nasal septum, and spray while the client inhales gently.

Patient Education and Rehabilitation for Sensory Deficits

Clients with permanent vision or hearing loss require comprehensive rehabilitation and education to maintain their independence, safety, and quality of life.

For clients with visual impairments, orient them to their hospital room environment using the clock method for food placement (e.g., meat is at six o'clock). Always announce your presence when entering the room and never rearrange their personal belongings without permission. Walk slightly ahead of the client, allowing them to hold your elbow for guidance.

For clients with hearing impairments, face the client directly when speaking, speak clearly and at a normal volume without shouting, and reduce background noise by turning off the television. Utilize written materials and visual aids to reinforce important health education.

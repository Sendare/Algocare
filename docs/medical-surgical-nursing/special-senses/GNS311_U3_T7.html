Nursing Management of Eye, Ear, Nose and Throat Disorders

Introduction to EENT Nursing Care

The sensory and respiratory systems managed under eye, ear, nose, and throat (EENT) nursing are vital for communication, environmental interaction, and basic survival. Because these structures are highly specialized, interconnected, and densely innervated, even minor pathology can cause profound distress or permanent functional loss. EENT nursing care demands precision, gentleness, and acute observational skills. In many Nigerian healthcare facilities, resource constraints mean nurses rely heavily on thorough clinical observation and basic equipment like penlights and otoscopes before specialized investigations become available. Recognizing early signs of sensory impairment or airway compromise prevents simple conditions from escalating into surgical emergencies.

Comprehensive EENT Assessment Techniques

An effective EENT assessment follows a systematic, head-to-toe or cranial-to-caudal progression. - **Visual Acuity**: Assessed using the Snellen chart for distance or a Jaeger card for near vision, noting any use of corrective lenses. - **External Eye Inspection**: Evaluates symmetry, eyelid margins, conjunctiva, sclera, and pupillary light reflex for equality and accommodation. - **Otoscopic Examination**: Involves gently pulling the pinna up and back in adults (down and back in children) to straighten the external auditory canal and inspect the tympanic membrane. - **Nasal Cavity Inspection**: Utilizes a nasal speculum and penlight to check for septal deviation, mucosal swelling, polyps, or active bleeding. - **Oropharyngeal Assessment**: Inspects the lips, buccal mucosa, teeth, tonsils, and posterior pharynx, noting exudates, inflammation, or asymmetry of the uvula during phonation.

Nursing Care in Common Eye Disorders

Eye disorders range from mild refractive errors and surface infections to sight-threatening conditions like glaucoma, cataracts, and corneal abrasions. - **Glaucoma**: Characterized by elevated intraocular pressure (IOP) leading to optic nerve damage. Nursing focuses on strict adherence to lifetime miotic or beta-blocker eyedrops and educating patients against activities that spike IOP, such as heavy lifting or straining. - **Cataracts**: Involves clouding of the natural lens. Preoperative care emphasizes patient reassurance, while postoperative care centers on preventing sudden increases in intraocular pressure, avoiding bending at the waist, and wearing protective eye shields. - **Conjunctivitis**: Can be viral, bacterial, or allergic. Intervention requires strict hand hygiene, teaching patients not to touch the unaffected eye, and proper administration of prescribed topical antimicrobials.

Nursing Management of Ear and Hearing Disorders

Disorders of the ear affect balance, communication, and psychosocial well-being. Common conditions include otitis media, otitis externa, presbycusis, and Meniere's disease. Otitis media frequently affects young children due to the shorter, more horizontal eustachian tube. Nursing care includes pain management, monitoring for fever, and completing the full course of prescribed antibiotics. For Meniere's disease—characterized by vertigo, tinnitus, and fluctuating hearing loss—management revolves around reducing endolymphatic hydrops through low-sodium diets and administering anti-vertigo medications. Safety precautions for sudden dizzy spells are paramount.

Care in Upper Respiratory and Nasal Conditions

Rhinitis, sinusitis, nasal polyps, and epistaxis are frequent upper airway complaints. Epistaxis (nosebleed) is particularly common in dry weather or hypertensive episodes. When managing acute epistaxis, seat the patient upright and leaning slightly forward to prevent blood from swallowing or tracking down the pharynx, which can cause vomiting or aspiration. Apply direct, steady pressure to the soft lower portion of the nose for at least 10 continuous minutes. Ice compresses across the bridge of the nose can promote localized vasoconstriction. Instruct the patient against nose-blowing or vigorous rubbing for several hours afterward.

Throat Disorders and Airway Maintenance

Pharyngitis, tonsillitis, peritonsillar abscesses, and laryngeal trauma place airway patency at immediate risk. The overriding nursing priority in any acute throat condition is maintaining a clear, patent airway and monitoring for signs of respiratory distress, such as stridor, restlessness, tachypnea, or intercostal retractions. Post-tonsillectomy patients require rigorous monitoring for hemorrhage. Frequent swallowing, restlessness, or clearing of the throat are classic early indicators of hidden bleeding, even before frank bright red blood appears from the mouth or nose. Cool liquids and soft foods are introduced once fully alert, avoiding red-colored fluids that can mimic blood upon emesis.

Pharmacological Therapy and EENT Medications

EENT medications include drops, ointments, sprays, and washes. Correct administration technique is essential because improper delivery drastically reduces drug efficacy or causes systemic side effects. - **Eyedrops**: Have the patient look up, pull down the lower lid to form a small conjunctival pocket, and instill drops into the pocket without touching the tip of the bottle to the eye. Apply gentle pressure to the nasolacrimal duct for one minute to prevent systemic absorption. - **Eardrops**: Straighten the canal, instill drops, and have the patient remain with the affected ear upward for a few minutes. Gently massaging the tragus helps distribute the medication. - **Nasal Sprays**: Instruct the patient to occlude one nostril, insert the nozzle slightly pointing away from the nasal septum to prevent mucosal trauma, and inhale gently as the canister is compressed.

Preoperative and Postoperative Care for EENT Surgeries

Surgical interventions in EENT nursing—such as myringotomy, tympanoplasty, corneal transplants, or septoplasty—require meticulous preparation and recovery monitoring. Preoperative care focuses on confirming informed consent, verifying baseline sensory function, and ensuring the patient understands required positional restrictions post-procedure. Postoperative nursing centers on pain management, preventing infection, and safeguarding surgical integrity. Nurses must instruct patients to avoid nose blowing, vigorous coughing, straining during bowel movements, or getting water directly into healing surgical sites.

Patient Education and Sensory Rehabilitation

Long-term success in EENT care relies heavily on patient education and rehabilitation for those with permanent sensory deficits. Nurses help patients adapt to reduced vision or hearing by teaching environmental modification strategies, such as clearing pathways of clutter, using adequate lighting, and labeling household items with high-contrast markers. When working with patients who have hearing loss, nurses should face the individual directly, speak in a normal tone without shouting, and minimize background noise. For irreversible vision loss, teaching cane techniques or referring patients to specialized community rehabilitation programs fosters independence and preserves quality of life.

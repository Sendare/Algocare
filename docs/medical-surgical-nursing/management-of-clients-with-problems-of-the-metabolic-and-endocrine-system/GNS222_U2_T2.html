Health History Assessment in Metabolic and Endocrine Disorders

Introduction to Endocrine and Metabolic Health History

Gathering a health history for endocrine and metabolic disorders requires a systematic approach. The endocrine system regulates complex physiological processes through hormone secretion, meaning subtle imbalances often manifest across multiple unrelated body systems. When interviewing a patient with suspected metabolic dysfunction, the nurse must listen carefully for vague complaints like chronic fatigue, unexpected weight shifts, or mood alterations that point toward hormonal deregulation.

Biographical Data and Demographic Risk Factors

Age, biological sex, and geographic background significantly influence endocrine disease patterns.

  • Age: Type 2 diabetes mellitus and hypothyroidism become increasingly prevalent in older adults, whereas type 1 diabetes typically manifests during childhood or adolescence.
  • Sex: Autoimmune thyroid disorders show a markedly higher incidence in women.
  • Geography: Endemic goiter remains a public health concern in regions with iodine-deficient soil, such as parts of the Nigerian hinterland.

Chief Complaint and History of Present Illness

Patients rarely walk into a clinic stating they have an endocrine problem; instead, they report specific functional changes. The History of Present Illness (HPI) must capture the onset, duration, severity, and triggering factors of these complaints. Common presenting triggers include polyuria, polydipsia, unexplained weight fluctuation, heat or cold intolerance, and sudden visual disturbances.

Comprehensive Review of Systems

Systematic questioning helps uncover hidden metabolic shifts. The nurse screens every major body system to capture cascading endocrine effects:

Past Medical, Surgical, and Family History

Previous illnesses often hold the key to current endocrine imbalances. Autoimmune diseases frequently cluster in individuals; a history of rheumatoid arthritis or pernicious anemia increases susceptibility to autoimmune thyroiditis. Surgical history requires careful review, particularly any prior neck operations, as accidental parathyroid damage during thyroidectomy causes lifelong hypocalcemia.

Medication and Substance History

Exogenous hormone use, herbal preparations, and over-the-counter drugs frequently disrupt endocrine feedback loops. Corticosteroids are notorious for inducing secondary Cushing's syndrome and masking infection signs. The nurse must also inquire about traditional remedies, as heavy metal content in some local preparations can impair renal and endocrine function.

Psychosocial, Nutritional, and Lifestyle Assessment

Endocrine function is deeply intertwined with daily habits and emotional stress. Chronic stress triggers continuous cortisol release from the adrenal cortex, exacerbating blood glucose instability and hypertension. The nurse evaluates dietary habits, sleep hygiene, alcohol intake, and physical activity levels to understand the complete picture of metabolic health.

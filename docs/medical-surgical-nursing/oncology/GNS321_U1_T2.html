Pathophysiology of the Malignant Process

Introduction to Cellular Growth and Regulation

Normally, human cells multiply, grow, and die in a carefully controlled cycle. This balance keeps body tissues healthy and repairs injuries when needed. The cell cycle is strictly managed by chemical signals that tell the cell when to divide and when to stop.

When these regulatory signals fail, cells begin to multiply uncontrollably. A neoplasm, or new growth, forms when this abnormal cell division creates a mass of tissue. Understanding how normal cells maintain balance is the first step in learning why cancer develops.

Characteristics of Benign Versus Malignant Neoplasms

Tumors are broadly grouped into two main categories based on how they behave in the body.

  • Benign neoplasms: These growths are localized, non-invasive, and do not spread to other parts of the body. They usually have a clear border and are encapsulated.
  • Malignant neoplasms: These are cancerous, invasive, and capable of spreading to distant organs through the blood or lymph system. They lack clear boundaries and grow rapidly.

Genetic Basis of Cancer: Proto-oncogenes and Tumor Suppressor Genes

Cancer is fundamentally a disease of the genes. Two main types of genes are responsible for keeping cell growth in check:

  1. Proto-oncogenes: These are normal genes that help cells grow. When mutations turn them into overactive oncogenes, they push cells to divide constantly without stopping.
  2. Tumor suppressor genes: These genes normally act as the brakes of the cell cycle by repairing DNA mistakes or telling damaged cells to self-destruct. When these genes are turned off or damaged, the brakes fail.

A person needs mutations in both types of regulatory systems for a tumor to truly take hold and grow.

The Multistep Process of Carcinogenesis

Developing cancer is rarely a sudden event. It happens in three distinct phases over time:

  • Initiation: A permanent change or mutation occurs in the cell's DNA caused by exposure to chemicals, radiation, or viruses.
  • Promotion: Various factors stimulate the initiated cell to divide and multiply repeatedly. Promoters do not cause mutations by themselves, but they encourage the growth of mutated cells.
  • Progression: The tumor becomes more aggressive and malignant. Cells acquire additional mutations, gain the ability to invade surrounding tissues, and develop new survival traits.

Tumor Angiogenesis and Blood Supply

A tumor cannot grow larger than a few millimeters without its own dedicated blood supply. To solve this problem, cancer cells send out chemical signals, such as vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), to stimulate nearby blood vessels to sprout new branches.

This process of building new blood vessels is called angiogenesis. The newly formed blood vessels feed the tumor with oxygen and nutrients while also providing a convenient highway for cancer cells to escape into the rest of the body.

Mechanisms of Tumor Invasion and Metastasis

Metastasis is the spread of cancer cells from the primary site to distant parts of the body, and it is the main cause of cancer deaths. The journey follows several steps:

  1. Cancer cells break away from the main tumor mass.
  2. They break through surrounding tissue barriers using special protein-digesting enzymes.
  3. They enter local blood vessels or lymphatic channels, a step called intravasation.
  4. They travel through circulation, survive immune attacks, and exit the vessels at a new location, called extravasation.
  5. They set up a new colony of tumor growth in a distant organ.

Host Immune Response and Tumor Evasion

The human body has an immune system designed to hunt down and destroy abnormal cells. Specialized white blood cells, such as T-cells and natural killer cells, constantly patrol tissues for signs of danger.

However, cancer cells find ways to evade detection. Some tumors disguise themselves by displaying normal surface proteins, while others release chemical signals that actively suppress nearby immune cells. When the immune system fails to recognize and destroy these rogue cells, the cancer is free to grow unchecked.

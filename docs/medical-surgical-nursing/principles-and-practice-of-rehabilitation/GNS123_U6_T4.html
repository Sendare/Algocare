Specific Rehabilitation Situations

Introduction to Rehabilitation Nursing in Nigeria

Rehabilitation nursing focuses on helping patients regain their highest level of independence and function after an injury, illness, or surgery. In the Nigerian healthcare context, this specialty requires a unique blend of clinical skill, cultural awareness, and resourcefulness. Nurses often work with limited hospital supplies and must creatively adapt care plans to fit the patient's home environment. The ultimate goal is not just physical healing, but restoring the whole person so they can return to their family, work, and community with dignity.

Comprehensive Patient Assessment and Goal Setting

An effective rehabilitation plan begins with a thorough baseline assessment that looks beyond the primary medical diagnosis. Nurses evaluate physical strength, cognitive function, emotional readiness, and the home support system. Goal setting must be collaborative, involving the patient and their family. Goals should follow the SMART framework—Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound—to keep the patient motivated and track meaningful progress over time.

Rehabilitation of Patients with Stroke and Neurological Impairments

Stroke is a leading cause of long-term disability, often leaving patients with hemiparesis, difficulty swallowing, or speech deficits. Nursing care centers on protecting the affected limbs, preventing contractures, and supporting safe swallowing techniques.

  • Positioning: Change the patient's position every two hours to prevent pressure ulcers and properly align weak joints.
  • Swallowing Safety: Assess for coughing or pocketing of food before feeding, and keep suction equipment nearby.
  • Mobility Support: Encourage the patient to use their stronger side while safely guiding the weaker side during transfers.

Care and Management of Spinal Cord Injuries

Spinal cord injuries drastically alter a patient's sensory and motor functions, requiring intensive, specialized nursing intervention. The level of injury dictates the extent of paralysis and independence. Nurses must vigilantly monitor for autonomic dysreflexia—a dangerous, sudden spike in blood pressure triggered by bladder distension or bowel impaction. Meticulous skin care, bowel training regimens, and emotional encouragement are vital components of daily care.

Musculoskeletal Rehabilitation and Amputation Care

Musculoskeletal rehabilitation focuses on restoring movement after fractures, joint replacements, or limb amputations. For patients with amputations, proper residual limb care is essential to prepare for a prosthesis. Nurses must inspect the stump daily for signs of skin breakdown, swelling, or infection. Applying figure-eight elastic bandages helps shape the residual limb and control edema. Pain management must also address phantom limb sensation, acknowledging that the pain is real even though the limb is gone.

Managing Chronic Respiratory and Cardiac Conditions

Patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) or heart failure often experience severe fatigue that limits their daily activities. Cardiac and pulmonary rehabilitation uses monitored, gradual exercise programs to improve endurance and heart-lung efficiency. Nurses teach energy conservation techniques, such as pacing activities and combining tasks. Breathing exercises, including pursed-lip breathing, help patients manage shortness of breath during exertion.

Psychosocial Support and Coping Strategies

A sudden, life-changing disability often triggers grief, anxiety, and depression as patients mourn their former lifestyle. Nurses play a crucial role by providing empathetic listening, creating a safe space for emotional expression, and connecting patients with peer support groups. Encouraging small victories helps rebuild self-esteem. Families also experience emotional strain and burnout, meaning caregiver support is just as vital to the rehabilitation process as patient care.

Multidisciplinary Team Collaboration and Community Reintegration

Successful rehabilitation requires seamless teamwork among nurses, physicians, physical therapists, occupational therapists, speech pathologists, and social workers. Effective communication ensures everyone works toward the same patient goals. The final phase of rehabilitation prepares the patient to return home and engage with their local community. Nurses coordinate home visits, check for architectural accessibility, and educate local family members to ensure a smooth, safe transition from hospital to everyday life.

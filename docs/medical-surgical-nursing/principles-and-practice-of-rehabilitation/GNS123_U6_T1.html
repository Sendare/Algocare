Philosophy, Scope and Objectives of Rehabilitation

Introduction and Philosophy of Rehabilitation Nursing

Rehabilitation nursing is a specialized practice that helps individuals with disabilities and chronic illnesses attain and maintain maximum function. The core philosophy centers on viewing the patient as a whole person rather than just a medical diagnosis. Instead of focusing solely on curing an acute disease, rehabilitation aims to maximize independence and improve the overall quality of life. Nurses in this field partner with patients and their families to adapt to altered lifestyles, utilizing remaining strengths to overcome functional deficits.

Core Principles of Rehabilitation

Effective rehabilitation relies on several foundational principles guiding clinical practice:

  • Holistic approach: Addressing physical, emotional, social, and spiritual needs.
  • Active participation: Encouraging the patient to drive their own recovery goals.
  • Early intervention: Starting rehabilitation measures as soon as the medical condition stabilizes.
  • Continuancy of care: Ensuring smooth transitions from hospital settings back to the community.

Scope of Rehabilitation Practice in Nigeria

The scope of rehabilitation nursing in Nigeria spans diverse healthcare settings, from tertiary teaching hospitals to community-based health centers. Nurses encounter conditions such as stroke, spinal cord injuries from road traffic accidents, management of post-burn contractures, and chronic degenerative disorders. Practice in resource-constrained environments requires creativity, heavy reliance on family caregivers, and community advocacy to bridge gaps in formal infrastructural support.

Objectives and Goals of Patient Rehabilitation

Rehabilitation objectives aim to restore optimal function, prevent secondary complications, and facilitate social integration. Goals must be SMART: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound. A primary objective is preventing contractures, pressure ulcers, and deep vein thrombosis through proper positioning, early mobilization, and skin care. Ultimately, the goal is returning the individual to a productive, satisfying life within their family and community.

The Multidisciplinary Rehabilitation Team

Rehabilitation is rarely a solo effort. Successful recovery depends on seamless collaboration among various healthcare professionals. The patient and family sit at the center of this team, supported by nurses, physicians, physical therapists, occupational therapists, speech therapists, medical social workers, and clinical psychologists. Each discipline contributes unique expertise to address the multifaceted challenges of disability.

Levels of Prevention and Disability

Understanding disability requires differentiating between impairment, disability, and handicap. Prevention operates at three levels:

  1. Primary prevention: Preventing the onset of disease or injury (e.g., road safety education).
  2. Secondary prevention: Early diagnosis and prompt treatment to halt disease progression (e.g., screening for hypertension).
  3. Tertiary prevention: Minimizing the effects of an existing disability and restoring function (e.g., rehabilitation exercises).

Challenges in Rehabilitation Practice

Providing rehabilitation care in developing settings like Nigeria comes with distinct obstacles. Shortages of specialized personnel, inadequate equipment, and limited financial insurance coverage often hinder optimal patient recovery. Additionally, societal stigma surrounding physical and mental disabilities can impede community reintegration. Nurses must advocate for policy changes, utilize low-cost locally available assistive devices, and educate communities to foster inclusive environments.

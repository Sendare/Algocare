Roles of the Nurse in Peri-operative Care

Introduction to Peri-operative Nursing

Peri-operative nursing describes the care given to surgical patients before, during, and after their operations. This timeline is broken down into three distinct phases: the pre-operative, intra-operative, and post-operative phases. Nurses working in this specialty must blend sharp clinical judgment, technical skill, and compassionate communication. A core duty in this field is minimizing the inherent risks of surgical intervention while helping patients and their families navigate a stressful life event.

The Pre-operative Phase: Assessment and Preparation

Before any incision is made, the pre-operative nurse lays the groundwork for a safe surgery. This phase begins when the decision for surgery is made and ends when the patient is transferred to the operating table.

  • Baseline Assessment: Collecting a thorough health history, noting previous reactions to anesthesia, current medications, and known allergies.
  • Informed Consent Verification: Confirming that the surgeon has explained the procedure and that the patient has signed the consent form voluntarily.
  • Physical Preparation: Performing skin preps, administering pre-operative medications, and ensuring the patient remains nil per os (NPO) to prevent aspiration.
  • Psychological Support: Addressing anxiety and answering questions to calm the patient and their relatives.

Intra-operative Roles: Scrub and Circulating Nurses

Inside the operating theatre, nursing responsibilities split into two distinct roles to maintain sterility and patient safety. The scrub nurse works within the sterile field, handing instruments to the surgeon and keeping an accurate count of sponges, needles, and tools. Meanwhile, the circulating nurse remains unsterile, managing the overall environment, documenting events, managing specimen collection, and acting as the patient's relentless advocate while they are unconscious.

Post-operative Care and Monitoring

Once surgery concludes, the patient moves to the post-anesthesia care unit, often called the recovery room. The immediate post-operative nurse focuses on stabilizing vital signs, managing surgical pain, monitoring wound drainage, and ensuring the airway remains patent as the effects of anesthesia wear off. Close observation during this window allows nurses to catch early signs of hemorrhage, respiratory depression, or shock.

Patient Safety and the Surgical Time-Out

Preventing wrong-site, wrong-procedure, and wrong-person surgery is a non-negotiable duty for the surgical team. The surgical time-out is a mandatory pause performed right before the incision where all team members stop and verbally verify the patient's identity, the correct surgical site, and the exact procedure to be performed. Nurses play an active role in enforcing this checklist, stopping work if any discrepancy arises.

Documentation and Legal-Ethical Considerations

Every action a peri-operative nurse takes must be accurately recorded in real-time. Detailed documentation protects both the patient and the healthcare provider by providing a clear legal record of care. Ethical principles such as autonomy, beneficence, and fidelity guide nurses when patients express fear, refuse treatment, or face unexpected complications. Upholding patient confidentiality and securing valid consent remain foundational pillars of professional surgical nursing.

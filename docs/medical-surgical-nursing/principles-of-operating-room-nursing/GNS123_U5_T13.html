Personnel in the Operating Theatre

Introduction to the Operating Theatre Team

The operating theatre environment operates under strict safety protocols and demands absolute coordination among its occupants. Personnel are divided into two distinct functional groups based on their positioning relative to the sterile field. **Sterile team members** scrub, gown, and glove according to aseptic technique, granting them direct contact with the surgical site and sterile equipment. This group typically includes the surgeon, surgical assistants, the scrub nurse, and sometimes surgical technologists. **Non-sterile team members** remain outside the sterile perimeter, managing circulating duties, monitoring equipment, and administering anesthesia. They include the anaesthetist, circulating nurse, and support staff such as health orderlies. Maintaining a clear boundary between these zones prevents hospital-acquired surgical site infections and ensures seamless patient care during high-pressure surgical procedures.

The Surgeon and Surgical Assistants

The lead surgeon holds ultimate legal and medical responsibility for the performance of the surgical procedure and the overall welfare of the patient while in the theatre. Duties extend far beyond the active incision, encompassing pre-operative assessment, precise intraoperative technique, and post-operative management planning. Assisting the surgeon are qualified registrars, medical officers, or specialized surgical assistants. Their roles involve tissue retraction, maintaining hemostasis using electrocautery or ties, suctioning fluids to maintain visibility, and occasionally closing fascial layers or skin. Effective communication between the primary surgeon and assistants dictates the pace and safety profile of any operation.

The Anaesthetist

Patient safety during unconsciousness rests squarely on the shoulders of the anaesthetist or consultant anesthesiologist. This specialist physician assesses the patient's pre-existing medical conditions, selects the appropriate anesthetic technique—whether general, regional, or local—and manages physiological parameters throughout the procedure. Continuous monitoring of vital signs, fluid balance, blood loss, and airway patency consumes the anaesthetist's attention. In busy Nigerian tertiary hospitals, where specialist anaesthetists may oversee multiple theatres, nurse anaesthetists play a crucial frontline role in maintaining continuous patient surveillance.

The Scrub Nurse

Operating as a vital sterile team member, the scrub nurse works within the sterile field, managing instruments, sutures, and equipment. Responsibilities begin long before skin incision with rigorous hand scrubbing, gowning, and setting up the instrument table in a methodical arrangement. - **Instrument Delivery**: Anticipates the surgeon's needs, handing instruments swiftly and securely. - **Aseptic Maintenance**: Constantly monitors the sterile field for any contamination breaches. - **Surgical Counting**: Conducts meticulous counts of sponges, needles, and instruments alongside the circulating nurse before and after wound closure. - **Specimen Care**: Receives, labels, and preserves tissue specimens correctly according to hospital protocol.

The Circulating Nurse

Operating entirely outside the sterile boundary, the circulating nurse manages the broader environment of the operating room. This clinician acts as the patient's advocate while unconscious, managing documentation, coordinating specimen handling, and overseeing room safety. Duties include assisting with patient positioning to prevent nerve injury or pressure sores, connecting electrosurgical grounding pads, and regulating theatre traffic. When unexpected items or extra blood products are needed, the circulator procures them promptly without breaking sterile protocol. Their situational awareness ensures the non-stop logistical flow required for complex surgical interventions.

Support Staff and Nursing Students

No operating theatre functions smoothly without the crucial support provided by health orderlies, porters, and learners. Health orderlies maintain environmental sanitation, transport patients safely between wards and the theatre suite, and assist with heavy lifting or patient transfers. Nursing students observe and participate under close clinical supervision, learning aseptic technique, surgical anatomy, and professional teamwork. In Nigerian teaching hospitals, students must remain acutely aware of spatial boundaries, avoiding inadvertent contamination of sterile zones while building confidence in high-stakes clinical environments.

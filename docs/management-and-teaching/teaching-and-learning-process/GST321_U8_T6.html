Methods and Techniques of Teaching

Introduction to Teaching Methods and Techniques

Teaching methods and techniques form the bedrock of effective nursing education, bridging the gap between theoretical knowledge and competent clinical practice. In the context of nursing education in Nigeria, as regulated by the Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria (NMCN), educators must master various instructional approaches to prepare student nurses for complex healthcare environments. A teaching method refers to the overarching plan or broad strategy used by an educator to achieve specific learning objectives, whereas a teaching technique represents the specific activities, steps, or procedures employed during the delivery of a lesson. For instance, lecturing is a method, while using a think-pair-share activity within that lecture is a technique. Understanding this distinction helps nurse educators select appropriate strategies that cater to diverse learning styles, foster critical thinking, and promote psychomotor skill development. Furthermore, the healthcare landscape is dynamic, requiring nurses to possess not only cognitive competence but also strong interpersonal and problem-solving abilities. Consequently, educators must move beyond rote memorization and embrace pedagogical frameworks that encourage active student participation. By systematically applying these methods and techniques, nursing instructors can cultivate competent, compassionate, and safety-conscious professionals equipped to address local and global health challenges.

Teacher-Centred Teaching Methods

Teacher-centred teaching methods are traditional instructional approaches where the educator assumes the primary role of knowledge transmitter, while students act as passive receivers of information. Common examples of teacher-centred methods frequently utilized in Nigerian nursing schools include the traditional lecture method, dictation, and structured demonstrations. In a lecture setting, the instructor organizes the curriculum content, delivers the material through spoken presentation, and controls the pace and direction of the classroom interaction. This method is exceptionally useful when conveying vast amounts of foundational medical-surgical or anatomical information to a large cohort of nursing students within a limited timeframe. However, teacher-centred methods have notable limitations, particularly regarding the development of critical thinking, clinical judgment, and active problem-solving skills among students. Because learners are largely passive, opportunities for immediate feedback and clarification are restricted unless deliberately integrated by the educator. To maximize the effectiveness of teacher-centred methods in nursing education, instructors are encouraged to incorporate interactive elements—such as pausing for quick question-and-answer intervals, utilizing visual aids, or summarizing key points at regular intervals—to maintain student engagement and bridge the gap toward more participatory learning frameworks.

Student-Centred and Interactive Teaching Methods

Student-centred and interactive teaching methods place the learner at the active center of the educational process, transforming them from passive listeners into engaged participants. In nursing education, these approaches are vital for developing the analytical and decision-making skills required in clinical practice. Popular student-centred techniques include problem-based learning (PBL), case studies, small group discussions, role-playing, and brainstorming. For instance, when using a case study on management of postpartum hemorrhage, nursing students must analyze patient data, collaborate in groups, identify nursing diagnoses, and formulate evidence-based care plans. This active engagement promotes deeper cognitive processing, improves long-term retention of medical knowledge, and fosters essential teamwork and communication skills. Furthermore, interactive methods mirror the collaborative nature of multidisciplinary healthcare teams in Nigerian hospitals. Challenges to implementing student-centred methods often include large class sizes, limited physical classroom space, and time constraints. Nevertheless, creative nurse educators can adapt these techniques—such as breaking a large lecture hall into buzz groups—to ensure that even in resource-constrained environments, students actively construct their own understanding of nursing concepts and patient care protocols.

Clinical and Practical Teaching Techniques in Nursing

Clinical and practical teaching techniques are the cornerstone of professional nursing education, ensuring that students safely translate theoretical concepts into hands-on patient care. These techniques take place in simulation laboratories (skills labs) and real clinical environments such as wards, community health centers, and operating theatres. Key practical teaching techniques include clinical demonstrations, bedside nursing clinics, nursing rounds, post-conference discussions, and high-fidelity simulation. During a clinical demonstration, the educator follows the 'see one, do one, teach one' or structured stepwise model: explaining the procedure, demonstrating it at normal speed, demonstrating it slowly with rationale, and then supervising the student as they practice. Simulation labs provide a safe, error-tolerant environment where student nurses can practice high-risk procedures—such as catheterization, cardiopulmonary resuscitation, or intravenous cannulation—before touching actual patients. Pre- and post-clinical conferences allow students to reflect on their clinical experiences, bridge theory with practice, and receive constructive feedback from their clinical instructors. Effective clinical teaching requires patience, excellent role modeling, and strict adherence to patient safety and ethical standards, preparing student nurses to deliver competent nursing care in Nigerian healthcare facilities.

Modern Technological Aids and Digital Teaching Tools

Modern technological aids and digital teaching tools have revolutionized the delivery of nursing education, offering innovative ways to visualize complex biological processes and engage tech-savvy students. Integrating technology into the nursing curriculum aligns with global educational trends and enhances teaching efficiency. Common digital tools include PowerPoint presentations, interactive e-learning management systems (LMS) such as Moodle or Google Classroom, virtual anatomical models, educational videos, and mobile health applications. In nursing schools across Nigeria, digital learning platforms enable educators to upload lecture notes, share clinical guideline documents, conduct online quizzes, and facilitate asynchronous discussions outside of formal class hours. Virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR) tools allow students to explore interactive 3D human anatomy, simulate surgical procedures, or visualize pharmacological mechanisms of action at a cellular level. However, utilizing technological aids effectively requires overcoming infrastructural hurdles such as erratic power supply, limited internet connectivity, and the need for digital literacy training for both faculty and students. When implemented thoughtfully, digital tools complement traditional classroom and clinical instruction, offering flexible, accessible, and enriched learning experiences for aspiring professional nurses.

Factors Influencing the Selection of Teaching Methods

The selection of appropriate teaching methods is a critical pedagogical decision that directly influences learning outcomes in nursing education. A nurse educator cannot rely on a single method for all topics; instead, method selection must be guided by several interrelated factors. First, the nature of the learning objectives dictates the method: cognitive objectives (e.g., recalling anatomy) may suit lectures or readings, affective objectives (e.g., developing professional ethics) benefit from group discussions or role-play, while psychomotor objectives (e.g., performing wound dressing) require clinical demonstrations and simulation. Second, learner characteristics—such as prior knowledge level, learning styles, cultural background, and class size—must be carefully considered. For instance, large cohorts require adaptable classroom management techniques, whereas smaller groups permit intense interactive sessions. Third, available resources and institutional constraints, including time allocation, availability of audio-visual equipment, laboratory supplies, and clinical placement sites, play a decisive role. Finally, the educator's own competence, comfort level, and confidence with a particular teaching strategy influence its successful execution. By balancing these factors, nurse educators can craft a cohesive instructional plan that maximizes student comprehension, engagement, and clinical competency.

Evaluation and Assessment of Teaching Effectiveness

Evaluation and assessment of teaching effectiveness are essential processes that ensure educational quality, accountability, and continuous improvement in nursing training programs. Assessment focuses on measuring student learning outcomes to determine if educational objectives have been met, whereas evaluation encompasses a broader judgment of the entire teaching and learning process, including curriculum design, instructional methods, and educator performance. In nursing schools, evaluation methods include formative assessments (such as quizzes, clinical checklists, and muddiest point reflections during a course) and summative assessments (such as end-of-semester examinations, objective structured clinical examinations (OSCEs), and final clinical practica). Additionally, student feedback questionnaires, peer evaluations of teaching, and self-reflection by the educator provide valuable data on instructional strengths and weaknesses. By systematically analyzing examination pass rates, clinical performance evaluations, and student feedback, nurse educators can identify pedagogical gaps, refine their teaching techniques, and adapt their instructional strategies to better support future generations of nursing students in meeting professional standards.

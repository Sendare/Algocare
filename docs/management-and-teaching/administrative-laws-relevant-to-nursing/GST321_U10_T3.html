Public Accountability, Checks and Balances

Introduction to Public Accountability in Nursing Administration

Public accountability in nursing administration means that nurse leaders answer for their actions, use of resources, and the quality of care provided in public health facilities. Because these institutions rely on taxpayers' money and government funding, administrators carry a heavy duty to show transparency. Accountability is not just about avoiding theft; it is about making sure every patient receives safe, dignified care and that human and material resources are never wasted. When a nurse manager runs a ward or a hospital, they act as a trustee of the public trust. If materials meant for patient care disappear or staff members neglect their duties without consequence, the public trust is broken. Understanding this concept forms the bedrock of good healthcare governance in Nigeria.

Legal Frameworks Governing Healthcare in Nigeria

Healthcare practice and administration in Nigeria are guided by specific laws and regulatory bodies. The Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria (NMCN) Act empowers the council to regulate education, set professional standards, and discipline erring practitioners. At the institutional level, public health facilities operate under civil service rules, financial regulations, and health acts passed by the legislature. Nurse leaders must operate strictly within these legal boundaries. Ignorance of the law does not excuse administrative failure. Complying with these frameworks protects both the patients and the institution from legal liability and ensures fair treatment for all healthcare workers.

The Principle of Checks and Balances in Public Health Institutions

Checks and balances prevent any single individual in a healthcare facility from holding absolute power. In a hospital setting, power is distributed so that administrative decisions, financial disbursements, and clinical policies are reviewed by different departments or committees. For example, the person who orders medical supplies is usually not the same person who approves the payment or receives the items into the store. This separation of duties reduces the risk of fraud and abuse. Nurse leaders must respect these internal control structures rather than trying to bypass them for quick results.

Financial Integrity, Budgeting, and Resource Allocation

Financial integrity requires nurse leaders to be honest, transparent, and accurate when handling institutional funds. Budgeting is the process of planning how expected revenue will be spent over a specific period, usually a fiscal year. In public hospitals, resources are rarely enough to meet every demand, making fair resource allocation critical. Nurse managers must prioritize patient safety needs—such as oxygen supplies, basic medications, and functional equipment—over less urgent administrative desires. Mismanaging public funds deprives vulnerable patients of essential care and violates financial regulations.

Auditing, Monitoring, and Compliance Mechanisms

  • Internal Audits: Routine financial and operational reviews conducted by staff within the institution to catch errors early.
  • External Audits: Independent reviews conducted by government bodies, such as the Auditor-General's office, to ensure lawful spending.
  • Monitoring: Ongoing daily oversight of activities to make sure clinical and administrative guidelines are followed.
  • Compliance: Adhering strictly to established laws, hospital policies, and professional codes of conduct.

These mechanisms act as safety nets. When audits are conducted regularly, staff members remain vigilant, and financial leakages are quickly identified and corrected.

Ethical Responsibilities and Professional Conduct

Nurse leaders carry heavy ethical duties that go beyond basic legal compliance. Principles like beneficence (doing good), non-maleficence (doing no harm), autonomy (respecting patient choices), and justice (fairness in care distribution) must guide daily decisions. Professional conduct requires nurses to maintain patient confidentiality, avoid conflicts of interest, and treat every patient with equal respect regardless of social status or wealth. When a leader models high ethical standards, junior staff members are inspired to follow suit, creating a culture of integrity across the entire healthcare team.

Managing Maladministration and Public Grievances

Maladministration occurs when public institutions fail to act properly, show unreasonable delay, or display unfairness in delivering services. In healthcare, this can look like patients being asked to pay illegal unofficial fees, severe staff rudeness, or abandoned projects. Public grievances must be handled through clear, accessible complaints channels rather than being swept under the rug. Nurse administrators must investigate complaints fairly, correct genuine mistakes, and implement changes to prevent recurrence. Ignoring public feedback breeds public distrust and destroys the credibility of the healthcare system.

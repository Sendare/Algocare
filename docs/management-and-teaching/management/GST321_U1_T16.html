Authority

Definition and Concept of Authority in Nursing Management

Authority is the formal, legitimate right of a manager to direct others, make decisions, and allocate resources to achieve organizational goals. In nursing management, this concept stems directly from an individual's official position within the hospital or clinic hierarchy rather than personal charm or popularity. When a nurse manager is appointed, the institution grants them the mandate to coordinate patient care units, set duty rosters, and enforce institutional policies. Without this recognized backing, nursing leaders would struggle to maintain the structure required for safe patient outcomes. Authority flows downward through the organizational chart, creating a clear chain of command that staff nurses rely on for operational guidance. Recognizing this official backing helps both new and experienced clinicians understand who holds ultimate accountability for unit-wide functions.

Sources and Types of Authority in Healthcare Organizations

Authority in healthcare does not come from just one place. Different types shape how nurses interact with managers, doctors, and support staff:

  • Formal Authority: Originates from the top of the organization and flows downward to subordinate positions, established by job descriptions and organizational charts.
  • Expert Authority: Earned through specialized knowledge, clinical skill, and advanced training. A wound care nurse specialist holds this type, influencing care plans because of recognized expertise.
  • Charismatic Authority: Rooted in personal appeal, strong interpersonal skills, and the ability to inspire others naturally without relying purely on a title.
  • Traditional Authority: Based on long-standing customs, habits, and accepted ways of doing things within a particular culture or institution.

Authority Versus Power and Responsibility

Although people often use authority, power, and responsibility interchangeably, they represent distinct concepts in nursing administration. Authority is the legitimate right to command. Power is the broader ability to influence others and control resources, which can exist independently of an official title. For example, a veteran auxiliary nurse might hold informal power over shift dynamics without having formal managerial authority.

Responsibility is the obligation to perform assigned tasks and achieve expected results. A fundamental rule in management states that authority and responsibility must be equal. If a head nurse is given the responsibility to reduce medication errors on the ward, they must also be given the authority to modify workflows or retrain staff. Giving responsibility without matching authority leads directly to frustration and burnout.

Delegation of Authority in Nursing Practice

Delegation is the transfer of responsibility for the performance of a task from one individual to another while retaining ultimate accountability for the outcome. Registered nurses use delegation to manage heavy workloads and ensure efficient patient care. When delegating, the nurse must consider the five rights: right task, right circumstances, right person, right direction, and right supervision.

A common misconception is that delegating a task removes the nurse's accountability. Even when a nursing assistant performs a vital sign check, the registered nurse remains responsible for analyzing those findings and deciding on subsequent clinical actions. Proper delegation requires clear communication, verifying the subordinate's competence, and remaining accessible for guidance throughout the shift.

Barriers to Effective Authority and Delegation

Several obstacles can hinder the smooth exercise of authority and delegation in clinical settings. On the manager's side, reluctance to let go of control—often driven by perfectionism or the belief that 'doing it myself is faster'—prevents effective delegation. Some supervisors fear losing authority if subordinates perform tasks too well.

On the subordinate's side, lack of self-confidence, fear of failure, or absence of proper training can cause resistance to accepting new responsibilities. Organizational bottlenecks, such as rigid bureaucratic rules and unclear job descriptions, further confuse who has the right to make specific decisions. Identifying these barriers is the first step toward creating a collaborative ward environment where tasks are shared safely and efficiently.

Maintaining Professional Accountability and Control

Authority cannot function without robust accountability and institutional control. Professional accountability means answering for one's own actions and accepting the consequences, whether positive or negative. Nurses demonstrate accountability by adhering to scope of practice laws, maintaining licensure, and following evidence-based guidelines.

Control mechanisms, such as clinical audits, peer reviews, and incident reporting systems, ensure that authority is not abused. When nursing leaders monitor ward performance and correct deviations from standard protocols, they exercise legitimate control. This oversight protects patients from substandard care and upholds the integrity of the nursing profession across all healthcare delivery points.

Application of Authority in Nigerian Healthcare Settings

Applying authority in Nigerian healthcare environments requires navigating a unique mix of bureaucratic structures, cultural dynamics, and resource constraints. Ward managers and nursing officers must balance formal institutional directives with traditional respect for seniority and community structures. Power dynamics between different healthcare cadres—such as physicians, pharmacists, and nurses—frequently influence how authority is exercised on the ward.

Resource shortages, including erratic electricity, limited medical supplies, and understaffing, often force Nigerian nurse leaders to use adaptive authority. Rather than relying solely on rigid rulebooks, successful managers build consensus, leverage expert knowledge, and mentor junior staff to maintain safe care delivery despite challenging infrastructural conditions.

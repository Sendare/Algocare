Classification of Foods

Introduction to Food and Nutrition in Nursing

Food provides the chemical energy and building blocks required to sustain life, maintain tissue integrity, and promote recovery from illness. In clinical practice, understanding what patients consume bridges the gap between medical intervention and physiological healing. Nutrients are chemical substances obtained from food that perform vital bodily functions, categorised broadly into those needed in large amounts and those required in trace quantities. Malnutrition, whether undernutrition or overnutrition, directly impairs immune competence, delays wound healing, and lengthens hospital stays. Assessing dietary intake is therefore as fundamental to holistic nursing assessment as monitoring vital signs.

Classification Based on Origin: Plant and Animal Sources

Dietary components are sourced from two primary kingdoms: plant and animal. Plant-based foods include grains, legumes, fruits, and vegetables, supplying complex carbohydrates, dietary fibre, and various phytochemicals. Animal-based foods comprise meat, fish, poultry, eggs, and dairy, providing high biological value proteins that contain all essential amino acids. In many Nigerian communities, dietary staples blend these sources—such as combining maize or beans with fish or crayfish—to achieve a complete amino acid profile despite economic or cultural constraints.

Classification Based on Functions: Energy-Giving, Body-Building, and Protective Foods

Grouping foods by their physiological function helps simplify nutritional education for patients and community groups.

  • Energy-Giving Foods: Cereals, roots, tubers, sugars, and fats supply the kilocalories needed for basal metabolism and physical activity.
  • Body-Building Foods: Protein-rich items like meat, dairy, fish, and legumes construct new tissues and repair damaged cells.
  • Protective Foods: Fruits and vegetables deliver vitamins and minerals that regulate metabolic processes and defend against infections.

Classification Based on Chemical Composition: Macronutrients and Micronutrients

Chemical classification divides nutrients by the absolute quantities required by the human body.

CategoryNutrient TypesPrimary RoleExamples
MacronutrientsCarbohydrates, Proteins, FatsProvide structure and energyYam, beans, palm oil
MicronutrientsVitamins, MineralsRegulate metabolic reactionsCitrus fruits, leafy greens

Carbohydrates, proteins, and fats are measured in grams, whereas vitamins and minerals are measured in milligrams or micrograms. Deficiencies in either category produce distinct clinical syndromes, ranging from severe caloric wasting to specific micronutrient deficiency diseases like scurvy or anaemia.

Perishable vs. Non-Perishable Foods in Dietary Management

Food preservation and storage characteristics directly impact food safety and availability in hospital and home settings. Perishable foods—such as fresh milk, raw meat, fish, and leafy vegetables—contain high moisture content and spoil rapidly through microbial proliferation if unfrigerated. Non-perishable items like dried grains, legumes, and canned goods have low moisture content, allowing prolonged storage without rapid spoilage. In resource-limited environments lacking continuous electricity, nurses must educate patients on safe food handling, sun-drying, or fermentation techniques to prevent foodborne illnesses.

Clinical Application: Assessing Patient Diets Using Food Classifications

Translating nutritional science into bedside care involves evaluating what patients eat against their physiological demands. A comprehensive nutritional screening identifies deficits before clinical signs of malnutrition become severe. Nurses examine cultural food preferences, financial accessibility of food groups, and metabolic restrictions imposed by disease states such as diabetes or renal failure. When a patient presents with generalized edema and sparse, depigmented hair, recognizing the lack of body-building foods allows the nurse to collaborate with dietitians in formulating an appropriate rehabilitation diet.

Prevention of Occupational Health Hazards

Introduction to Occupational Health and Safety

Occupational health and safety focuses on protecting workers from physical, chemical, biological, and psychosocial hazards in their work environments. Healthcare workers face unique daily risks, including needlestick injuries, exposure to infectious diseases, back strain from lifting patients, and high levels of work-related stress. Prevention starts by recognizing that work should not cause injury or illness. A safe workplace protects both staff and patients, ensuring continuous and reliable care delivery across all healthcare settings.

The Hierarchy of Controls

The hierarchy of controls is a framework used in industry to minimize or eliminate exposure to hazards. Arranged from most effective to least effective, the levels are:

  1. Elimination: Physically removing the hazard entirely from the workplace.
  2. Substitution: Replacing the hazardous material or process with a safer alternative.
  3. Engineering Controls: Isolating workers from the hazard using physical changes to the environment.
  4. Administrative Controls: Changing the way people work through policies, training, and scheduling.
  5. Personal Protective Equipment (PPE): Wearing specialized gear as the last line of defense.

Lower levels like PPE rely heavily on human behavior and fail more easily than structural changes.

Engineering and Administrative Controls

Engineering controls involve structural or equipment changes that isolate workers from hazards without relying on their daily choices. Examples include safety-engineered syringes with retractable needles, sharps disposal containers mounted at eye level, and proper negative pressure rooms for airborne isolation.

Administrative controls adjust work practices and schedules to reduce exposure time. These include rotating staff away from high-risk areas, implementing strict no-recap policies for needles, and providing adequate rest breaks to combat fatigue-related errors.

Selection and Proper Use of Personal Protective Equipment

Personal protective equipment acts as the final barrier between a healthcare worker and infectious or chemical hazards. Common items include gloves, face masks, eye shields, gowns, and respirators like N95 masks. Correct usage is just as important as selection. Donning and doffing must follow strict sequences to prevent self-contamination. A common error is touching the outer surface of a used gown or mask during removal, which transfers pathogens directly to the skin or scrubs.

Ergonomics and Injury Prevention

Ergonomics is the science of fitting the workplace and tools to the worker's body. In nursing, musculoskeletal disorders—especially lower back injuries—are widespread due to frequent patient transfers, repositioning, and heavy lifting. Prevention requires using mechanical lifting devices, adjusting bed heights, and practicing proper body mechanics. Nurses should avoid twisting the spine while lifting and always seek assistance from colleagues for heavy or dependent patients.

Infection Control and Biological Hazards

Biological hazards include bacteria, viruses, fungi, and parasites that can cause infections in staff. Standard precautions must be applied to every patient interaction, treating all blood and body fluids as potentially infectious. Hand hygiene remains the single most effective method to stop cross-contamination. Vaccination programs, such as hepatitis B and tetanus immunizations, provide active protection against specific biological threats encountered in clinical practice.

Workplace Health Surveillance and Screening

Health surveillance involves ongoing monitoring of workers to detect early signs of work-related illness or injury. Pre-employment medical examinations establish a baseline health status and check for fitness to perform specific duties. Periodic screenings—such as annual tuberculosis skin tests or chest X-rays, titer checks for immunity, and hearing tests for staff in noisy environments—help catch health issues early before they become chronic problems.

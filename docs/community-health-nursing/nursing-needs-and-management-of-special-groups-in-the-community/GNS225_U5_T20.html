Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs)

Introduction to Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs)

Internally displaced persons, commonly known as IDPs, are individuals or groups who have been forced to flee their homes or places of habitual residence—typically as a result of armed conflict, generalized violence, human rights violations, or natural disasters—and who have not crossed an internationally recognized state border. Unlike refugees, IDPs remain within the legal boundaries of their own country and technically retain the protection of their national government, even when that government may be a contributing factor to their displacement. In Nigeria, internal displacement is a major humanitarian crisis driven by prolonged insurgency in the northeast, violent communal clashes, banditry, and severe environmental degradation in the Niger Delta and northern agricultural belts. Because they remain within national borders, IDPs often fall into a legal and administrative gray area regarding direct international aid protection, relying heavily on local emergency agencies and community goodwill. Recognizing the distinct status of IDPs helps community health nurses tailor interventions that respect national rights while addressing immediate survival needs.

Causes and Drivers of Internal Displacement in Nigeria

Understanding why populations flee is essential for anticipating public health demands and planning community nursing interventions. In Nigeria, the drivers of displacement are complex, intersecting, and deeply rooted in regional socio-economic and political dynamics. - **Armed Insurgency**: Ongoing extremist violence in the Northeast zone has uprooted millions, destroying infrastructure and disrupting local agrarian economies. - **Communal and Farmer-Herder Clashes**: Competition over dwindling arable land and water resources in the Middle Belt frequently triggers violent confrontations, forcing entire villages to abandon their settlements. - **Banditry and Kidnapping**: Widespread criminal syndicates in the Northwest terrorize rural communities, looting property and forcing mass abandonment of agrarian lands. - **Environmental Degradation and Flooding**: Annual torrential rainfall causes devastating riverine and flash floods across various states, destroying homes, contaminating local water sources, and displacing vulnerable rural populations.

Vulnerabilities and Special Health Needs of IDPs

Displacement strips individuals of their normal support systems, exposing them to profound physical, emotional, and social vulnerabilities. While entire camp populations suffer, certain demographic groups experience magnified health risks that demand specialized nursing attention. - **Women and Girls**: Face heightened risks of gender-based violence, sexual exploitation, and lack of menstrual hygiene management resources. - **Children Under Five**: Highly susceptible to acute malnutrition, vaccine-preventable childhood illnesses, and severe dehydration from diarrheal diseases. - **Pregnant and Lactating Mothers**: Experience limited access to antenatal care, skilled birth attendants, and safe delivery environments, increasing maternal mortality rates. - **Older Adults and Persons with Disabilities**: Often abandoned during chaotic flights, lacking necessary mobility aids, chronic disease medications, and specialized nutritional support.

Public Health Challenges in IDP Camps

The physical environment of a typical IDP camp creates a breeding ground for communicable diseases and public health emergencies. Overcrowding, inadequate shelter, compromised water sanitation, and broken waste management systems combine to accelerate disease transmission. Waterborne diseases such as cholera, typhoid, and acute gastroenteritis spread rapidly when safe drinking water is scarce and open defecation occurs near living quarters. Vector-borne diseases, particularly malaria, thrive due to poor drainage, sleeping in poorly protected open shelters, and the lack of insecticide-treated bed nets. Furthermore, overcrowded living conditions in tents or makeshift structures facilitate the airborne transmission of respiratory infections like measles, meningitis, and tuberculosis. Malnutrition acts as a silent multiplier, synergizing with recurrent infections to deepen the cycle of disease and mortality across the camp.

Community Health Nursing Assessment for IDPs

Effective care in displacement settings begins with a comprehensive, systematic community health assessment. Nurses cannot rely on standard hospital-based diagnostic routines; instead, they must engage directly within the camp environment to map health profiles, environmental hazards, and available resources. The assessment process encompasses epidemiological surveillance to track disease outbreaks, nutritional screenings using Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC) tapes for children, and environmental audits of water points and latrine distance from shelters. Nurses also evaluate local socio-cultural dynamics, identifying informal camp leaders, traditional birth attendants, and youth groups who can facilitate trust and communication. Documenting these findings accurately ensures that scarce medical supplies and personnel are deployed where they are most urgently needed.

Primary Health Care and Management Strategies in Camps

Delivering healthcare in IDP settlements requires shifting from curative hospital paradigms to robust Primary Health Care (PHC) principles, focusing on accessibility, community participation, and preventive services. Clinic operations in camps are typically established using mobile health teams, tent clinics, or rehabilitated local structures. Essential interventions include: - **Immunization Campaigns**: Rapid catch-up vaccinations for measles, polio, and tetanus to prevent explosive epidemic outbreaks among mobile populations. - **Syndromic Case Management**: Standardized treatment protocols for malaria, acute respiratory infections, and diarrheal dehydration. - **Maternal and Child Health Services**: Antenatal monitoring, emergency contraception, safe delivery kits, and growth monitoring. - **Referral Pathways**: Establishing reliable communication and transport mechanisms to transfer severe surgical or complicated medical emergencies to secondary-tier regional hospitals.

Psychosocial Support and Mental Health Care for Displaced Populations

The psychological toll of displacement is often as devastating as physical injuries. Witnessing violence, losing loved ones, surviving traumatic attacks, and enduring the daily stress of uncertainty leave deep emotional scars. Nurses must recognize and address conditions such as acute stress reaction, depression, anxiety disorders, and post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD). Psychosocial support in camps follows a tiered approach, ranging from basic community-level activities that restore social cohesion and daily routines to specialized counseling for severe trauma. Community health nurses play a vital role in providing psychological first aid (PFA), listening actively without judgment, normalizing emotional reactions, and connecting severely distressed individuals to trained mental health professionals. Creating safe recreational spaces for children also helps restore a sense of normalcy and emotional resilience in young survivors.

Multi-Sectoral Collaboration and Policy Frameworks for IDP Care

Addressing the multifaceted needs of internally displaced persons extends far beyond the capacity of the healthcare sector alone. Successful management requires robust multi-sectoral collaboration involving government agencies, non-governmental organizations (NGOs), international bodies, and local community structures. In Nigeria, institutions like the National Emergency Management Agency (NEMA) and State Emergency Management Agencies (SEMAs) coordinate disaster response alongside health ministries. Community health nurses must coordinate closely with water and sanitation (WASH) engineers, food security coordinators, camp managers, and security personnel. Furthermore, understanding legal frameworks such as the Kampala Convention—the African Union Convention for the Protection and Assistance of Internally Displaced Persons—empowers nurses to advocate for the rights, safety, and humane treatment of displaced populations.

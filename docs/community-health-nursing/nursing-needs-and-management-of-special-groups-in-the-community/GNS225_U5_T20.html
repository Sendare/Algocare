Internally Displaced Persons (IDPs)

Understanding Internally Displaced Persons and Causes in Nigeria

An internally displaced person (IDP) is someone forced to flee their home who has not crossed an internationally recognized state border. Unlike refugees, IDPs remain under the legal protection of their own government, even when that government struggles to provide safety. In Nigeria, internal displacement is a major humanitarian concern affecting millions, particularly in the northern regions.

  • Insurgency and Armed Conflict: Groups like Boko Haram and banditry create violent environments that force entire communities to abandon their homes overnight.
  • Communal and Farmer-Herder Clashes: Competition over land and water resources leads to violent disputes, displacing rural farming populations.
  • Natural Disasters: Severe seasonal flooding, especially along the River Niger and River Benue, washes away homes and farmlands, rendering families temporarily homeless.

Public Health Challenges and Vulnerabilities in IDP Camps

Life inside overcrowded IDP camps creates severe health risks. When thousands of displaced individuals share limited sanitation facilities, clean water, and shelter, communicable diseases spread rapidly.

Malnutrition is a constant threat, especially among children under five and pregnant women, due to sudden food insecurity and the disruption of agricultural livelihoods. Poor waste management and open drainage systems attract vectors like mosquitoes, leading to high rates of malaria. Furthermore, vulnerable groups such as women, children, and the elderly face heightened risks of physical exhaustion, psychological trauma, and exploitation.

Community Health Nursing Assessment in Displaced Settings

Conducting a health assessment in an IDP camp requires patience, cultural sensitivity, and structured observation. Nurses must look beyond individual clinical presentations to evaluate environmental determinants of health.

The assessment process includes evaluating water sources for contamination, inspecting waste disposal methods, and tallying vulnerable populations like pregnant women, infants, and chronically ill patients. Nurses also assess nutritional status using mid-upper arm circumference (MUAC) tapes for young children. Building trust with camp leaders is essential to gather accurate health data and encourage community participation.

Primary Health Care and Disease Prevention Strategies

Primary health care (PHC) forms the backbone of medical intervention in displacement settings. Clinics set up in camps must prioritize preventive services alongside basic curative care.

Immunization campaigns are urgent priorities to prevent outbreaks of vaccine-preventable diseases like measles, polio, and tetanus. Routine clinics must stock essential medicines for common ailments such as malaria, respiratory infections, and minor injuries. Health education sessions conducted in local languages empower residents to practice good hand hygiene, treat drinking water safely, and report early signs of infectious outbreaks to camp clinic staff.

Psychosocial Support and Mental Health Care

Displaced persons frequently experience profound psychological trauma. Witnessing violence, losing loved ones, and abandoning lifelong property leaves deep emotional scars.

Community health nurses play a vital role in identifying signs of post-traumatic stress, severe anxiety, and depression. Mental health care in camps relies heavily on psychological first aid (PFA) and community support groups rather than relying solely on psychiatric medications. Creating safe spaces for children to play and talking openly about emotional distress helps normalize grief and promotes community resilience.

Multi-Sectoral Collaboration and Advocacy

No single nursing agency or healthcare team can manage the complex needs of an IDP camp alone. Effective care requires active multi-sectoral collaboration involving government bodies, non-governmental organizations (NGOs), security agencies, and community leaders.

Nurses act as strong patient advocates, communicating the urgent needs of vulnerable groups to camp coordinators and humanitarian partners. By coordinating with water and sanitation (WASH) engineers, food distribution teams, and shelter providers, nurses help ensure that environmental conditions support overall health and disease prevention.

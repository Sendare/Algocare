Legal Aspects of Female Genital Mutilation

Understanding Female Genital Mutilation and Human Rights

Female Genital Mutilation encompasses all procedures involving partial or total removal of the external female genitalia or other injury to the female genital organs for non-medical reasons. International human rights law firmly classifies this practice as a violation of the fundamental rights of women and girls. These violations include the right to health, security, and physical integrity, as well as the right to be free from torture and cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment. Furthermore, when the procedure is performed on minors, it violates their rights as children to grow up safe from harm and discrimination. Recognizing FGM as a human rights abuse shifts the conversation away from viewing it as a mere harmless cultural tradition, establishing it instead as a punishable act of violence that demands a strong legal response.

International Conventions and Global Legal Frameworks

Various international and regional treaties explicitly protect women and children from harmful traditional practices. Major global instruments, such as the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women and the Convention on the Rights of the Child, place binding obligations on signatory nations to take all appropriate measures to modify or abolish customs based on the idea of the inferiority of either sex. At the regional level, the Maputo Protocol—the Protocol to the African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights on the Rights of Women in Africa—explicitly calls for the prohibition and condemnation of FGM in all its forms. These frameworks provide the foundational legal backing that compels individual African nations to enact strict domestic legislation against the practice.

National Legislation and the VAPP Act in Nigeria

At the federal level in Nigeria, the Violence Against Persons Prohibition Act enacted in 2015 represents a major milestone in criminalizing harmful practices. The VAPP Act explicitly prohibits female genital mutilation, making it a serious criminal offense punishable by imprisonment, fines, or both. The law targets not only the traditional practitioners who perform the cutting but also anyone who incites, aids, abets, or attempts to commit the offense. By establishing federal jurisdiction, the VAPP Act provides a strong legal framework that supersedes local customary defenses, sending a clear message that bodily mutilation of women and girls will face severe legal consequences.

State-Level Laws and Enforcement Challenges

Although federal legislation exists, Nigeria operates under a federal system where criminal law implementation often falls under state jurisdiction. Many individual states have domesticated the VAPP Act or passed their own specific anti-FGM edicts. However, translating these written laws into effective courtroom prosecutions remains a major hurdle.

  • Cultural Secrecy: The practice is often hidden within family compounds, making it difficult for law enforcement agents to detect.
  • Complicity of Victims: Victims and families frequently refuse to testify due to deep-rooted cultural beliefs and fear of community ostracization.
  • Resource Deficits: Local police and judiciary often lack adequate training, funding, and logistical support to investigate and prosecute offenders effectively.

Legal Responsibilities and Professional Liability for Healthcare Workers

A common misconception is that FGM is safer if performed by trained medical personnel in a sterile clinical environment. This practice, known as medicalization, is strictly illegal and violates professional ethics. Nursing and medical regulatory bodies, alongside national laws, hold healthcare professionals to strict standards of accountability. Any nurse, midwife, or doctor who performs, assists with, or facilitates FGM—regardless of whether it involves a lesser degree of cutting—faces severe professional consequences. These include immediate revocation of their practicing license, heavy financial fines, and lengthy prison sentences under criminal law. Healthcare workers have a legal duty to protect patients from harm, meaning their involvement in any form of genital cutting is a direct betrayal of their professional oath.

Ethical Dilemmas and Mandatory Reporting

Nurses frequently encounter ethical dilemmas when balancing patient confidentiality with the legal duty to protect vulnerable minors from harm. While professional codes emphasize maintaining patient trust and privacy, the laws against child abuse and FGM override standard confidentiality rules when a minor is in imminent danger. When a nurse suspects or discovers that a child is scheduled to undergo FGM during a family trip or local ceremony, they have a professional and legal obligation to act. This requires reporting the threat to hospital social services, child protection agencies, or law enforcement authorities. Navigating this tension demands courage and a clear understanding that protecting a child's life and physical integrity supersedes family privacy.

Community Health Nursing Strategies for Legal Advocacy

Community health nurses play a frontline role in eradicating FGM through grassroots advocacy, education, and legal empowerment. Rather than relying solely on punitive measures, nurses work directly with community and religious leaders to shift harmful social norms from within. They conduct health talks that clearly explain both the severe medical complications and the legal consequences of the practice under national laws. Furthermore, nurses collaborate with local women's groups, school teachers, and youth organizations to create safe spaces for dialogue and support networks for girls at risk. By empowering communities with knowledge and legal facts, nurses help dismantle the cultural justifications that sustain FGM.

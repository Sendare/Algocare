Delivery Kit

Introduction and Purpose of the Clean Delivery Kit

A clean delivery kit is a portable, pre-packaged bundle of basic supplies designed to promote safe childbirth, particularly in resource-limited or community settings where hospital access is delayed or unavailable. Childbirth in unhygienic environments exposes both mother and newborn to severe infections, notably tetanus and sepsis. The primary objective of providing these kits is to reduce maternal and neonatal morbidity and mortality by standardizing the conditions of delivery and enforcing the critical six cleans: clean hands, clean surface, clean perineum, clean cord-cutting instrument, clean cord tie, and clean cloth for the baby.

Standard Components and Contents of the Kit

While contents can vary slightly depending on local health guidelines, a standard delivery kit contains specific items tailored to safeguard the birthing process.

  • Soap: Used by the birth attendant to thoroughly wash hands before touching the mother or baby.
  • Plastic sheet: Spread out to create a clean, impermeable surface for the laboring mother to lie on, preventing contact with contaminated dirt or floors.
  • Cord tie or thread: Used to securely ligate the umbilical cord before it is cut.
  • Blade or razor: A sterile cutting instrument used to sever the umbilical cord cleanly.
  • Cloth or towel: Clean fabric used to dry and wrap the newborn immediately to prevent heat loss.

Preparation and Inspection Before Use

Before opening or utilizing any delivery kit, the birth attendant must conduct a thorough inspection. Checking the expiration date is critical, as sterilized items lose their sterility over time if packaging degrades. The outer wrapper must be inspected for tears, punctures, or dampness, any of which compromises the sterile barrier inside. The workspace must be organized in advance so that clean items can be accessed smoothly without breaking sterile technique during the urgency of active labor.

Step-by-Step Utilization During Childbirth

Managing childbirth with a kit requires a calm, methodical approach that respects hygiene protocols at every stage.

  1. Hand Hygiene: Wash hands thoroughly using the soap and clean water provided in the kit.
  2. Surface Preparation: Lay out the plastic sheet on a flat, dry area where the mother will deliver.
  3. Delivery Support: Assist the mother through pushing while maintaining clean contact points.
  4. Cord Management: Once the baby is delivered and crying, wait for cord pulsation to slow, then tie the cord securely in two places and cut cleanly between the ties using the provided blade.
  5. Drying and Warming: Immediately wrap the baby in the clean cloth to prevent hypothermia.

Aseptic Technique and Infection Prevention

Aseptic technique is the backbone of safe delivery kit utilization. Every touch point carries the risk of introducing pathogens into the reproductive tract or the freshly cut umbilical stump, which can lead to postpartum metritis or neonatal tetanus. Birth attendants must avoid touching non-sterile surfaces after washing their hands. If a sterile component drops onto the floor or touches an unwashed area, it must be discarded immediately rather than reused.

Immediate Postpartum Care and Newborn Management

After the delivery of the placenta, attention shifts immediately to stabilizing both mother and infant. The birth attendant must monitor the mother for excessive vaginal bleeding, gently massaging the uterus to encourage contraction. For the newborn, the Apgar score assessment begins, checking color, heart rate, reflex irritability, muscle tone, and respiration. Skin-to-skin contact should be encouraged if the infant is stable, promoting bonding and stable body temperature.

Maintenance, Replenishment, and Safe Disposal

Proper management of a delivery kit does not end when the birth is complete. Used sharps, such as razor blades, must be placed into a puncture-proof container to prevent needle-stick or sharp injuries to healthcare workers or family members. Disposable wrappings and soiled materials should be bagged and disposed of according to local biomedical waste guidelines. Community health workers must regularly audit their supply stock, replacing expired components and restocking kits to ensure readiness for the next emergency call.

Home Visiting Kit

Introduction to the Home Visiting Kit in Community Health Nursing

The public health nurse's bag is an essential tool for delivering safe care outside the hospital environment. In Nigerian community health settings, visiting clients in their homes bridges the gap between institutional healthcare and grassroots populations. The home visiting kit contains basic equipment needed to assess, diagnose minor conditions, and provide treatments within a domestic space.

Using this kit requires strict adherence to institutional standards to prevent cross-contamination between households. Because domestic environments lack the controlled sterility of hospital wards, the kit itself acts as a mobile clean zone. Mastery of its contents and mechanics forms a cornerstone of practical community nursing assessments.

Standard Contents and Equipment of a Home Visiting Kit

A well-stocked community nursing bag is organized logically to ensure rapid access during emergencies. Contents are categorized into non-infected, clean, and sterile compartments.

  • Barrier items: Disposable hand gloves, plastic lining, and a clean apron to protect the nurse and client.
  • Diagnostic tools: Sphygmomanometer, stethoscope, clinical thermometer, and a tape measure.
  • Treatment supplies: Cotton wool balls, gauze pads, adhesive plaster, bandage rolls, and waste disposal bags.
  • Antiseptics and medications: Methylated spirit, iodine solution, hypochlorite solution, and basic analgesics or oral rehydration salts.

General Principles of Bag Technique

Bag technique is a procedural method used by community nurses to render nursing care effectively while preventing the spread of infection. The foundational rule centers on treating the interior of the bag as clean and the exterior or immediate environment as potentially contaminated.

  1. Never place the bag directly on a dusty or unwashed floor; always use a protective lining beneath it.
  2. Wash hands thoroughly before opening the bag and after completing the procedure.
  3. Take out only the specific equipment needed for the current client to minimize exposure.
  4. Keep the bag closed when not actively retrieving or returning items.

Step-by-Step Procedure for Bag Technique during Home Visits

Executing a home visit requires a methodical sequence of actions from the moment the nurse enters the doorway to the final departure steps.

Upon arrival, select a clean, dry surface, spread the protective paper lining, and place the bag on top. Perform hand hygiene before opening the outer clasp. Pull out the apron and hand towel, arranging them for personal use. Next, retrieve only the tools required for the specific assessment or treatment.

After rendering care, clean used non-disposable instruments before returning them to their designated compartments. Perform a final hand wash, fold the protective lining inward, secure the bag, and record all findings in the client's home-based record card.

Infection Control and Sterility Maintenance

Infection control in community nursing relies on strict physical separation between clean and dirty zones. The nurse's hands are the most common vector for pathogen transmission during home visits.

  • Aseptic transfer: Forceps or clean technique must be used when handling dressings so that fingers do not touch sterile surfaces.
  • Waste management: Infectious waste such as blood-stained cotton must be placed in a leak-proof disposal bag and managed according to local environmental health guidelines, avoiding indiscriminate disposal in domestic trash bins.
  • Hand hygiene: Alcohol-based hand rubs or soap and clean running water must be utilized at critical transition points during every visit.

Care, Maintenance, and Storage of the Home Visiting Kit

The longevity and safety of a home visiting kit depend heavily on routine maintenance after every field trip. Leather or heavy-duty canvas bags require periodic cleaning with soap and warm water, followed by thorough drying to prevent mold growth.

Instruments must be washed, dried, and autoclaved or chemically sterilized before being repacked. Expired medications or compromised antiseptic solutions must be discarded and replaced immediately. Store the kit in a clean, dry, secure cupboard at the health facility, well out of reach of children and pests.

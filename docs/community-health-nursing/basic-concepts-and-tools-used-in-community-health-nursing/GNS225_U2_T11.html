Home Visiting Kit

Introduction and Objectives of Home Visits

Home visits form the backbone of community health nursing. They allow nurses to meet patients and families in their natural living environment, offering a clear picture of their daily struggles, family dynamics, and living conditions that a hospital bed can never reveal. The primary goals are prevention, health promotion, early diagnosis, and continuous care for chronic illnesses. Through these visits, nurses bridge the gap between institutional healthcare and community living, making health services accessible to those who cannot easily travel to a clinic.

Standard Contents of the Home Visiting Kit

The public health nurse's bag is a well-organized mobile clinic. Inside, every item has a designated spot to save time and prevent cross-contamination.

  • Clean apron: Worn over the uniform to protect clothing during patient care.
  • Hand towel and soap: Essential for performing proper hand hygiene before and after procedures.
  • Thermometers and stethoscope: Basic diagnostic tools for checking vital signs.
  • First aid supplies: Cotton balls, gauze, bandages, adhesive tape, and scissors.
  • Antiseptics and disinfectants: Solutions like methylated spirit, hypochlorite, or savlon for wound care and equipment cleaning.
  • Waste disposal bags: Separate polythene bags for infectious and non-infectious waste.

General Principles of Bag Technique

Bag technique is an infection control procedure used during home visits. It allows the nurse to carry out nursing care safely without transferring germs from one home to another, or from the bag to the patient.

  • Absolute cleanliness: The inside of the bag and its contents are considered clean, while the outside and the floor are considered contaminated.
  • Barrier use: Always place a clean paper lining or linen underneath the bag and equipment before setting them down.
  • Segregation: Never place used or contaminated equipment back into the clean compartment of the bag until it is properly cleaned and disinfected.
  • Minimal handling: Take out only the specific items needed for the current procedure.

Step-by-Step Procedure for Bag Technique

Executing bag technique requires a calm, methodical approach right upon entering the client's home.

  1. Greeting and placement: Greet the family, assess a suitable clean surface, and place a protective paper lining down. Set the bag on top of the lining.
  2. Hand hygiene: Wash your hands thoroughly with soap and water using a clean towel from the bag.
  3. Preparation: Open the bag, take out the apron, and put it on. Remove only the equipment needed for the procedure.
  4. Execution: Perform the required nursing care, ensuring clean items never touch contaminated surfaces.
  5. Pack up: Clean all reusable instruments, wash hands again, remove and fold the apron with the clean side inward, and secure the bag before leaving.

Infection Control and Waste Disposal

Community nursing demands strict attention to infection control because nurses operate without standard hospital utility rooms. Proper waste management protects both the household and the community refuse collectors. Infectious waste—such as blood-stained cotton, used gauze, and disposable syringes—must go into a designated infectious waste bag. Sharp objects like needles must never be tossed loosely into household bins; use a puncture-resistant safety box or take them back to the clinic for proper incineration. Always wash and disinfect reusable tools as soon as you return to the health facility.

Care, Maintenance, and Storage of the Kit

A poorly maintained kit can become a mobile breeding ground for bacteria. Regular checks and cleaning keep the bag safe and ready for action.

  • Routine cleaning: Wipe the interior and exterior of the leather or canvas bag with a disinfectant solution weekly or after any suspected contamination.
  • Inventory check: Restock used items immediately after returning to the clinic so the next visit is never delayed by missing supplies.
  • Expiration monitoring: Regularly check expiry dates on antiseptics, ointments, and sterile packaging.
  • Proper storage: Store the bag in a clean, dry, and secure place away from pests, direct sunlight, and moisture.

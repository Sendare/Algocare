Midwife as a Member of the Health Team

Introduction to the Midwife's Role in Healthcare

A midwife is a qualified professional who partners with women to provide necessary support, care, and advice during pregnancy, labor, and the postpartum period. Beyond attending births, the role encompasses conducting preventative measures, promoting normal birth, detecting complications in mother and child, and accessing medical or other appropriate assistance. In Nigeria's healthcare system, midwives form the backbone of maternal and child health services, particularly in primary health care facilities where access to specialized doctors is limited. The professional midwife operates autonomously within their scope of practice while recognizing when a situation requires higher-level medical intervention. Understanding this position helps clarify how maternal health services function across urban and rural communities.

Core Responsibilities and Scope of Practice

The scope of practice for a midwife is defined by regulatory bodies such as the Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria. It dictates what interventions a midwife can independently perform and where professional boundaries lie.

  • Antenatal care: Monitoring fetal growth, assessing maternal vital signs, and providing nutritional counseling.
  • Intrapartum care: Managing normal deliveries, monitoring uterine contractions, and providing pain relief support.
  • Postnatal care: Assessing uterine involution, monitoring lochia, and supporting early initiation of breastfeeding.
  • Family planning: Counseling clients and providing various contraceptive methods.
  • Health education: Facilitating community sessions on hygiene, nutrition, and danger signs in pregnancy.

The Healthcare Team Structure in Nigeria

Maternal care is rarely delivered by a single practitioner. In the Nigerian health sector, the multidisciplinary team consists of various cadres working together across primary, secondary, and tertiary levels.

Health WorkerPrimary Role in Maternal Health
Obstetrician/GynecologistManages complex surgical deliveries and high-risk pathology
Midwife/Nurse-MidwifeProvides frontline pregnancy, birth, and postnatal care
Community Health Extension Worker (CHEW)Assists with basic health promotion and routine screenings
PharmacistDispenses essential medications including oxytocics and antihypertensives
Laboratory ScientistProcesses diagnostic blood tests and urinalysis

Every member holds specific competencies that intersect to ensure a safe continuum of care for mothers and newborns.

Collaborative Practice and Interprofessional Communication

Clear communication forms the bedrock of safe patient outcomes. Midwives must interact constantly with doctors, laboratory staff, pharmacists, and administrative personnel. Poor communication often leads to delayed interventions, medication errors, and increased maternal morbidity. Utilizing standardized handover tools, such as the SBAR (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation) format, helps bridge professional gaps and ensures crucial clinical details are never missed during shift changes or emergency patient transfers. Mutual respect and a clear understanding of each team member's competence foster a collaborative environment where patient safety remains the primary focus.

Community Engagement and Primary Health Care Integration

Midwives do not work in isolation behind clinic walls. Effective maternal care requires active outreach into the communities they serve. Building trust with traditional birth attendants, village leaders, and women's groups helps bridge the gap between traditional practices and formal health facilities. By engaging the community, midwives can identify pregnant women early, encourage early antenatal booking, and dispel harmful cultural myths surrounding childbirth. Integrating primary health care principles ensures that maternal services remain accessible, culturally acceptable, and affordable for rural populations who might otherwise rely solely on unverified home remedies.

Referral Systems and Managing Obstetric Emergencies

Recognizing deviation from normal physiological processes is a critical competency for any midwife. When complications such as postpartum hemorrhage, eclampsia, prolonged obstructed labor, or severe sepsis arise at a primary health care level, immediate stabilization and prompt referral are mandatory. A functional referral system requires a reliable communication channel, readily available transport, and a receiving secondary or tertiary facility prepared to accept the patient. Midwives must accompany high-risk referrals when possible or send clear, written documentation detailing the interventions already administered to prevent duplication and save precious time.

Ethical, Legal, and Professional Standards

Professional practice is guided by strict ethical principles: beneficence, non-maleficence, autonomy, and justice. Midwives hold a legal obligation to maintain patient confidentiality, obtain informed consent before performing any procedure, and document accurately in patient case notes. Negligence occurs when a practitioner fails to meet the standard of care, resulting in harm to the mother or newborn. Knowing the legal boundaries protects both the patient and the healthcare provider. Accountability means taking responsibility for one's clinical decisions, admitting errors transparently, and participating in maternal death surveillance and response audits to improve future care.

Overcoming Challenges in Team-Based Maternal Care

Maternal care teams in resource-limited settings frequently encounter systemic obstacles. Staff shortages, erratic power supply, lack of functional diagnostic equipment, and interprofessional rivalries can severely undermine patient safety. Midwives often find themselves working long, exhausting shifts without adequate security or logistics. Overcoming these hurdles requires strong local leadership, continuous professional development, strict adherence to protocols, and fierce advocacy for resource allocation. Building resilience within the healthcare team ensures that despite infrastructural deficits, mothers receive compassionate, evidence-based, and dignified care.

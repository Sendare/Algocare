Antenatal Care at Home

Introduction and Scope of Home-Based Antenatal Care

Home-based antenatal care brings maternal health services directly to pregnant women within their households and communities. This approach is particularly vital in Nigeria, where geographical barriers, cultural preferences, and financial constraints limit regular hospital attendance. Domiciliary care bridges the gap between primary health facilities and remote populations, ensuring that mothers who cannot easily travel still receive essential clinical supervision throughout gestation. The core scope encompasses early pregnancy registration, ongoing physical monitoring, risk identification, and structured birth planning delivered right at the doorstep.

Role of the Community Health Nurse in Domiciliary Visits

The community health nurse acts as the primary clinical link between the formal health system and the household. During home visits, the nurse performs dual roles as a clinician and an educator. Responsibilities include assessing maternal and fetal well-being, screening for danger signs, administering routine prophylaxis such as intermittent preventive treatment for malaria, and counseling families on nutrition. Building trust with household members is essential, as a receptive environment encourages pregnant women to disclose traditional practices or hidden health concerns that they might hide from facility staff.

Initial Assessment and Risk Stratification

The first home visit establishes a comprehensive baseline for the pregnancy. The nurse conducts a detailed obstetric history, calculates the estimated due date, and evaluates past medical and surgical conditions.

  • High-Risk Indicators: History of previous caesarean section, grand multiparity, severe anaemia, chronic hypertension, or teenage pregnancy.
  • Low-Risk Indicators: Normal spacing between pregnancies, previous spontaneous vaginal deliveries without complications, and stable vital signs.

Risk stratification allows the nurse to prioritize referral pathways, ensuring high-risk mothers receive targeted interventions while low-risk clients continue with routine home-based schedules.

Routine Physical Examination and Monitoring

Conducting physical examinations in a home setting requires adaptation while maintaining clinical accuracy. Using portable equipment, the nurse checks vital signs, assesses for dependent edema, and tests urine for protein and glucose using dipsticks.

Abdominal palpation follows Leopold's maneuvers to determine fetal lie, presentation, and engagement. Fetal heart sounds are auscultated using a fetoscope or a handheld Doppler device. Any sudden deviation from normal baseline parameters triggers an immediate reassessment and potential escalation of care.

Health Education, Nutrition, and Birth Preparedness

Empowering the pregnant woman and her family with actionable knowledge remains a cornerstone of domiciliary care. Nurses provide tailored guidance on balanced local diet formulation, emphasizing iron-rich foods to prevent anaemia. Birth preparedness plans are drawn up collaboratively, detailing the designated health facility for delivery, emergency transport arrangements, and the identification of a blood donor if necessary. Families are also educated on recognizing early danger signs such as vaginal bleeding, severe headaches, and blurred vision.

Identification and Management of Complications

A community health nurse must maintain high vigilance to spot early signs of obstetric emergencies. Common complications encountered in home settings include severe malaria, pre-eclampsia, antepartum hemorrhage, and premature rupture of membranes. When warning signs appear, initial stabilization must occur on-site while arranging prompt transfer. Mismanagement or delays in recognizing these crises significantly increase maternal and perinatal morbidity.

Emergency Referral Protocols and Transport Logistics

When a complication exceeds the scope of domiciliary care, an efficient referral protocol is activated. The nurse must communicate swiftly with the receiving facility, complete a formal referral note detailing findings and interventions given, and accompany the patient if possible. Transport logistics often present formidable challenges in rural Nigerian settings; therefore, pre-arranged community transport schemes, motorbikes, or emergency vehicles must be mobilized without delay.

Documentation and Follow-Up Planning

Thorough documentation safeguards the legal and clinical integrity of home-based care. Every visit requires precise recording of vital signs, interventions, health education topics discussed, and scheduled follow-up dates in both the family health record and the client's home-held antenatal card. Follow-up plans must be clearly communicated to the woman and her caregivers, ensuring seamless continuity until delivery and into the postnatal period.

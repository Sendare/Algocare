Sources of Research Problems

Introduction to Research Problems in Nursing

A research problem is an educational and scientific foundation for any formal inquiry in nursing. In the context of nursing education and practice in Nigeria, identifying a solid research problem is essential for improving patient outcomes, shaping health policy, and advancing the nursing profession. A research problem generally stems from a gap in knowledge, a puzzling clinical phenomenon, or a contradiction in existing healthcare delivery that requires systematic investigation. For student nurses, recognizing how research problems originate helps bridge the gap between theoretical classroom knowledge and dynamic clinical environments. Nursing research is distinct because it centers on holistic patient care, nursing interventions, health promotion, and the management of symptoms. By mastering the sources of research problems, student nurses can formulate relevant research questions that address local healthcare challenges, such as maternal mortality, infectious disease management, and healthcare resource allocation within Nigerian hospitals and primary health centers. Ultimately, starting with a well-defined problem ensures that nursing studies remain purposeful, ethical, and directly applicable to clinical practice.

Clinical Practice and Patient Care Observations

Clinical practice and direct patient care observations are among the most fertile grounds for generating nursing research problems. As student nurses and professional clinicians provide bedside care, they frequently encounter recurring challenges, unexpected patient responses, or inefficiencies in ward routines. For example, a nurse might observe that a particular wound care dressing protocol takes longer to heal than another, or that elderly patients on a specific medical ward experience higher rates of hospital-acquired pressure ulcers despite standard turning schedules. These day-to-day encounters spark curiosity and highlight discrepancies between ideal standards of care and actual clinical outcomes. In Nigerian clinical settings, overcrowding, nurse-to-patient ratios, and resource constraints frequently create unique operational hurdles that require empirical investigation. When nurses document these practical dilemmas, they translate routine clinical frustrations into structured research opportunities. Investigating these bedside issues leads to evidence-based practice changes, optimized nursing workflows, enhanced patient safety, and better overall quality of care delivered across diverse healthcare facilities.

Existing Nursing Literature and Previous Studies

Existing nursing literature and previous studies represent another vital source for identifying and shaping research problems. When conducting a literature review, nursing students and researchers critically appraise published articles, systematic reviews, and scholarly journals to see what is already known about a topic. Crucially, authors of these studies almost always conclude their papers by outlining 'recommendations for further research' or highlighting unresolved gaps in the current evidence base. By analyzing these recommendations, a researcher can identify areas that require replication, extension to new populations, or testing under different cultural conditions. For instance, a study evaluating pain management techniques in Western countries might need validation within the Nigerian socio-cultural context to determine if similar clinical outcomes are achieved. Furthermore, literature reviews help prevent the duplication of completed studies and ensure that new research builds progressively upon established scientific knowledge, thereby strengthening the scientific rigor of the nursing profession.

Professional Theories and Conceptual Frameworks

Professional nursing theories and conceptual frameworks provide a structural lens through which nursing phenomena can be systematically viewed and questioned. Nursing models—such as Dorothea Orem's Self-Care Deficit Theory, Sister Callista Roy's Adaptation Model, or Florence Nightingale's Environmental Theory—offer organized ways of understanding patient behavior, health status, and nursing interventions. When researchers apply these conceptual frameworks to clinical scenarios, they often uncover theoretical gaps or inconsistencies that demand empirical testing. For example, a researcher might use Orem's theory to investigate self-care agency among patients managing chronic hypertension in urban Nigerian communities. If a framework's propositions do not fully explain patient outcomes in a specific local context, a research problem is born. Testing and validating these theories through research not only expands scientific knowledge but also strengthens the connection between abstract nursing philosophy and concrete bedside care, proving the practical value of nursing science.

National and International Healthcare Priorities

National and international healthcare priorities serve as macro-level sources for nursing research problems. Global agendas such as the United States Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), World Health Organization (WHO) initiatives, and national health policies formulated by the Federal Ministry of Health in Nigeria highlight critical areas needing urgent attention. These priorities often focus on reducing maternal and child mortality, combating infectious diseases like malaria and tuberculosis, managing the rising burden of non-communicable diseases, and strengthening primary healthcare delivery systems. When nursing students align their research interests with these broader health initiatives, their findings hold greater relevance for policymakers, healthcare administrators, and funding agencies. Addressing national healthcare priorities ensures that nursing research contributes directly to policy formulation, resource allocation, and large-scale health promotion efforts, empowering nurses to act as key drivers of national health reform.

Personal Experiences and Professional Interactions

Personal experiences and professional interactions among healthcare team members also generate meaningful research problems. Nurses interact daily with patients, families, physicians, pharmacists, and hospital administrators. These interactions often reveal communication breakdowns, interprofessional friction, ethical dilemmas, or challenges in patient education and discharge planning. Furthermore, a nurse's personal background, encounters during clinical rotations, or exposure to community health outreaches can spark deep-seated curiosity about specific social determinants of health. For instance, observing how cultural beliefs influence a family's acceptance of orthodox medical treatment in rural Nigerian communities can inspire qualitative studies on health-seeking behaviors. While personal interest is a powerful motivator for sustaining a researcher's drive through a long project, student nurses must ensure that their personal curiosities are translated into objective, scientifically sound, and ethically manageable research problems rather than mere subjective biases.

Evaluating and Refining a Research Problem

Once a potential research problem has been identified from any of these diverse sources, it must undergo rigorous evaluation and refinement. A compelling idea does not automatically make a good research problem. Students must evaluate the problem's significance to nursing practice, ensure it is researchable through empirical data collection, and verify that it is ethical and feasible in terms of time, access, and financial resources. Furthermore, the problem must be narrowed down from a broad topic (such as 'maternal health') into a specific, focused research question (such as 'What are the barriers to exclusive breastfeeding among adolescent mothers in primary health centers in Lagos State?'). Refining a problem also involves checking whether it has already been exhaustively answered or if it adds new insight to the field. Through careful critique, consultation with faculty mentors, and pilot testing, student nurses can shape raw ideas into polished, actionable research problems ready for scientific inquiry.

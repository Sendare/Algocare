Drug Under-Prescription

Introduction to Drug Under-Prescription

Drug under-prescription occurs when a patient fails to receive a necessary medication that is clinically indicated for their diagnosed condition. Unlike over-prescription or polypharmacy—which dominate clinical discussions—under-prescription represents a silent failure of healthcare delivery. It leads to missed opportunities for disease prevention, symptom control, and mortality reduction. In many healthcare settings, particularly within resource-limited environments across Nigeria, under-prescription often stems from a complex interplay of drug scarcity, financial constraints, and prescriber caution. Recognizing this gap is the first step toward achieving truly comprehensive patient care.

Rational Prescribing and Therapeutic Gaps

Rational prescribing demands that patients receive medications appropriate to their clinical needs, in doses that meet their individual requirements, for an adequate period, and at the lowest cost to them and their community. When prescribers deviate from evidence-based guidelines, therapeutic gaps emerge. These gaps are frequently observed in chronic disease management—such as hypertension or diabetes—where guidelines recommend combination therapies, but patients receive only single-agent therapy despite uncontrolled parameters.

Patterns and Manifestations of Omission

Under-prescription manifests in several recognizable patterns within clinical environments: - **Omission of Preventive Therapy**: Failing to prescribe statins for high-risk cardiovascular patients or antithrombotic therapy in atrial fibrillation. - **Subtherapeutic Dosing**: Prescribing a medication at a dose too low to achieve the desired therapeutic effect, often out of excessive fear of toxicity. - **Age-Related Omission**: Withholding necessary guideline-directed medical therapies from older adults purely based on chronological age rather than biological status. - **Comorbidity Neglect**: Treating the primary admitting diagnosis while completely ignoring secondary chronic conditions like depression or osteoarthritis.

Etiology and Contributing Factors

The drivers of under-prescription are multifaceted, rooted in systemic, prescriber, and patient-level dynamics: | Factor Category | Primary Driver | Clinical Impact | | :--- | :--- | :--- | | **Systemic** | Drug out-of-stock syndromes | Prescribers intentionally omit unavailable essentials | | **Prescriber** | Therapeutic inertia | Reluctance to initiate or intensify therapy despite uncontrolled disease | | **Patient** | Out-of-pocket financial burden | Inability to afford multiple costly medications | Therapeutic inertia remains a particularly powerful psychological barrier among clinicians, where comfort with the status quo overrides the necessity for treatment escalation.

Clinical Assessment and Identification

Identifying under-prescription requires a systematic review of the patient's health status against current, evidence-based clinical guidelines. Nurses play a pivotal role during admission and routine assessments by performing thorough medication reconciliations and clinical history evaluations. By comparing what the patient is currently taking against what their disease severity demands, nursing staff can flag missing interventions. Recognizing persistent symptoms—such as uncontrolled pain, ongoing breathlessness, or recurrent hypertensive spikes—often serves as a vital red flag pointing toward omitted therapies.

Patient Safety and Healthcare Complications

While much patient safety discourse focuses on medication errors of commission—such as wrong doses or drug interactions—omissions carry equally severe consequences. Under-prescription directly compromises patient safety by allowing underlying pathologies to progress unchecked. For instance, failing to prescribe anticoagulation in high-risk patients dramatically elevates stroke vulnerability. Similarly, omitting secondary prevention post-myocardial infarction increases the likelihood of fatal recurrent events. These systemic omissions culminate in higher rates of preventable hospital readmissions, prolonged morbidity, and diminished overall quality of life.

Nursing Interventions and Mitigation Strategies

Nurses are uniquely positioned to advocate for patients and bridge gaps caused by under-prescription. Through proactive clinical advocacy, nurses can participate in multidisciplinary ward rounds, gently highlighting missing evidence-based therapies to medical colleagues. Patient education remains paramount; nurses must empower individuals to ask informed questions about their treatment plans. Additionally, fostering interprofessional collaboration ensures that nursing observations regarding unmanaged symptoms directly inform prescribing revisions before patient discharge.

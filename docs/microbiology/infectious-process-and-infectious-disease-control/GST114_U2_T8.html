Handling and Disposal of Sharps

Introduction and Classification of Sharps

Sharps are medical devices with sharp points or edges that can puncture or lacerate skin. Proper handling is a cornerstone of infection prevention in any healthcare setting. Understanding what constitutes a sharp ensures that healthcare personnel apply appropriate caution during clinical procedures, patient care, and waste management.

  • Hypodermic needles: Hollow needles used for injecting medications or drawing blood.
  • Surgical blades: Scalpels and other cutting instruments used during operations.
  • Lancets: Small instruments used for capillary blood sampling, common in blood glucose testing.
  • Broken glass: Ampoules, vials, or contaminated laboratory slides that can cause lacerations.

Risks of Needle-Stick Injuries and Pathogen Transmission

A needle-stick injury occurs when a sharp object unintentionally punctures the skin of a healthcare worker. These incidents carry a severe occupational risk due to the potential transmission of bloodborne pathogens. The severity of the risk depends on the pathogen concentration, the depth of the injury, and the hollow bore status of the needle, which holds a larger volume of contaminated fluid than solid needles.

  • Hepatitis B Virus (HBV): Possesses the highest risk of transmission following a single percutaneous exposure among common bloodborne viruses.
  • Hepatitis C Virus (HCV): Transmitted primarily through blood exposure, with a persistent risk of chronic liver disease following infection.
  • Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV): Carries a lower statistical transmission rate per needle-stick compared to hepatitis, but remains a major psychological and clinical concern.

Standard Precautions and Safe Handling Practices

Standard precautions dictate that every patient's blood and body fluids must be treated as potentially infectious. When handling sharps, behavioral habits dictate safety. Rushing procedures or improvising disposal methods directly contributes to accidental injuries.

Never recap used needles using a two-handed technique, as this dramatically increases the likelihood of digital puncture. If recapping is absolutely unavoidable due to specific clinical workflows, utilize a single-handed scoop technique or a mechanical device holding the cap. Always point sharp tips away from the body and dispose of them immediately at the point of use rather than carrying them across the room.

Proper Utilization and Placement of Sharps Containers

Sharps containers must meet specific engineering standards: they must be puncture-resistant, leak-proof on the sides and bottom, and equipped with a secure closure lid. Proper placement is just as critical as container quality.

Containers should be positioned at eye level and within arm's reach wherever sharps are actively used, but kept safely out of the reach of children and confused patients. Crucially, staff must never overfill these containers. Once the contents reach the designated fill line—usually indicated by a manufacturer's fill indicator or three-quarters full—the container must be permanently sealed to prevent protruding items from scratching passing personnel.

Post-Exposure Management Following a Needle-Stick Injury

Despite rigorous preventive measures, accidental exposures still happen. Immediate action following a needle-stick injury can significantly reduce the risk of permanent infection. Staff must never panic, but instead execute a calm, standardized protocol.

  1. First Aid: Immediately wash the puncture wound with soap and running water. Do not scrub, scratch, or squeeze the wound to bleed it out, as this can increase local tissue damage and pathogen entry.
  2. Reporting: Report the incident immediately to the ward manager, infection control nurse, or occupational health unit.
  3. Evaluation: Assess the source patient's infection status and the exposed worker's baseline serology.
  4. Prophylaxis: Initiate post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) for HIV or hepatitis B immunoglobulin when clinically indicated, ideally within hours of the exposure.

Definition of Microbiological Terms

Core Concepts and Taxonomy in Microbiology

Microbiology is the study of microorganisms, a diverse group of microscopic entities that exist as single cells, cell clusters, or acellular particles. Understanding clinical microbiology begins with taxonomy, the science of naming, describing, and classifying these organisms. In healthcare settings, precise identification dictates the choice of antimicrobial therapy and dictates isolation protocols. - **Pathogenicity**: The inherent biological capacity of a microorganism to cause disease in a host. - **Virulence**: The degree of pathogenicity, or how severely a pathogen damages its host, often measured by the severity of the disease it produces. - **Colonization**: The presence and multiplication of microorganisms on a host surface or tissue without causing tissue invasion or cellular damage. - **Infection**: The invasion and multiplication of pathogenic microbes in body tissues, resulting in local cellular injury and a host immune response. - **Commensalism**: A symbiotic relationship where the microorganism benefits while the human host is neither harmed nor helped, forming the basis of our normal flora.

Bacteriology and Virology Terminology

Bacteria are single-celled prokaryotic organisms characterized by rigid cell walls, lacking membrane-bound nuclei. Their study requires specific structural and metabolic vocabulary. Viruses, conversely, are obligate intracellular parasites containing genetic material encased in a protein coat, requiring living host machinery to replicate. 1. **Gram-staining**: A differential staining technique that divides bacteria into positive (thick peptidoglycan wall retaining crystal violet) or negative (thin wall taking up safranin counterstain). 2. **Bacteriostatic**: Agents that inhibit bacterial growth and multiplication without directly killing them, relying on host immunity to clear the organism. 3. **Bactericidal**: Agents that actively destroy and kill bacteria outright. 4. **Capsid**: The protective protein shell enclosing the viral genome. 5. **Virion**: A complete, infectious viral particle outside of a host cell, capable of surviving long enough to infect a new host.

Mycology, Parasitology, and Atypical Pathogens

Beyond bacteria and viruses, clinical microbiology encompasses fungi, parasites, and unconventional infectious agents. Fungi are eukaryotic organisms with chitinous cell walls, including yeasts and molds. Parasites live on or within a host organism at the host's expense. - **Mycosis**: Any infectious disease caused by a fungus, ranging from superficial skin infections to life-threatening systemic mycoses. - **Dermatophytes**: A specialized group of fungi that thrive on keratin found in skin, hair, and nails, causing conditions like ringworm. - **Trophozoite**: The active, motile, feeding stage of a protozoan parasite during which it causes clinical symptoms. - **Cyst (Parasitic)**: The dormant, highly resilient stage surrounded by a thick wall that allows protozoa to survive harsh environmental conditions outside the host. - **Prion**: An infectious agent composed entirely of misfolded protein material that lacks nucleic acids, causing fatal neurodegenerative disorders like Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease.

Infection Control and Decontamination Terms

Controlling the spread of microorganisms in Nigerian hospitals requires rigorous adherence to standardized definitions of decontamination. Misunderstanding these terms can lead to healthcare-associated infections. | Term | Target Level | Primary Application | Examples | |---|---|---|---| | **Sterilization** | Complete elimination of all forms of microbial life, including spores | Surgical instruments, implants | Autoclaving, ethylene oxide | | **Disinfection** | Elimination of most pathogenic microbes on inanimate surfaces | Treatment tables, bed rails | Chemical germicides like bleach | | **Antisepsis** | Reduction of microbial load on living tissue | Skin preparation before venipuncture | Chlorhexidine, iodine | | **Sanitization** | Reduction of microbial count to safe public health levels | Food utensils, hospital floors | Detergents and hot water washing |

Clinical and Diagnostic Microbiology Terminology

Diagnostic microbiology links bedside presentations with laboratory findings. Terms used in pathology reports guide targeted antimicrobial stewardship. - **Empiric Therapy**: Initiating broad-spectrum antimicrobial treatment based on clinical presentation and local epidemiological data before definitive lab culture results are available. - **Culture Sensitivity**: A laboratory test identifying the specific causative pathogen and determining which antimicrobial agents will successfully inhibit or kill it. - **Minimum Inhibitory Concentration (MIC)**: The lowest concentration of an antimicrobial agent that prevents visible growth of a bacterium after overnight incubation. - **Superinfection**: A secondary infection that develops during treatment of an initial infection, typically caused by the eradication of normal flora allowing resistant opportunists to flourish.

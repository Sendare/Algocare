Classification of Health Care Waste

Introduction and Significance of Health Care Waste

Health care waste encompasses all the waste generated during medical diagnosis, treatment, immunisation, or research involving humans and animals. Approximately 75 to 90 percent of this waste is non-hazardous general waste, comparable to domestic refuse. The remaining 10 to 25 percent is categorized as hazardous, presenting various biological, chemical, or physical risks. Improper handling of these materials places health workers, waste handlers, patients, and the surrounding community at severe risk of infections, toxic injuries, and environmental contamination. Sound waste management begins at the exact point of generation, requiring every clinician to understand waste categories before disposal.

General Health Care Waste

General health care waste poses no biological, chemical, or radioactive hazards. This category includes kitchen leftovers from hospital canteens, administrative paper, cardboard packaging, plastic bottles, and office supplies. While it does not require special treatment, managing large volumes efficiently prevents vector breeding and maintains facility hygiene. In resource-constrained settings, keeping this stream entirely separate from clinical refuse drastically reduces the operating costs of high-temperature incineration.

Infectious and Pathological Waste

  • Infectious Waste: Materials contaminated with blood and body fluids, including cotton swabs, disposable gowns, culture dishes, and infected animal carcasses.
  • Pathological Waste: Human tissues, organs, body parts, and recognizable fetuses derived from surgeries or autopsies.

Both types demand rigorous handling because they contain pathogens capable of causing disease in humans. Pathological waste often carries significant cultural and aesthetic sensitivities, requiring respectful handling alongside strict biohazard containment.

Sharps Waste and Injury Prevention

Sharps include any objects that can puncture or lacerate skin, such as hypodermic needles, infusion sets with attached needles, scalpels, lancets, and broken glass ampoules. These items carry the dual hazard of physical trauma and blood-borne pathogen transmission, particularly Hepatitis B, Hepatitis C, and HIV. Safety protocols dictate that needles must never be recapped after use. Instead, the entire unit goes directly into a puncture-resistant safety box immediately following the procedure.

Pharmaceutical and Cytotoxic Waste

Pharmaceutical waste comprises expired, unused, spilled, and contaminated drugs, vaccines, and sera. This category requires careful oversight to prevent active pharmacological agents from entering public water systems or local soil through improper dumping. Cytotoxic waste represents a specialized, highly hazardous subset consisting of drugs with teratogenic, mutagenic, or carcinogenic properties—most commonly used in cancer chemotherapy. These substances demand designated, heavy-duty containers and high-temperature incineration to ensure complete molecular destruction.

Chemical and Radioactive Waste

Chemical waste consists of discarded solid, liquid, and gaseous chemicals from diagnostic work, cleaning agents, disinfectants, and heavy metals like mercury from broken sphygmomanometers. Radioactive waste includes liquids from radiotherapy, sealed radiation sources, and contaminated glassware or urine from patients undergoing isotope treatments. Both categories require specialized containment protocols, secure storage, and strict regulatory oversight to prevent environmental catastrophe and acute human poisoning.

Color-Coding and Containerization Standards in Nigeria

Standardized color-coding systems ensure instant visual recognition of waste categories, reducing sorting errors across Nigerian healthcare institutions. While local facility protocols can vary slightly based on state guidelines and available resources, standard national frameworks generally follow specific colour conventions:

Waste CategoryStandard Container / ColorCommon Examples
General WasteBlack or Transparent BinOffice paper, food wrappers, packaging
Infectious WasteYellow Bin with Biohazard SymbolSoiled dressings, swabs, disposable linen
Sharps WasteYellow Puncture-Resistant BoxNeedles, scalpel blades, lancets
Highly Hazardous / PathologicalRed or Yellow Heavy-Duty BagsHuman tissues, anatomical parts

Adhering to these containerization standards protects sanitation workers and facilitates safe municipal final disposal.

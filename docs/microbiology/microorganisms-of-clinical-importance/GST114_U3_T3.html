Microbiology of Water

Introduction to Water Microbiology

Water microbiology studies the microscopic life inhabiting natural and engineered water systems. Microorganisms in water include bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa, and microscopic algae. While many of these organisms are harmless or play vital ecological roles in nutrient cycling, others pose severe health risks to humans. Understanding this microbial community helps nurses and public health practitioners trace outbreaks, interpret water quality reports, and educate communities on safe water practices.

Natural Aquatic Flora and Fauna

Natural water bodies like rivers, lakes, and oceans host indigenous microbial populations adapted to specific environmental conditions.

  • Autotrophic microbes: Phytoplankton and cyanobacteria perform photosynthesis, producing the oxygen required by other aquatic life.
  • Heterotrophic bacteria: These decompose organic matter, breaking down dead plants and animals to recycle nutrients.
  • Benthic microorganisms: Living at the bottom sediment, these organisms manage anaerobic and aerobic breakdown of mineral deposits.

Sources and Types of Water Pollution

Water pollution occurs when foreign contaminants enter natural water bodies, altering their quality and making them unsafe. Pollution sources are broadly classified into point sources, which come from a distinct location like a factory discharge pipe, and non-point sources, which wash off large land areas like agricultural farmlands during rainfall. Biological pollution primarily consists of human and animal waste introducing pathogenic microbes, while chemical pollution involves heavy metals, fertilizers, and industrial toxins.

Major Water-Borne Diseases

Water-borne pathogens enter the human body primarily through the fecal-oral route, often when drinking or washing with contaminated water.

  • Cholera: Caused by *Vibrio cholerae*, leading to severe watery diarrhea and rapid dehydration.
  • Typhoid Fever: Caused by *Salmonella typhi*, characterized by sustained high fever and systemic illness.
  • Amebic Dysentery: Caused by *Entamoeba histolytica*, resulting in painful, bloody stools.
  • Hepatitis A: A viral infection spreading through contaminated water that targets the liver.

Indicator Organisms and Water Quality Testing

Testing water for every known pathogen is impractical and expensive. Instead, microbiologists test for indicator organisms, specifically coliform bacteria like *Escherichia coli*. Coliforms reside safely in the intestines of warm-blooded animals. Their presence in water signals recent fecal contamination and suggests that dangerous pathogens may also be present. Standard tests check for total coliforms and thermotolerant coliforms using membrane filtration or multiple-tube fermentation techniques.

Water Treatment and Purification Methods

Making raw water safe for human consumption requires multiple treatment steps to remove suspended particles, bacteria, and chemicals.

  1. Coagulation and Flocculation: Adding chemicals like alum causes fine suspended particles to clump together into larger flocs.
  2. Sedimentation: Heavy flocs settle to the bottom of the basin by gravity.
  3. Filtration: Water passes through beds of sand, gravel, and charcoal to trap remaining tiny particles.
  4. Disinfection: Chlorine, ozone, or ultraviolet light is applied to kill remaining pathogenic microorganisms.

Public Health Nursing Implications in Nigeria

In Nigeria, community health nurses frequently manage water-borne disease outbreaks like cholera and typhoid, particularly during rainy seasons when floods overwhelm local sanitation systems. Nurses play a crucial role in promoting household water safety through boiling, chlorination tablets, and safe storage containers. Advocating for infrastructural improvements, community hygiene education, and active disease surveillance forms a core part of effective public health nursing practice across primary health care centers.

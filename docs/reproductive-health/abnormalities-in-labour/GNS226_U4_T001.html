Preterm Labour

Definition and Diagnostic Criteria

Preterm labour refers to the onset of regular uterine contractions that result in progressive cervical changes before the completion of 37 weeks of gestation, specifically between 20 and 36 completed weeks. In Nigerian clinical practice, establishing an accurate gestational age is vital because interventions change drastically after 34 weeks. Diagnostic confirmation requires documented uterine contractions occurring at a frequency of at least four every 20 minutes, or eight every 60 minutes, alongside documented cervical effacement of 80% or greater, or cervical dilation of 2 cm or more.

Etiology and Risk Factors

  • Previous preterm birth: The single strongest predictor for a subsequent preterm delivery.
  • Genitourinary infections: Conditions like bacterial vaginosis, urinary tract infections, and untreated malaria trigger inflammatory pathways.
  • Multiple gestations: Twins or triplets overdistend the myometrium, stimulating premature contractility.
  • Uterine or cervical anomalies: Structural weaknesses like an incompetent cervix or uterine fibroids limit the womb's capacity.
  • Maternal medical disorders: Chronic hypertension, severe anaemia, and gestational diabetes compromise the intrauterine environment.

Clinical Assessment and Diagnostic Tests

Evaluating a pregnant woman presenting with abdominal tightening requires a systematic approach. The nurse monitors uterine activity with a cardiotocograph while palpating the abdomen for tone and contraction duration. A sterile speculum examination is performed to inspect the cervix, collect high vaginal swabs for infection screening, and test for amniotic fluid leakage. The fetal fibronectin test, when available, helps predict the absence of delivery within the next week if the result is negative.

Pharmacological Management and Tocolytic Therapy

Tocolytics are medications administered to temporarily suppress uterine contractions. Their primary goal is not to halt labour permanently, but to buy 48 hours for maternal transport to a well-equipped facility and the administration of corticosteroids.

Common agents used include:

  • Nifedipine: A calcium channel blocker that blocks calcium entry into myometrial cells, reducing smooth muscle contraction.
  • Atosiban: An oxytocin receptor antagonist with a favorable maternal side effect profile.
  • Magnesium sulfate: Primarily used for fetal neuroprotection rather than long-term tocolysis in developing settings.

Fetal Lung Maturation and Corticosteroid Administration

Antenatal corticosteroids accelerate the production of surfactant in the fetal lungs, significantly reducing the incidence of respiratory distress syndrome, intraventricular hemorrhage, and neonatal mortality. Standard protocol involves administering dexamethasone or betamethasone intramuscularly to women at risk of preterm delivery between 24 and 34 weeks of gestation. Nurses must monitor maternal blood glucose levels closely after administration, as steroids frequently induce transient hyperglycemia, especially in women with gestational diabetes.

Intrapartum Care and Delivery Management

Managing the delivery of a preterm infant requires specialized intrapartum attention. The lower birth weight and fragile tissues increase vulnerability to birth trauma. Continuous electronic fetal monitoring is essential to detect signs of fetal distress early. Obstetricians often recommend an episiotomy during the second stage of delivery to minimize intracranial pressure on the delicate preterm fetal skull. Pediatric and neonatal nursing teams must be present at the bedside immediately upon birth to initiate resuscitation and thermal care.

Nursing Interventions and Maternal Support

Holistic nursing care addresses both the physical stability of the mother and her profound psychological distress. The nurse maintains the client on strict bed rest in a left lateral position to optimize uteroplacental blood flow and reduce pressure on the cervix. Establishing clear communication channels helps alleviate anxiety regarding the baby's survival. Nurses facilitate early kangaroo mother care or skin-to-skin contact once the newborn is stable, promoting bonding, temperature regulation, and successful breastfeeding initiation.

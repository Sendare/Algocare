Preterm Labour

Definition and Diagnostic Criteria of Preterm Labour

Preterm labour refers to the onset of regular uterine contractions that result in progressive changes of the cervix, occurring before the completion of 37 weeks of gestation, or specifically between 20 and 36 completed weeks. The clinical diagnosis requires two distinct components: documented uterine contractions occurring at a frequency of at least four every 20 minutes (or eight in 60 minutes), and concurrent documented cervical change, such as an effacement of 80% or greater, or a cervical dilation of 2 cm or more. When a pregnant woman presents with lower abdominal cramping and backache before term, simple contractions alone without cervical remodeling are classified as preterm uterine irritability rather than true preterm labour.

Etiology and Risk Factors for Preterm Birth

The triggers leading to preterm delivery are diverse, categorized into spontaneous pathways and provider-initiated interventions. Spontaneous preterm birth often stems from infection, uterine overdistension, decidual hemorrhage, or cervical insufficiency.

  • Previous preterm birth: The single strongest predictor of a future early delivery.
  • Multiple gestations: Twins or triplets stretch the myometrium beyond its normal physiological capacity.
  • Genital tract infections: Bacterial vaginosis and urinary tract infections trigger inflammatory cytokines that stimulate contractions.
  • Low socio-economic status: Often linked to poor nutrition, high stress, and limited access to early antenatal care.
  • Uterine anomalies: Fibroids or structural malformations that limit intrauterine space.

Pathophysiology and Mechanisms of Preterm Initiation

The biological pathways triggering early labour mirror term parturition but activate prematurely through distinct pathological stimuli. These pathways converge on a common final pathway involving uterine activation, cervical ripening, and membrane rupture.

  • Inflammation and Infection: Pathogens or sterile tissue stress release pro-inflammatory cytokines like interleukins and tumor necrosis factor, stimulating prostaglandins.
  • Hormone Withdrawal: A functional decrease in progesterone activity removes the natural inhibition keeping the myometrium relaxed.
  • Decidual Hemorrhage: Bleeding behind the placenta releases thrombin, which directly stimulates myometrial contraction receptors.
  • Oxytocin Sensitivity: Upregulation of oxytocin receptors in the uterine wall increases tissue responsiveness to circulating hormones.

Clinical Assessment and Diagnostic Evaluation

Evaluating a patient with suspected preterm labour demands a systematic approach to confirm gestational age, assess cervical status, and rule out contraindications to conservative management. The examination begins with a sterile speculum inspection to visualize the cervix, assess dilation, and collect samples for testing.

Pharmacological Management and Tocolytic Therapy

Tocolytic medications are administered to suppress uterine contractions temporarily. The primary goal of tocolysis is not to halt labour indefinitely, but rather to buy a vital 48-hour window. This brief delay allows for the administration of corticosteroids to accelerate fetal lung maturity and permits safe maternal transfer to a facility equipped with a neonatal intensive care unit.

  • Nifedipine: A calcium channel blocker that prevents calcium entry into myometrial cells, generally favored due to a mild side-effect profile.
  • Indomethacin: A prostaglandin inhibitor used primarily before 32 weeks of gestation; prolonged use can cause premature closure of the fetal ductus arteriosus.
  • Magnesium Sulfate: Used primarily for fetal neuroprotection rather than its weak tocolytic properties, helping reduce the risk of cerebral palsy.
  • Atosiban: An oxytocin receptor antagonist that offers targeted uterine relaxation with minimal maternal cardiovascular side effects where available.

Fetal Lung Maturation and Corticosteroid Administration

Antenatal corticosteroid therapy represents one of the most effective interventions in obstetrics for reducing neonatal morbidity and mortality associated with prematurity. Steroids accelerate the structural and functional development of fetal lungs by stimulating the synthesis and release of pulmonary surfactant, which prevents alveolar collapse.

  • Target Population: Administered to women between 24 and 34 weeks of gestation who are at risk of delivery within seven days.
  • Deregulated Regimen: Typically consists of betamethasone given intramuscularly in two doses, 24 hours apart.
  • Secondary Benefits: Beyond respiratory distress syndrome, steroids significantly lower the incidence of intraventricular hemorrhage and necrotizing enterocolitis in the newborn.

Intrapartum Care and Delivery Management

Managing the delivery of a preterm infant requires specialized intrapartum care to minimize birth trauma and optimize newborn resuscitation outcomes. The mode of delivery depends on gestational age, fetal presentation, and maternal stability. While breech presentation often warrants a caesarean section in very early gestations, vertex presentations can frequently be delivered vaginally with careful monitoring.

Continuous electronic fetal heart rate monitoring is essential because preterm fetuses tolerate labour poorly and show distress quickly. Paediatric and neonatal resuscitation teams must be present at the delivery bed, prepared with pre-warmed radiant warmers and equipment sized appropriately for a low-birth-weight infant. Episiotomy may be used selectively to reduce pressure on the fragile preterm skull.

Complications and Neonatal Prognosis

Babies born before term face a range of short-term and long-term health challenges due to the immaturity of their organ systems. The severity of these complications correlates directly with the gestational age at birth.

  • Respiratory Distress Syndrome (RDS): Caused by a lack of surfactant, leading to labored breathing and hypoxia.
  • Intraventricular Hemorrhage (IVH): Bleeding into the delicate brain ventricles, common in extremely premature infants.
  • Necrotizing Enterocolitis (NEC): A serious inflammatory condition of the intestinal tissue, particularly affecting formula-fed preterm infants.
  • Retinopathy of Prematurity (ROP): Abnormal blood vessel growth in the retina that can impair vision if unmonitored.

Nursing Interventions and Maternal Support

Nursing care for a woman experiencing preterm labour encompasses both physiological monitoring and intensive emotional support. Nurses must monitor vital signs, track contraction patterns, and administer prescribed medications accurately while watching for adverse reactions, such as pulmonary edema from tocolytics.

Beyond clinical tasks, the psychological impact is profound. Mothers often experience acute anxiety, guilt, and fear regarding their baby's survival. Nurses provide clear explanations of procedures, facilitate early contact with neonatal intensive care staff before delivery, and encourage kangaroo mother care when the infant stabilizes. Creating a supportive environment helps alleviate trauma and promotes maternal coping.

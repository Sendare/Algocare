Preterm Labour

Definition and Diagnostic Criteria of Preterm Labour

Preterm labour is clinically defined as the onset of regular, painful uterine contractions accompanied by progressive cervical changes occurring before the completion of 37 weeks of gestation, specifically between 20 and 36 completed weeks. In Nigerian obstetric practice, accurate gestational age dating is critical because intervention thresholds differ starkly between pre-viable, extremely preterm, and moderately preterm neonates. Diagnostic confirmation requires documenting both uterine activity and cervical effacement or dilation. Clinicians look for cervical dilation of 2 centimeters or greater, or a documented change in cervical length via transvaginal ultrasound alongside regular contractions occurring at least every four minutes. When contractions are present without cervical modification, the condition is classified as threatened preterm labour rather than true active labour.

Etiology and Risk Factors for Preterm Birth

The triggers leading to early uterine activation are multifaceted, involving maternal, fetal, and placental pathways. Identifying underlying predispositions allows midwives to target high-risk antenatal clients effectively.

  • Infection: Ascending genital tract infections, such as bacterial vaginosis and urinary tract infections, release inflammatory cytokines that stimulate prostaglandin production.
  • Uterine Overdistension: Multi-fetal gestations and polyhydramnios stretch myometrial cells, triggering gap junction formation and premature contractility.
  • Cervical Insufficiency: Structural weakness of the cervix permits painless dilation and early descent of the amniotic sac.
  • Placental Ischemia: Conditions like chronic hypertension and severe pre-eclampsia compromise uteroplacental blood flow, inducing early vascular compromise and ischemic stress.
  • Demographic Predispositions: History of prior preterm delivery, extreme maternal ages, and low socioeconomic status correlate with higher incidence rates.

Pathophysiology and Mechanisms of Preterm Initiation

The biochemical pathways triggering early parturition mirror term labour but activate prematurely. Four major interrelated cascades drive this process: activation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis, decidual-chorioamniotic inflammation, decidual hemorrhage, and pathological uterine stretch.

Inflammatory stimuli cause macrophages and neutrophils to release cytokines like interleukins and tumor necrosis factor. These chemical messengers upregulate cyclooxygenase-2, increasing the synthesis of prostaglandins E2 and F2-alpha, which soften the cervix and stimulate myometrial contractions. Simultaneously, functional progesterone withdrawal occurs at the cellular level. Even though circulating serum progesterone levels might remain normal, myometrial progesterone receptors shift isoforms, removing the relaxant effect and allowing oxytocin receptor upregulation.

Clinical Assessment and Diagnostic Evaluation

A systematic diagnostic workup confirms active preterm labour and rules out confounding conditions. Evaluation begins with a sterile speculum examination to inspect cervical dilation, assess for amniotic fluid leakage, and obtain high vaginal swabs for microbiological culture. Digital cervical examinations are minimized if membrane rupture is suspected or confirmed to reduce ascending infection risks.

Biochemical markers assist when clinical presentations are equivocal. A negative fetal fibronectin test has a high negative predictive value, reliably indicating that delivery within the next fortnight is unlikely. Transvaginal ultrasound measurement of cervical length provides objective data; a length greater than 30 millimeters strongly correlates with the absence of imminent delivery, whereas a length below 20 millimeters warrants urgent intervention.

Pharmacological Management and Tocolytic Therapy

Tocolytics are medications administered to temporarily suppress uterine contractions, buying a critical 48-hour window for maternal corticosteroid administration and safe transfer to a tertiary health facility equipped with neonatal intensive care units.

Fetal Lung Maturation and Corticosteroid Administration

Antenatal corticosteroid therapy represents the single most effective pharmacological intervention to reduce neonatal morbidity and mortality associated with prematurity. Administration accelerates type II pneumocyte maturation, stimulating the synthesis and release of pulmonary surfactant, which decreases alveolar surface tension and prevents respiratory distress syndrome.

Standard regimens utilize betamethasone administered intramuscularly as two doses of 12 milligrams given 24 hours apart, or dexamethasone given as four doses of 6 milligrams every 12 hours. The maximal benefit occurs when delivery takes place between 24 hours and 7 days after completing the course. These drugs are indicated for pregnancies between 24 and 34 weeks of gestation where preterm birth is threatened, and they are administered even if membrane rupture has already occurred.

Intrapartum Care and Delivery Management

Managing delivery in preterm labour requires balancing the risks of operative intervention against the vulnerability of the premature neonate. Continuous electronic fetal heart rate monitoring is essential because preterm fetuses tolerate intrapartum hypoxia poorly.

Routine prophylactic episiotomy is not recommended, but controlled delivery of the fragile fetal head using a modified Ritgen maneuver helps prevent intracranial hemorrhage. For breech presentations at very early gestational ages, obstetricians frequently recommend a lower segment caesarean section to minimize head entrapment and traumatic birth injury. A multidisciplinary team including a pediatrician or neonatologist must be present at every preterm delivery to initiate immediate resuscitation and thermal care.

Complications and Neonatal Prognosis

The severity of complications in preterm birth correlates inversely with gestational age at delivery. Immature organ systems predispose the newborn to several major pathological conditions:

  • Respiratory Distress Syndrome: Caused by surfactant deficiency, leading to atelectasis, hypoxia, and prolonged mechanical ventilation.
  • Intraventricular Hemorrhage: Bleeding into the fragile germinal matrix of the developing brain, which can cause long-term neurological deficits.
  • Necrotizing Enterocolitis: Ischemic and inflammatory necrosis of the bowel mucosa, exacerbated by early enteral feeding formulas.
  • Retinopathy of Prematurity: Abnormal vascular proliferation in the retina driven by high oxygen exposure and immature retinal development.
  • Sepsis: Neonatal immune system immaturity increases susceptibility to overwhelming systemic bacterial infections.

Nursing Interventions and Maternal Support

Holistic care during preterm labour addresses both physiological stabilization and severe psychological distress. Midwives maintain a calm, quiet environment to reduce maternal anxiety, which can otherwise elevate catecholamine levels and stimulate further uterine activity. Continuous monitoring of vital signs, contraction frequency, and fluid balance ensures early detection of complications such as chorioamnionitis or adverse drug reactions.

Preparing the family for the realities of the neonatal intensive care unit is an essential nursing responsibility. Facilitating early maternal-infant contact, initiating gentle expression of breast milk for future feeding, and offering empathetic listening help alleviate feelings of guilt and helplessness experienced by mothers facing unexpected preterm delivery.

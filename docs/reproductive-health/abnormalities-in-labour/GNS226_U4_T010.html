Prolonged Labour

Definition and Normal Partographic Progress

Prolonged labour, sometimes called obstructed labour when mechanical failure is present, occurs when cervical dilation and fetal descent do not progress at the expected rate during the active phase of the first stage of labour. Conventionally, a rate of cervical dilation slower than one centimetre per hour in nulliparous women raises concern. The partograph serves as the primary graphical tool used in Nigerian maternity units to track this progress against time. Two key lines guide the midwife on the partograph:

  • Alert line: Starts at four centimetres of dilation and slopes at one centimetre per hour. Crossing this line indicates the need for closer observation and evaluation.
  • Action line: Parallels the alert line four hours to the right. Crossing or reaching this line demands immediate medical intervention and reassessment of the labour process.

Understanding normal progress prevents unnecessary surgical interference while ensuring that true delays are caught before maternal or fetal compromise sets in.

Pathophysiology and Underlying Causes of Prolonged Labour

Labour progress depends on three interacting variables often called the powers, the passenger, and the passage. When any of these components fails, labour becomes prolonged.

  • The Powers: Inadequate or uncoordinated uterine contractions fail to dilate the cervix or push the baby down.
  • The Passenger: Abnormal fetal size, such as macrosomia, or abnormal presentation like breech or brow presentation, prevents smooth descent through the pelvis.
  • The Passage: Cephalopelvic disproportion occurs when the maternal bony pelvis is too small or abnormally shaped to let the baby pass safely.

Fetal head moulding and caput succedaneum often develop as the baby's head presses against a tight birth canal for too long. If these mechanical blocks are ignored, the uterus attempts to overcome the resistance through increasingly stronger contractions before eventually tiring out and stopping altogether.

Clinical Assessment and Diagnosis in the Nigerian Context

Assessing a woman with suspected prolonged labour requires a calm, systematic clinical evaluation. The midwife must first review the partograph to verify how long the patient has spent in active labour. Vital signs, including maternal blood pressure, pulse, and temperature, help detect early signs of dehydration, exhaustion, or infection like chorioamnionitis.

Abdominal palpation checks the strength and frequency of uterine contractions, fetal lie, presentation, and the degree of fetal head descent, often measured in fifths palpable above the pubic bone. Vaginal examination reveals cervical dilation, the state of the membranes, and the presence of significant caput or moulding. In many Nigerian primary health settings, delayed referral is a major driver of severe complications, making prompt recognition of abnormal progress vital before the patient travels to a secondary or tertiary facility.

Maternal and Fetal Complications

Prolonged labour carries severe risks for both mother and baby if left uncorrected. Prolonged pressure on soft maternal tissues can lead to ischemic necrosis, ultimately causing vesicovaginal fistula or rectovaginal fistula, which are devastating conditions marked by continuous incontinence. Other maternal dangers include postpartum hemorrhage, uterine rupture, severe dehydration, and ascending uterine infections.

For the baby, restricted oxygen supply from compressed umbilical vessels and a stressed placenta causes acute fetal distress, low Apgar scores, meconium aspiration syndrome, and neonatal encephalopathy. Early detection remains the single best safeguard against these lifelong morbidities.

Immediate Nursing Care and Supportive Management

Supportive nursing care preserves maternal stamina and improves clinical outcomes while medical plans are being formed. Midwives must ensure adequate hydration through oral fluids or intravenous lines if oral intake is restricted. Monitoring fluid balance via input and output charts prevents fluid overload or severe dehydration.

Pain management through reassuring words, back rubs, and safe pharmacological options helps reduce maternal anxiety and stress hormone spikes. Continuous monitoring of maternal pulse and blood pressure, alongside regular fetal heart auscultation, ensures rapid identification of worsening conditions. Finally, keeping the bladder empty by encouraging regular voiding or using intermittent catheterization prevents the bladder from obstructing fetal descent.

Medical and Surgical Interventions

When supportive measures fail to correct slow progress, medical or surgical interventions become necessary. If poor uterine contractions are the primary issue without mechanical obstruction, healthcare providers may administer oxytocin infusion to stimulate stronger and more regular contractions. Careful monitoring is mandatory during oxytocin use to avoid uterine hyperstimulation.

When cephalopelvic disproportion or fetal distress is confirmed, emergency caesarean section is performed to deliver the baby safely. In cases where the head is fully descended and the pelvis is adequate, instrumental delivery using forceps or a vacuum extractor may be chosen by the doctor. Midwives must prepare the patient psychologically and physically for theatre while ensuring consent and baseline checks are complete.

Prevention Strategies and Partograph Utilization

Preventing prolonged labour begins during antenatal care by identifying risk factors such as previous difficult deliveries, small stature, or suspected fetal macrosomia. Routine plotting on the partograph for every woman in active labour remains the single most effective strategy for early detection. Midwives must plot cervical dilation, descent, contractions, and fetal heart rate consistently and accurately.

Regular supportive supervision and training updates for healthcare staff in primary health clinics ensure that referral criteria are strictly followed. Community education also encourages pregnant women to seek skilled attendance early rather than relying solely on traditional birth attendants when complications arise.

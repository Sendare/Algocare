Prolonged Labour

Definition and Normal Partographic Progress

Prolonged labour occurs when cervical dilation and fetal descent do not progress at the expected rate during the active phase of the first stage of labour. Traditionally defined as active labour lasting beyond 12 hours in nulliparas and 8 hours in multiparas, modern obstetric practice relies heavily on the active phase limit where cervical dilation crosses to the right of the **alert line** on the WHO partograph. - **Latent Phase**: Extends up to 4 cm of cervical dilation and should not exceed 8 hours. Prolongation here is termed prolonged latent phase. - **Active Phase**: Begins at 5 cm dilation in current WHO guidelines, with a normal cervical dilation rate of at least 1 cm per hour. - **Alert Line**: Represents the slowest 10% of primigravid cervical dilation; crossing this line signals the need for increased vigilance. - **Action Line**: Placed 4 hours to the right of the alert line; reaching or crossing this boundary mandates immediate medical intervention and evaluation. Understanding these thresholds helps prevent exhaustion and uterine rupture, particularly in busy Nigerian health facilities where delayed referrals compound obstetric risks.

Pathophysiology and Underlying Causes

The progression of labour relies on the interplay of the **Three Ps**: Power (uterine contractions), Passenger (fetus size, presentation, and position), and Passage (pelvic skeletal structure). Dysfunction in any of these components halts normal delivery mechanics. - **Power Failure**: Hypotonic uterine inertia occurs when contractions are too weak or infrequent to dilate the cervix. This often stems from maternal exhaustion, severe dehydration, or prolonged bedrest. - **Passenger Anomalies**: Malpositions like persistent occipitoposterior or malpresentations such as brow or face presentations create mechanical obstruction because the widest fetal diameters present to the pelvis. - **Passage Obstructions**: Contracted pelvis, pelvic tumors, or a rigid perineum prevent the fetus from descending, despite strong and regular contractions. Cephalopelvic disproportion (CPD) remains a predominant driver of obstructed labour in West Africa, frequently compounded by adolescent early marriage and chronic childhood malnutrition affecting pelvic bone development.

Clinical Assessment and Diagnosis in the Nigerian Context

Identifying prolonged labour promptly requires systematic physical evaluation and accurate partographic documentation. Midwives and nurses must perform comprehensive abdominal palpation, sterile vaginal examinations, and vital sign monitoring upon admission and at mandatory intervals. Abdominal examination assesses uterine tone, fetal lie, presentation, engagement, and contraction frequency over a ten-minute window. Vaginal examination evaluates cervical effacement, dilation, membrane status, and the presence of caput succedaneum or severe molding. In many resource-constrained primary and secondary healthcare centres across Nigeria, late presentation due to reliance on traditional birth attendants (TBAs) remains a major diagnostic hurdle. Patients often arrive exhausted, dehydrated, and septic, with ketoacidosis and a prominent Bandl's ring—a pathological retraction ring indicating impending uterine rupture. Nurses must recognize these signs of severe obstruction immediately to fast-track emergency obstetric care and minimize delays.

Maternal and Fetal Complications

Prolonged and obstructed labour inflicts severe physical and psychological trauma on both mother and baby. When labour is unnecessarily prolonged, tissue anoxia, pressure necrosis, and metabolic exhaustion cascade into life-threatening emergencies. | System / Recipient | Complication | Underlying Mechanism | |---|---|---| | **Maternal** | Postpartum Hemorrhage | Uterine atony secondary to muscular exhaustion from prolonged contractions | | **Maternal** | Obstetric Fistula | Ischemic necrosis of the vesicovaginal or rectovaginal septum from trapped fetal head pressure | | **Fetal** | Birth Asphyxia | Reduced placental blood flow and prolonged umbilical cord compression | | **Fetal** | Intracranial Hemorrhage | Severe molding of the fetal skull bones and prolonged mechanical pressure | Beyond physical injuries, surviving mothers often face profound social stigmatization, chronic pelvic pain, and depression, particularly following vesicovaginal fistula (VVF) development.

Immediate Nursing Care and Supportive Management

When managing a patient experiencing prolonged labour without immediate surgical indication, supportive nursing care focuses on hydration, comfort, and monitoring. Physical and emotional exhaustion severely compromises coping mechanisms. - **Fluid Balance**: Maintain adequate hydration via intravenous access, administering normal saline or Ringer's lactate to correct dehydration and ketoacidosis. - **Bladder Care**: Encourage frequent voiding every two hours. A distended bladder displaces the uterus, worsens uterine inertia, and obstructs fetal descent; catheterization may be necessary. - **Pain and Comfort**: Provide continuous emotional reassurance, back rubs, and position changes (avoiding prolonged supine positioning to prevent aortocaval compression). - **Vital Signs Monitoring**: Track maternal pulse, blood pressure, temperature, and fetal heart rate every thirty minutes to detect early signs of fetal distress or chorioamnionitis.

Medical and Surgical Interventions

When conservative measures fail to correct prolonged labour, targeted medical or surgical interventions become necessary. The choice of intervention depends directly on whether obstruction is present. - **Augmentation**: If the pelvis is adequate and CPD is ruled out, low-dose **oxytocin** infusion may be commenced to stimulate stronger and more regular uterine contractions. - **Amniotomy**: Artificial rupture of membranes can enhance uterine activity by releasing prostaglandins and allowing the fetal head to apply direct pressure to the cervix. - **Operative Vaginal Delivery**: Vacuum extraction or forceps delivery is utilized if the second stage is prolonged, the cervix is fully dilated, and the head is low in the pelvis. - **Cesarean Section**: Immediate surgical delivery is mandatory if cephalopelvic disproportion, fetal distress, or obstructed labour is diagnosed. Administering oxytocin in the presence of an obstructed pelvis is a dangerous clinical error that rapidly precipitates uterine rupture.

Prevention Strategies and Partograph Utilization

Preventing prolonged labour requires robust antenatal education, early identification of high-risk pregnancies, and universal, accurate utilization of the WHO partograph in all maternity units. Community midwifery programs in Nigeria play a vital role in educating women to seek hospital-based delivery rather than relying solely on home births. Health workers must receive continuous training on plotting cervical dilation and fetal heart rates correctly. The partograph acts as an early warning system, highlighting deviation from normal curves before exhaustion and sepsis set in. Regular facility audits, ensuring functional emergency transport systems, and eliminating financial barriers to emergency obstetric care remain essential pillars in reducing maternal morbidity and mortality nationwide.

Referral to Health Centre

Introduction to Postnatal Referral Systems

The postnatal period carries significant maternal and neonatal vulnerability, particularly within the first week after delivery. A functional referral system acts as a crucial safety net, linking primary health care clinics with secondary or tertiary facilities when complications exceed local management capacity. In many resource-constrained settings across Nigeria, delays in recognizing complications and executing timely transfers contribute heavily to preventable maternal and perinatal mortality. Understanding the three delays model—delay in deciding to seek care, delay in reaching a health facility, and delay in receiving adequate treatment—helps contextualize why structured referral pathways are mandatory for safe maternity care.

Indications for Referral During the Puerperium

During the postnatal period, routine assessments performed at primary health centres may uncover clinical findings that necessitate escalation of care. Midwives must remain vigilant for deviations from normal physiological recovery.

  • Persistent Heavy Bleeding: Lochia that remains bright red, saturates more than two pads per hour after the immediate postpartum period, or contains large clots.
  • Severe Unresolved Hypertension: Blood pressure readings consistently at or above 140/90 mmHg accompanied by persistent frontal headaches or visual disturbances.
  • Signs of Pelvic Infection: Uterine tenderness, foul-smelling lochia, and a sustained pyrexia exceeding 38°C after the first 24 hours.
  • Wound Breakdown: Severe pain, gaping wound edges, purulent discharge, or spreading cellulitis at an episiotomy or caesarean section site.
  • Mental Health Crises: Severe depressive symptoms, hallucinations, or signs of postpartum psychosis that threaten maternal safety or infant wellbeing.

Maternal Warning Signs Requiring Urgent Transfer

Certain clinical presentations demand immediate, emergency transfer without delay. These red flags signify impending cardiovascular collapse, eclampsia, or overwhelming infection. Midwives encountering any of the following must act swiftly:

  • Convulsions or fits occurring during the postpartum period.
  • Severe shortness of breath or chest pain indicative of pulmonary embolism or acute heart failure.
  • Profound lethargy, altered mental state, or unconsciousness.
  • Marked pallor accompanied by tachycardia, tachypnea, and hypotension pointing toward acute hypovolemic shock.
  • Inability to pass urine within six to eight hours post-delivery despite bladder distension.

Neonatal Red Flags and Indications for Escalation

Newborns deteriorate rapidly when faced with infection, respiratory distress, or metabolic imbalance. Because compensatory mechanisms in neonates are fragile, signs of illness must never be dismissed as transient adaptation.

  • Respiratory Distress: Grunting, nasal flaring, chest indrawing, or a respiratory rate consistently above 60 breaths per minute.
  • Thermal Instability: Persistent hypothermia (axillary temperature below 36.5°C) or fever (above 37.5°C) that fails to resolve with skin-to-skin care.
  • Feeding Intolerance: Inability to attach to the breast, persistent vomiting, or regurgitating all feeds.
  • Jaundice: Yellow discoloration of the skin and sclera appearing within the first 24 hours of life or extending to the palms and soles.
  • Infection Markers: Lethargy, umbilical redness spreading to the abdominal wall, pus discharge, or multiple skin pustules.

Roles and Responsibilities of the Midwife in the Referral Process

The midwife acts as the primary coordinator, clinical decision-maker, and advocate during any emergency transfer. When a complication arises, the midwife must quickly assess the patient, stabilize vital signs within available means, and prepare necessary documentation. Clear communication with the receiving facility ensures they anticipate the arrival of a critical patient. Furthermore, providing psychological support to the mother and her family helps alleviate panic and secures vital consent and cooperation for the journey.

Pre-Referral Stabilization and Emergency Management

Before dispatching a patient, life-threatening imbalances must be addressed to improve survival odds during transit. Stabilization is not designed to treat the root cause definitively, but to buy time and preserve vital organ perfusion. Interventions depend on the specific presentation:

  • Establishing secure intravenous access with a large-bore cannula for fluid resuscitation or blood administration.
  • Administering prescribed emergency loading doses of anticonvulsants (such as magnesium sulfate) for eclampsia.
  • Initiating broad-spectrum intravenous antibiotics when sepsis is suspected.
  • Ensuring a patent airway, providing supplemental oxygen if respiratory distress is present, and keeping the neonate warm.

Communication, Documentation, and Referral Form Completion

A poorly documented referral compromises continuity of care and forces receiving clinicians to waste precious minutes re-evaluating baseline findings. Every transfer must be accompanied by a completed official referral form containing essential data: patient demographics, clinical history, vital signs upon departure, interventions administered, and the explicit reason for transfer. Accompanying notes should be clear, concise, and handed directly to the receiving healthcare team along with the patient's antenatal and postnatal records.

Overcoming Barriers to Effective Maternal and Neonatal Referral in Nigeria

Implementing seamless referral systems in Nigeria requires navigating multifaceted operational challenges. Financial constraints, commonly referred to as out-of-pocket expenditure for transport and medications, frequently stall emergency transfers. Transportation deficits, including poor road networks and a shortage of functional ambulances, further exacerbate delays. Additionally, bureaucratic friction between primary health care boards and tertiary hospital management can impede smooth patient handovers. Mitigating these hurdles requires community sensitization, functional emergency transport schemes, and inter-facility collaboration.

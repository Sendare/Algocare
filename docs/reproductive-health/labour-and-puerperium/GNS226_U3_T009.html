Essential Obstetric Care

Introduction to Essential Obstetric Care

Essential Obstetric Care encompasses a spectrum of life-saving interventions designed to manage pregnancy, childbirth, and the postpartum period, specifically targeting the reduction of maternal and neonatal morbidity and mortality. In many resource-constrained settings, delays in recognizing complications or reaching capable health facilities remain primary drivers of adverse outcomes. The framework categorizes care into basic and comprehensive levels, ensuring that health workers at primary and secondary tiers can deliver timely interventions.

Key underlying principles focus on universal access, prompt triage, and stabilization. When a pregnant woman presents with acute distress, immediate assessment prevents minor physiological deviations from escalating into catastrophic obstetric emergencies. Midwives and nurses form the backbone of this delivery network, serving as the first line of clinical defense in community health centres and comprehensive hospitals alike.

Components of Basic Emergency Obstetric Care (BEmOC)

Basic Emergency Obstetric Care is delivered at primary healthcare facilities and consists of seven critical signal functions that can stabilize a deteriorating patient before surgical intervention becomes necessary.

  • Administration of parenteral antibiotics: Used to treat maternal sepsis, chorioamnionitis, and postpartum infections.
  • Administration of uterotonic drugs: Oxytocin, ergometrine, or misoprostol administered for the prevention and treatment of postpartum hemorrhage.
  • Administration of parenteral anticonvulsants: Magnesium sulfate given to manage severe pre-eclampsia and eclampsia.
  • Manual removal of placenta: Performed to treat retained placenta and control subsequent bleeding.
  • Removal of retained products of conception: Using manual vacuum aspiration for incomplete miscarriages or abortions.
  • Assisted vaginal delivery: Vacuum extraction or forceps application to shorten the second stage of labor.
  • Neonatal resuscitation: Initial bag-mask ventilation and stimulation for newborns who fail to breathe spontaneously at birth.

Components of Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric Care (CEmOC)

Comprehensive Emergency Obstetric Care includes all seven BEmOC signal functions with the addition of two major life-saving surgical and blood-banking capacities. Facilities offering CEmOC operate as referral centers for complicated cases that exceed the scope of primary health clinics.

  1. Perform surgery: Specifically, life-saving cesarean sections for obstructed labor, fetal distress, or failed inductions.
  2. Blood transfusion service: The capability to safely screen, store, and transfuse whole blood or blood products to treat severe hemorrhage and profound anemia.

Identification and Management of Obstetric Complications

Obstetric complications can arise without warning, demanding rapid clinical recognition. Postpartum hemorrhage, eclampsia, obstructed labor, sepsis, and complications from unsafe abortion constitute the major triad of direct maternal mortality causes.

When managing postpartum hemorrhage, nurses must quickly quantify blood loss, massage the uterus, empty the bladder, and administer prescribed uterotonics while establishing large-bore intravenous access. For eclamptic fits, maintaining a patent airway, placing the patient in the left lateral position to prevent aspiration, and administering magnesium sulfate take precedence over aggressive physical restraint. Obstructed labor is recognized through signs of severe dehydration, exhaustion, rising maternal pulse, a high-riding Bandl's ring, and non-reassuring fetal heart tones, requiring immediate fluid resuscitation and urgent surgical referral.

The Role of the Nurse-Midwife in Maternal Resuscitation

Maternal collapse requires a structured, team-based approach where the nurse-midwife coordinates immediate life support. The primary objective centers on restoring adequate tissue perfusion and oxygenation to both mother and fetus.

Initial steps involve calling for emergency assistance, securing the airway, and delivering high-flow oxygen via a non-rebreather mask. Left uterine displacement is vital in pregnant patients past twenty weeks gestation to relieve aortocaval compression from the gravid uterus, which otherwise impairs venous return and cardiac output. Establishing two large-bore peripheral intravenous lines allows rapid infusion of crystalloids or blood products. Monitoring vital signs every five to fifteen minutes evaluates the response to fluid loading and medical therapy.

Partograph Utilization and Monitoring During Labour

The partograph is a graphical tool used to record maternal and fetal well-being alongside the progress of labor. Regular plotting prevents prolonged and obstructed labor by providing an early warning system when labor deviates from normal parameters.

Crossing the alert line indicates slow progress and necessitates transfer to a higher-level facility, while crossing the action line mandates immediate intervention or surgical delivery.

Infection Prevention and Universal Precautions in Labour Wards

Puerperal sepsis remains a leading cause of maternal mortality and preventable morbidity across Nigeria. Strict adherence to infection prevention protocols breaks the chain of transmission for healthcare-associated pathogens.

Hand hygiene forms the cornerstone of prevention before and after every patient contact. Healthcare workers must routinely don personal protective equipment, including sterile gloves during vaginal examinations and surgical procedures, water-resistant aprons, and eye protection against splashes of amniotic fluid or blood. Proper biomedical waste segregation prevents needle-stick injuries and environmental contamination. Instruments require thorough decontamination, cleaning, and autoclaving before reuse. Maintaining a clean delivery environment directly correlates with lower rates of postpartum infection and neonatal sepsis.

Referral Systems and Emergency Transport in Nigeria

An efficient referral system links primary healthcare clinics with secondary and tertiary medical centers, bridging the gap between community needs and advanced care. Many maternal deaths in Nigeria stem from the second delay—reaching a suitable health facility.

Effective referrals require prior communication with the receiving facility, ensuring bed availability and specialized staff readiness. The patient must be stabilized as much as possible before transit, accompanied by a trained nurse-midwife carrying essential emergency supplies, partograph records, and a referral summary. Overcoming transportation barriers involves community-based emergency transport schemes, functioning ambulance networks, and clear communication channels, ensuring that distance does not translate into a death sentence for obstetric emergencies.

Safe Delivery

Introduction to Safe Delivery and the Clean Birth Kit

Safe delivery practices aim to prevent maternal and neonatal morbidity and mortality by minimizing infection risks and ensuring prompt intervention during childbirth. A cornerstone of this approach, particularly in resource-limited settings or community outposts across Nigeria, is the utilization of a clean birth kit. This simple yet vital package provides barrier methods and sterile instruments required when formal hospital delivery suites are inaccessible. The standard kit typically contains a clean plastic sheet to provide a dry, non-contaminated surface for delivery, soap for handwashing, a sterile blade for cutting the umbilical cord, clean ties or clamps to secure the cord, and a clean cloth to wrap the newborn. Using these components systematically interrupts the transmission chains responsible for tetanus and puerperal sepsis.

Preparation of the Delivery Environment and Equipment

A well-prepared environment directly influences maternal comfort and safety during childbirth. The delivery room should be warm, draft-free, and well-lit to facilitate clear visualization of the perineum and the newborn. Privacy must be respected to reduce maternal anxiety and promote a calm physiological response to labor.

  • Thermal Comfort: Maintain ambient room warmth to prevent newborn hypothermia, closing windows and doors against strong drafts.
  • Emergency Equipment: Ensure functional suction apparatus, bag-valve-mask devices for neonatal resuscitation, and a working light source are immediately accessible.
  • Instrument Trays: Arrange sterile delivery packs, cord clamps, scissors, and gauze in an orderly fashion on a clean surface before labor enters the second stage.

Monitoring and Assessment During Labor

Continuous observation safeguards both mother and fetus throughout the dynamic phases of labor. The partograph remains the gold standard tool for tracking cervical dilation, descent of the fetal head, and maternal-fetal well-being. Regular auscultation of the fetal heart rate using a Pinard stethoscope or Doppler ultrasound identifies early signs of fetal distress. Simultaneously, maternal pulse, blood pressure, temperature, and contraction frequency are recorded to detect physiological exhaustion or impending complications like obstructed labor or hypertensive crises. Frequent abdominal palpation confirms fetal lie, presentation, and engagement, ensuring that the labor process adheres to expected safe parameters.

The Clean Delivery Process: The Essential Cleans

The World Health Organization emphasizes the implementation of the essential cleans during childbirth to drastically lower infection rates. These practices govern every physical contact with the birth canal and the neonate. Adherence to these principles protects against ascending pathogens and neonatal infections.

Clean InterventionPrimary Clinical Purpose
Clean HandsPrevents introduction of exogenous bacteria via examiner touch
Clean SurfaceEnsures the mother and baby avoid contaminated flooring or bedding
Clean PerineumMinimizes bacterial load around the birth canal before crowning

Immediate Care of the Newborn and Apgar Scoring

The transition from intrauterine to extrauterine life requires rapid adaptation of the respiratory and circulatory systems. Immediately after birth, dry the infant thoroughly with a warm towel while assessing crying and tone. Skin-to-skin contact promotes thermal regulation, maternal bonding, and successful early initiation of breastfeeding. The Apgar score is assessed at exactly one and five minutes after birth, evaluating five physiological parameters: Appearance, Pulse, Grimace, Activity, and Respiration. Each parameter receives a score from zero to two, yielding a maximum total of ten. A score below seven at five minutes warrants continued evaluation and potential resuscitation measures.

Management of the Third Stage of Labour and Prevention of PPH

The third stage spans from the birth of the baby until the complete expulsion of the placenta and membranes. This window carries the highest risk of postpartum hemorrhage, a leading cause of maternal mortality in Nigeria. Active management of the third stage of labour is the standard intervention to reduce this risk. It consists of three interdependent steps: administering a uterotonic drug within one minute of birth, performing controlled cord traction while counter-applying pressure to the uterus abdominally, and massaging the uterine fundus after placental delivery to ensure adequate tone. Oxytocin is the preferred uterotonic due to its rapid onset and minimal side-effect profile.

Infection Prevention and Control in Obstetric Practice

Infection control protects both patients and healthcare personnel from cross-contamination in busy maternity units. Universal precautions treat all blood and body fluids as potentially infectious. Hand hygiene before and after every patient contact remains the single most effective intervention to stop hospital-acquired infections. Safe disposal of sharps prevents needlestick injuries and subsequent transmission of blood-borne pathogens such as human immunodeficiency virus and hepatitis B or C. Instruments must undergo thorough decontamination, cleaning, and autoclaving or high-level disinfection before reuse. Maintaining clean delivery spaces protects postpartum mothers from uterine infections that can lead to chronic morbidity.

Identification and Emergency Management of Intrapartum Complications

Intrapartum emergencies require swift recognition and coordinated multidisciplinary teamwork to avert catastrophe. Obstructed labor occurs when fetal descent is arrested mechanically, often resulting in uterine rupture if unmanaged. Postpartum hemorrhage demands immediate bimanual uterine compression and rapid fluid resuscitation. Shoulder dystocia requires specific maneuvers such as McRoberts positioning and suprapubic pressure to dislodge the impacted anterior fetal shoulder. Eclamptic seizures necessitate immediate administration of magnesium sulfate and airway protection. Recognizing early warning signs on the partograph prevents prolonged labor and reduces ischemic birth injuries.

Postnatal Maternal Care and Documentation

The immediate postnatal period requires rigorous surveillance as the mother transitions through the first two hours after placental delivery, a high-risk window for hemorrhage and shock. Vital signs, uterine tone, and lochial discharge are monitored every fifteen minutes for the first hour, then every thirty minutes for the second hour. Accurate and contemporaneous documentation of all intrapartum events, medications administered, fluid balance, and Apgar scores is legally and clinically mandatory. Clear records ensure seamless communication across nursing shifts and provide essential data for audit and legal protection. Finally, discharge counseling must cover danger signs such as heavy vaginal bleeding, severe headache, or fever.

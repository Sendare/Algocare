FGM Types

Introduction and Global Classification Framework

Female Genital Mutilation encompasses all procedures involving partial or total removal of the external female genitalia or other injury to the female genital organs for non-medical reasons. The World Health Organization categorizes these practices into four distinct types based on the anatomical structures altered and the severity of the procedure. This classification standardizes documentation, clinical management, and epidemiological research across global health sectors.

Type I: Clitoridectomy

Type I involves the partial or total removal of the clitoris and/or the prepuce, which is the clitoral hood. In sub-types where only the prepuce is removed, the alteration is minimal, though total clitoridectomy involves excising the sensitive erectile body. Patients presenting with a history of Type I procedure will exhibit structural absence of the visible clitoral glans upon perineal inspection.

Type II: Excision

Type II goes beyond the clitoris to include partial or total removal of the labia minora. Frequently, this procedure also involves the excision of the labia majora, making it more extensive than Type I. Physical examinations reveal varying degrees of missing external labial architecture alongside clitoral ablation, altering the protective mucosal folds of the vulva.

Type III: Infibulation

- **Definition**: The narrowing of the vaginal orifice with the creation of a covering seal. - **Method**: The seal is formed by cutting and repositioning the labia minora or labia majora, sometimes through stitching, with or without removal of the clitoris. - **Clinical Presentation**: A dense scar tissue bridge covers the vaginal introitus, leaving only a tiny pinhole for menstrual flow and urine elimination.

Type IV: Unclassified and Emerging Variations

Type IV encompasses all other harmful procedures to the female genitalia for non-medical purposes. Examples include pricking, piercing, incising, scraping, and cauterization of the genital area. Because these methods do not fit neatly into the structural definitions of the first three groups, they are grouped as unclassified modifications.

Clinical Complications Across Classification Types

Immediate complications include severe hemorrhage, acute pain, shock, and urine retention secondary to local swelling or structural obstruction. Long-term burdens vary by severity but frequently include chronic pelvic infections, dysmenorrhea, painful sexual intercourse, and extensive keloid scar formation. Psychological trauma, including anxiety and post-traumatic stress disorder, persists across all categories regardless of the specific anatomical extent.

Nursing Assessment and Sensitive Care Principles

Clinical encounters demand a trauma-informed approach that prioritizes privacy, non-judgmental communication, and gentle physical examination techniques. Nurses must document findings objectively without using stigmatizing language. When caring for women with Type III infibulation, providers must anticipate obstetric challenges and collaborate closely with multidisciplinary teams to ensure respectful, culturally competent care.

FGM Elimination Strategies and Policies in Nigeria

Understanding Female Genital Mutilation in Nigeria

Female Genital Mutilation encompasses all procedures involving partial or total removal of the external female genitalia or other injury to the female genital organs for non-medical reasons. In Nigeria, the practice is deeply rooted in cultural beliefs surrounding chastity, marriageability, and social conformity. Although prevalence rates vary widely across geopolitical zones—being highest in the South-East and South-West—the consequences remain universally severe. Immediate complications include hemorrhagic shock, severe pain, and acute urinary retention. Long-term burdens involve chronic pelvic infections, painful intercourse, psychological trauma, and complex obstetric complications during childbirth.

Legislative Frameworks and the VIO Act

The legal battle against this harmful practice received a monumental boost with the enactment of the Violence Against Persons Prohibition Act in 2015. The VIO Act explicitly prohibits female circumcision and prescribes clear criminal penalties for perpetrators, including those who perform the act and those who abet it. At the federal level, this statute provides a solid legal foundation for human rights protection. However, because Nigeria operates a federal system of government, the VIO Act initially applied only to the Federal Capital Territory, requiring individual states to domesticate the law before it could take full effect across the federation.

State-Level Policies and Anti-FGM Laws

State governments bear the responsibility of domesticating the national framework to protect women and girls locally. Several states have successfully passed dedicated child rights laws and anti-FGM edicts that empower state prosecutors and law enforcement agencies. These state laws often establish specialized family courts and referral pathways for survivors. Despite these progressive statutes, enforcement varies dramatically. States with strong political will and active ministries of women affairs report better prosecution rates, whereas others face legislative inertia where laws exist merely on paper without implementation structures.

National Action Plans and Inter-Agency Collaborations

Eradicating a deeply entrenched social practice requires coordinated multi-sectoral strategies. The Federal Ministry of Health, in partnership with agencies like the National Agency for the Prohibition of Trafficking in Persons and international bodies such as UNFPA and UNICEF, developed the National Action Plan for the Elimination of FGM. This framework synchronizes efforts across health, education, justice, and information sectors. Inter-agency task forces facilitate data sharing, community sensitization campaigns, and the training of law enforcement officers to handle sensitive gender-based violence cases appropriately.

Community-Based Approaches and Grassroots Mobilization

Top-down legislative measures alone cannot dismantle traditions sustained by community consensus. Grassroots mobilization focuses on empowering local actors to drive behavioral change from within. Interventions utilize community dialogues, town hall meetings, and peer-education models involving youth and women groups. A prominent strategy is the public declaration approach, where entire communities collectively pledge to abandon the practice. This public commitment eliminates the fear of social stigma for families choosing not to circumcise their daughters, as the social norm shifts collectively.

Role of Healthcare Professionals in FGM Prevention

Healthcare workers occupy a strategic front-line position in combating this practice. Beyond managing acute injuries and obstetric complications, nurses and midwives provide vital counseling to expectant mothers and families. A dangerous emerging trend is the medicalization of FGM—where health practitioners perform the procedure under the assumption that it is safer. Professional regulatory bodies, such as the Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria, strictly prohibit medicalized FGM, viewing it as a gross violation of medical ethics and human rights. Clinicians must educate patients, identify at-risk infants, and report violations appropriately.

Engaging Traditional and Religious Leaders

Traditional rulers and religious clerics hold immense moral authority in Nigerian society. When these opinion leaders speak out against harmful traditional practices, their followers listen. Advocacy programs actively engage emirs, obas, pastors, and imams, providing them with evidence regarding the health harms of FGM. Many religious leaders have successfully used their platforms to declare that neither Islamic tenets nor Christian doctrines mandate female circumcision. Securing the buy-in of these custodians of culture transforms them from potential resistance figures into powerful champions of eradication.

Challenges and Barriers to Policy Implementation

Despite robust policy frameworks, several structural obstacles impede the complete eradication of FGM in Nigeria. Deep-seated cultural secrecy often drives the practice underground, making clandestine procedures difficult for law enforcement officers to detect. Widespread poverty and inadequate funding for grassroots sensitization limit the reach of government agencies. Furthermore, inadequate training for judicial and law enforcement personnel leads to poor investigation and low prosecution rates. Addressing these bottlenecks requires sustained budgetary allocation, whistleblower protection, and rigorous community trust-building.

Monitoring, Evaluation, and the Path Forward

Progress tracking relies heavily on robust data collection mechanisms, such as the Nigeria Demographic and Health Survey. These datasets help policymakers identify shifting prevalence trends, evaluate the impact of interventions, and redirect resources to high-risk zones. The path forward requires strengthening the enforcement of existing laws, expanding youth-focused educational campaigns in schools, and integrating comprehensive reproductive health curricula into training institutions. Sustained political commitment and community ownership remain the ultimate guarantees of a generation free from FGM.

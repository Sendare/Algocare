Reasons for Early and Forced Marriage

Introduction to Early and Forced Marriage

Early and forced marriage remains a widespread global public health and human rights issue affecting millions of young girls each year. An early marriage, often referred to as child marriage, involves any formal marriage or informal union where at least one party is under the age of eighteen. A forced marriage occurs when one or both parties have not given free, full, and informed consent to the union. While boys can also be affected, young girls bear the heaviest burden, facing severe physical, psychological, and social consequences that can last a lifetime.

Poverty and Economic Hardship

Poverty stands as one of the most powerful drivers of early marriage. In economically disadvantaged households, a young daughter is often viewed as an economic burden. Families struggling to provide basic daily needs such as food, shelter, and school fees may marry off a young girl to reduce household expenses or to secure a bride price or dowry that can sustain the rest of the family. In some cases, parents genuinely believe they are securing a financially stable future for their child, unaware of the lifelong poverty trap this practice actually creates.

Cultural Traditions and Societal Norms

Deep-seated cultural and religious traditions frequently dictate life trajectories in many communities. When a practice has been passed down through generations, questioning it can lead to community ostracization.

  • Age-old customs: Communities often view marriage as a rite of passage into adulthood for young girls.
  • Religious misinterpretations: Certain local beliefs mistakenly use religious texts to justify marrying off minors.
  • Peer and community pressure: Families fear being socially isolated if they break away from established marital traditions.

Gender Inequality and Patriarchal Systems

Patriarchal societies place a lower value on the lives, education, and autonomy of females compared to males. When gender inequality shapes social structures, a girl's primary role is often reduced to domestic labor and childbearing. Decisions about her body, education, and future are made by male relatives. This lack of personal autonomy strips young girls of their voice, making it nearly impossible to refuse a marriage arrangement imposed by family elders.

Protection of Family Honor and Social Status

In many conservative societies, a family's social standing is closely tied to the perceived sexual purity and chastity of its female members. Premarital relationships or perceived impropriety can bring deep shame upon an entire household. To prevent this, families may force young daughters into early marriages to safeguard family honor. Furthermore, marrying off daughters early is sometimes used to forge political or economic alliances between prominent families, reinforcing social hierarchies.

Insecurity and Humanitarian Crises

Conflict, natural disasters, and displacement camps drastically increase the risk of early and forced marriage. During humanitarian crises, traditional protection structures collapse, and families face extreme destitution. Parents often view marriage as a protective shield for their daughters against sexual violence, harassment, or abduction in lawless environments. Unfortunately, these unions rarely provide true security and instead expose young girls to domestic abuse and early childbearing without adequate healthcare support.

Lack of Access to Education and Legal Protection

Education acts as a powerful deterrent against early marriage, yet millions of girls lack access to schools due to distance, cost, or discrimination. When girls drop out of school, their options narrow drastically, making marriage seem like their only viable path. Additionally, weak legal frameworks and poor enforcement of child protection laws allow perpetrators to act with impunity. Where birth and marriage registration systems are absent or corrupt, setting a legal age limit for marriage becomes difficult to enforce.

Implications for Nursing Practice and Community Advocacy

Nurses often stand on the front lines of healthcare, encountering young brides presenting with complications from early pregnancy, sexually transmitted infections, or psychological trauma. Recognizing the signs of forced marriage is essential for holistic patient care. Nurses must provide non-judgmental support, maintain strict confidentiality, and connect vulnerable patients to social work and legal resources. Beyond individual bedside care, nurses have a professional duty to participate in community education, challenging harmful practices and advocating for the health and rights of women and children.

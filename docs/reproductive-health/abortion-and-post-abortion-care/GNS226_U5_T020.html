Establishing PAC Services

Introduction and the Five Essential Elements of Post-Abortion Care

Post-Abortion Care, commonly known as PAC, is a global strategy designed to reduce maternal mortality and morbidity resulting from unsafe abortions and spontaneous miscarriages. In many Nigerian tertiary and secondary health facilities, PAC serves as a critical entry point for emergency lifesaving interventions and reproductive health services.

The framework rests on five interconnected components:

  • Community and service provider partnership: Bridging the gap between grassroots communities and health facilities to ensure prompt care-seeking behavior.
  • Counseling: Providing confidential, empathetic support to address emotional, psychological, and physical needs.
  • Treatment of complications: Managing incomplete abortions, sepsis, hemorrhage, or genital trauma using evidence-based methods.
  • Contraceptive and family planning services: Offering a chosen method before the client leaves the facility to prevent rapid repeat unintended pregnancies.
  • Reproductive and other health services: Linking clients to broader health networks for STI screening, cervical cancer checks, and immunization.

Needs Assessment and Community Readiness

Before rolling out a new PAC service point, healthcare planners must evaluate the target population's reproductive health indicators, sociocultural barriers, and existing health infrastructure. A proper needs assessment uncovers the true burden of post-abortion complications in the local catchment area, guiding resource allocation and staffing patterns.

Community readiness involves engaging local leaders, traditional birth attendants, youth groups, and religious figures. In diverse settings like Nigeria, community gatekeepers heavily influence health-seeking behaviors. When these stakeholders understand that PAC is a lifesaving medical service rather than a moral debate, they actively encourage women experiencing hemorrhage or sepsis to seek timely facility care rather than hiding at home.

Facility Readiness, Infrastructure, and Essential Supplies

A PAC clinic cannot function effectively on goodwill alone; it requires dedicated physical space, privacy, reliable utilities, and steady commodity supplies. Clinics must have a clean treatment room, examination couches, running water, and adequate lighting.

Essential equipment and consumable inventories must include:

  • Manual Vacuum Aspiration (MVA) sets and spare valves
  • Uterotonic drugs such as oxytocin, misoprostol, and ergometrine
  • Broad-spectrum antibiotics to treat or prevent pelvic infection
  • Intravenous infusion sets, cannulae, and crystalloid fluids
  • Personal Protective Equipment (gloves, aprons, eye protection)
  • A range of modern contraceptive commodities including pills, injectables, implants, and intrauterine devices

Provider Training and Competence-Based Capacity Building

Clinical proficiency in PAC requires hands-on training rather than passive lectures. Healthcare providers—including midwives, general practitioners, and community health extension workers where legally appropriate—must master infection prevention, MVA techniques, pain management, and non-judgmental counseling.

Values clarification and attitude transformation workshops are vital components of capacity building. Personal biases regarding abortion can severely compromise the quality of care, leading to punitive treatment, delayed interventions, or denial of services. Training programs create safe spaces for staff to examine their personal beliefs, ensuring they can deliver compassionate, patient-centered care to every client regardless of the circumstances surrounding their pregnancy loss.

Clinical Management of Abortion Complications

Managing abortion complications demands rapid assessment and stabilization using the ABCDE triage approach. Life-threatening conditions such as severe hemorrhage, septic shock, or visceral perforation take immediate priority over uterine evacuation.

For incomplete abortion with hemodynamic stability, uterine evacuation is performed using MVA or medical regimens involving misoprostol. If signs of pelvic infection or sepsis are present, broad-spectrum antibiotic therapy must be initiated promptly, and evacuation should proceed without unnecessary delay to remove infected retained products of conception. Pain management—ranging from verbal reassurance and paracervical block to systemic analgesia—is an absolute standard of care that must accompany every invasive procedure.

Integrating Family Planning and Counseling Services

The PAC counseling session serves as a vital bridge between emergency treatment and long-term reproductive health. Because a woman's fertility returns almost immediately after an abortion or miscarriage, initiating contraception before discharge is essential to prevent rapid repeat unintended pregnancies.

Counseling must be voluntary, confidential, and client-centered. Providers should present a wide range of contraceptive methods—including short-acting, long-acting reversible contraceptives (LARCs) like implants and intrauterine devices, and permanent methods—allowing the client to make an informed choice based on her life goals. Whenever possible and desired, the chosen contraceptive method should be provided right in the PAC clinic before the client leaves.

Monitoring, Evaluation, and Quality Improvement

Sustainable PAC services require robust monitoring systems to track clinical outcomes, complication rates, supply chain reliability, and client satisfaction. Facility managers utilize registers and health management information systems to record service uptake and monitor key performance indicators.

Quality improvement initiatives rely on regular clinical audits and confidential morbidity and mortality reviews. By analyzing near-miss cases and adverse events without a punitive mindset, multidisciplinary healthcare teams identify systemic gaps—such as delayed referrals, stockouts of uterotonics, or inconsistent infection control practices—and implement targeted corrective actions to elevate overall standards of care.

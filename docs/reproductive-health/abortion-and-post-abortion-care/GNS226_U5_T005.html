Classification of Abortion

Introduction and Definition of Abortion in Nursing

In clinical nursing, **abortion** refers to the termination of pregnancy before the fetus reaches the period of viability, which is globally defined as under 24 weeks of gestation or a fetal weight of less than 500 grams. Understanding this threshold is critical for documentation, legal reporting, and determining appropriate patient care pathways in Nigerian obstetric wards. Clinically, pregnancy loss is divided into two broad categories: those that occur naturally without human intervention and those that are deliberately initiated. Grasping these foundational distinctions allows nursing students to communicate accurately with multidisciplinary teams, provide empathetic, non-judgmental care, and recognize early signs of physiological instability in women presenting with acute pelvic pain or bleeding.

Spontaneous versus Induced Abortion: The General Framework

- **Spontaneous Abortion**: Commonly known as a miscarriage, this occurs naturally due to chromosomal abnormalities, maternal systemic illnesses, or anatomical defects without external interference. - **Induced Abortion**: This involves the intentional termination of a pregnancy through medicinal agents or surgical procedures. It can be further subdivided based on legal frameworks and safety standards. Recognizing the etiology of pregnancy loss dictates the immediate clinical approach. Spontaneous losses often require emotional support and management of incomplete tissue passage, whereas induced cases—particularly in resource-constrained environments where restrictive laws force underground procedures—frequently present with severe sepsis, hemorrhage, and trauma requiring emergency resuscitation.

Detailed Subtypes of Spontaneous Abortion

Spontaneous abortion manifests in several distinct clinical stages, each presenting unique diagnostic criteria and nursing management strategies: 1. **Threatened Abortion**: Vaginal bleeding occurs with or without mild cramping, but the cervical os remains closed, and the pregnancy continues. 2. **Inevitable Abortion**: Bleeding and cramping worsen, and the internal cervical os is dilated, making the continuation of the pregnancy impossible. 3. **Incomplete Abortion**: Some, but not all, products of conception are expelled from the uterus, requiring medical or surgical evacuation to prevent hemorrhage. 4. **Complete Abortion**: All products of conception are naturally and entirely expelled, verified by a closed cervix and subsequent ultrasound. 5. **Missed Abortion**: The embryo or fetus dies in utero, but all products are retained for a prolonged period without immediate expulsion. 6. **Septic Abortion**: Any form of spontaneous or induced abortion complicated by intrauterine infection, presenting a high risk for septic shock.

Classification of Induced Abortion: Legal and Safe versus Unsafe

Induced abortion is classified based on the legality of the procedure, safety protocols, and the clinical environment in which it is performed. Safe induced abortion is carried out using WHO-recommended methods, such as medical regimens or manual vacuum aspiration, performed by trained personnel within legal frameworks. Conversely, unsafe abortion remains a major public health challenge in developing regions, including Nigeria. It is defined as a procedure for terminating an unintended pregnancy carried out either by persons lacking necessary skills or in an environment that does not conform to minimal medical standards. Unsafe methods frequently involve toxic herbal concoctions, mechanical trauma via foreign objects, and unsterile instrumentation, leading directly to severe pelvic infections, uterine perforation, and high maternal mortality.

Gestational Age and Anatomical Classifications

Classifying abortions by gestational age determines the clinical method used for uterine evacuation. Early abortions occur within the first trimester (up to 12 completed weeks) and are typically managed safely with medical abortion using mifepristone and misoprostol, or manual vacuum aspiration. Second-trimester abortions occur between 13 and 24 weeks and carry significantly higher physiological and psychological risks, often requiring specialized surgical dilation and evacuation or induction protocols. Anatomical classifications focus on the physical site of pregnancy implantation outside the normal uterine cavity, such as ectopic pregnancies or molar pregnancies (gestational trophoblastic disease). These abnormal implantations mimic abortion symptoms but require entirely different surgical and oncological management pathways.

Post-Abortion Care and Complications Management

Comprehensive post-abortion care (PAC) is a vital nursing responsibility designed to treat acute complications, provide compassionate emotional support, and offer immediate contraceptive counseling to prevent repeat unintended pregnancies. Nurses must monitor for delayed hemorrhage, persistent pelvic pain, and signs of endometritis or pelvic inflammatory disease. Furthermore, for Rh-negative women experiencing any type of abortion, the timely administration of **Rho(D) immune globulin** within 72 hours of the event is mandatory to prevent maternal sensitization and future hemolytic disease of the newborn. Empathic communication during this vulnerable period helps alleviate the stigma and psychological distress frequently experienced by patients.

Management of Abortion

Classification and Clinical Types of Abortion

Abortion refers to the termination of pregnancy before 20 weeks of gestation, or the expulsion of a fetus weighing less than 500 grams. Clinically, it is classified into distinct categories that dictate immediate nursing and medical action.

  • Threatened abortion: Vaginal bleeding occurs before 20 weeks with a closed internal cervical os; the pregnancy may continue.
  • Inevitable abortion: Heavy bleeding and uterine contractions present with an open cervical os, meaning expulsion is certain.
  • Incomplete abortion: Some products of conception are expelled while others remain inside the uterine cavity, causing persistent bleeding.
  • Complete abortion: All products of conception have spontaneously and entirely passed out of the uterus.
  • Missed abortion: The embryo or fetus dies in utero without products being expelled, often discovered incidentally on ultrasound.
  • Septic abortion: Any form of abortion complicated by intrauterine infection, a frequent emergency in Nigerian tertiary centres due to unsafe procedures.

Clinical Assessment and Diagnostic Evaluation

Evaluating a patient presenting with suspected abortion requires a rapid, systematic approach. Begin by assessing hemodynamic stability through vital signs, looking out for tachycardia and hypotension which signal significant hemorrhage or sepsis.

Obtain a detailed menstrual and obstetric history, noting the exact gestational age and characteristics of vaginal bleeding or expelled tissue. Perform a gentle speculum examination to visualize the cervix, quantify bleeding, and check for products of conception protruding through the os. Bimanual examination estimates uterine size, consistency, and adnexal tenderness.

Laboratory and imaging investigations confirm the diagnosis. A qualitative or quantitative serum beta-hCG test establishes pregnancy status, while a transvaginal ultrasound identifies intrauterine contents, cardiac activity, or retained tissue. Always screen for rhesus status; unsensitized Rh-negative women require anti-D immunoglobulin within 72 hours of symptom onset to prevent alloimmunization.

Immediate Resuscitation and Hemodynamic Stabilization

Patients presenting with heavy bleeding or septic shock secondary to an abortion demand immediate emergency intervention before any definitive uterine evacuation is attempted.

Secure large-bore intravenous access immediately using 16-gauge or 18-gauge cannulae to enable rapid fluid resuscitation with normal saline or Ringer's lactate. Draw blood samples concurrently for a complete blood count, grouping and cross-matching, and coagulation profile. Administer supplemental oxygen via face mask to maintain tissue perfusion. If signs of infection or septic shock are present, initiate broad-spectrum intravenous antibiotics promptly. Monitoring intake and output via an indwelling urinary catheter ensures adequate renal perfusion is maintained.

Medical Management Protocols

Medical management offers a non-surgical alternative for incomplete, missed, or inevitable abortions up to a certain gestational age, usually under 12 to 14 weeks.

  • Mifepristone: An antiprogestin that primes the cervix and sensitizes the myometrium, used in combination regimens.
  • Misoprostol: A prostaglandin analogue that binds to myometrial cells, causing strong uterine contractions and cervical softening to expel products of conception.

Nursing responsibilities include preparing the patient for anticipated cramping and heavy bleeding, providing prescribed analgesics, and monitoring vital signs and expelled tissue to confirm complete evacuation.

Surgical Evacuation Techniques

Surgical intervention is indicated when medical management fails, heavy hemorrhage continues, or signs of intrauterine infection require immediate tissue clearance.

Nursing care involves ensuring informed consent, administering pre-procedural pain relief or paracervical block, monitoring vital signs, and observing for post-procedural complications such as uterine perforation or excessive bleeding.

Post-Abortion Care (PAC) and Patient Counseling

Post-abortion care is a comprehensive model designed to treat abortion complications, provide contraceptive counseling, and link women to broader reproductive health services.

Nursing counseling must be empathetic, non-judgmental, and confidential, addressing both physical recovery and emotional well-being. Discuss normal physiological changes, including intermittent cramping and mild bleeding expected over the next two weeks. Emphasize warning signs that require immediate hospital return, such as foul-smelling vaginal discharge, severe pelvic pain, or high fever.

Crucially, ovulation can resume as early as two weeks following an abortion. Therefore, contraception counseling should be initiated before hospital discharge, offering options ranging from barrier methods to long-acting reversible contraceptives (LARCs) based on patient preference.

Prevention and Management of Complications

Complications arising from unsafe or incomplete abortions contribute significantly to maternal morbidity and mortality. Immediate complications include severe hemorrhage, hypovolemic shock, and uterine perforation. Delayed complications involve pelvic inflammatory disease (PID), chronic pelvic pain, Asherman's syndrome from intrauterine adhesions, and secondary infertility.

Management focuses on prompt identification and aggressive treatment. Administer blood transfusions for severe anemia, broad-spectrum intravenous antibiotics for septic abortion, and prepare the patient for exploratory laparotomy if uterine perforation or bowel injury is suspected. Preventive strategies centre on expanding access to safe post-abortion care, comprehensive family planning education, and safe legal termination services where permitted.

Legal, Ethical, and Professional Responsibilities in Nigeria

In Nigeria, the legal framework governing abortion is restrictive, derived from the Criminal Code in the southern states and the Penal Code in the northern states. Generally, abortion is illegal unless performed strictly to save the pregnant woman's life. Performing or procuring an illegal abortion carries severe legal penalties for both the practitioner and the patient.

Despite legal restrictions, professional ethics mandate that nurses and midwives must provide emergency life-saving care to any patient presenting with abortion complications, regardless of the circumstances surrounding the procedure. Upholding patient confidentiality, maintaining a non-judgmental attitude, and prioritizing immediate resuscitation over moral evaluation are paramount nursing duties.

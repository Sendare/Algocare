Maternal Mortality from Unsafe Abortion

Introduction and Epidemiology of Unsafe Abortion in Nigeria

Maternal mortality remains a pressing public health challenge in Nigeria, with unsafe abortion contributing a significant percentage of preventable maternal deaths. An unsafe abortion is defined by the World Health Organization as a procedure for terminating an unintended pregnancy carried out either by persons lacking the necessary skills or in an environment that does not conform to minimal medical standards, or both. In many resource-constrained settings, restrictive legal frameworks, societal stigma, and limited access to modern contraception force women and girls to seek clandestine terminations. These procedures are frequently performed using dangerous methods—ranging from ingestion of toxic chemicals and herbal concoctions to the insertion of unsterile foreign objects—which drastically elevate the risk of severe morbidity and mortality.

Direct Medical Causes of Mortality and Pathophysiology

  • Hemorrhage: Severe bleeding resulting from uterine atony, cervical lacerations, or retained products of conception.
  • Sepsis: Systemic infection triggered by the introduction of unsterile instruments or unhygienic substances into the uterine cavity.
  • Visceral Injury: Direct mechanical perforation of the uterus, bladder, or bowel by sharp objects.
  • Toxicity: Systemic poisoning and multi-organ failure caused by ingestion of toxic concoctions, caustic chemicals, or traditional herbs.

When these pathological insults occur, they rapidly progress through hypovolemic shock, bacteremia, and disseminated intravascular coagulation if not aggressively intercepted by emergency medical interventions.

Clinical Assessment and Recognition of Complications

Nurses must maintain a high index of suspicion when assessing female patients of reproductive age presenting with acute abdomen, abnormal vaginal bleeding, or signs of systemic shock, regardless of whether they admit to a prior termination attempt.

Initial assessment focuses on the vital signs to identify hemodynamic instability. A rigid, tender abdomen combined with rebound tenderness strongly indicates uterine perforation or pelvic peritonitis. Speculum examination may reveal active cervical bleeding, tissue protruding through the os, or chemical burns on the vaginal walls. Laboratory investigations should immediately include a complete blood count, blood typing and cross-matching, serum electrolytes, and a pregnancy test to guide urgent resuscitation.

Emergency Nursing Management and Resuscitation

Stabilizing a patient with severe abortion complications follows standard trauma and resuscitation principles. The immediate nursing priorities focus on the ABCs: securing a patent airway, ensuring adequate breathing, and restoring circulatory volume. Two large-bore intravenous cannulas must be inserted instantly to infuse isotonic crystalloids and whole blood or packed cells as ordered.

If the patient is actively bleeding and products of conception remain in the cervical os, ring forceps may be used to gently remove loose tissue if trained and authorized. Prepare the patient for emergency surgical evacuation, such as manual vacuum aspiration or dilation and curettage, while administering broad-spectrum intravenous antibiotics to combat potential sepsis.

Post-Abortion Care (PAC) Protocols and Value Clarification

Post-Abortion Care is an essential health-service model designed to reduce maternal death and injury. It encompasses emergency treatment of incomplete and unsafe abortion, contraceptive counseling, and the linkage of women to broader reproductive health services.

Beyond clinical skills, healthcare providers must undergo value clarification exercises. These structured workshops help nurses and midwives recognize and set aside personal, moral, or religious biases so they can deliver non-judgmental, compassionate, and life-saving care to every patient regardless of the circumstances surrounding her pregnancy.

Preventative Strategies and Universal Access to Reproductive Health

Preventing maternal mortality from unsafe abortion requires addressing the root causes through robust primary healthcare initiatives. Community-based sex education, youth-friendly reproductive health clinics, and unrestricted access to modern, effective contraceptives significantly reduce unintended pregnancies. Nurses play a frontline role in educating adolescents, dispelling myths regarding family planning, and offering confidential contraceptive services. Furthermore, reforming restrictive legal frameworks and expanding access to safe, legal abortion services where permitted by law remain vital policy measures for safeguarding women's health and lowering national maternal mortality ratios.

Termination of Unwanted Pregnancy

Legal Framework and Realities of Pregnancy Termination in Nigeria

In Nigeria, the legal framework governing the termination of pregnancy is strictly regulated by the Criminal Code (applicable in the Southern states) and the Penal Code (applicable in the Northern states). Under these laws, procuring an abortion is generally illegal unless it is performed solely to save the pregnant woman's life. Performing an illegal termination carries severe prison sentences for both the provider and the woman. Despite these restrictive laws, unwanted pregnancies and subsequent terminations occur frequently, often driven by poverty, lack of sex education, and limited access to modern contraceptives. When safe, legal options are unavailable or out of reach, many women resort to clandestine, unsafe procedures performed by untrained individuals using crude methods. As healthcare professionals, nurses must understand these legal boundaries while prioritizing compassionate, non-judgmental emergency care for patients suffering from complications of unsafe procedures, as saving a life supersedes punitive approaches.

Safe Versus Unsafe Abortion: Definitions and Classifications

Understanding the distinction between safe and unsafe termination is vital for clinical triage and public health nursing.

  • Safe Abortion: A procedure performed using methods recommended by the World Health Organization, appropriate to the gestational age, and carried out by trained healthcare personnel with proper medical equipment and sanitary standards.
  • Unsafe Abortion: A procedure for terminating an unwanted pregnancy carried out either by persons lacking the necessary skills or in an environment that does not conform to minimal medical standards, or both.

Unsafe procedures often involve caustic herbal insertions, abdominal massage, blind uterine instrumentation with knitting needles or sticks, and ingestion of toxic chemicals. These methods frequently cause severe trauma, heavy bleeding, and fatal systemic infections, turning what should be a routine healthcare intervention into a life-threatening emergency.

Clinical Assessment and Pre-Termination Counseling

When a patient presents seeking assistance or experiencing complications related to an unwanted pregnancy, a thorough, empathetic clinical assessment is mandatory. The nurse must:

  1. Obtain an accurate history: Determine the last menstrual period, gestational age estimate, and details of any interventions already attempted.
  2. Evaluate vital signs: Screen immediately for signs of hemorrhagic shock, sepsis, or acute pain.
  3. Perform physical and pelvic examinations: Assess uterine size, cervical dilation, and active bleeding.
  4. Provide pre-procedure counseling: Offer a safe space to discuss feelings, ensure the decision is voluntary, and outline what to expect during the procedure or recovery.

Non-judgmental communication builds trust. Many patients fear hospital staff due to societal stigma and legal threats; nurses must maintain strict confidentiality to ensure patients feel secure disclosing dangerous self-induced attempts.

Medical and Surgical Methods of Safe Termination

Where legal and clinical conditions permit, safe pregnancy termination relies on evidence-based medical regimens or minor surgical evacuation.

Medical termination typically utilizes a combination of mifepristone (which blocks progesterone) followed by misoprostol (which induces uterine contractions) for early first-trimester pregnancies. This regimen mimics a natural miscarriage and avoids surgical instruments.

Surgical termination is predominantly performed using Manual Vacuum Aspiration (MVA). MVA is a safe, highly effective outpatient procedure that uses a gentle hand-held syringe to empty the uterine contents under local anesthesia. It is preferred over older sharp curettage methods because it carries a significantly lower risk of uterine perforation and severe scarring.

Complications of Unsafe Termination and Early Recognition

Unsafe terminations are responsible for a high proportion of maternal morbidity and mortality. Nurses must be vigilant in recognizing early signs of impending disaster.

ComplicationEarly Clinical SignsNursing Action
HemorrhageHeavy vaginal bleeding, tachycardia, pale skin, dizzinessAdminister uterotonics, start IV fluids, notify doctor
SepsisFoul-smelling vaginal discharge, fever, lower abdominal painCollect blood cultures, administer broad-spectrum antibiotics
PerforationSevere acute abdominal pain, rigid abdomen, shoulder tip painPrepare for emergency exploratory laparotomy

Recognizing these complications early prevents progression to irreversible septic or hemorrhagic shock.

Comprehensive Post-Abortion Care (PAC) Nursing Interventions

Post-Abortion Care (PAC) is an emergency response model designed to treat abortion-related complications, but it extends far beyond immediate physical treatment. Comprehensive PAC nursing interventions encompass five essential elements:

  1. Emergency treatment: Managing bleeding, shock, and sepsis promptly.
  2. Counseling: Addressing emotional needs, anxiety, and psychological trauma associated with the ordeal.
  3. Contraceptive services: Helping the patient choose and initiate a suitable family planning method before discharge, as fertility returns almost immediately.
  4. Reproductive health services: Screening for sexually transmitted infections and offering cervical cancer screening where appropriate.
  5. Community linkage: Connecting patients to social support networks and follow-up care.

Holistic PAC reduces repeat unwanted pregnancies and ensures the patient heals both physically and mentally.

Contraceptive Counseling and Preventing Future Unwanted Pregnancies

The ultimate goal of reproductive health education is the prevention of unwanted pregnancies and unsafe abortions through effective contraception. Nurses serve as educators and advocates, guiding clients through a diverse range of modern options including barrier methods, hormonal pills, injectables, implants, and intrauterine devices (IUDs). Effective counseling is tailored to the client's lifestyle, reproductive goals, and health history rather than imposing a single method. Dispelling common cultural myths—such as the belief that contraceptives cause permanent infertility or accumulate in the stomach—is crucial for improving uptake. By empowering women and adolescents with accurate knowledge and accessible contraceptive supplies, nurses play a frontline role in reducing maternal mortality associated with unsafe abortion.

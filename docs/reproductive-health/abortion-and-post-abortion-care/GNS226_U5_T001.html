Maternal Morbidity from Unsafe Abortion

Introduction to Maternal Morbidity and Unsafe Abortion in Nigeria

Maternal morbidity refers to any short- or long-term health condition attributed to or aggravated by pregnancy and childbirth. In Nigeria, unsafe abortion remains a major driver of preventable maternal death and severe illness. When pregnancy termination is performed either by persons lacking the necessary skills or in an environment that does not conform to minimal medical standards, the risk of physical harm increases exponentially.

Many young women and girls resort to clandestine procedures due to strict legal restrictions, social stigma, and poor access to safe reproductive health services. These unsafe methods often involve harmful traditional practices, ingestion of toxic concoctions, or invasive mechanical interventions using unsterile instruments. Understanding the scope of this public health challenge helps nurses approach affected patients with empathy, free from judgment, while delivering prompt, life-saving care.

Pathophysiology of Abortion-Related Injury

Injuries resulting from unsafe termination generally stem from mechanical trauma, introduction of pathogens, or systemic toxicity. When unsterile objects like knitting needles, twigs, or dirty catheters are inserted into the uterine cavity, they can easily puncture the uterine wall, lacerate the cervix, or damage adjacent pelvic organs such as the bowel and bladder.

Furthermore, incomplete expulsion of products of conception prevents the uterus from contracting effectively. This leads to persistent open blood vessels at the placental site, causing severe hemorrhage. If bacteria ascend from the lower genital tract during the procedure, endometritis rapidly develops, which can progress to pelvic inflammatory disease, septicemia, and irreversible multi-organ failure if left untreated.

Classification and Types of Complications

Complications arising from unsafe abortion are broadly divided into immediate, delayed, and long-term categories. Recognizing these categories allows nurses to anticipate clinical needs during the patient's recovery trajectory.

  • Immediate Complications: Severe hemorrhage, uterine perforation, visceral injury to the bowel or bladder, and acute anaphylactic shock from toxic substances.
  • Delayed Complications: Pelvic sepsis, retained products of conception, parametritis, and thromboembolic events.
  • Long-term Complications: Secondary infertility, chronic pelvic pain, tubal blockage, menstrual irregularities, and psychological trauma such as depression or post-traumatic stress.

Clinical Assessment and Diagnostic Evaluation

A thorough nursing and medical assessment begins with a rapid primary survey focusing on airway, breathing, and circulation. Nurses must evaluate vital signs for signs of shock, such as tachycardia, tachypnea, and hypotension. A detailed history should be gathered gently, paying attention to the last menstrual period, the estimated gestational age, and any history of interference.

Diagnostic investigations are vital to confirm the diagnosis and guide treatment:

Emergency Nursing Management and Stabilization

Emergency management follows the ABCDE resuscitation framework. Establishing large-bore intravenous access is an immediate priority for fluid resuscitation and administration of prescribed uterotonics like oxytocin or misoprostol to encourage uterine contraction and reduce bleeding.

When sepsis is suspected, blood cultures must be drawn promptly, followed by the immediate administration of broad-spectrum intravenous antibiotics as ordered. Nurses should monitor urine output hourly using an indwelling urinary catheter, as urine output is a sensitive indicator of renal perfusion and overall shock recovery. If uterine perforation or intra-abdominal injury is suspected, prepare the patient urgently for exploratory laparotomy while offering constant reassurance.

Post-Abortion Care (PAC) and Holistic Rehabilitation

Post-abortion care is a comprehensive approach that extends far beyond immediate physical stabilization. Once the acute crisis is managed, nurses must address the emotional and psychological needs of the patient. Shame, guilt, fear of legal consequences, and grief are common emotional responses that require a compassionate, non-judgmental listening ear.

PAC services also incorporate contraceptive counseling and provision before discharge. Fertility can return as early as two weeks after an abortion, meaning patients need clear guidance on preventing rapid repeat unintended pregnancies. Nurses should provide information on various family planning methods and supply the chosen method prior to discharge, ensuring the patient feels supported and empowered in her reproductive health choices.

Preventative Strategies, Counseling, and Policy

Preventing maternal morbidity from unsafe abortion requires a multi-layered approach involving community education, youth-friendly healthcare services, and supportive health policies. Nurses play a frontline role in advocacy and education, working to dispel myths about contraception and reduce the social stigma surrounding reproductive health.

Community outreach programs should focus on educating adolescents and women about effective family planning options to prevent unintended pregnancies. Additionally, healthcare facilities must ensure that emergency contraceptive pills and barrier methods are readily accessible. By participating in policy dialogues and promoting harm-reduction strategies, nursing professionals contribute significantly to lowering the national burden of unsafe abortion.

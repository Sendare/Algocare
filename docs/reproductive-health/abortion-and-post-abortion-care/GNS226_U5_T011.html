Medication Abortion

Introduction and Mechanism of Action

Medication abortion involves using specific pharmaceutical agents to terminate an early pregnancy without surgical instruments. The standard protocol globally relies on a combination of two primary drugs: mifepristone and misoprostol.

  • Mifepristone: A synthetic steroid compound that acts as a progesterone receptor antagonist. By blocking progesterone, it causes the decidua basalis to break down, softening the cervix and detaching the gestational sac.
  • Misoprostol: A synthetic prostaglandin E1 analog. Taken typically 24 to 48 hours after mifepristone, it stimulates forceful contractions of the myometrium, leading to the expulsion of pregnancy tissue.

Indications and Eligibility Criteria

Medication abortion is approved and safe primarily for early pregnancies. Clinical guidelines generally set the gestational age limit at up to 10 to 11 weeks of pregnancy, calculated from the first day of the last menstrual period. Beyond this window, the risk of incomplete abortion and heavy bleeding rises significantly.

Absolute contraindications to this regimen include confirmed or suspected ectopic pregnancy, chronic adrenal failure, concurrent long-term systemic corticosteroid therapy, and known allergy to either mifepristone or misoprostol. Additionally, patients with an intrauterine device (IUD) in place must have it removed prior to initiating the medication protocol.

Pre-Procedure Assessment and Counseling

Before dispensing any abortion medications, a thorough clinical assessment is mandatory. This process confirms the intrauterine location of the pregnancy and establishes accurate gestational dating, typically via transvaginal ultrasound or serial beta-hCG blood tests. Baseline hemoglobin or hematocrit checks help evaluate for pre-existing anemia.

Counseling focuses on setting realistic expectations. Patients need to understand that the process resembles a heavy miscarriage, accompanied by significant cramping and blood clots. Providing a clear timeline helps alleviate anxiety and prepares them for what to expect at home.

Clinical Protocol and Administration Steps

  1. Mifepristone Administration: The patient swallows 200 mg of mifepristone orally, either at the clinic or at home depending on local regulations.
  2. Misoprostol Timing: Between 24 and 48 hours later, the patient administers 800 mcg of misoprostol. Routes include buccal, sublingual, or vaginal.
  3. Pain Management: Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, such as ibuprofen, are commonly provided to manage uterine cramping.
  4. Rest and Observation: The patient rests in a comfortable environment while awaiting the onset of uterine contractions and bleeding.

Expected Side Effects and Normal Process

The process of medical abortion involves predictable physiological symptoms that can be intense. Once misoprostol is taken, cramping usually starts within hours, accompanied by vaginal bleeding that is heavier than a normal menstrual period. Passing blood clots and small tissue fragments is expected and indicates that the regimen is working.

Systemic side effects from misoprostol are temporary and self-limiting. They frequently include low-grade fever, chills, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and dizziness. These symptoms usually resolve within 24 hours of taking the second medication without specific medical intervention.

Identifying Complications and Warning Signs

Although medication abortion is generally safe, nurses must educate patients on red flags that require urgent medical evaluation.

  • Heavy Bleeding: Soaking through two full-sized sanitary pads per hour for two consecutive hours is a warning sign of hemorrhage.
  • Infection: A fever of 38°C (100.4°F) or higher persisting past 24 hours after taking misoprostol, or severe pelvic pain that does not improve with pain relievers, suggests possible endometritis.
  • Ongoing Pregnancy: A lack of bleeding altogether after taking both medications indicates treatment failure, requiring further clinical management.

Post-Abortion Care, Contraception, and Follow-Up

Fertility returns very quickly following a medication abortion, sometimes within two weeks. Therefore, providing immediate post-procedure contraception is a vital component of holistic care. Most contraceptive methods—including oral contraceptive pills, injectables, implants, and intrauterine devices—can be initiated on the same day the misoprostol is taken.

A follow-up check is scheduled typically 1 to 3 weeks later. This visit confirms complete expulsion of the pregnancy tissue, assesses emotional well-being, and ensures that the patient is recovering without lingering complications.

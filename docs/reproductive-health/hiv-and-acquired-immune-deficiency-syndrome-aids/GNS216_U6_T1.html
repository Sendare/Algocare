Basics of HIV and AIDS

Introduction and Pathophysiology of HIV

Human Immunodeficiency Virus is a retrovirus that fundamentally alters the immune system by targeting and destroying CD4+ T-lymphocytes, which act as the primary coordinators of cellular immunity. Without these crucial helper cells, the body loses its ability to fight off opportunistic pathogens. Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome represents the most advanced stage of this viral destruction. The hallmark of progression is a severe drop in the absolute CD4 count, typically falling below 200 cells per microliter, leaving the host vulnerable to specific life-threatening infections and malignancies that a healthy immune system easily repels.

Modes of Transmission

  • Sexual Contact: Unprotected vaginal, anal, or oral intercourse with an infected partner accounts for the majority of global transmissions through contact with mucosal membranes or seminal and vaginal fluids.
  • Blood and Blood Products: Sharing contaminated needles, syringes, or equipment among people who inject drugs, along with transfusion of unscreened blood products, provides direct systemic entry.
  • Mother-to-Child Transmission: Vertical transmission can occur in utero, during labor and delivery, or via postpartum breastfeeding if maternal viral load is unsuppressed.

Stages of Infection Progression

  1. Acute Infection: Occurs two to four weeks after exposure, marked by a massive viral spike and flu-like symptoms as the body mounts an initial immune response.
  2. Clinical Latency: Also known as chronic HIV, the virus reproduces at very low levels while remaining active. This phase can last for decades with antiretroviral medications or roughly a decade without them.
  3. AIDS: The final, most severe stage characterized by systemic failure of the immune system, severe opportunistic infections, and extreme weight loss or wasting.

Clinical Manifestations and Opportunistic Infections

As immunodeficiency deepens, unusual pathogens take advantage of the compromised host defenses. Common opportunistic infections include Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia, oral candidiasis (thrush), cryptococcal meningitis, and severe tuberculosis. Neoplasms like Kaposi sarcoma and non-Hodgkin lymphoma also frequently appear. In many regions of sub-Saharan Africa, chronic wasting syndrome and persistent diarrheal illnesses remain major indicators of advanced disease progression that require vigilant nutritional and symptomatic nursing care.

Diagnostic and Laboratory Testing Methods

Accurate detection relies on serological assays and virological testing. Rapid diagnostic tests using finger-prick blood or oral fluid provide preliminary screening results within minutes. Confirmatory testing often involves fourth-generation combination assays that detect both HIV antibodies and the p24 antigen, shortening the window period. Polymerase Chain Reaction tests measure viral load to monitor disease progression and the effectiveness of therapeutic regimens.

Antiretroviral Therapy and Clinical Management

Antiretroviral therapy combines multiple medications from different drug classes to block viral replication at various stages of its lifecycle. The primary goal is achieving and maintaining an undetectable viral load, which halts disease progression and prevents sexual transmission. Adherence to the daily regimen is critical; missing doses allows resistant viral strains to emerge, rendering standard drug combinations ineffective. Nurses play a pivotal role in counseling clients on adherence strategies and managing adverse drug reactions.

Prevention Strategies and Nursing Interventions

Comprehensive prevention combines biomedical, behavioral, and structural approaches. Pre-exposure prophylaxis offers uninfected individuals a way to prevent acquisition through daily medication use. Post-exposure prophylaxis provides a short course of antiretroviral drugs following occupational or non-occupational exposure. Nursing interventions focus on destigmatizing the condition, promoting barrier contraception, offering confidential counseling, and supporting holistic wellbeing through nutrition and mental health resources.

Postpartum Care

Introduction and Definition of Postpartum Care

The postpartum period, or **puerperium**, begins immediately after the delivery of the placenta and typically extends for six weeks. This phase represents a critical transition where the maternal body reverses the profound anatomical and physiological adaptations achieved during pregnancy. Postpartum care encompasses the systematic monitoring, physical support, and psychological evaluation of the mother to prevent complications, promote recovery, and facilitate successful parenting. In many Nigerian healthcare settings, this window spans from the delivery room through the crucial postnatal clinic visits at six weeks. Historically, clinical focus heavily favored the newborn, yet contemporary nursing recognizes maternal survival and well-being as equally paramount. Comprehensive care addresses the triad of physical healing, emotional stabilization, and social adaptation.

Immediate Physiological Changes and Involution

Uterine **involution** is the rapid reduction in the size of the uterus returning to its pre-pregnant state. Immediately following placental expulsion, the fundus should feel firm, globular, and sit roughly at the level of the umbilicus. Over the next two weeks, the uterus descends into the true pelvis at a rate of approximately one centimeter per day. - **Lochia Rubra**: Deep red discharge occurring during the first one to three days, consisting of blood, decidual tissue, and mucus. - **Lochia Serosa**: Pinkish-brown discharge persisting from around day four to day ten, composed of serous exudate, erythrocytes, and leukocytes. - **Lochia Alba**: Yellowish-white discharge lasting from week two to week six, containing leukocytes, epithelial cells, and mucus. Simultaneously, the cardiovascular system undergoes rapid shifts. Blood volume decreases due to the elimination of uteroplacental circulation, often resulting in transient postpartum diuresis and diaphoresis.

Comprehensive Maternal Assessment in the Puerperium

Systematic assessment in the early postpartum hours relies on the **BUBBLE-EE** mnemonic to structure clinical evaluation: - **Breasts**: Assess contour, nipple integrity, and engorgement status. - **Uterus**: Check fundal tone, height, and midline position. - **Bladder**: Monitor spontaneous voiding, distension, and urinary retention. - **Bowel**: Evaluate passage of flatus, bowel sounds, and presence of constipation. - **Lochia**: Observe color, quantity, odor, and presence of clots. - **Episiotomy/Perineum**: Inspect lacerations or repair sites for edema, hematoma, and approximation. - **Emotions**: Observe mood, bonding, fatigue levels, and signs of distress. - **Extremities**: Screen for unilateral swelling, warmth, or tenderness indicating deep vein thrombosis. Failing to check the lower extremities for deep vein thrombosis is a common clinical oversight, particularly given the hypercoagulable state of pregnancy.

Routine Nursing Interventions and Comfort Measures

Physical comfort interventions mitigate the immediate pain associated with childbirth, surgical incisions, and uterine contractions known as afterpains. For perineal trauma or episiotomy repairs, cold therapy (ice packs) applied during the first twenty-four hours reduces localized edema and numbs nerve endings. Following the initial day, sitz baths promote hygiene, soothe discomfort, and enhance local circulation. Analgesia should be administered according to the ladder of pain management, combining non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs with mild opioids when necessary, while keeping breastfeeding compatibility in mind. Nurses also encourage early ambulation to stimulate gastrointestinal motility and prevent venous stasis. Bladder distension must be aggressively prevented; encouraging frequent voiding keeps the bladder empty, thereby preventing uterine displacement and subsequent hemorrhage.

Nutrition, Hygiene, and Self-Care Education

Recovery demands increased nutritional intake, particularly for lactating mothers who require an additional supply of calories, protein, and calcium. Iron supplementation is routinely maintained to counter anemia caused by blood loss during delivery. Hydration is strongly emphasized, as fluid deficits can compromise milk production and exacerbate constipation. Perineal hygiene education focuses on proper cleansing techniques—wiping from front to back after elimination to prevent ascending bacterial infections from the rectum to the vaginal and urethral meatus. Regular handwashing before handling the baby or managing perineal pads remains non-negotiable. Rest is another vital component; mothers are advised to sleep when the infant sleeps to mitigate the physiological toll of fragmented sleep cycles.

Postpartum Psychological Adjustment and Mental Health Screening

The emotional landscape of the puerperium spans a wide spectrum, ranging from transient mood changes to severe clinical disorders. | Condition | Onset and Characteristics | Management Approach | | --- | --- | --- | | **Postpartum Blues** | Appears within days, resolves in two weeks; marked by tearfulness and mood swings. | Reassurance, rest, and family support. | | **Postpartum Depression** | Develops within weeks to months; persistent sadness, withdrawal, and inability to care for self or baby. | Professional counseling and pharmacotherapy. | | **Postpartum Psychosis** | Rapid onset; hallucinations, delusions, and disorganized behavior. | Psychiatric emergency requiring immediate hospitalization. | Mental health screening tools, such as the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale, should be utilized proactively during postnatal visits. Normalizing emotional vulnerability helps reduce the stigma that often prevents mothers from seeking help.

Identification and Management of Postpartum Complications

Postpartum hemorrhage remains a leading cause of maternal mortality, classically defined as cumulative blood loss exceeding 500 mL following a vaginal birth or 1000 mL following a cesarean birth. The most common culprit is **uterine atony**, characterized by a soft, boggy uterus that fails to contract effectively. Immediate nursing actions include vigorous fundal massage, administering uterotonic medications like oxytocin, and monitoring vital signs for signs of hypovolemic shock. Infection, or puerperal sepsis, represents another major threat. Fever exceeding 38 degrees Celsius on any two of the first ten postpartum days, excluding the first twenty-four hours, warrants evaluation for endometritis, urinary tract infections, or wound sepsis. Prompt antibiotic therapy and supportive care are essential.

Neonatal Transition Support and Breastfeeding Guidance

Transitioning from intrauterine to extrauterine life requires the newborn to establish effective respiration, thermoregulation, and feeding. Skin-to-skin contact immediately after birth stabilizes newborn temperature, heart rate, and blood glucose while fostering maternal-infant attachment. Breastfeeding initiation should occur within the first hour of life. The nurse plays a pivotal role in observing the latch technique, ensuring the infant takes in both the nipple and a large portion of the areola to prevent maternal nipple trauma. Colostrum, the early milk rich in protective antibodies, provides vital immunological defense. Education on hunger cues—such as rooting, sucking motions, and hand-to-mouth movements—helps mothers feed on demand rather than strict schedules.

Family Planning and Postpartum Discharge Counseling

Discharge preparation equips the mother and her family with the knowledge required for safe home recovery. Education covers warning signs that necessitate immediate medical re-evaluation, including heavy vaginal bleeding, foul-smelling lochia, severe headaches, visual disturbances, and calf pain. Family planning counseling is an integral component before hospital discharge. Sexual activity can safely resume once the perineum has healed and lochia has ceased, usually around two to six weeks. Nurses must discuss contraceptive options compatible with lactation, such as progestin-only pills, barrier methods, or intrauterine devices, ensuring that child-spacing preferences are fully respected and supported.

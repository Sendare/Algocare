Types of Gender Based Violence

Introduction to Gender-Based Violence in Nursing Practice

Gender-based violence constitutes a major public health crisis and a severe violation of human rights. In clinical settings, encountering survivors requires a profound understanding of how power imbalances drive these acts. Violence is rarely a single isolated event; rather, it often represents a chronic pattern of coercive control. Healthcare facilities in Nigeria frequently receive patients presenting with physical injuries whose underlying etiology is rooted in domestic or societal abuse. Recognizing these patterns extends beyond treating acute trauma, demanding an environment of absolute safety, confidentiality, and empathy to encourage disclosure.

Physical, Emotional, and Psychological Abuse

Abuse manifests across multiple dimensions, often overlapping to entrap a victim. - **Physical Abuse**: Involves any intentional use of bodily force resulting in pain, injury, or physical impairment, ranging from slaps and burns to severe fractures. - **Emotional Abuse**: Consists of verbal assaults, constant humiliation, intimidation, and isolation from friends and family, systematically eroding self-worth. - **Psychological Abuse**: Involves threats of abandonment, harm to loved ones, destruction of personal property, and extreme gaslighting that destabilizes the victim's perception of reality.

Intimate Partner Violence and Domestic Abuse

Intimate partner violence encompasses physical aggression, sexual coercion, psychological abuse, and controlling behaviors perpetrated by a current or former spouse or partner. Domestic violence shares a similar profile but often extends to other members of the household. In many Nigerian communities, traditional family structures can inadvertently silence victims, as elders or family councils may pressure individuals to endure abuse to preserve family honor. Clinicians must observe subtle indicators, such as unexplained chronic pain syndromes, frequent emergency room visits, and partners who refuse to leave the patient's bedside during examinations.

Sexual Violence and Exploitation

Sexual violence involves any sexual act, attempt to obtain a sexual act, or other act directed against a person's sexuality using coercion, by any person regardless of their relationship to the victim. This category includes rape, attempted rape, forced marriage, and human trafficking for sexual exploitation. Survivors often face severe physical trauma, sexually transmitted infections, unwanted pregnancies, and profound psychological distress. Providing care requires strict adherence to forensic evidence collection protocols, prophylaxis against infections, emergency contraception, and non-judgmental trauma-informed counseling.

Harmful Traditional Practices and Socio-Cultural Vulnerabilities

Gender-based violence is frequently perpetuated by deeply entrenched socio-cultural norms and harmful traditional practices. In various regions, these practices include female genital mutilation, child or forced marriages, widowhood rites that violate human dignity, and harmful beliefs surrounding barrenness or the birth of female children only. Economic dependency, patriarchal structures, and limited legal literacy further trap victims. Nurses must navigate these sensitive cultural landscapes with localized insight, offering education and support without alienating community members whose cooperation is vital for sustainable change.

Clinical Identification and Comprehensive Healthcare Response

Identifying gender-based violence requires routine screening during healthcare interactions, particularly in emergency departments, antenatal clinics, and general outpatient units. Nurses must use clear, non-threatening language and ensure absolute privacy. - **Assessment**: Inquire directly and gently when suspicious physical or behavioral signs appear. - **Documentation**: Record exact patient quotes and objective descriptions of injuries without judgmental phrasing. - **Referral**: Connect the patient to social services, legal aid, and psychological counseling networks. - **Safety Planning**: Assist the patient in identifying safe havens and emergency contacts before departure.

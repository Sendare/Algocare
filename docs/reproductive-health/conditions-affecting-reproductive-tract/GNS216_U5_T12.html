Vesicovaginal Fistula

Introduction and Definition of Vesicovaginal Fistula

A vesicovaginal fistula (VVF) is an abnormal fistulous tract extending between the bladder and the vagina, resulting in continuous, involuntary leakage of urine. This condition strips away a woman's dignity, social standing, and physical comfort. In resource-constrained regions, particularly sub-Saharan Africa, it remains a major public health challenge. The constant moisture leads to severe vulvar excoriation, ammoniacal dermatitis, and social isolation. Affected individuals frequently face abandonment by spouses and ostracization from their communities, making VVF not merely a surgical pathology, but a profound social crisis requiring comprehensive nursing intervention.

Pathophysiology and Anatomical Mechanisms

The formation of a vesicovaginal fistula stems from ischemic necrosis of the vesicovaginal septum. During prolonged, obstructed labor, the fetal head is jammed against the maternal pelvic bones, specifically the pubic symphysis. This relentless pressure traps the soft tissues of the bladder base and anterior vaginal wall, crushing their microvasculature. Deprived of oxygen and nutrients, the compressed tissue undergoes ischemic necrosis and sloughs off within days of delivery, leaving an open tract. Gynaecological surgeries, such as radical hysterectomies, can also precipitate fistulas through direct, accidental laceration or thermal injury from electrocautery.

Aetiology and Risk Factors in the Nigerian Context

In Nigeria, prolonged and obstructed labor accounts for the vast majority of vesicovaginal fistula cases, often linked to socioeconomic and cultural determinants. Early marriage and teenage pregnancy result in an anatomically immature pelvis, heightening the risk of cephalopelvic disproportion. Poor access to emergency obstetric care, delayed referrals from traditional birth attendants, and long distances to healthcare facilities exacerbate the problem. While obstetric causes predominate, iatrogenic injuries during poorly executed caesarean sections or hysterectomies contribute a smaller, yet significant, percentage of cases in tertiary referral centers.

Clinical Presentation and Diagnostic Evaluation

The hallmark clinical presentation of a vesicovaginal fistula is continuous, painless leakage of urine following delivery or pelvic surgery. Patients often report complete inability to control micturition despite normal voiding urges if urethral integrity is preserved. Diagnostic confirmation relies on thorough physical assessment and specialized tests:

  • Speculum Examination: Direct visualization of the anterior vaginal wall to identify the fistulous opening, assess its size, and evaluate tissue health.
  • Three-Swab Test: Instillation of methylene blue dye into the bladder followed by placing sterile cotton swabs in the vagina to trace dye leakage.
  • Cystoscopy: Direct endoscopic inspection of the bladder mucosa, ureteric orifices, and fistula margins.
  • Urinalysis and Culture: Essential for detecting and treating concurrent urinary tract infections prior to any surgical repair.

Pre-operative Nursing Assessment and Management

Pre-operative care focuses on optimizing the patient's physical and psychological status before surgical repair. Nurses assess nutritional status, correct anemia, and treat active urinary tract infections with targeted antibiotics based on culture results. Local tissue health is paramount; if inflammation or severe excoriation is present, the surgeon may delay the operation for weeks, prescribing estrogen creams to improve vascularity. Continuous psychological support is critical, as patients frequently experience profound depression, shame, and anxiety regarding the upcoming procedure.

Surgical Intervention and Post-operative Care

Surgical closure of a vesicovaginal fistula aims to mobilize healthy, well-vascularized tissue layers and close the defect without tension. Post-operative nursing care centers on maintaining continuous, uninterrupted bladder drainage to keep the repair site free of tension and hydrostatic pressure. Nurses must monitor catheter patency meticulously, watching for blockage by blood clots or mucus. Any sign of catheter obstruction must be reported immediately, as bladder distension can disrupt the fresh suture line and cause surgical failure. Patients are encouraged to maintain high fluid intake to ensure a continuous, dilute urinary output.

Psychosocial Impact, Rehabilitation, and Prevention

Healing from vesicovaginal fistula extends far beyond physical closure. Reintegration into family and community life requires targeted psychosocial counseling, vocational training, and peer support groups. Many survivors face stigma, divorce, and economic destitution. Preventive strategies remain the ultimate solution to eradicating the condition. Community-based nursing interventions focus on promoting girl-child education, delaying marriage, advocating for skilled birth attendance, and establishing prompt referral systems for women experiencing obstructed labor.

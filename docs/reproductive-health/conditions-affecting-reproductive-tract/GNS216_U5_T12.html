Vesicovaginal Fistula

Introduction and Definition of Vesicovaginal Fistula

A vesicovaginal fistula is an abnormal, epithelium-lined opening between the bladder and the vagina, resulting in the continuous, uncontrollable leakage of urine. This condition severely compromises a woman's physical comfort and social well-being. While rare in high-income countries, it remains a major public health challenge in sub-Saharan Africa, including Nigeria, where it disproportionately affects vulnerable young women living in rural areas with limited access to emergency obstetric care.

Aetiology and Risk Factors in Nigeria

Prolonged obstructed labour is the leading cause of vesicovaginal fistulae in Nigeria. During obstructed labour, the baby's head presses the base of the bladder tightly against the pelvic bone. This prolonged pressure cuts off the blood supply, causing the delicate tissue to die and slough off, leaving an opening behind. Other contributing factors include traditional surgical practices, gynaecological surgical complications, pelvic malignancies, and direct physical trauma.

Clinical Presentation and Diagnostic Evaluation

Patients typically present with complaints of constant, painless wetness, often accompanied by excoriation of the vulva and thighs due to constant urine exposure. Many women also develop a characteristic ammonia-like odour. Diagnostic evaluation involves a thorough clinical history and a physical examination using a speculum. Nurses assist with tests like the methylene blue dye test or cystoscopy to confirm the exact location and size of the opening.

Pre-operative Nursing Assessment and Management

Before surgery, comprehensive nursing care focuses on physical and psychological preparation. Nurses assess the patient's nutritional status, screen for concurrent infections like urinary tract infections or vaginitis, and encourage high fluid intake. Psychological support is vital, as these patients often face severe social isolation, depression, and low self-esteem. Pre-operative counselling helps alleviate anxiety and prepares the patient for post-operative expectations.

Surgical Intervention and Post-operative Care

Surgical closure is the definitive treatment for vesicovaginal fistula. The procedure involves dissecting the fistula tract and closing the bladder and vaginal walls in separate layers without tension. Post-operative care requires continuous bladder drainage, usually via an indwelling urinary catheter for 10 to 14 days, to allow the repair site to heal completely. Nurses monitor urine output, watch for catheter blockages, and ensure the patient maintains high fluid intake.

Psychosocial Impact, Rehabilitation, and Prevention

Healing extends beyond physical closure. Rebuilding a patient's life involves community reintegration, vocational training, and psychological counseling. Many survivors face marital abandonment and stigma. Prevention strategies remain paramount: expanding access to emergency obstetric care, discouraging child marriage, promoting skilled birth attendance, and ensuring the timely use of Caesarean sections when labour becomes obstructed.

STI Information and Counselling (Male)

Introduction to Sexually Transmitted Infections in Males

Sexually transmitted infections represent a major public health concern globally, affecting men across all demographics. Many male clients harbour infections without exhibiting outward signs, which inadvertently perpetuates transmission within communities. Pathogens can be bacterial, viral, or parasitic, each requiring distinct therapeutic approaches. Nurses play a frontline role in demystifying these conditions, dismantling social stigma, and encouraging early health-seeking behaviour among male patients who traditionally avoid clinical spaces.

Common Bacterial and Viral STIs in Men

Understanding the specific causative agents helps nurses anticipate clinical needs and provide accurate client education.

  • Gonorrhoea: Caused by *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*, presenting frequently with purulent urethral discharge.
  • Chlamydia: Caused by *Chlamydia trachomatis*, often milder but can lead to long-term reproductive complications if left untreated.
  • Syphilis: Caused by *Treponema pallidum*, progressing through distinct stages starting with a painless chancre.
  • Human Papillomavirus (HPV): A viral infection linked to genital warts and various malignancies.
  • Herpes Simplex Virus (HSV): Causes painful vesicular lesions and recurrent outbreaks.

Clinical Presentation and Recognition of Symptoms

Male clients may present with a variety of localized or systemic complaints. Common clinical indicators include dysuria, urethral discharge, scrotal swelling or pain, and cutaneous lesions such as ulcers, bumps, or blisters. Nurses must perform thorough visual inspections when appropriate and ask targeted screening questions. A common misconception is that if there is no pain, there is no infection; however, conditions like early chlamydia or latent syphilis can be entirely painless while still causing internal damage or spreading to partners.

Comprehensive STI Assessment and History Taking

Taking a sexual history requires a non-judgmental, confidential, and empathetic approach to build trust. Nurses should inquire about recent sexual encounters, number of partners, condom use consistency, and history of past infections. Asking open-ended questions reduces client defensiveness. It is essential to explore oral, anal, and genital exposure histories equally, ensuring that screening tests are ordered for all relevant anatomical sites.

Principles of Pre-Test and Post-Test Counselling

Counselling is a cornerstone of effective sexual health nursing. Pre-test counselling prepares the client for diagnostic procedures, explains the window periods of specific infections, and addresses anxiety. Post-test counselling involves delivering results clearly, explaining treatment regimens, and offering emotional support if results are positive. For clients receiving a positive diagnosis, the nurse provides guidance on risk reduction and initiates partner notification discussions.

Partner Notification and Contact Tracing

Partner notification is vital to break the chain of infection and prevent reinfection of the index client. Nurses can assist through provider referral, where public health staff confidentially notify partners, or client referral, where the patient informs them directly. Confidentiality must be strictly maintained to protect the client's privacy while ensuring exposed partners receive timely evaluation and presumptive treatment.

Medical Management and Treatment Compliance

Bacterial infections are typically cured with appropriate antibiotic regimens, whereas viral infections require management of symptoms and suppression strategies. Nurses must emphasise the importance of completing the full course of medication, even if symptoms disappear early. Furthermore, clients must be advised to abstain from sexual activity until they and their partners have completed treatment and are confirmed cured, preventing ping-pong infections.

Preventive Education and Risk Reduction

Prevention is more effective than any treatment regimen. Nurses should educate male clients on consistent and correct condom use, regular screening schedules for sexually active individuals, and the availability of vaccinations such as HPV and Hepatitis B. Discussions should also cover harm reduction strategies and the role of pre-exposure prophylaxis where applicable, empowering men to take active control of their sexual health.

Handover Documentation

Introduction to Handover Documentation in Midwifery

Handover documentation is the formal process of passing vital patient information from one healthcare provider to another during shift changes. In midwifery practice across Nigeria, this ensures continuity of care for both mother and baby. A safe delivery continuum relies heavily on clear communication between the incoming and outgoing shifts. When a midwife hands over a patient in active labor or a postpartum mother, every critical detail must be documented accurately to prevent dangerous gaps in care.

Core Principles of Accurate and Concise Patient Records

Good documentation follows strict clinical standards. It must be timely, factual, and objective. Midwives should record observations as they happen rather than relying on memory at the end of a long shift. Avoid vague terms like 'patient is fine' or 'normal delivery.' Instead, record measurable facts, such as 'blood pressure is 120/80 mmHg and lochia is moderate and rubra.' Legibility is equally important; scribbled or illegible notes can lead to grave medication or clinical errors.

The SBAR Framework in Clinical Handovers

  • Situation: State clearly who the patient is, her parity, and the immediate issue.
  • Background: Give relevant past obstetric history, admission diagnosis, and treatment so far.
  • Assessment: Summarize current clinical findings, vital signs, and recent changes.
  • Recommendation: State what actions are needed next or what the incoming shift must monitor.

Legal and Ethical Responsibilities in Midwifery Documentation

Patient records are legal documents that can be subpoenaed in a court of law. Midwives have an ethical duty to maintain strict confidentiality regarding patient information. If an error is made in a paper record, the midwife must draw a single neat line through the mistake, write the correct information, and sign and date it. Never use correction fluid or erase entries, as this raises suspicion of tampering and can ruin a professional defense in malpractice suits.

Common Pitfalls and Avoidance of Documentation Errors

Busy shifts often lead to dangerous shortcuts in report writing. Avoid leaving blank spaces in clinical notes where others can add unauthorized entries later. Never document care before it is actually carried out, as unforeseen emergencies can change the plan. Ensure every entry includes your full signature, date, and time. Clear, honest, and complete entries protect both the patient and the midwife.

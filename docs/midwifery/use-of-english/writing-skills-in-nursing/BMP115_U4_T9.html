Formal Correspondence and Official Letter Writing

Introduction to Formal Correspondence in Nursing

Formal correspondence refers to structured, written communication used within professional, administrative, and legal contexts. In Nigerian healthcare practice, midwives and nurses rely on formal letters to communicate with hospital management, the Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria, government health ministries, and external agencies. Unlike informal notes or casual text messages, official letters create a permanent, legally binding record of professional actions, requests, and disciplinary matters. Mastery of this skill ensures your message is taken seriously, protecting both your professional standing and the interests of your patients.

Core Principles of Official Letter Writing

Effective official communication rests on three pillars: clarity, conciseness, and courtesy.

  • Clarity: State your purpose immediately without hiding behind complicated jargon. The reader should understand what you want within the first few sentences.
  • Conciseness: Cut out unnecessary words and irrelevant personal stories. Stick strictly to the facts and operational details.
  • Courtesy: Maintain a professional and respectful tone, even when writing a letter of complaint or grievance. Emotional outbursts weaken an administrative argument.

In Nigerian bureaucratic settings, letters that violate these core principles are often delayed or ignored entirely because busy administrators lack the time to decode rambling text.

Standard Structure and Layout of Professional Healthcare Letters

A standard official letter follows a strict layout to ensure easy filing and tracking. In most Nigerian public health institutions, the block or semi-block format is used.

  1. Letterhead: The official logo, name, and address of the hospital or organization.
  2. Date: Written in full (e.g., 14th October, 2023).
  3. Receiver's Designation and Address: The exact title and office address of the person receiving the letter.
  4. Salutation: Formal greeting such as 'Dear Sir' or 'Dear Madam'.
  5. Reference / Subject Line (RE): A concise summary of the letter's contents, usually underlined or bolded.
  6. Body Paragraphs: Introduction, main details, and a concluding call to action.
  7. Complimentary Close: 'Yours faithfully' (when addressing someone whose name you do not know) or 'Yours sincerely' (when addressing a named person).
  8. Signature, Name, and Designation: Your handwritten signature, typed name, and official rank.

Tone, Diction, and Register in Nursing Administration

The language of healthcare administration is objective, formal, and authoritative without being arrogant. Avoid slang, colloquialisms, and emotional accusations. Instead of writing, 'The matron refused to listen to my wahala,' use formal administrative diction: 'The Nursing Officer in Charge declined to review the operational concern.' Maintain an active, professional register that reflects your status as a trained health professional. Every term used should align with standard medical and administrative nomenclature.

Common Types of Official Communications in Nigerian Practice

Midwives encounter several standard categories of official letters throughout their careers.

  • Application for Annual Leave: Requests submitted weeks in advance to allow for duty roster adjustments.
  • Query Response: A formal defense or explanation written in response to an administrative query regarding a clinical incident.
  • Letter of Resignation: Official notice of intent to leave employment, adhering to the notice period specified in the employment contract.
  • Request for Transfer: Formal application to move from one facility or department to another.

Each type follows specific internal protocols within Nigerian health institutions, requiring proper routing through immediate supervisors.

Drafting, Reviewing, and Avoiding Common Pitfalls

Before dispatching any official correspondence, thorough review is essential. Many letters fail or cause unnecessary conflict due to avoidable mistakes.

  • Spelling and Grammar Errors: Misspelled names of senior officials or poor grammar undermine your credibility.
  • Missing Enclosures: Forgetting to attach supporting documents (such as medical reports or certificates) mentioned in the text.
  • Failure to Route Through Proper Channels: Skipping your immediate supervisor when submitting a letter breaks administrative protocol and causes friction.

Always proofread your draft twice, check all names and dates, and ensure your supervisor is kept in the loop through proper forwarding.

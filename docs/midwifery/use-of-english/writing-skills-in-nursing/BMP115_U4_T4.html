Expository Essay Writing

Understanding Expository Essays in Midwifery and Nursing Communication

An expository essay is a type of writing designed to explain, illustrate, or clarify a specific topic based on facts rather than personal feelings. In the daily practice of midwifery and nursing, clear communication saves lives. Midwives write expository pieces when updating policy guidelines, explaining antenatal care protocols to junior colleagues, or drafting health education brochures for pregnant mothers attending community clinics across Nigeria.

Unlike narrative writing that tells a story or persuasive writing that tries to change a reader's mind, expository writing focuses entirely on presenting balanced, factual information. A student nurse writing this style must set aside personal opinions about traditional birth practices or clinical preferences and rely instead on verifiable data, anatomy, and approved medical procedures.

Core Characteristics: Informing Without Biasing

  • Objectivity: The tone remains neutral, professional, and detached from personal emotion.
  • Fact-Based Content: Every major point rests on scientific research, textbooks, or clinical guidelines rather than anecdotal beliefs.
  • Logical Organization: Information flows in a predictable, easy-to-follow sequence so the reader does not get confused.
  • Clarity and Precision: Medical and midwifery terms are used accurately without unnecessary flowery language.

Common Types of Expository Writing in Healthcare Contexts

Expository writing in nursing and midwifery takes several distinct forms. Recognizing these formats helps students choose the right approach for their assignments and clinical duties.

  • Process (How-To) Essay: Explains the step-by-step procedure of a clinical skill, such as how to properly perform sterile catheterization or conduct a bimanual pelvic examination.
  • Comparison and Contrast Essay: Examines the similarities and differences between two clinical approaches, such as active versus expectant management of the third stage of labor.
  • Cause and Effect Essay: Explores the roots and consequences of a health issue, such as the causes of maternal anemia in rural Nigerian communities and its effect on birth weight.
  • Definition Essay: Explores the deeper meaning and clinical implications of a medical term, such as defining what 'partograph' truly means in obstetric monitoring.

Structuring the Expository Essay: Introduction, Body, and Conclusion

A well-organized expository essay follows a predictable three-part structure that guides the reader logically from start to finish.

  1. The Introduction: Starts with a broad hook about the clinical topic, provides necessary background context, and ends with a strong, focused thesis statement.
  2. The Body Paragraphs: Each paragraph explores one main supporting point, beginning with a clear topic sentence, followed by evidence, and closing with a sentence that ties back to the thesis.
  3. The Conclusion: Restates the thesis in different words, summarizes the main body points without adding new facts, and leaves the reader with a final thought on the clinical significance.

Selecting Clinical Topics and Formulating a Clear Thesis Statement

Choosing a manageable topic is the secret to writing a strong essay. Nursing students often make the mistake of choosing a topic that is too broad, such as 'Maternal Health in Africa,' which cannot be covered thoroughly in a short paper. Instead, narrow the focus to something specific, such as 'The Impact of Community Health Extension Workers on Antenatal Attendance in Kaduna State.'

Once the topic is narrowed, draft a thesis statement that acts as a single-sentence summary of your core argument or explanation. A good thesis for an expository essay does not announce the essay ('I am going to write about eclampsia'); rather, it states the specific angle directly: 'Magnesium sulfate reduces maternal mortality from eclampsia by controlling seizures and stabilizing blood pressure.'

Integrating Evidence, Statistics, and Professional Guidelines

An expository essay carries weight only when backed by credible evidence. In healthcare writing, students must rely on peer-reviewed journals, World Health Organization (WHO) reports, Nigerian Federal Ministry of Health guidelines, and standard midwifery textbooks.

When inserting evidence, avoid dropping quotes blindly into the text without context. Instead, introduce the source, present the data clearly, and explain what the statistic means for clinical practice. For instance, rather than just writing a random number, write: 'According to recent reports by the Federal Ministry of Health, postpartum hemorrhage remains a leading cause of maternal mortality in Nigeria, accounting for roughly a quarter of all maternal deaths.'

Common Pitfalls to Avoid When Explaining Medical Concepts

Even diligent nursing students sometimes struggle with expository writing because of a few recurring habits. Being aware of these traps helps elevate the quality of academic work.

  • Drifting into Persuasion: Accidentally shifting from explaining a fact to arguing that a law should be changed or that readers must agree with a personal viewpoint.
  • Overusing Jargon: Packing sentences with dense medical acronyms without defining them, which alienates readers who are not specialists in that exact sub-field.
  • Unsupported Generalizations: Making sweeping claims like 'All pregnant women in Nigeria refuse antenatal care' without citing supporting demographic data.
  • Plagiarism: Copying sentences word-for-word from textbooks or websites without proper citation or paraphrasing.

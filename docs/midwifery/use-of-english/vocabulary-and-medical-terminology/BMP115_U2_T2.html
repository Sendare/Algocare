Medical Prefixes, Roots and Suffixes Common in Nursing

Introduction to Medical Terminology in Midwifery and Nursing

Medical terminology forms the primary language of healthcare professionals across Nigeria and the world. Grounded mostly in Greek and Latin, these terms allow midwives, nurses, and doctors to communicate complex clinical conditions quickly and accurately. Instead of writing long descriptive sentences in patient case notes, clinical staff use concise compound words built from structured building blocks. Learning this vocabulary requires breaking unfamiliar terms down into their component parts: prefixes, roots, and suffixes. Once these individual pieces are mastered, reading and understanding advanced medical charts becomes straightforward, ensuring safer patient care and seamless handover communication during shift changes.

Medical Roots: The Core Meaning of Terms

The root word gives a medical term its primary subject or core meaning, usually referring to a specific body organ, tissue, or system. For instance, the root hyster- or metr- refers to the uterus, while card- refers to the heart. Most roots cannot stand alone as complete words; they require either a prefix, a suffix, or both to form a complete clinical term. When a suffix is added to a root, the combination creates a medical condition, a diagnostic test, or a surgical procedure related to that specific body part.

Medical Prefixes: Modifying Location, Time, and Status

A prefix sits at the very beginning of a medical word to modify or alter the core meaning of the root. Prefixes frequently describe location, direction, measurement, frequency, or temporal status.

  • Pre-: Before or in front of (as in prenatal care before birth)
  • Post-: After or behind (as in postpartum care after delivery)
  • Hyper-: High, excessive, or above normal (as in hypertension)
  • Hypo-: Low, deficient, or below normal (as in hypoglycemia)
  • Poly-: Many or excessive (as in polyuria)

Recognizing these modifying chunks helps nurses immediately spot abnormalities or timelines when scanning patient charts.

Medical Suffixes: Indicating Conditions, Procedures, and Diagnostics

A suffix is attached to the end of a medical root to complete its meaning, usually indicating a disease process, surgical procedure, diagnostic test, or clinical specialty.

  • -itis: Inflammation (as in appendicitis or mastitis)
  • -ectomy: Surgical removal or excision (as in hysterectomy)
  • -rrhea: Flow or excessive discharge (as in menorrhagia ororrhea)
  • -gram: A recorded picture or written tracing (as in electrocardiogram)
  • -logy: The study of a specific subject (as in neonatology)

When translating an unfamiliar term, experienced clinical staff often look at the suffix first to understand what is happening to the body part named by the root.

Common Obstetrical and Gynecological Root Words

Midwifery practice requires familiarity with specialized anatomical and physiological roots unique to pregnancy, childbirth, and the female reproductive system. Mastering these specific terms prevents dangerous clinical misunderstandings in the labor ward.

Combining these roots with suffixes like -itis or -ectomy allows midwives to document maternal conditions with absolute precision.

Putting It All Together: Decoding Complex Nursing Terms

Decoding complex medical words is a step-by-step analytical skill. When faced with an intimidating clinical term on a patient chart, break it down by starting at the suffix, moving to the prefix, and finishing with the root. For example, consider the term postpartum. Breaking it down reveals the prefix post- (after), attached to the root part- (labor/childbirth), creating the clinical definition of the period following childbirth. Practicing this breakdown technique transforms confusing medical jargon into clear, actionable clinical information for safe midwifery practice.

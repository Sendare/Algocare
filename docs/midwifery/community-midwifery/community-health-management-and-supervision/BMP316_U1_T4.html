Data Collection and Reporting to National Health Management Information Systems (NHMIS)

Introduction to the National Health Management Information Systems (NHMIS)

The National Health Management Information System (NHMIS) serves as the official platform for gathering, analyzing, and using health data across Nigeria's health sector. Midwives and community health workers play a vital role in this system because every birth, antenatal visit, immunization, and clinical diagnosis recorded at the primary health care level forms the bedrock of national health planning. Without accurate data from local clinics, the government cannot forecast drug supplies, allocate funding, or respond effectively to disease outbreaks. Understanding the NHMIS helps every health worker see beyond their immediate clinical tasks to the broader public health picture.

Data Collection Tools and Primary Health Care Registers

Primary health care facilities in Nigeria rely on standardized paper and digital tools to capture patient encounters daily. Midwives must master these specific instruments to ensure accountability and continuity of care.

  • Antenatal Care (ANC) Register: Captures demographic details, gestational age, tetanus toxoid vaccination status, and routine lab results for pregnant women.
  • Child Health and Immunization Register: Tracks vaccine doses administered, growth monitoring metrics, and vitamin A supplementation for infants.
  • Maternity and Delivery Register: Records intrapartum details, labor progress, delivery outcomes, birth weights, and immediate postpartum complications.
  • Daily Outpatient Attendance Register: Summarizes general complaints, diagnoses, and treatments for all clinic visitors.

Routine Data Collection and Reporting Timelines

Data collection is a continuous daily duty, but reporting follows strict calendar schedules. At the close of each month, midwives and facility records officers compile daily tallies from individual registers into summary forms. These completed summary forms must be submitted to the Local Government Area (LGA) Monitoring and Evaluation officer by a specified deadline, usually on or before the fifth day of the new month. Missing these deadlines creates delays that stall regional and national health bulletins.

Data Validation, Collation, and Quality Control at the Facility Level

Before sending reports upward, facility staff must check their work for accuracy. Data validation involves cross-checking daily tallies against monthly summaries to catch mathematical errors or missing entries. Common pitfalls include double-counting immunization doses or leaving columns blank when a service was simply not documented. Facilities that run internal review meetings before reporting drastically reduce errors and improve overall data reliability.

Electronic and Paper-Based Reporting Pathways in Nigeria

Nigeria operates a hybrid reporting system combining traditional paper forms with electronic platforms like the District Health Information Software version 2 (DHIS2). In this pathway, midwives and records officers first capture data in physical registers, summarize them on paper monthly forms (such as the NHMIS summary booklets), and then upload or input those figures into the web-based DHIS2 portal at the LGA or facility level. While electronic platforms speed up data visibility, unreliable internet connectivity and power outages often force facilities to rely heavily on physical paper copies.

Using Local Health Data for Community-Based Decision Making

Data is not just something to be collected and sent upward; it is a powerful tool for local action. Midwives who review their monthly facility data can easily spot troubling trends, such as a sudden drop in antenatal bookings or a spike in childhood diarrhea cases. By analyzing these patterns locally, health workers can organize targeted community sensitization campaigns, request specific drug replenishments, or adjust clinic outreach days to better serve their catchment population.

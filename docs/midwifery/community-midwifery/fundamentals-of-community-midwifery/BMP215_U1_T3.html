Home Visiting Principles, Equipment, and Domiciliary Midwifery Bag

Introduction and Principles of Domiciliary Midwifery Practice

Domiciliary midwifery brings professional maternal and newborn care directly to the family's environment. This practice requires midwives to respect local customs, maintain privacy in crowded living spaces, and establish trust with community leaders and household heads. Unlike the structured, brightly lit environment of a hospital ward, home visiting places the midwife as a guest in the client's space, requiring a high degree of cultural sensitivity, adaptability, and clinical independence.

Essential Contents of the Domiciliary Midwifery Bag

The domiciliary bag is the mobile workstation of the community midwife and must be organized logically to ensure rapid access during emergencies. It is typically structured into separate compartments or removable trays for clean, sterile, and contaminated items.

  • Diagnostic tools: Fetoscope or Doppler, sphygmomanometer, adult stethoscope, thermometer, and weighing scale.
  • Clinical supplies: Umbilical cord clamp, sterile scissors, cotton wool, gauze, syringes, and needles.
  • Medications: Oxytocin, ergometrine, vitamin K, eye ointment, and local antiseptics.
  • Documentation: Client records, referral forms, and a pen.

Infection Prevention and Aseptic Technique in the Field

Maintaining clean practices outside a formal health facility is challenging but vital to prevent puerperal sepsis and neonatal tetanus. The midwife must establish a clean working field using a freshly laundered plastic sheet or towel before opening the midwifery bag. Hand hygiene remains the single most effective intervention; clean water and soap or an alcohol-based hand rub must be used before touching any client. Used instruments and waste must be segregated properly into safety boxes and leak-proof bags to protect the household and the community.

Bag Maintenance, Cleaning, and Stock Management

A neglected midwifery bag can easily harbor harmful bacteria and rust expensive instruments. After every home visit, the bag must be emptied completely, cleaned with a mild disinfectant solution, and left open to dry in the air. Instruments must be washed, dried, and re-sterilized as soon as the midwife returns to the clinic base. Expiry dates on drugs and single-use sterile packs must be checked routinely every week to prevent administering spoiled medications to a client.

Safety Protocols and Emergency Preparedness

Home visits carry inherent safety risks for the healthcare provider, ranging from difficult terrain and inclement weather to community unrest or aggressive domestic animals. Midwives should inform their base clinic of their itinerary and expected return time before setting out. Emergency preparedness also means having a clear, pre-planned transport strategy—such as a reliable local motorcycle rider or community vehicle—to transfer a woman experiencing severe hemorrhage, obstructed labor, or eclampsia to a comprehensive facility without delay.

Selection and Eligibility Criteria for Home Delivery

Introduction to Domiciliary Midwifery and Place of Birth

Domiciliary midwifery involves providing professional antenatal, intrapartum, and postnatal care within a woman's private home. Choosing where to give birth is a deeply personal decision that balances maternal comfort with clinical safety. For centuries in Nigeria, home birth supported by traditional birth attendants was the primary norm. Modern domiciliary midwifery brings professional, skilled attendance directly to the community, aiming to reduce the barriers of distance, transport costs, and hospital bureaucracy. Midwives practicing in the community must maintain high standards of clinical judgment, ensuring that care delivered outside a hospital setting never compromises the safety of mother and baby.

Low-Risk Screening and Baseline Evaluation

Careful risk identification forms the bedrock of safe domiciliary practice. Midwives conduct systematic assessments during antenatal booking to verify that a pregnancy is truly low-risk.

  • Obstetric History: Reviewing past pregnancies, parity, previous caesarean sections, or history of postpartum hemorrhage.
  • Medical Screening: Checking for chronic conditions like hypertension, diabetes, or severe sickle cell disease.
  • Current Pregnancy Status: Confirming a single fetus, normal cephalic presentation near term, and normal placental location.

Any deviation from these normal parameters shifts the woman out of the low-risk category, making home delivery unsafe.

Specific Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Clear boundaries protect both the client and the midwife. Inclusion criteria define who is eligible for a home birth, while exclusion criteria outline conditions that demand hospital-based delivery.

ParameterInclusion Criteria (Home Birth Safe)Exclusion Criteria (Hospital Birth Required)
Gestational Age37 completed weeks to 41 completed weeksPreterm (<37 weeks) or post-date (>42 weeks)
Fetal PresentationCephalic (head down) presentationBreech, transverse, or unstable lie
ParityMultiparous with history of normal birthsGrand multiparity (para 5 or more) or primigravida with risks
Liquor AmniiClear liquor upon spontaneous ruptureMeconium-stained liquor or premature rupture of membranes

Maternal, Environmental, and Social Assessment

Clinical health is only one piece of the puzzle. A midwife must also evaluate the physical environment and social support surrounding the client. The home must have adequate lighting, clean water access, and a warm, private space for labor. Logistic factors matter profoundly; the midwife must inspect the roads and transport availability to calculate exact travel time to the nearest comprehensive emergency obstetric care facility if an emergency arises. Furthermore, family acceptance and a supportive partner are vital, as stress or opposition from household members can severely complicate labor progress.

Informed Consent and Shared Decision-Making

Informed consent in domiciliary midwifery goes far beyond signing a routine form. It is an ongoing conversation where the midwife explains the benefits, potential risks, and limitations of giving birth at home. The pregnant woman and her family must understand that while home birth is normal and safe for low-risk cases, unexpected emergencies can happen. The plan must explicitly outline what triggers a transfer to the hospital. Midwives must respect maternal autonomy while ensuring clients do not take uninformed risks due to cultural pressure or misinformation.

Emergency Preparedness and Transfer Protocols

Even with meticulous screening, obstetric emergencies can arise without warning in any birth setting. Every domiciliary plan must include a robust emergency preparedness scheme.

  1. Emergency Plan: Pre-identifying the receiving hospital and specific referral contact person.
  2. Transport Logistics: Ensuring a reliable vehicle or motorcycle is on standby with fuel.
  3. Communication: Having a charged mobile phone with credit to alert the referral center before departure.
  4. Midwife Kit: Carrying essential emergency items like oxytocin, a resuscitation bag-valve-mask, and manual vacuum aspiration tools.

Prompt decision-making and smooth transfer save lives when complications occur.

Functions of Food in the Human Body

Introduction to Nutritional Science in Midwifery

Nutritional science forms the bedrock of safe maternal and neonatal care. Midwives in Nigeria constantly encounter women whose pregnancy outcomes depend directly on their dietary intake. Food is not merely something that satisfies hunger or provides comfort; it is a complex biochemical mixture that supplies raw materials for cellular life, energy for physical exertion, and regulatory agents for all metabolic pathways. In clinical practice, understanding these nutritional principles allows midwives to bridge the gap between cultural food practices and evidence-based dietary needs during antenatal care.

Energy-Yielding Nutrients and Basal Metabolic Support

The human body requires a continuous supply of energy just to keep vital organs functioning at rest. This baseline energy requirement is known as the basal metabolic rate. Carbohydrates, fats, and proteins supply this necessary fuel, measured in kilocalories.

  1. Carbohydrates: The body's preferred and most rapid source of energy, breaking down into glucose for cellular respiration.
  2. Fats: Concentrated energy reserves that provide long-term fuel storage and protect internal organs.
  3. Proteins: Primarily used for growth and repair, but can yield energy when carbohydrate and fat stores are insufficient.

Building Blocks: Tissue Growth, Repair, and Maintenance

Proteins and specific minerals act as the structural framework of the human body. During pregnancy, this role becomes profoundly critical as a woman forms a placenta, expands her blood volume, and grows a new fetus.

  • Amino Acids: The individual building blocks of proteins required to construct new maternal and fetal tissues.
  • Calcium and Phosphorus: Mineral nutrients embedded into the organic matrix to give rigidity and strength to bones and teeth.
  • Cellular Turnover: Ongoing biological maintenance that replaces worn-out mucosal linings, skin cells, and blood components using dietary building blocks.

Regulatory and Protective Roles of Micronutrients

Vitamins and minerals do not yield energy or build large structural tissues, yet life cannot proceed without them. They act as coenzymes, regulate fluid balance, support immune defense, and prevent specific deficiency diseases.

Nutritional Demands During Pregnancy and Lactation

Maternal nutrition uniquely impacts two lives simultaneously. During pregnancy, blood volume increases by nearly fifty percent, necessitating extra iron and protein. Lactation places a similarly high metabolic demand on the mother as her body synthesizes nutrient-rich breast milk to nourish the newborn.

A common misconception in some communities is that a pregnant woman should eat less to keep the baby small for an easy delivery. Midwives must actively dispel this myth through patient education, explaining that adequate nutrition prevents low birth weight and protects maternal health.

Nutritional Status Assessment: Anthropometric, Biochemical, Clinical, Dietary (ABCD)

Introduction to Nutritional Assessment in Pregnancy and Midwifery

Nutritional assessment is a fundamental part of routine antenatal care in Nigerian midwifery practice. Pregnancy places heavy physiological demands on a woman's body, requiring extra energy, protein, vitamins, and minerals to support both maternal tissue growth and fetal development. Failing to identify nutritional deficits early can lead to severe complications such as iron-deficiency anaemia, intrauterine growth restriction, and low birth weight.

The primary goal of nutritional screening is to catch subtle deficiencies before they become clinical emergencies. Midwives use a systematic framework known by the acronym ABCD: Anthropometric, Biochemical, Clinical, and Dietary methods. Each letter represents a different angle of investigation, allowing the midwife to build a complete picture of the pregnant woman's health status. Combining these tools ensures that dietary counseling, micronutrient supplementation, and medical referrals are tailored to the individual needs of every mother walking into an antenatal clinic.

Anthropometric Assessment Methods

Anthropometry involves measuring the physical dimensions and body composition of a pregnant woman. These measurements are quick, non-invasive, and cost-effective, making them ideal for busy primary health care clinics across Nigeria.

  • Weight and Weight Gain: Regular weighing at every antenatal visit tracks gestational weight gain. Inadequate gain signals undernutrition, while sudden spikes may indicate pre-eclampsia.
  • Body Mass Index (BMI): Calculated using pre-pregnancy weight and height to classify mothers as underweight, normal, overweight, or obese.
  • Mid-Upper Arm Circumference (MUAC): A simple tape measurement around the mid-point of the upper arm. It is exceptionally useful for quickly screening acute malnutrition in resource-limited settings where weighing scales may be unavailable.

Biochemical Assessment

Biochemical assessment relies on laboratory analyses of bodily fluids, primarily blood and urine, to provide objective data on nutritional status. These tests detect deficiencies long before physical signs appear on the body.

  • Haemoglobin (Hb) Estimation: The standard screening test for anaemia during antenatal booking and follow-up visits. In Nigeria, an Hb level below 11 g/dL indicates anaemia.
  • Urinalysis: Routinely checks for protein, glucose, and ketones. Proteinuria warns of renal stress or pre-eclampsia, while glucosuria suggests gestational diabetes.
  • Serum Micronutrient Levels: Where available, tests measure specific vitamins and minerals like iron, folate, and calcium to guide targeted supplementation.

Clinical Assessment

Clinical assessment involves a physical examination focused on outward signs of nutrient deficiencies. Midwives inspect the skin, eyes, hair, mouth, and nails for clues.

  • Pallor: Paleness in the conjunctiva, nail beds, or palms points strongly toward anaemia.
  • Goitre: Enlargement of the thyroid gland in the neck suggests iodine deficiency, which remains a public health concern in certain regions.
  • Skin and Mucosa: Dry skin, cracked lip corners (angular stomatitis), or bleeding gums can indicate shortages of B-complex vitamins or vitamin C.

Clinical signs usually appear late in the deficiency process. By the time physical changes manifest, the internal depletion has often reached an advanced stage, making prompt intervention necessary.

Dietary Assessment

Dietary assessment evaluates the quality, quantity, and frequency of foods consumed by the pregnant woman. Understanding eating habits helps midwives identify the root causes of nutrient deficiencies rather than just treating the symptoms.

  • 24-Hour Dietary Recall: The mother lists everything she ate and drank over the past 24 hours. While helpful, it relies heavily on her memory.
  • Food Frequency Questionnaire: Asks how often specific food groups—such as green leafy vegetables, proteins, and grains—are consumed per week.
  • Cultural and Economic Factors: In Nigeria, food choices are heavily influenced by local availability, seasonal changes, and household finances. Midwives must consider affordability when recommending dietary improvements, encouraging nutrient-dense local foods like beans, local fish, and seasonal vegetables.

Integrating the ABCD Approach for Comprehensive Care

No single assessment method provides a complete picture of nutritional health on its own. Anthropometry tells you about body size and mass, biochemistry reveals internal chemical levels, clinical exams highlight physical damage, and dietary histories explain the underlying eating patterns.

By weaving these four pillars together, Nigerian midwives can formulate accurate nutritional diagnoses and deliver holistic care. For example, a low MUAC reading (Anthropometry) combined with pale conjunctiva (Clinical) and a low haemoglobin test (Biochemical) points directly to severe anaemia, which can then be addressed by exploring poor dietary iron intake (Dietary). This comprehensive approach transforms routine clinic visits into powerful opportunities for maternal and fetal protection.

Data Quality Control, Cleaning, Coding, and Entry

Introduction to Midwifery Research Data Management

Data management in midwifery research is the systematic process of planning, collecting, organizing, protecting, and storing information gathered from clinical studies or community health surveys. Good data management ensures that the findings published in nursing and midwifery journals are trustworthy and can be used to improve maternal and child health policies in Nigeria.

Every research project begins with a clear protocol. Midwives must handle data responsibly right from the point of booking a pregnant woman in the antenatal clinic or administering a postpartum questionnaire. Poorly managed data leads to false conclusions, which can negatively affect patient care guidelines.

Data Quality Control

Data quality control involves the steps taken during fieldwork to catch mistakes before the data reaches the analysis stage. In busy Nigerian primary health centres, research assistants might rush through interviews, leading to missing values or contradictory answers.

Supervisors play a key role here by reviewing filled questionnaires daily. If a midwife records a birth weight of 50 kilograms, a quality control check immediately flags this as an impossible or erroneous entry that requires verification.

Data Coding

Coding is the process of translating descriptive survey responses or clinical notes into numerical values so that statistical software can process them easily. For example, in a study assessing parity among nursing mothers, response categories are assigned numbers:

  • Primipara: Assigned code 1
  • Multipara: Assigned code 2
  • Grand multipara: Assigned code 3

Clear coding frames must be designed before data collection begins to prevent confusion and maintain consistency across different research assistants.

Data Entry

Data entry moves completed information from paper forms or digital logs into a computer spreadsheet or database program like SPSS, Epi Info, or Microsoft Excel. Errors frequently happen during this manual typing phase.

To minimize mistakes, researchers use double-entry verification, where two different clerks type the same dataset independently, and the computer highlights any discrepancies. Designing entry masks with built-in range checks—such as preventing the system from accepting an age over 100 for an antenatal client—also protects data accuracy.

Data Cleaning

Data cleaning is the final review process used to spot and correct anomalies, missing values, and outliers in a dataset before running statistical tests. Even with careful entry, mistakes slip through.

Midwives running data analysis will check frequency tables to look for wild codes—such as finding a '3' in a gender variable where only '1' (male) and '2' (female) were expected. Handling missing data is another critical task; researchers must decide whether to exclude incomplete records or use statistical imputation methods depending on the study design.

Ensuring Data Integrity and Ethical Handling in Healthcare Fieldwork

Data integrity means keeping research records accurate, complete, and uncompromised throughout their lifecycle. In midwifery and healthcare fieldwork, data integrity is tied closely to medical ethics and patient confidentiality.

Field questionnaires containing sensitive information—such as HIV status, obstetric history, or teenage pregnancies—must be stored in locked cabinets or password-encrypted folders. Revealing a patient's identity during data coding or cleaning violates the core ethical principles of nursing and research practice.

Drawing Conclusions, Formulating Policy and Clinical Recommendations

Synthesizing Findings for Midwifery Practice

Research data alone does not change maternal and child health outcomes in a Nigerian tertiary hospital or a primary health care clinic. Midwives must pull together scattered statistical results and qualitative interviews into a clear, unified story. This synthesis looks at what the numbers truly mean when caring for pregnant women in resource-constrained environments. Avoid repeating every single table or percentage from the results chapter. Instead, focus on the overarching patterns that answer the main research question. Connect local findings back to broader African and global maternal health realities.

Drawing Valid Conclusions

A conclusion must stem directly from the analyzed data, not from personal wishes or institutional biases. Many student researchers make the mistake of introducing entirely new information in the conclusion chapter that was never tested or measured. Keep conclusions concise, objective, and strictly aligned with the original research objectives. If a study on postpartum hemorrhage interventions showed no significant difference between two methods, state that clearly without exaggerating clinical impact.

Formulating Actionable Clinical Recommendations

Clinical recommendations bridge the gap between academic research and hands-on midwifery care in the labor ward or antenatal clinic. To be useful, a recommendation must be realistic, specific, and achievable within the current Nigerian healthcare structure.

  • Targeted: Specify who must act, such as staff midwives, hospital management, or pregnant women.
  • Feasible: Account for common limitations like erratic power supply or shortages of essential drugs like oxytocin.
  • Direct: Use active verbs that clearly state what change needs to happen.

Avoid vague statements like "midwives should provide better care." Instead, recommend specific actions like "antenatal clinics should integrate routine depression screenings using the Edinburgh scale during booking visits."

Developing Policy Recommendations

Policy recommendations address systemic issues that individual midwives cannot fix on the floor of a ward alone. These guidelines target hospital management boards, state ministries of health, or professional bodies like the Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria. When writing policy points, focus on resource allocation, staffing ratios, standing clinical orders, or continuing education mandates. A strong policy recommendation explains not just what rule to change, but why the change improves maternal survival on a national or regional scale.

Addressing Limitations and Areas for Future Research

Every research study has flaws, and acknowledging them demonstrates academic maturity rather than weakness. Discuss sample size constraints, potential selection biases, or missing data caused by patient dropouts. Following these limitations, propose logical next steps for future researchers. Good recommendations for further study build directly on unanswered questions from your current thesis rather than suggesting completely unrelated topics.

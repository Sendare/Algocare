Electronic Mail (Email) Setup, Etiquette, and Attachments

Introduction to Professional Email in Midwifery Practice

As a midwife in Nigeria, communicating clearly is just as important in the digital space as it is in the labour ward. Email is a vital tool for communicating with hospital management, referring physicians, and colleagues across different health facilities. A professional email address establishes immediate credibility. Using a playful address created during secondary school looks unprofessional when discussing clinical reports or official postings.

Your display name should always reflect your actual name and professional title, such as Midwife Amina Bello or Nurse-Midwife Chidi Okoro. When recipients glance at their inbox, they should instantly recognize who is writing to them without needing to open the message. Treat every professional email with the same formal consideration you would give a signed paper memo on institutional letterhead.

Core Components of a Professional Email

A well-structured email guides the reader efficiently through your message. Every professional communication requires specific foundational elements to avoid confusion in a busy healthcare environment.

  • Subject Line: This must be concise, specific, and clear. Instead of writing "Hello," write "Refusal of Transfer for Patient Jane Doe - Ward 4."
  • Salutation: Address the recipient formally using their professional title and surname, such as "Dear Dr. Adebayo" or "Good morning, Matron."
  • Body Paragraphs: Keep your points direct. State the purpose of your message in the opening sentences rather than burying the main point at the end.
  • Sign-off: Close with a polite professional phrase like "Yours sincerely" or "Best regards," followed by your full name, title, and institutional phone number.

Mastering Email Etiquette in Healthcare

Digital communication lacks the tone of voice and facial expressions we rely on in person. This makes word choice and manners critical.

Avoid typing entire sentences in capital letters, as this is interpreted in digital communication as shouting at the reader. Always proofread your message for spelling and grammar errors before clicking send. A message riddled with typos can undermine confidence in your clinical competence.

Before hitting "Reply All" to a thread involving an entire department, ask yourself if every single person truly needs to read your response. Unnecessary replies clutter inboxes and cause important messages to get missed during busy hospital shifts.

Handling Email Attachments Safely

Midwives frequently share duty rosters, clinical guidelines, and administrative reports using file attachments. Knowing how to manage attachments prevents frustrating delays.

Always mention in the body of your email that a file has been attached, such as "Please find attached the updated antenatal clinic roster for November." This alerts the recipient to look for the file even if their email client displays attachments differently.

Keep file sizes manageable. If a scanned document is too large, compress it or convert it to a standard PDF format before sending. Never send unrecognized file types or unknown web links, as these can introduce computer viruses into hospital computer systems.

Security, Privacy, and Patient Confidentiality

Patient health information is sacred. Midwives are legally and ethically bound to protect patient confidentiality at all times, including when using digital communication tools.

Never send identifiable patient names, case notes, or photographs through ordinary, unsecured personal email accounts. If you must discuss a clinical case digitally, use secure hospital-approved servers and remove direct identifiers where possible. Always log out of your email account when using shared computers in the midwifery station or nurses' lounge so that unauthorized persons cannot access your messages.

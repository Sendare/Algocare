Ethical and Legal Issues in Healthcare IT

Introduction to Healthcare Technology and Professional Responsibility

Digital tools transform how midwives and nurses document care, track vital signs, and share client records across Nigerian health facilities. Transitioning from paper registers to electronic systems improves legibility and speeds up emergency referrals. Yet, this shift introduces new duties for healthcare professionals. Using technology demands the same strict adherence to professional ethics as direct bedside nursing. Midwives remain personally accountable for clinical decisions made using data retrieved from software applications, electronic health records, or mobile messaging groups.

Ethical Principles in Digital Health

Providing safe digital care rests on core ethical pillars.

  • Autonomy: Respecting a patient's right to control who views their health information.
  • Beneficence: Ensuring that every software tool or digital intervention genuinely improves client outcomes.
  • Non-maleficence: Protecting clients from digital harm, such as data breaches or software calculation errors.
  • Justice: Guaranteeing fair access to digital health innovations across urban and rural maternity centers.

Data Privacy and Confidentiality in Electronic Health Records

Client confidentiality is a cornerstone of midwifery practice, extending fully into electronic health records. Digital files travel across networks instantly, making them vulnerable to unauthorized viewing by staff members who are not directly involved in a patient's care. Midwives must never share their login passwords or leave active client portals unattended on shared ward computers. Unauthorized browsing of electronic case notes—often called snooping—violates professional codes of conduct and destroys patient trust.

Informed Consent and Telehealth Considerations

Telehealth and mobile health consultations require clear, documented informed consent just like physical procedures in a clinic. Clients must understand the limits of remote assessments, potential technology failures, and risks to data privacy over internet connections. Midwives need to explain how video or audio calls work and confirm that the client agrees to this format of care before starting the session. Documenting this consent in the electronic file protects both the practitioner and the client.

Legal Frameworks and Regulatory Compliance

Healthcare IT operates under national data protection laws and professional regulatory guidelines. In Nigeria, bodies like the National Information Technology Development Agency provide rules governing how personal data is collected, processed, and stored. Midwifery practice is also guided by the Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria regarding digital professionalism and record-keeping standards. Failing to comply with these legal structures can lead to heavy fines for health facilities and disciplinary action, including license suspension, for individual practitioners.

Liability and Malpractice in Digital Health Systems

Digital tools can sometimes fail, leading to legal questions around medical malpractice. If a software glitch provides incorrect drug dosage calculations, liability may be shared between the software vendor, the hospital administration, and the clinician who accepted the output without verifying it. Midwives must exercise independent clinical judgment. Blindly trusting automated system prompts without cross-checking patient conditions does not excuse a clinician from liability when an adverse event occurs.

Best Practices for Maintaining Compliance and Patient Trust

Building a secure digital environment requires continuous vigilance from all healthcare personnel. Simple daily habits protect sensitive data and reinforce client confidence.

  • Lock Screens: Always lock workstation terminals when stepping away from a desk.
  • Verify Identity: Confirm patient details before entering data into a digital file.
  • Report Glitches: Immediately report software errors or suspected security breaches to the IT department.
  • Continuous Education: Stay updated on institutional policies regarding data privacy and digital tool usage.

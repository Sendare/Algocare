Oral Presentation and Peer Defense of Clinical Case Studies

Introduction to Clinical Case Presentation in Midwifery

Clinical case presentation is a vital teaching and communication tool used in midwifery practice across Nigeria. It allows practitioners to share complex, unusual, or educational patient encounters with colleagues, mentors, and students. By reviewing real-life scenarios, the entire clinical team learns from individual experiences, which ultimately improves maternal and newborn care outcomes.

A well-prepared presentation bridges the gap between academic theory and the realities of the labour ward or antenatal clinic. It moves beyond simply reciting patient files to critically evaluating clinical decision-making under pressure. Midwives present cases to demonstrate competence, reflect on errors or near-misses, and contribute to the growing body of evidence-based midwifery practice.

Structuring the Midwifery Case Study for Maximum Impact

A logical structure keeps the audience engaged and ensures no critical clinical detail is missed. Follow this standard framework when organizing your presentation:

  1. Introduction and Demographic Data: State the patient's age, parity, gestational age, and booking status without revealing personal identifiers.
  2. Presenting Complaint and History: Describe why the woman sought care and detail her current pregnancy, past obstetric history, and relevant medical background.
  3. Clinical Findings: Summarize physical examination results, vital signs, and key laboratory or ultrasound investigations.
  4. Management and Interventions: Outline the emergency measures, medical treatments, or surgical procedures performed.
  5. Outcome and Follow-up: Detail the condition of the mother and baby at discharge, along with postnatal care plans.

Delivering an Engaging Oral Presentation

Public speaking in a clinical setting requires clarity, confidence, and professional poise. Avoid reading directly from dense slides or lengthy notes; instead, use bullet points as memory prompts. Maintain steady eye contact with your peers and senior colleagues to keep them engaged.

Pace your delivery carefully, speaking clearly without rushing through complex timelines. Use visual aids like charts or partograph excerpts sparingly to illustrate key points rather than overwhelm the audience. Remember that confidence stems from thorough preparation and deep familiarity with the patient's clinical file.

Anticipating Questions and Mastering the Peer Defense

The peer defense is not an attack; it is an academic discussion designed to test your clinical reasoning. Before your presentation, put yourself in the shoes of your audience. Ask yourself: Why was this specific drug chosen? What alternative management options existed? What complications could have been avoided?

During the question-and-answer session, listen actively to the entire question before formulating your response. If you do not know an answer or if a specific investigation was omitted, be honest rather than guessing. Acknowledging a gap in care or documentation shows high-level professional maturity and a genuine commitment to continuous learning.

Professional Etiquette and Handling Constructive Criticism

Receiving feedback on clinical work can feel personal, but maintaining composure is a core professional skill. When colleagues offer differing opinions on your management choices, receive their comments with grace and an open mind.

Avoid becoming defensive or argumentative when challenged. Instead, thank the person for their insight, even if you disagree with their conclusion. Cultivating a respectful environment during case reviews encourages other junior midwives to share their experiences without fear of ridicule or harsh judgment.

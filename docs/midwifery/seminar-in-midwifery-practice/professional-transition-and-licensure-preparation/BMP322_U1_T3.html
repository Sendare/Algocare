Professional Registration, Licensing Requirements, and Continuing Professional Development (CPD)

Understanding Professional Registration and the Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria (NMCN)

Professional registration is a legal requirement that protects the public by ensuring only qualified persons practice midwifery. In Nigeria, the Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria (NMCN) is the statutory body established by law to regulate the training and practice of nursing and midwifery. Registration means your name is entered into the official register kept by the council, giving you the legal backing to practice.

  • Mandatory Registration: Every midwife must be registered upon completion of training before offering care.
  • Legal Protection: The register safeguards the title of 'Midwife' so unqualified persons cannot deceive the public.
  • Standard Enforcement: The NMCN sets the curriculum, inspects training schools, and maintains professional discipline.
  • Public Trust: Registration assures mothers and families that their caregiver has met the required national standards.

Initial Licensure Requirements for Newly Qualified Midwives

Passing your final professional examinations is a major milestone, but it does not automatically grant you the right to walk into a labor ward and start attending births. You must first fulfill the initial licensure requirements specified by the NMCN. This process transitions you from a student to a legally recognized practitioner.

First, you must complete your approved midwifery program from an accredited institution. Second, you must pass the council's professional qualifying examination. Following success in the exams, you apply for indexing conversion, submit your credentials, pay the prescribed registration fees, and take the official oath of allegiance to the profession. Once these steps are completed, the council issues your permanent registration certificate and your first practicing license.

The Annual Practicing License Renewal Process

Having a certificate of registration proves you completed your training, but your practicing license gives you the ongoing legal permission to work. Unlike the certificate, which is issued once, the practicing license must be renewed periodically.

In Nigeria, practicing licenses for midwives are renewed every three years. To successfully renew your license, you must present evidence of completing the required Continuing Professional Development (CPD) credit units and pay the stipulated renewal fees to the council. Failing to renew means your name moves to an inactive status, and you cannot legally practice until regularized.

Continuing Professional Development (CPD): Purpose and Mandatory Points

Medicine and midwifery change constantly with new research, guidelines, and technologies. Continuing Professional Development (CPD) ensures that practicing midwives do not rely solely on outdated knowledge acquired years ago during basic training.

CPD activities include attending workshops, seminars, conferences, and approved online courses. The NMCN ties CPD participation directly to license renewal. Midwives must accumulate a specific number of credit units within the licensing cycle. This system forces practitioners to update their skills in areas like emergency obstetric care, neonatal resuscitation, and maternal health policies, directly improving patient safety across Nigerian health facilities.

Consequences of Lapsed Licensure and Professional Misconduct

Practicing midwifery without a valid license or engaging in professional misconduct carries severe professional and legal consequences. When a midwife's license expires and she continues to attend to pregnant women and deliver babies, she is practicing illegally. This act is classified as quackery under the law, even though she holds a genuine training certificate.

If complications arise during illegal practice, the midwife lacks legal protection and can face criminal prosecution, imprisonment, or heavy fines. Furthermore, professional misconduct—such as gross negligence, patient abuse, or financial exploitation—leads to disciplinary hearings before the NMCN tribunal. Punishments range from suspension to complete deregistration, stripping the individual of their right to ever practice as a midwife again.

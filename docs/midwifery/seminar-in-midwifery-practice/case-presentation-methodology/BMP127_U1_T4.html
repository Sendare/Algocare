Presentation Skills and Professional Communication

The Role of Effective Communication in Midwifery Practice

Clear communication forms the foundation of safe maternal care in every Nigerian health facility. Midwives transmit critical data daily during shift handovers, patient referrals, and formal case presentations. When clinical information is muddled, vital details about a laboring woman's progress or postpartum hemorrhage risk can be lost. Professional communication bridges the gap between scientific knowledge and practical patient care. It guarantees that multidisciplinary teams—including doctors, community health extension workers, and laboratory scientists—act with one unified purpose.

Effective verbal exchanges reduce clinical errors and build trust with women and their families. Midwifery students must cultivate active listening alongside precise speaking. This means minimizing medical jargon when addressing patients while maintaining accurate terminology during peer reviews and grand rounds.

Structuring a Professional Clinical Case Presentation

A well-organized case presentation follows a logical flow that keeps the audience engaged and highlights clinical reasoning. In busy Nigerian teaching hospitals, listeners quickly lose interest if a speaker rambles.

  1. Introduction and Demographics: State the patient's age, parity, gestational age, and presenting complaint concisely.
  2. History of Presenting Pregnancy: Outline the antenatal course, relevant symptoms, and timeline of admission.
  3. Past Medical and Obstetric History: Highlight previous scars, medical conditions like hypertension or diabetes, and past delivery outcomes.
  4. Physical Examination and Investigations: Summarize vital signs, abdominal findings, and laboratory or ultrasound results.
  5. Management and Progress: Detail the interventions administered, surgical procedures, or labor progress.
  6. Conclusion and Lessons Learned: Summarize the final clinical outcome and key takeaways for practice.

Mastering Public Speaking and Delivery in Healthcare Settings

Standing before senior colleagues and peers to present a complex obstetric case can trigger significant anxiety. Controlling this nervousness relies heavily on preparation, posture, and voice modulation. Stand upright with feet slightly apart to project confidence and support diaphragmatic breathing. Avoid reading word-for-word from slides or a dense manuscript, as this disconnects the speaker from the audience.

Pace your delivery deliberately, pausing briefly after stating critical clinical values like blood pressure or hemoglobin levels. Eye contact builds a connection with the room, turning a rigid report into an engaging professional discussion. Practice articulation in front of a mirror or colleagues to eliminate nervous verbal fillers such as "um" or "you know."

Designing Clear and Impactful Visual Aids

Visual aids should reinforce spoken words, not replace them. When creating PowerPoint slides for a midwifery seminar, keep designs clean and uncluttered. Avoid dense paragraphs of text; instead, use short bullet points that highlight key data.

Font size matters immensely in crowded lecture halls. Use a minimum font size of 28 points for body text and 36 to 44 points for headings. High contrast between text and background prevents eye strain—dark text on a light background works best for well-lit rooms. Limit colorful animations and distracting transition effects. Graphs and diagrams should be labeled clearly so the audience can interpret vital statistics, such as maternal mortality trends, at a single glance.

Handling Q&A Sessions and Constructive Feedback

The question and answer session tests true mastery of a presentation topic. Anticipate challenging questions about your clinical management choices by reviewing the literature before your presentation. When a senior clinician asks a difficult question, listen completely without interrupting or becoming defensive.

Acknowledge valid points made by questioners, even if they highlight an oversight in your management plan. If you do not know the answer to a specific clinical query, state honestly that you will investigate further rather than guessing. Treat feedback not as a personal attack, but as an essential tool for clinical growth and improving future patient care.

Ethical Considerations, Confidentiality, and Professional Etiquette

Protecting patient privacy is a legal and moral obligation in every healthcare institution. During case presentations and seminars, all identifying details—including names, hospital numbers, and specific geographic locations—must be completely anonymized or removed. Obtaining informed consent from patients before using their photographs or unique clinical histories in academic settings is mandatory.

Professional etiquette also dictates respectful interactions with colleagues, tutors, and junior staff. Punctuality, appropriate dressing in clean professional uniforms, and attentive listening while others speak reflect core nursing and midwifery values. Upholding these standards elevates the profession and safeguards the dignity of the women entrusted to our care.

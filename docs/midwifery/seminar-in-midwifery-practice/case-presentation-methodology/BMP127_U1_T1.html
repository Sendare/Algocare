Structure and Components of a Clinical Case Study/Presentation

Introduction to Clinical Case Presentations in Midwifery

A clinical case presentation is a structured way for student midwives to share patient encounters, review clinical reasoning, and discuss evidence-based management. In Nigerian midwifery practice, these presentations bridge theoretical classroom knowledge and hands-on clinical experience in maternity wards and primary health centres. Presenting a case requires organizing patient data logically so that clinical instructors and colleagues can follow the progression of care from admission to discharge.

Patient Biographic Data and Chief Complaint

Every case study opens with demographic details and the primary reason the woman sought care.

  • Biographic Data: Full name or identifier, age, parity, occupation, marital status, and residential address. Age and parity are vital for assessing obstetric risk.
  • Chief Complaint: The primary symptom or problem stated in the patient's own words, accompanied by its duration.

Beginners often confuse the chief complaint with a formal medical diagnosis. Record what brought the mother to the clinic, such as 'severe lower abdominal pain for three hours,' rather than writing 'threatened miscarriage' as the opening line.

Obstetric, Gynecological, and Medical History

A thorough history provides the foundation for identifying risk factors that could complicate pregnancy, labor, or the puerperium.

  • Obstetric History: Detailed record of past pregnancies, including gestational ages, modes of delivery, birth weights, and neonatal outcomes. Calculate the gravida and para accurately.
  • Gynecological History: Menstrual history, age of menarche, cycle length, contraceptive use, and screening history for cervical abnormalities.
  • Medical and Surgical History: Chronic illnesses like hypertension or sickle cell trait, previous surgeries, and known drug allergies.

Conducting and Documenting Physical and Obstetric Examinations

The physical examination begins with a general survey of the woman's appearance, followed by vital signs measurement. In antenatal and intrapartum cases, the abdominal examination follows the standard sequence: inspection, palpation using Leopold's maneuvers, and auscultation of fetal heart tones. Documenting these findings requires objective language. Avoid vague terms like 'normal examination' when you can record specific measurements such as 'symphysis-fundal height of 34 centimeters, vertex presentation, engaged.'

Laboratory Investigations and Diagnostic Findings

Diagnostic results validate clinical suspicions and guide safe intervention. Common baseline investigations in Nigerian maternity units include packed cell volume, blood grouping and rhesus genotype, urinalysis for protein and glucose, and screening for infections like HIV and syphilis. When presenting a case, state the investigation, the patient's specific result alongside the normal reference range, and the clinical implication of an abnormal finding.

Formulating Midwifery Care Plans and Nursing Diagnoses

A midwifery care plan translates assessment data into actionable, patient-centred care. Using identified problems, student midwives formulate clear statements of actual or potential health risks. Interventions must be prioritized based on urgency—such as managing postpartum hemorrhage before addressing nutritional counseling. Each planned action should have a clear rationale rooted in evidence-based midwifery practice.

Case Discussion, Progress Notes, and Patient Outcome

The final section evaluates the effectiveness of the care provided. Progress notes track the mother's daily or hourly response to treatment using objective observations. The case discussion critically analyzes why specific clinical decisions were made, comparing the patient's actual course with textbook expectations. Finally, the summary outlines the ultimate patient outcome at discharge, including postnatal and family planning counseling given before leaving the facility.

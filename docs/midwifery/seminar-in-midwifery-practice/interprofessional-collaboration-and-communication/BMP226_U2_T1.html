Multidisciplinary Teamwork in Maternity Care

Introduction to Collaborative Maternity Care

Childbirth is a physiological life event that occasionally presents unpredictable risks. Because a mother's and baby's condition can change rapidly, no single healthcare professional works in isolation within a safe maternity unit. Collaborative maternity care brings together midwives, obstetricians, anaesthetists, paediatricians, and pharmacists to share clinical judgment, pool skills, and coordinate actions. In Nigerian healthcare facilities, this teamwork bridges the gap between primary community clinics and tertiary teaching hospitals, ensuring that women experiencing complications receive seamless referrals without administrative or professional friction.

The Core Role of Midwives in Normal and Complex Births

The midwife remains the primary caregiver for women experiencing normal physiological pregnancy and childbirth. Midwives monitor progress, provide continuous emotional support, and identify early deviations from the norm. When complications such as postpartum haemorrhage or prolonged labour arise, the midwife initiates emergency first-line management while simultaneously summoning medical colleagues. This dual capacity—leading normal care while acting as an early detector of danger—places the midwife at the very center of the multidisciplinary network.

Obstetricians and High-Risk Pregnancies

Obstetricians are specialist physicians trained in the surgical and medical management of high-risk pregnancies. They step in when pre-existing conditions like severe hypertension, diabetes, or previous uterine scars threaten maternal or fetal wellbeing. Effective teamwork relies on midwives recognizing these high-risk triggers early and handing over care smoothly. A delayed referral can transform a manageable complication into a life-threatening emergency, making clear threshold criteria for obstetric consultation vital.

Anaesthetists: Pain Relief and Surgical Support

Anaesthetists manage pain relief during labor and provide critical life support during surgical interventions such as caesarean sections. Their expertise extends to stabilizing women with severe obstetric shock, eclamptic fits, or massive haemorrhage. In busy Nigerian maternity units, anaesthetic coverage can be scarce, requiring midwives and obstetricians to anticipate surgical needs early and communicate theatre bookings well in advance to prevent dangerous treatment delays.

Paediatricians and Neonatal Resuscitation

  • Birth asphyxia: A leading cause of newborn morbidity that requires immediate pediatric or advanced neonatal support.
  • Routine newborn care: Usually managed successfully by midwives immediately following birth.
  • Specialist involvement: Paediatricians attend high-risk deliveries proactively when fetal distress or prematurity is anticipated.

Communication Strategies for Effective Teamwork

Poor communication remains a major root cause of preventable maternal mortality. Standardized tools like the SBAR framework (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation) help team members exchange concise, vital information during handovers and emergencies. Using structured communication reduces misunderstandings between midwives and doctors, ensuring everyone operates with the same clinical picture.

Overcoming Barriers to Interprofessional Practice in Nigerian Hospitals

Interprofessional collaboration in Nigerian healthcare frequently faces hurdles such as rigid hierarchy, professional rivalries, and resource constraints. Overcoming these barriers requires deliberate cultural shifts. Hospital managements must foster mutual respect, encourage flat communication structures where junior staff can voice safety concerns, and implement joint training simulations that break down silos between midwifery and medical departments.

Handling Negligence, Malpractice, and Professional Misconduct Cases

Defining Negligence, Malpractice, and Professional Misconduct

Understanding the boundaries of your professional duties starts with knowing the exact legal terms used when things go wrong in clinical practice. Negligence occurs when a midwife fails to provide the standard of care that a reasonable and prudent midwife would have provided under similar circumstances, resulting in harm to the mother or baby. It is essentially a careless omission or error.

Malpractice is a more specific and severe form of negligence committed by a professional. It happens when a licensed midwife strays below the required professional standard through ignorance, lack of skill, or intentional wrongdoing, leading directly to injury or death.

Professional misconduct covers a broader range of behavioral and ethical breaches defined by the regulatory body. This includes actions that bring the profession into disrepute, such as financial exploitation of patients, breach of confidentiality, substance abuse while on duty, or practicing outside the approved scope of midwifery.

Legal Frameworks and the Role of the NMCN

In Nigeria, the practice of midwifery is strictly regulated by law to protect the public from incompetent and unethical practitioners. The primary legal instrument governing the profession is the Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria (NMCN) Act. The NMCN is empowered by the federal government to set educational standards, maintain registers of qualified practitioners, and discipline those who violate professional codes.

When a midwife is accused of professional misconduct, the council acts through established investigative panels and a disciplinary tribunal. These bodies have quasi-judicial powers to summon witnesses, review medical records, and hear testimonies. Penalties handed down by the tribunal range from formal admonishment and suspension of license to complete removal of the midwife's name from the register, effectively ending their right to practice in Nigeria.

Common Scenarios Leading to Professional Misconduct Claims

Maternity units are high-pressure environments where minor oversights can rapidly escalate into catastrophic emergencies. Reviewing frequent pitfalls helps midwives avoid legal exposure during daily shifts.

  • Poor fetal monitoring: Failing to detect or act upon abnormal fetal heart rate patterns during the first or second stage of labor.
  • Delayed referrals: Hesitating to transfer a patient with obstructed labor or severe postpartum hemorrhage to a higher facility due to pride or poor judgment.
  • Medication errors: Administering incorrect doses of uterotonic drugs like oxytocin or misoprostol, leading to uterine rupture.
  • Abandonment of labor: Leaving a woman in active labor unattended for extended periods without handing over care to a competent colleague.
  • Breach of patient privacy: Disclosing confidential medical information or HIV status without consent.

Step-by-Step Procedure for Handling Allegations

When a formal complaint of negligence or professional misconduct is lodged against a midwife, a structured administrative and legal process follows. Knowing these steps helps reduce panic and ensures a fair hearing.

  1. Receipt of Complaint: A written petition is submitted to the hospital management, state nursing committee, or directly to the NMCN by the patient, family, or a colleague.
  2. Preliminary Review: The regulatory body or hospital administration reviews the petition to determine if there is a prima facie case worth investigating.
  3. Notice to the Practitioner: The accused midwife is formally served with a copy of the petition and required to submit a written defense within a specified timeframe.
  4. Panel Investigation: An investigative committee summons witnesses, examines case notes, and evaluates expert opinions to uncover the facts.
  5. Tribunal Hearing: If the panel finds merit in the allegations, the case proceeds to the disciplinary tribunal where both sides present evidence under formal rules.

Investigative Processes and Disciplinary Tribunals

The investigative panel and the Nursing and Midwifery Disciplinary Tribunal operate as the judicial arm of the regulatory framework. The investigative panel functions much like a grand jury, gathering facts to decide whether the accused midwife has a case to answer. Midwives called before this panel must cooperate fully, as withholding records or showing hostility can worsen their standing.

If referred to the tribunal, the proceedings are formal and quasi-judicial. The tribunal consists of legal assessors and experienced senior nursing and midwifery professionals. Evidence must be substantiated through medical records, expert testimonies, and witness accounts. If found guilty, the judgment is documented and published in official gazettes, and the midwife's employer is notified immediately to effect suspension or dismissal.

Patient Rights and Documentation Best Practices

In any legal dispute regarding clinical care, patient records serve as the ultimate source of truth. The legal maxim states: 'If it is not documented, it was not done.' Accurate, contemporaneous, and legible documentation is a midwife's strongest shield against false accusations of negligence.

Midwives must respect patient rights, including the right to informed consent, privacy, and respectful maternity care. Documentation must never be altered or backdated after an adverse event occurs, as falsifying records is a grave criminal offense that guarantees disciplinary failure before any tribunal. Every entry should include the date, exact time, clear descriptions of clinical findings, treatments administered, and the signature of the attending midwife.

Preventative Strategies and Professional Indemnity

Preventing professional liability requires proactive risk management and continuous adherence to clinical guidelines. Midwives should constantly update their clinical competencies, practice strictly within their approved scope, and maintain open, empathetic communication with patients and their families.

Additionally, obtaining professional indemnity insurance is a vital safeguard. While not always legally mandated for every employment category, indemnity coverage provides financial protection and legal representation in the event of a civil lawsuit. Practicing without insurance leaves individual midwives personally vulnerable to heavy financial damages if found liable in court.

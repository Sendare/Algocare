Analysis of Ethical Dilemmas in Clinical Practice

Understanding Ethical Dilemmas in Midwifery

An ethical dilemma arises in clinical practice when a midwife faces a situation where two or more moral principles apply, but following one means violating another. There is no simple right or wrong answer, leaving the clinician torn between competing professional duties and personal values. In Nigerian maternity wards, this often surfaces when cultural beliefs clash with standard biomedical protocols, or when scarce resources force painful choices about who receives immediate care. Midwives must recognize that these situations differ from routine clinical errors because they involve deeply held values rather than technical mistakes. Grasping this distinction helps clinicians approach distressing cases with clarity and professional composure instead of panic.

Core Ethical Principles in Maternity Care

Four foundational pillars guide modern healthcare ethics. Midwives use these principles to weigh the rightness of their clinical decisions.

  • Autonomy: The patient's right to make independent choices about her own body and healthcare.
  • Beneficence: The duty to act in ways that actively promote the well-being and best interest of the mother and baby.
  • Non-Maleficence: The obligation to do no harm, minimizing preventable risks during labour and delivery.
  • Justice: Fair and equitable distribution of healthcare resources, ensuring every woman gets the care she needs regardless of status.

Common Conflict Scenarios in Maternal and Newborn Care

Daily practice in Nigerian hospitals frequently throws midwives into emotionally charged standoffs. A classic clash occurs when a labouring woman refuses a medically recommended caesarean section due to religious faith or fear of surgery, putting her baby's life at risk. Another frequent tension involves family members demanding to make decisions for an adult parturient, overriding the patient's own voice. Resource scarcity also breeds bitter ethical friction; when only one functioning incubator is available for two critically ill neonates, the midwife faces a agonizing test of distributive justice. Recognizing these recurring patterns prepares students to handle the emotional weight of clinical practice before facing them alone on the night shift.

Navigating Autonomy Versus Professional Duty

Balancing a mother's right to choose with the midwife's duty to protect fetal life is one of the toughest tightropes in obstetrics. When a competent adult woman makes a choice that healthcare providers know is dangerous, tension peaks. Midwives must first assess whether the patient truly understands the consequences of her refusal. Coercion, threats, and forced procedures violate human rights and professional codes of conduct. Yet, standing by while a preventable tragedy unfolds tests the emotional endurance of any clinician. Clear communication, thorough documentation of informed refusal, and involving senior colleagues or hospital ethics committees provide vital safeguards during these standoffs.

Step-by-Step Framework for Ethical Decision-Making

Navigating moral distress requires a structured approach rather than knee-jerk reactions. Midwives can use a systematic framework to untangle complex cases.

  1. Identify the Dilemma: Clearly state the core conflict and determine which ethical principles are clashing.
  2. Gather Facts: Collect all relevant clinical data, legal guidelines, and personal preferences of the patient and family.
  3. Explore Options: Brainstorm potential courses of action without immediately ruling out unconventional choices.
  4. Consult and Weigh: Discuss the case with multidisciplinary colleagues, mentors, or hospital ethics committees.
  5. Act and Evaluate: Implement the chosen path and later reflect on the outcome to improve future moral reasoning.

Legal Implications and Professional Accountability in Nigeria

Ethics and law are closely intertwined in Nigerian healthcare, though they are not identical. While ethical codes guide moral excellence, the law sets the minimum mandatory standards for professional conduct. The Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria (NMCN) holds statutory power to investigate professional misconduct, negligence, and breaches of scope of practice. Midwives must understand that operating outside their legal scope—such as performing unauthorized surgical procedures in primary health centres—leaves them vulnerable to criminal prosecution and license revocation. Professional accountability means standing ready to defend your clinical decisions in a court of law or disciplinary tribunal based on established standards of care.

Reflective Practice and Peer Debriefing

Carrying the emotional weight of moral distress without an outlet leads straight to professional burnout and compassion fatigue. Reflective practice allows midwives to look back at difficult cases, examine their emotional and clinical responses, and learn for the future. Regular peer debriefing sessions provide a safe space within the unit to talk about traumatic births, ethical dilemmas, and near-misses without fear of judgment. Creating a culture of open discussion in Nigerian health facilities transforms isolated trauma into shared professional wisdom, making the entire maternity team stronger and more resilient.

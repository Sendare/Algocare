Inevitable Abortion/Miscarriage

Defining Inevitable Abortion in Early Pregnancy

An inevitable abortion is a clinical complication of early pregnancy where the products of conception have begun to separate from the uterine wall, but have not yet been expelled. Clinically, the process cannot be stopped, meaning pregnancy continuation is impossible. This state sits between a threatened abortion, where the pregnancy might still be saved, and an incomplete or complete abortion where tissue expulsion has started or finished. Midwives in Nigerian healthcare settings frequently encounter this condition in emergency obstetric care units. Recognizing the condition early prevents prolonged maternal distress and reduces the risk of heavy hemorrhage.

Pathophysiology and How It Differs from Threatened Abortion

The underlying mechanism of an inevitable abortion involves progressive cervical dilation coupled with uterine contractions. Unlike a threatened abortion, where bleeding occurs through a closed cervical os and the pregnancy remains viable inside a closed uterus, an inevitable abortion features an open or effaced cervical os. Once the internal os opens, the amniotic sac loses its structural support and balloons or bulges into the cervical canal. This mechanical opening breaks the decidual attachment, making detachment permanent and progression to expulsion certain.

Clinical Features and Presentation

Patients presenting with an inevitable abortion typically report specific, distressing symptoms that require prompt evaluation by the midwifery team.

  • Vaginal bleeding: Usually heavier than normal menstruation, often containing bright red blood and small clots.
  • Lower abdominal pain: Cramping or colicky pain similar to labor contractions, caused by uterine activity trying to empty its contents.
  • Cervical changes: Physical examination reveals a dilated cervical os, sometimes with tissue or the amniotic sac palpable at the opening.
  • Systemic signs: Vital signs are generally stable unless heavy hemorrhage has caused hypovolemic shock.

Diagnostic Assessment and Ultrasonographic Findings

Diagnosis relies heavily on clinical history and a careful physical examination, including a sterile speculum examination to visualize the cervix. Pelvic ultrasonography confirms the diagnosis by showing specific internal features.

Midwifery Emergency Care and Stabilization

When a woman arrives with an inevitable abortion, immediate nursing and midwifery actions focus on physiological stabilization. First, assess the airway, breathing, and circulation. Secure a reliable intravenous line using a large-bore cannula and draw blood for hemoglobin estimation, blood grouping, and cross-matching. If bleeding is heavy, commence intravenous fluids like normal saline or Ringer's lactate. Reassure the patient, monitor vital signs closely every fifteen minutes until stable, and prepare the woman for either medical evacuation or surgical curettage as ordered by the attending clinician.

Medical and Surgical Management Protocols

Management aims to empty the uterus completely to stop bleeding and prevent infection. Depending on gestational age, hospital guidelines, and patient preference, two main approaches are used:

  • Medical Management: Administration of uterotonic drugs such as misoprostol to help the uterus contract and expel the remaining products of conception.
  • Surgical Management: Manual vacuum aspiration (MVA) or suction curettage performed under appropriate pain relief or local anesthesia to empty the uterine cavity swiftly.

Midwives must monitor vital signs, watch for excessive bleeding post-procedure, and administer anti-D immunoglobulin to Rh-negative mothers where indicated.

Potential Complications and Prevention of Sepsis

An inevitable abortion carries several clinical risks if treatment is delayed or incomplete. The most immediate danger is severe hemorrhage leading to hypovolemic shock. Another major concern is pelvic infection, which can progress to septic abortion, pelvic inflammatory disease, and long-term tubal factor infertility. Midwives play a crucial role in preventing sepsis through strict aseptic technique during examinations, ensuring complete evacuation of the uterine cavity, monitoring for fever or foul-smelling lochia, and administering prophylactic antibiotics when prescribed.

Psychological Support and Bereavement Care

The loss of a pregnancy, regardless of gestational age, is a deeply emotional and painful experience for the woman and her partner. Cultural beliefs surrounding pregnancy loss in Nigeria often mean women grieve silently without adequate support systems. Midwives must provide compassionate, non-judgmental counseling, validating their grief. Creating a private, quiet space away from busy labor wards helps protect their dignity. Providing clear information, answering questions patiently, and offering follow-up counseling resources assist families in beginning their healing journey.

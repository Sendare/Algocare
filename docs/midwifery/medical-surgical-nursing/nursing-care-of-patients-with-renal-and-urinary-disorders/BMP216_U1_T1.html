Assessment of Renal and Urinary Function

Introduction to Renal and Urinary Assessment in Midwifery

Kidney function is vital for maintaining fluid, electrolyte, and acid-base balance, which becomes even more critical during pregnancy and the postpartum period due to significant physiological adaptations. Midwives must understand normal renal anatomy and physiology to detect early signs of urinary tract complications, ranging from asymptomatic bacteriuria to pre-eclampsia-induced renal impairment. Routine assessment helps prevent minor issues from escalating into severe maternal morbidity.

Comprehensive Health History and Symptom Analysis

A thorough history uncovers hidden urinary tract issues before they manifest physically. Midwives should inquire about urinary frequency, urgency, dysuria, and changes in urine color or odor. It is also essential to ask about a past medical history of urinary tract infections, kidney stones, hypertension, or diabetes, as these conditions directly impact renal health.

Physical Examination Techniques

Physical examination of the urinary system follows a structured clinical approach. Inspection involves checking the face and extremities for dependent edema. Palpation of the kidneys is rarely successful in healthy adults due to their deep retroperitoneal position, but tenderness over the costovertebral angle strongly suggests renal inflammation or infection.

Urinalysis and Laboratory Markers

Urinalysis provides rapid insight into a patient's renal and metabolic status. Dipstick testing evaluates pH, specific gravity, protein, glucose, ketones, and blood. Laboratory markers like serum creatinine and blood urea nitrogen measure the glomerular filtration rate and clearance capacity of the kidneys.

Monitoring Fluid Balance and Output

Accurate fluid balance tracking is indispensable for high-risk obstetric patients. All fluids entering the body through oral, enteral, or intravenous routes must be measured alongside total fluid output, including urine, emesis, and drainage. Normal adult urine output should generally not fall below 30 milliliters per hour over consecutive hours.

Special Renal Considerations in Pregnancy

Pregnancy alters the urinary system structurally and functionally. The renal pelvis and ureters dilate due to hormonal relaxation and mechanical compression by the growing uterus, increasing the risk of urinary stasis and pyelonephritis. Furthermore, glomerular filtration rate increases significantly, leading to lower baseline serum creatinine values during normal gestation.

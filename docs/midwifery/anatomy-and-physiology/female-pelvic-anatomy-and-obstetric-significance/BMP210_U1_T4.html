Android

Introduction to the Android Pelvis

The android pelvis is one of the four main pelvic types classified under the Caldwell-Moloy system, famously known as the funnel-shaped or male-type pelvis. While it occurs in a small percentage of women, its presence significantly alters the normal biomechanics of childbirth. Midwives in Nigeria frequently encounter women with varied pelvic architectures due to nutritional histories, childhood growth patterns, and genetic diversity. Recognizing this pelvic type early during antenatal booking or labor assessment helps prevent prolonged and obstructed labor, a major cause of maternal morbidity in resource-constrained settings.

Morphological Characteristics and Skeletal Features

The skeletal architecture of the android pelvis is distinct and heavy. Unlike the rounded gynaecoid brim, the pelvic inlet is heart-shaped or wedge-shaped with a narrow, pointed forepelvis. The sacrum tends to be straight, long, and inclined forward into the pelvic cavity, effectively reducing available space. Furthermore, the ischial spines are prominent and point inwards, and the subpubic arch is narrow and acute, contrasting sharply with the wide, rounded arch required for smooth fetal passage.

Pelvic Inlet, Cavity, and Outlet Dimensions

Dimensions within the android pelvis decrease progressively from the brim to the outlet, creating a true funnel shape.

  • Inlet: The anteroposterior diameter may be adequate, but the transverse diameter is wide posteriorly and narrow anteriorly.
  • Midcavity: Deep and narrow, bounded by prominent ischial spines and a forward-projecting sacrum.
  • Outlet: Characterized by a narrow interspinous and intertuberous diameter, alongside an acute subpubic angle.

Obstetric Implications and Labor Dynamics

Labor progress is notoriously difficult in an android pelvis. Because the forepelvis is narrow, the fetal head often struggles to engage in the optimal occipito-anterior position. Instead, the head may engage in a posterior position or remain high and unengaged at the onset of active labor. As the fetus descends into the narrowing midcavity, descent slows down or arrests completely due to mechanical resistance from the prominent ischial spines and straight sacrum.

Midwifery Assessment and Clinical Management

Midwives manage women with android pelvises through careful clinical evaluation and vigilant intrapartum monitoring. Pelvimetry during antenatal visits helps identify women at risk, though labor behavior remains the ultimate test of pelvic capacity. Midwives must use the partograph diligently to spot prolonged latent or active phases of labor. When arrest of descent occurs despite adequate uterine contractions, prompt referral to a physician for safe operative delivery, such as a caesarean section, is lifesaving.

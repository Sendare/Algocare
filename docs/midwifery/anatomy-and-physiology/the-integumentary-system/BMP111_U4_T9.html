Nails

Introduction to Nail Anatomy and Physiology

Nails are hard keratin plates situated on the dorsal surfaces of the distal phalanges of the fingers and toes. They serve several essential functions in humans, including enhancing precise tactile sensation of the fingertips, protecting the delicate digital tips from trauma, and assisting with picking up small objects. Structurally, the nail unit forms a functional complex composed of the nail plate, nail bed, matrix, cuticle, and proximal nail fold. In clinical midwifery practice across Nigeria, observing the nails provides a quick, non-invasive window into a pregnant woman's general state of health, particularly regarding oxygenation and nutritional status.

Detailed Structure of the Nail Unit

The nail apparatus comprises several distinct anatomical landmarks:

  • Nail Plate: The hard, translucent keratinized structure commonly called the nail.
  • Nail Bed: The skin beneath the nail plate, richly supplied with blood vessels that give nails their pink color.
  • Matrix: The germinal epithelium hidden beneath the proximal fold responsible for nail formation and growth.
  • Cuticle (Eponychium): A narrow band of dead skin sealing the space between the skin of the finger and the nail plate.
  • Lunula: The visible whitish, crescent-shaped area at the base of the nail plate overlying the matrix.

Physiology of Nail Growth and Regeneration

Nail growth is a continuous process driven by mitotic division within the matrix. Fingernails grow at an average rate of approximately 3 millimeters per month, while toenails grow significantly slower, roughly one-third of that speed. Complete replacement of a human fingernail takes about six months, whereas a toenail can take up to eighteen months. Local factors such as trauma, ambient temperature, and systemic factors like severe illness or nutritional deficiencies can temporarily interrupt this growth rate, leaving visible transverse depressions across the nail plate known as Beau's lines.

Systemic and Local Factors Affecting Nail Health

Nail appearance often reflects internal health. Nutritional deficiencies, particularly in iron, zinc, and protein, alter nail structure significantly. Iron deficiency anemia commonly presents with koilonychia, where the nails become thin and concave like a spoon. Chronic hypoxia from cardiac or respiratory disorders leads to digital clubbing, characterized by an increased angle at the nail base and bulbous enlargement of the fingertips. Locally, repeated immersion in water, harsh chemical exposure, and fungal or bacterial infections frequently damage the nail plate and surrounding tissues.

Common Pathologies and Clinical Manifestations in Nigerian Clinical Practice

Midwives in Nigeria frequently encounter various nail abnormalities during antenatal and general clinical duties:

  • Tinea Unguium (Onychomycosis): Fungal infections causing thick, brittle, discolored, and crumbly nails, thriving in warm and humid environments.
  • Paronychia: A painful bacterial or fungal infection of the skin folds surrounding the nail, often triggered by frequent handwashing or minor manicure injuries.
  • Leukonychia: White spots or streaks on the nail plate, usually resulting from minor trauma to the nail matrix rather than a calcium deficiency as popularly rumored.
  • Splinter Hemorrhages: Tiny vertical reddish-brown lines under the nail, which can indicate local trauma or systemic conditions like infective endocarditis.

Nursing Assessment and Physical Examination of the Nails

Physical assessment of the nails forms an integral part of the general systemic examination during antenatal booking and routine clinical evaluations. Midwives should inspect nail color, shape, texture, and capillary refill time. Capillary refill is tested by pressing firmly on the nail tip until it blanches and measuring how quickly the pink color returns upon release; a refill time exceeding two seconds suggests poor peripheral perfusion or anemia. Documenting abnormal findings accurately helps monitor patient recovery and alerts the healthcare team to underlying systemic pathologies.

Definition, Purpose, and Characteristics of Scientific Research

Introduction to Inquiry in Healthcare

Clinical practice in Nigerian hospitals often relies on tradition. Elders say things have always been done a certain way, so juniors follow without asking. Yet, healthcare changes rapidly as new diseases emerge and old treatments fail. Systematic inquiry steps into this gap by replacing guesswork with documented facts. When midwives question routine practices like routine perineal shaving or fasting in labor, they start a journey toward better care. This investigative mindset transforms ordinary clinical duties into a professional science. Without curiosity, maternity care stagnates, leaving mothers and babies exposed to outdated and potentially harmful interventions.

Definition of Scientific Research

Scientific research is a structured and controlled process used to discover new knowledge or verify existing facts. It is not mere opinion or casual observation. Instead, it follows a strict methodology designed to minimize human bias and error. In a midwifery context, this means studying maternal mortality or labor pain management using clear rules, structured data collection, and logical analysis. Every step, from framing the question to publishing the final report, is transparent so other researchers can check the work.

Purpose and Goals of Research in Midwifery

Midwifery research exists to solve practical problems faced in the labour ward, antenatal clinic, and community health post. Its main goals are:

  • Improving Maternal Survival: Finding direct ways to reduce postpartum hemorrhage, eclampsia, and sepsis.
  • Optimizing Clinical Interventions: Testing whether a new position for second-stage labor actually shortens delivery time.
  • Informing Policy: Providing local data that helps the Federal Ministry of Health allocate resources where they are most needed.
  • Validating Indigenous Practices: Studying local herbs or traditional birth attendant methods to see which ones are safe and effective.

Key Characteristics of Scientific Research

True scientific research possesses distinct features that separate it from guesswork or everyday problem-solving:

  • Empirical: Based on direct observation and measurable evidence gathered from the real world, not just thoughts.
  • Logical: Follows strict rules of reasoning where conclusions are drawn from valid data, not jumping to conclusions.
  • Replicable: Other researchers should be able to repeat the study using the same steps and get similar results.
  • Critical: Demands careful scrutiny of procedures and eliminates flaws to ensure the findings are trustworthy.

Translating Evidence into Clinical Practice

Conducting research is only half the battle; the real value lies in changing how midwives care for patients. Evidence-based practice means combining the best available research findings with clinical expertise and the cultural preferences of the mother. Sometimes, midwives face resistance when trying to introduce new evidence-based guidelines into busy public hospitals. Overcoming this barrier requires effective communication, peer leadership, and continuous education so that research papers do not simply gather dust on library shelves, but actively save lives in the delivery room.

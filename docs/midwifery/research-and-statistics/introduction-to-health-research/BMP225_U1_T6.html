Applied

Defining Applied Research in Midwifery and Healthcare

Applied research is a form of systematic investigation designed to solve practical, real-world problems. Unlike studies that focus purely on expanding human knowledge for its own sake, applied research asks: how can we fix this specific problem right now? In the context of Nigerian healthcare and midwifery, this means investigating everyday clinical and community challenges, such as why mothers delay seeking antenatal care or how to reduce postpartum hemorrhage rates in rural primary health centres. Midwives rely on this type of research because the findings translate directly into better patient care, improved clinical guidelines, and more efficient health service delivery.

Applied Research for Practical Solutions

  • Direct Interventions: Testing whether a new community education strategy increases hospital deliveries.
  • Process Improvement: Evaluating how patient waiting times in antenatal clinics affect attendance rates.
  • Resource Optimization: Assessing the most cost-effective ways to distribute mama kits to pregnant women in remote areas.

When a maternity unit in Lagos wants to know if a new nursing shift pattern reduces birth asphyxia rates, they use applied research. The findings guide immediate operational changes.

How Applied Differs from Basic Research in Clinical Settings

It is easy to confuse basic research with applied research, but their purposes are distinct. Basic research seeks to expand fundamental knowledge. For instance, studying the exact cellular mechanism by which oxytocin binds to uterine receptors is basic science. Applied research takes that established knowledge and asks how to use it—such as testing whether a new room-temperature stable oxytocin formulation prevents postpartum hemorrhage effectively in remote Nigerian health posts without reliable electricity.

Examples of Applied Research in Maternal and Child Health

Applied research shapes modern midwifery practice across Nigeria by investigating common clinical challenges. Common areas of study include:

  1. Eclampsia Management: Comparing different magnesium sulfate administration protocols to see which reduces maternal side effects faster.
  2. Breastfeeding Initiation: Evaluating whether peer counseling in rural villages increases exclusive breastfeeding rates in the first six months.
  3. Labor Companion Support: Assessing how the presence of a chosen companion during labor affects the rate of emergency caesarean sections.

These examples show how researchers work directly within healthcare facilities and communities to generate evidence that saves mothers and babies.

Translating Research Findings into Midwifery Practice and Policy

Conducting applied research is only the first step; the real value lies in translation. When a study proves that a specific intervention saves lives—such as misoprostol distribution by community health workers for postpartum hemorrhage prevention—that evidence must reach policymakers and clinical instructors. Midwives play a crucial role in bridging this gap. By participating in research and adopting evidence-based protocols, midwifery practitioners help update standing orders, improve nursing school curricula, and ultimately ensure that mothers receive safe, modern, and effective care.

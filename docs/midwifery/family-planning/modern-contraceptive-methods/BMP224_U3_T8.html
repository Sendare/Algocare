Copper T 380A

Introduction to the Copper T 380A

The Copper T 380A is a small, T-shaped plastic device wrapped with fine copper wire and copper sleeves on its arms. It belongs to the family of Intrauterine Contraceptive Devices (IUCDs) and is one of the most widely available, safe, and effective reversible methods of birth control used in Nigerian health facilities. Midwives frequently recommend it because it contains no hormones, meaning it does not disrupt a woman's natural menstrual cycle or interfere with breastfeeding. The number 380 refers to the total surface area of copper in square millimeters, which is the key factor that makes the device so reliable. Two thin polyethylene threads are tied to the stem of the T and hang slightly out of the cervix into the upper vagina, allowing both the client and the healthcare provider to check that the device remains properly positioned.

Mechanism of Action

The primary way the Copper T 380A prevents pregnancy is through a sterile inflammatory reaction inside the uterine cavity caused by the presence of the plastic frame and copper. This local reaction is toxic to sperm and eggs, preventing fertilization.

  • Sperm Immobilization: Copper ions released into the uterine fluid impair sperm motility and viability, stopping them from swimming up toward the fallopian tubes.
  • Inhibition of Fertilization: Biochemical changes in the endometrium and cervical mucus prevent sperm from capacitating and reaching an egg.
  • Prevention of Implantation: Even if fertilization somehow occurs, the hostile uterine environment prevents a fertilized egg from implanting in the uterine lining.

Duration of Use and Efficacy

One of the greatest benefits of the Copper T 380A is its long-lasting nature. Current clinical evidence proves that the device is approved for continuous use for up to 12 years, though it can be removed safely at any time the client desires pregnancy or a change in method. Its failure rate is extremely low, making it more effective than pills, injectables, or condoms in typical use because it does not depend on daily user compliance. Fertility returns immediately upon removal of the device, with no delay in the client's ability to conceive.

Clinical Considerations for Insertion and Removal

Midwives must perform careful screening before inserting a Copper T 380A to rule out pregnancy, pelvic inflammatory disease, or active cervical infections. Insertion is easiest and safest during menstruation or towards the end of a menstrual period, when the cervical os is slightly open and pregnancy can be confidently ruled out. A bimanual examination is mandatory beforehand to determine the size and position of the uterus.

During removal, the midwife gently pulls on the visible threads using sterile forceps. If the threads are missing, special retrieval hooks or professional medical referral may be necessary. A common misconception among clients is that removal is a surgical operation; midwives must reassure them that it is a quick outpatient procedure done in minutes.

Potential Side Effects and Management

While the Copper T 380A is safe, clients need thorough counseling on normal side effects so they do not panic. The most common changes involve heavier, longer, or more painful menstrual periods, especially during the first three to six months after insertion. Intermenstrual spotting can also occur.

Midwives manage mild cramping and pain with simple analgesics like ibuprofen or paracetamol. If heavy bleeding causes anemia, iron supplements may be prescribed. However, if a client experiences severe lower abdominal pain, abnormal vaginal discharge, or fever, the midwife must evaluate for pelvic inflammatory disease (PID) and consider removing the device.

Client Counseling in Nigerian Midwifery Practice

Effective counseling bridges the gap between fear and acceptance in many Nigerian communities where myths about family planning persist. Midwives must address local misconceptions—such as the false belief that the IUCD can travel to the heart or cause permanent sterility. Counseling should cover what to expect regarding bleeding changes, how to check for the threads after each menstrual period, and when to return for check-ups. Encouraging open communication ensures that clients feel supported and are less likely to discontinue the method prematurely due to manageable side effects.

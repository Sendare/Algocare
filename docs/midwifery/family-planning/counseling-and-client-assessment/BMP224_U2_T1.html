Principles of Counseling in Family Planning (GATHER Approach)

Introduction and Core Principles of Family Planning Counseling

Family planning counseling is a confidential dialogue between a midwife and a client designed to help them make voluntary, informed decisions about their reproductive lives. In Nigerian clinical practice, effective counseling reduces maternal mortality by helping women space births and prevent unintended pregnancies. Midwives must approach every session with empathy, treating the client with dignity regardless of age, parity, or marital status.

  • Confidentiality: Clients must be assured that their private health details will not be shared with family members or the community.
  • Voluntarism: The final choice of contraceptive method rests entirely with the client, free from provider bias or coercion.
  • Impartiality: Midwives must set aside personal religious or cultural beliefs to present all available methods fairly.

Overview and Step-by-Step Breakdown of the GATHER Framework

The GATHER acronym is an internationally recognized framework that guides family planning counseling. It provides a structured yet flexible pathway to ensure no vital clinical or personal detail is missed during the consultation.

  1. G - Greet: Welcome the client respectfully, establish rapport, and ensure privacy.
  2. A - Ask: Inquire about their reproductive goals, medical history, and past contraceptive experiences.
  3. T - Tell: Provide accurate, balanced information about the various methods available.
  4. H - Help: Assist the client in selecting the method that best fits their lifestyle and health needs.
  5. E - Explain: Give clear instructions on how to use the chosen method, including potential side effects.
  6. R - Return: Schedule a follow-up visit and encourage the client to return whenever they have concerns.

Executing the GATHER Steps in Clinical Practice

Moving through the GATHER steps requires active listening and effective communication skills. During the Ask phase, use open-ended questions to understand if the client wants to space births or limit them entirely. When you reach the Tell phase, prioritize methods the client expresses interest in, but mention other suitable options.

A common mistake during the Explain phase is failing to warn clients about normal side effects like temporary amenorrhea with injectables, which often leads to unnecessary discontinuation. Use simple local terminology when describing anatomy and drug functions, and always invite the client to repeat key instructions to confirm understanding.

Navigating Cultural Realities and Communication Barriers

In many Nigerian communities, family planning decisions involve partners, mothers-in-law, or religious leaders. Midwives must acknowledge these social dynamics without compromising the client's right to confidential care. Language barriers can also impede understanding; where possible, conduct the session in the client's primary local language or use a neutral interpreter.

Non-verbal cues such as avoiding eye contact or crossed arms may signal anxiety or distrust. Midwives should maintain an open posture, nod encouragingly, and use reflective listening to confirm that the client feels heard and respected.

Ensuring Informed Choice and Client Autonomy

Informed choice means a client voluntarily decides on a contraceptive method after receiving a full, balanced explanation of its risks, benefits, efficacy, and side effects. Midwives must never coerce clients, rush their decisions, or deny methods based on arbitrary criteria such as parity or age, provided there are no true medical contraindications according to the World Health Organization Medical Eligibility Criteria (WHO MEC).

Autonomy empowers women to take charge of their health, which ultimately improves continuation rates and overall satisfaction with maternal health services.

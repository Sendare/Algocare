Metrorrhagia

Understanding Intermenstrual Bleeding

Metrorrhagia refers to uterine bleeding that occurs at completely irregular intervals, distinct from normal menstruation. The total blood loss or the duration of flow may vary, but the defining feature is the unpredictability and occurrence between expected cycles. Midwives in Nigerian clinical practice often encounter women who describe this as "spotting between periods" or erratic bleeding patterns.

It is vital to distinguish metrorrhagia from other common menstrual abnormalities:

  • Menorrhagia: Excessive or prolonged bleeding occurring at regular intervals.
  • Metrorrhagia: Bleeding at irregular, frequent intervals.
  • Menometrorrhagia: Heavy bleeding occurring at irregular intervals.
  • Polymenorrhea: Regular cycles that occur too frequently, usually under 21 days.

Underlying Etiology and Risk Factors

The causes of metrorrhagia are broad and range from simple hormonal imbalances to serious organic pathology. Midwives use the standard international PALM-COEIN classification system to categorize these causes into structural and non-structural groups.

  • Structural causes (PALM): Polyps, adenomyosis, leiomyomas (fibroids), and malignancy or hyperplasia.
  • Non-structural causes (COEIN): Coagulopathies, ovulatory dysfunction, endometrial disorders, iatrogenic factors, and not-yet-classified causes.

Common triggers seen in local practice include uterine fibroids, chronic pelvic infections, hormonal contraceptive side effects (such as missed pills or progestin-only injectables), and early pregnancy complications like incomplete miscarriages or ectopic pregnancies.

Clinical Assessment and Diagnostic Evaluation

Evaluating a patient with metrorrhagia requires a detailed clinical history and a systematic physical examination. The midwife must ascertain the exact onset, duration, quantity, and associated symptoms such as pelvic pain, fever, or foul-smelling discharge.

Essential diagnostic steps typically include:

  1. Urinary or serum pregnancy test: To rule out gestational complications immediately.
  2. Pelvic examination: To inspect the cervix, vagina, and assess uterine size and tenderness.
  3. Pelvic ultrasound: To visualize the endometrial thickness, ovarian cysts, or uterine fibroids.
  4. Laboratory investigations: Full blood count to check for anemia resulting from chronic blood loss, alongside cervical screening tests where indicated.

Failing to assess hemoglobin levels in women with prolonged spotting is a common oversight that can lead to severe, unmanaged iron-deficiency anemia.

Medical and Surgical Management Strategies

Management of metrorrhagia depends entirely on the underlying cause, the patient's age, and her desire for future fertility. Medical management often serves as the first line of treatment, utilizing hormonal therapy such as combined oral contraceptive pills or progestin therapy to regulate the endometrial lining. Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) may also be used to reduce heavy flow and associated cramping.

When medical treatments fail or when structural abnormalities like large fibroids, polyps, or endometrial hyperplasia are identified, surgical interventions become necessary. These range from minor procedures like dilatation and curettage (D&C) or hysteroscopic polyp removal to definitive surgeries such as myomectomy or hysterectomy for women who have completed their families.

Midwifery Nursing Care and Patient Support

Comprehensive nursing care for a woman with metrorrhagia extends beyond medical administration to encompass psychological support, health education, and symptom monitoring. Many women experience anxiety regarding unpredictable bleeding episodes and fears about potential underlying malignancies like cervical or uterine cancer. The midwife provides a safe space for the patient to express these concerns.

Educational efforts should focus on helping the patient track her menstrual cycles using a calendar or mobile application, adhere strictly to prescribed hormone regimens, and recognize warning signs that require urgent medical attention, such as soaking through more than two pads per hour or experiencing severe dizziness. Nutritional counseling emphasizing iron-rich foods is also incorporated to combat mild anemia.

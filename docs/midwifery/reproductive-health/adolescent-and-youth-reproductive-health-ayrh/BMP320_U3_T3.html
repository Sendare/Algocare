Unsafe Abortion

Defining Unsafe Abortion in the Context of Nigerian Youth

An unsafe abortion is defined by the World Health Organization as a procedure for terminating an unintended pregnancy carried out either by persons lacking the necessary skills or in an environment that does not conform to minimal medical standards, or both. Among Nigerian adolescents, this issue carries an exceptionally heavy burden. Young girls face strict societal taboos against premarital sex, severe economic vulnerability, and restrictive legal frameworks surrounding pregnancy termination. Consequently, many adolescents hide their pregnancies until advanced gestational ages. When they finally seek help, they often bypass formal healthcare facilities due to fear of stigma, scolding, or arrest by healthcare workers. This drives them toward covert, dangerous procedures performed in backstreets by unqualified practitioners. Recognizing this definition helps midwives understand that adolescent abortion is rarely a casual choice, but rather a desperate response to overwhelming social pressures.

Why Adolescents Resort to Unsafe Termination Methods

  • Societal Stigma: Deep-cultural and religious disapproval of premarital pregnancy forces young girls to conceal their condition at all costs.
  • Financial Constraints: Lack of independent income prevents adolescents from affording safe, legal care where available.
  • Ignorance of Services: Many youths do not know where to access youth-friendly reproductive health services or confidential counseling.
  • Restrictive Laws: Nigeria's stringent abortion laws create an atmosphere of secrecy, pushing patients away from safe medical providers.
  • Fear of Provider Bias: Adolescents frequently report being verbally abused or turned away by judgmental nurses and doctors when seeking help.

Common Methods and Dangerous Concoctions Used Locally

Desperation drives young girls to experiment with alarming substances and physical methods. Locally, concoctions containing caustic potash, concentrated bleach, detergent solutions, or over-doses of unprescribed malaria drugs and herbal mixtures are frequently ingested or inserted vaginally. Mechanically, unqualified providers often use crude instruments such as knitting needles, bicycle spokes, feathers, or dirty catheters to dilate the cervix and rupture the amniotic sac. These methods rarely result in a clean, complete evacuation. Instead, they cause severe tissue lacerations, chemical burns of the vaginal vault and uterus, and direct introduction of tetanus spores and other virulent bacteria into the pelvic cavity.

Immediate Physical Complications and Emergency Presentation

The clinical presentation of an incomplete or septic unsafe abortion is a true obstetric emergency. Patients typically arrive at the emergency ward presenting with heavy vaginal bleeding, lower abdominal cramping, and signs of hypovolemic shock such as tachycardia, pallor, and hypotension. When uterine perforation has occurred from sharp instruments, acute peritonitis develops rapidly, manifesting as board-like abdominal rigidity and severe rebound tenderness. Furthermore, ascending infections frequently progress to septic shock, multi-organ dysfunction, and death if prompt resuscitation is delayed. Midwives must maintain a high index of suspicion for clandestine abortion when treating any young female presenting with unexplained lower abdominal sepsis or shock.

Long-Term Reproductive Consequences for Young Women

For those who survive the immediate crisis, unsafe abortion casts a long shadow over their future reproductive health. Chronic pelvic inflammatory disease frequently develops from ascending bacterial infections, leading to persistent pelvic pain and blocked fallopian tubes. This tubal blockage is a leading cause of secondary infertility among young Nigerian women. Additionally, vigorous or deep curettage performed by unskilled hands can damage the basal layer of the endometrium, resulting in intrauterine adhesions known as Asherman's syndrome, which causes secondary amenorrhea. Should a young woman successfully conceive again after an unsafe abortion, her risk of ectopic pregnancy, spontaneous miscarriage, and placental abnormalities increases significantly.

Post-Abortion Care and Empathetic Midwifery Management

Post-Abortion Care (PAC) is a vital emergency intervention strategy designed to save lives and restore health. When managing an adolescent presenting with abortion complications, the midwife's attitude can make the difference between recovery and medical abandonment. Midwives must set aside personal moral judgments, offering non-judgmental, compassionate, and strictly confidential care. Clinical management involves treating hemorrhage and sepsis, followed by uterine evacuation using Manual Vacuum Aspiration (MVA) or medical methods where appropriate. Crucially, PAC includes comprehensive counseling, emotional support, and immediate provision of modern contraception before the patient leaves the facility to prevent recurrent unwanted pregnancies.

Prevention Strategies: Contraception and Youth-Friendly Services

The ultimate solution to the public health crisis of unsafe abortion is primary prevention through robust education and accessible services. Midwives play a central role in advocating for and establishing youth-friendly health services (YFHS). These are clinics where adolescents feel welcomed, respected, and assured of absolute confidentiality without parental notification hurdles. Prevention requires dispelling widespread myths about modern contraceptives—such as beliefs that birth control causes permanent sterility in young girls. By providing comprehensive sex education in schools and communities, midwives empower adolescents with accurate knowledge on how to protect their bodies and avoid unintended pregnancies altogether.

Observation and Recording on Temperature Charts and Nurses Notes

Introduction to Clinical Documentation in Midwifery Practice

Clinical documentation serves as the written backbone of maternity care. Every assessment, intervention, and clinical observation must be captured clearly to ensure patient safety and continuity. Midwives in Nigeria manage fast-paced wards where verbal communication alone fails under pressure. A well-kept record acts as a legal safeguard, a research tool, and a communication link between the antenatal, labor, and postnatal wards.

Systematic Observation of Maternal and Neonatal Vital Signs

Assessing a mother or newborn requires a structured approach. Vital signs include temperature, pulse, respiration, and blood pressure, alongside fetal heart rate or newborn respiratory effort. Midwives must take these measurements accurately before recording them. Relying on guesswork or estimating values puts vulnerable patients at high risk of unmanaged postpartum hemorrhage or neonatal sepsis.

Accurate Recording on Temperature Charts

The temperature chart, often called the TPR chart, provides a visual timeline of a patient's physiological stability. Midwives plot temperature, pulse, and respiration using standardized symbols and lines.

  • Temperature: Usually plotted with a dot or cross and connected with straight lines.
  • Pulse: Frequently plotted in a contrasting color or distinct symbol.
  • Blood Pressure: Recorded numerically at designated intervals on the chart.

Errors in plotting can obscure a rising fever or developing shock, delaying vital medical intervention.

Documenting Care and Observations in Nurses and Midwives Notes

Nurses and midwives notes provide a chronological, narrative account of nursing care delivered and the patient's response. Entries must be factual, objective, and timely. Avoid vague terms like 'patient slept well' when specific details like 'patient slept for four hours following administration of analgesia' offer better clinical context. Every entry must bear the date, exact time, signature, and professional designation of the midwife.

Legal, Ethical, and Professional Standards of Record Keeping

Clinical records are legal documents admissible in a court of law. A common error among trainees is erasing mistakes with correction fluid; errors should instead be crossed out with a single neat line so the original entry remains legible, followed by the correct entry, date, and initials. Confidentiality is paramount. Patient records must never be left lying around where unauthorized persons can read them.

Handover Communication and Continuity of Care

The shift handover is a critical vulnerability point in healthcare delivery. Midwives must communicate patient status clearly using structured formats like SBAR (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation). A proper handover ties together the written chart and the verbal summary, ensuring no critical laboratory result, pending medication, or unstable patient is overlooked as one shift relieves another.

Vital Signs and Apex Beat

Clinical Baseline Assessment

Vital signs form the foundation of clinical assessment in midwifery practice. When a pregnant or postpartum woman walks into a clinic in Nigeria, capturing her baseline measurements allows you to spot sudden physiological changes. Baseline values provide a comparative reference point throughout labour, the puerperium, or during management of obstetric complications like pre-eclampsia. These parameters reflect the functional status of the vital organs: the brain, heart, and lungs. Always ensure the client rests for a few minutes before taking these measurements, as anxiety or physical exertion can falsely elevate readings.

Temperature Measurement

Body temperature indicates the balance between heat production and heat loss. In tropical healthcare settings, malaria and urinary tract infections frequently cause pyrexia during pregnancy and the puerperium. Midwives commonly use digital thermometers via the axillary or sublingual route.

  • Normal Range: 36.5°C to 37.5°C.
  • Pyrexia (Fever): Any reading above 38.0°C.
  • Hypothermia: Readings below 35.0°C, more commonly seen in neonates or shocked patients.

Ensure the axilla is dry before placing the probe, as sweat cools the skin and yields a falsely low reading.

Pulse Assessment

Pulse evaluation measures heart rate, rhythm, and volume by palpating an artery over a bony prominence. The radial artery at the wrist is standard, but the carotid, femoral, or dorsalis pedis arteries are assessed when peripheral perfusion is poor.

Count the pulse for a full minute if you notice any irregularity, though thirty seconds multiplied by two is common for a regular rhythm.

  • Tachycardia: Heart rate above 100 beats per minute, often pointing to pain, anxiety, hemorrhage, or sepsis.
  • Bradycardia: Heart rate below 60 beats per minute, seen in well-conditioned athletes or cases of raised intracranial pressure.

Respiration

Assessing respiration involves observing the rise and fall of the client's chest without drawing their attention to it, as conscious control alters breathing patterns. Count for a full minute because irregular breathing is common in clinical emergencies.

  • Normal Adult Rate: 12 to 20 breaths per minute.
  • Tachypnea: Rapid breathing above 20 breaths per minute, an early indicator of respiratory distress, metabolic acidosis, or severe anemia.
  • Bradypnea: Slow breathing below 12 breaths per minute, which can result from opioid toxicity.

Note the depth, character, and use of accessory muscles.

Blood Pressure Measurement

Blood pressure measures the lateral pressure exerted by circulating blood on the walls of the arteries. A standard mercury or aneroid sphygmomanometer with an appropriately sized cuff is essential. In antenatal care, monitoring blood pressure is vital for early detection of gestational hypertension and pre-eclampsia.

Ensure the client's arm is supported at heart level. Inflate the cuff until the pulse disappears, then slowly deflate while listening with the stethoscope over the brachial artery. The first clear tapping sound represents the systolic pressure, and the disappearance of sound marks the diastolic pressure.

Apex Beat Assessment

The apex beat, or point of maximal impulse, represents the thrust of the left ventricle against the chest wall during systole. Locating it helps assess heart size and position.

To perform this assessment, place your fingers over the fifth intercostal space along the midclavicular line on the left side of the chest. In healthy adults, it occupies a small area, roughly the size of a coin. Displacement laterally or downward suggests cardiac enlargement, which may occur in chronic hypertension or heart failure.

Documentation and Clinical Significance

Accurate documentation in the client's case notes completes the vital sign assessment process. Recording numbers without noting trends can lead to missed warning signs. Midwives must plot findings on partographs during labour or graphical charts during antenatal visits.

Every recorded value must include the exact time and date. Quick recognition of abnormal patterns allows for prompt interdisciplinary referrals, improving maternal and fetal survival rates across healthcare facilities in Nigeria.

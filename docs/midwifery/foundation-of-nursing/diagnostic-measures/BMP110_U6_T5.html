Collection and Observation of Specimens: Vomitus

Clinical Significance of Emesis in Patient Assessment

Analyzing vomitus offers a direct window into upper gastrointestinal health. Midwives assess emesis to detect bleeding, obstruction, or infection early. Recognizing what normal digestion looks like helps you immediately spot abnormal gastric contents during labor or the postpartum period.

Preparation and Safety Precautions Before Collection

Before handling any bodily fluid, protect yourself and your patient. Gather a clean kidney basin, non-sterile gloves, a waterproof apron, and a proper specimen container if laboratory testing is ordered. Always explain the procedure clearly to the woman to reduce anxiety and secure her cooperation.

Vomitus Collection and Clinical Observation Steps

  1. Positioning: Help the patient sit up or turn her head to the side to prevent aspiration.
  2. Collection: Provide a clean emesis basin to catch the specimen securely.
  3. Inspection: Observe the contents immediately while fresh under good lighting.
  4. Measurement: Quantify the volume accurately using a graduated container if necessary.

Key Characteristics to Inspect: Color, Consistency, and Odor

  • Color: Bright red blood indicates active bleeding, while dark brown or 'coffee-ground' material suggests older, digested blood.
  • Consistency: watery fluid differs greatly from thick, sticky mucus or undigested food particles.
  • Odor: A foul, fecal smell often points to a serious lower intestinal obstruction.

Identifying Associated Pathological Findings and Foreign Matter

Look closely for unintended substances within the emesis. Undigested pills reveal medication failure or poisoning. Mucus strands point toward heavy gastric irritation, while bilious green or yellow fluid shows that the vomitus comes from beyond the pyloric sphincter.

Accurate Documentation and Reporting Protocol

Record your findings immediately in the patient chart. Note the exact time, estimated volume in milliliters, color, consistency, odor, and any trigger factors like meals or medication. Promptly inform the doctor or senior midwife if you observe blood, bile, or unusual foreign objects.

Infection Control and Proper Specimen Disposal

Once observation and testing are complete, empty the basin into the clinical sluice hopper. Clean and decontaminate reusable basins according to hospital policy. Remove your gloves carefully, wash your hands thoroughly with soap and water, and discard single-use waste into the appropriate infectious waste bin.

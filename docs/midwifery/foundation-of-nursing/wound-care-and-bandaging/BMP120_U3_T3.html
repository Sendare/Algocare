Aseptic Technique in Wound Dressing

Introduction to Aseptic Technique

Aseptic technique is a set of practices designed to prevent microorganisms from entering a sterile body site or wound. In midwifery practice in Nigeria, this technique is your primary defence during procedures like episiotomy repair, catheterization, and post-operative wound dressing. While sterility means the complete absence of all living micro-organisms, asepsis means freedom from infection or infectious material. The goal is never total sterility of a treatment room—which is practically impossible—but rather the strict maintenance of a sterile field to protect vulnerable tissues from pathogenic invasion.

Principles of Antisepsis and Sterility

To maintain a safe environment, you must master a few non-negotiable rules. A sterile field, once set up, can only touch other sterile items. If a sterile item touches something clean or dirty, it is immediately considered contaminated. When in doubt about whether an item is sterile, consider it contaminated and discard it. Keep sterile items within your line of vision at all times; turning your back on an open dressing tray invites contamination.

Equipment and Sterile Field Preparation

Before touching any wound, gather all necessary supplies to avoid leaving the patient unattended. A standard dressing pack typically includes forceps, cotton balls, gauze pads, and a disposal tray.

  • Check expiry dates: Always inspect packaging integrity and sterilization indicator strips.
  • Open packaging carefully: Peel wrappers back without touching the inner contents or allowing your sleeves to brush the sterile field.
  • Establish boundaries: Consider the outer one-inch border of a sterile drape as contaminated.

Step-by-Step Wound Dressing Procedure

  1. Explain and prepare: Inform the patient about the procedure, screen the bed for privacy, and position her comfortably.
  2. Hand hygiene and PPE: Perform handwashing and put on clean non-sterile gloves to remove the old dressing.
  3. Assess the wound: Inspect the incision site or ulcer for redness, swelling, heat, pain, or abnormal discharge.
  4. Discard old waste: Remove your dirty gloves, perform hand hygiene again, and open your sterile pack.
  5. Cleanse the wound: Using sterile forceps and swabs soaked in antiseptic, clean from the center outward to avoid dragging external skin flora into the healing bed.
  6. Apply new dressing: Cover the wound with sterile gauze and secure it gently with tape or a bandage.

Infection Control and Waste Management

Proper waste disposal protects both healthcare workers and the community. Sharp instruments like suture removal scissors must go into a puncture-resistant safety box. Soiled dressings soaked in blood or lochia go into infectious waste bins, usually color-coded red or yellow depending on local hospital guidelines. Never recap used needles, and ensure your hands are washed thoroughly immediately after removing your final pair of gloves.

Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

Even experienced clinicians sometimes break sterile technique due to fatigue or rushing. Common errors include reaching across a sterile field, allowing sterile forceps to point downward where unsterile drops can flow back onto the working tips, and letting moisture soak through a sterile paper drape from a wet table underneath. Always keep your hands above your waist level and below your neck to maintain constant visual control.

Hair Care

Introduction to Hair and Scalp Hygiene in Patient Care

Hair and scalp hygiene is an essential component of total patient care, particularly for individuals who are bedridden, frail, or recovering from surgery. Maintaining clean hair does more than just improve appearance; it supports psychological well-being, boosts self-esteem, and prevents physical complications such as matting, fungal infections, and severe pruritus. When caring for patients in Nigerian healthcare settings, midwives and nurses must balance clinical priorities with cultural preferences regarding hair styling and maintenance. A clean scalp promotes comfort and signals high standards of holistic nursing care.

Assessing the Patient's Hair and Scalp Condition

Before initiating any hair care procedure, a thorough assessment of the hair and scalp is mandatory. Midwives should inspect for oiliness, dryness, flaking, foul odors, and signs of infestation such as head lice or nits. The scalp must be gently palpated for tenderness, lesions, swelling, or trauma, especially in patients who have experienced falls or head injuries. Documenting baseline findings ensures that any pre-existing conditions are noted and prevents healthcare providers from misattributing prior scalp issues to hospital-acquired complications.

Equipment Preparation and Setup for Bedside Hair Washing

Gathering appropriate supplies beforehand prevents unnecessary delays and keeps the patient comfortable throughout the procedure.

  • Shampoo tray or trough: Directs water away from the bed and into a collection bucket.
  • Water pitcher and basin: Holds warm water for wetting and rinsing the hair.
  • Towels and waterproof sheet: Protects the bed linen and keeps the patient's shoulders dry.
  • Gentle shampoo and comb: Cleans the hair safely without causing harsh friction.
  • Pitcher of warm water: Ensures comfortable rinsing temperatures.

Always test the water temperature on the inside of your wrist before pouring it over the patient's head.

Performing Bedside Hair Washing and Scalp Care

Bedside hair washing requires careful coordination to prevent water from entering the patient's ears and eyes. Position the patient comfortably, placing a rolled towel beneath the neck for support. Place cotton balls loosely in the patient's external ear canals to block water entry, and provide a folded washcloth for them to hold over their eyes. Wet the hair gradually, apply a mild shampoo, and use the pads of your fingers—never your fingernails—to massage the scalp thoroughly. Rinse completely to remove all soap residue, which can otherwise cause dryness and itching, then wrap the hair in a dry towel immediately.

Managing Special Hair Types and Complications

Patients possess diverse hair textures, ranging from fine and straight to tightly coiled and coarse. Tightly coiled and textured hair requires special attention, as it is prone to dryness and breakage when manipulated roughly. Avoid dry brushing on tightly coiled hair unless it is damp or properly lubricated with a suitable conditioner or oil. For severely tangled or matted hair, never cut the hair without explicit patient consent. Instead, work patiently from the ends up to the roots using a wide-toothed comb and a detangling agent.

Documenting and Promoting Patient Comfort Post-Procedure

Once hair care is complete, dry the hair gently using a clean towel or a low-heat hair dryer if appropriate. Style the hair according to the patient's personal preference to restore their sense of normalcy and dignity. Ensure the patient's gown and surrounding bed linens are dry, and reposition them comfortably in bed. Finally, document the procedure in the nursing notes, recording the date, time, condition of the scalp, any abnormalities observed such as lesions or lice, and the patient's tolerance of the procedure.

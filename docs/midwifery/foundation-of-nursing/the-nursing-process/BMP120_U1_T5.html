Implementation of Nursing Care

Understanding the Implementation Phase in Nursing Practice

The implementation phase represents the action step of the nursing process. During this stage, the midwife or nurse carries out the specific interventions outlined in the care plan to help the patient achieve set health goals. It transforms planned strategies into real-world actions, ranging from administering prescribed medications and monitoring vital signs to providing health education and comfort measures. This phase requires critical thinking, clinical competence, and continuous communication with the woman and her family. Successful implementation relies heavily on accurate data gathered during earlier assessment and diagnostic steps.

Preparation and Reassessment Before Action

Before executing any nursing intervention, a vital reassessment must take place. Patient conditions can change rapidly in a busy Nigerian maternity ward or clinic. Rechecking the client's current status ensures that the planned intervention remains safe and appropriate. Preparation also involves gathering all necessary equipment, reviewing facility protocols, and ensuring the patient understands and consents to the procedure. Skipping this step risks applying outdated care plans to a patient whose clinical state has already evolved.

Delegation and Supervision in Nigerian Healthcare Settings

In many Nigerian health facilities, staff shortages mean midwives must frequently delegate tasks to lower cadres, such as community health extension workers or student nurses. Delegation involves transferring the responsibility of performing a task while retaining overall accountability for the patient's outcome.

  • Right Task: Assigning duties that match the worker's training level.
  • Right Circumstance: Evaluating patient stability before delegating.
  • Right Person: Ensuring the delegatee possesses the required competency.
  • Right Supervision: Monitoring and providing guidance during and after task execution.

Execution of Planned Nursing Interventions

Executing interventions requires adherence to clinical guidelines, infection prevention standards, and respect for patient dignity. Interventions generally fall into independent, dependent, and interdependent categories. Independent actions—such as positioning a labouring woman for comfort—require no medical order. Dependent actions—like administering oxytocin or intravenous antibiotics—require a valid prescription from a medical officer. Throughout execution, the nurse observes for immediate responses, managing any adverse reactions promptly and adapting the approach as patient needs dictate.

Documentation of Care Provided

Proper documentation is the final, non-negotiable step of the implementation phase. If an intervention is not documented, legally it was never done. Midwives must record actions promptly, accurately, and objectively in the patient's case notes. Avoid vague phrases like 'patient had a good day'; instead, write measurable observations such as 'patient reported pain reduction from level 8 to level 3 after analgesic administration.' Good records protect the legal rights of both the patient and the healthcare provider while ensuring continuity of care across different shifts.

Reception, Admission, Referral and Discharge Procedures

Introduction to the Patient Journey in Nigerian Healthcare Settings

The patient journey in Nigerian healthcare facilities encompasses every touchpoint a client experiences, from initial entry at the gate to final departure. Midwives and nurses often serve as the primary face of the institution, guiding patients through complex administrative and clinical pathways. A smooth transition between departments reduces patient anxiety and builds trust in the health system. Navigating this journey requires an understanding of both public and private sector workflows, resource constraints, and cultural expectations of patients and their families.

Warm Patient Reception and First Impressions

First impressions heavily influence a patient's compliance and willingness to share accurate health histories. A professional reception involves polite greeting, immediate assessment of acute distress, and respectful communication in the client's preferred language where possible.

  • Triage Assessment: Quickly identifying emergency conditions like postpartum hemorrhage or severe pre-eclampsia.
  • Environmental Comfort: Ensuring a clean, well-ventilated waiting area with adequate seating.
  • Attentive Listening: Allowing the patient or accompanying relative to explain the main presenting complaint without interruption.

Comprehensive Admission Protocols and Documentation

Admission formalizes the entry of a patient into an inpatient ward for continuous care and observation. Midwives must execute this process methodically to establish baseline health data and legal protection.

  1. Identity Verification: Confirming the patient's full name, age, and hospital number using an identification band.
  2. Consent Acquisition: Securing informed consent for procedures, admissions, and planned treatments.
  3. Baseline Charting: Recording vital signs, weight, obstetric history, and current medications on the admission sheet.
  4. Personal Valuables Inventory: Documenting and securely storing patient belongings according to facility policy.

Navigating Healthcare Referral Systems and Inter-Facility Transfers

When a patient's medical needs exceed the capacity of a primary health centre, an organized referral becomes life-saving. The transferring midwife must stabilize the client, compile a comprehensive referral letter, and communicate directly with the receiving facility.

Delays in transit or poor communication often worsen maternal and neonatal outcomes in the Nigerian context. Ensuring an accompanying skilled birth attendant and adequate supplies during transit mitigates these risks.

Effective Discharge Planning and Patient Education Procedures

Discharge planning begins not on the day the patient leaves, but ideally at the point of admission. Midwives must prepare mothers for home care by offering clear instructions on postpartum hygiene, newborn care, warning signs, and follow-up clinic dates.

Providing written discharge summaries alongside verbal counseling bridges communication gaps between hospital care and home management, reducing preventable readmissions and maternal complications.

Legal, Ethical, and Professional Responsibilities of the Nurse

Every phase of admission, reception, referral, and discharge is bound by professional codes of conduct and legal statutes. Midwives must maintain patient confidentiality, obtain valid consent, and document all care accurately.

Failure to document or abandoning a patient during an unstable transfer can lead to professional negligence claims and disciplinary action by the Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria. Upholding ethical standards protects both the patient's rights and the practitioner's license.

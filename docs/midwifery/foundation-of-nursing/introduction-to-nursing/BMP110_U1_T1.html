Definition of Nursing and Nurse

Introduction to the Profession

Nursing stands as both an art and a science, forming the backbone of healthcare delivery systems worldwide. At its core, the profession focuses on the protection, promotion, and optimization of health and human abilities. It involves preventing illness and injury, alleviating suffering through the diagnosis and treatment of human responses, and advocating for the care of individuals, families, communities, and populations. In clinical settings, professional nursing requires a unique blend of sharp critical thinking, specialized scientific knowledge, and deep compassionate human interaction.

Definitions of Nursing

Various global and local bodies have defined nursing to capture its dynamic scope. Florence Nightingale historically described nursing as putting the patient in the best condition for nature to act upon them. Modern definitions, such as those by the International Council of Nurses (ICN), expand this view to include autonomous and collaborative care of individuals of all ages, families, groups, and communities, whether sick or well, in all settings. This encompasses the promotion of health, prevention of illness, and the care of ill, disabled, and dying people.

Who is a Nurse?

A nurse is a person who has completed a rigorous program of basic nursing education and is authorized by the regulatory authority of the country to practice. In Nigeria, this authority is the Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria (NMCN). A qualified nurse integrates scientific principles, physical assessment skills, and therapeutic communication to deliver holistic care. Beyond bedside duties, a registered nurse functions as a patient advocate, educator, counselor, and coordinator of multidisciplinary health services.

Core Roles and Responsibilities

  • Caregiver: Provides direct physical and emotional care, ensuring comfort and dignity.
  • Communicator: Facilitates clear exchange of information between the patient, family, and medical team.
  • Teacher/Educator: Empowers patients with knowledge regarding self-care, nutrition, and disease prevention.
  • Advocate: Protects patient rights and ensures their wishes and safety are prioritized within the health system.
  • Leader/Manager: Coordinates care teams, allocates resources efficiently, and oversees safe ward administration.

The Evolution of Nursing in Nigeria

Nursing in Nigeria evolved from traditional home-based care provided by family matriarchs and local healers into a structured, professional discipline. Western nursing practices were introduced primarily through missionary activities and colonial healthcare expansion in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Early formal training institutions were established to prepare local personnel to assist expatriate doctors. Over decades, the profession grew immensely. Today, Nigerian nursing features advanced university degree programs, specialized post-basic certifications, and independent regulatory standards championed by national icons and professional associations.

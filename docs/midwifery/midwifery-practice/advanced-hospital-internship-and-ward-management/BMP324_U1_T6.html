Pre-Graduation Clinical Competency Verification Across All Maternity Units

Introduction to Pre-Graduation Clinical Competency Verification Across All Maternity Units

Transitioning from a midwifery student to a licensed professional requires rigorous validation of practical skills. In Nigerian healthcare settings, this phase bridges the gap between classroom theory and autonomous ward practice. Clinical competency verification ensures that graduating interns can safely manage routine and emergency obstetric cases without direct supervision. Maternity units handle high patient volumes, making it essential that every trainee masters fundamental procedures before entering the workforce.

Competency verification is not merely about ticking boxes on a checklist. It involves structured assessments where senior midwives and clinical preceptors evaluate psychomotor skills, clinical reasoning, and professional attitude. Without this systematic gateway, newly qualified midwives might face real-world obstetric emergencies unprepared, risking maternal and neonatal morbidity. Establishing a transparent verification protocol protects both the patients and the professional integrity of the nursing and midwifery services.

Core Clinical Domains in Antenatal, Labour, and Postnatal Care

A competent graduating intern must demonstrate proficiency across three major maternity care domains. Each domain requires distinct clinical actions and critical thinking.

  • Antenatal Care: Accurate gestational age estimation, identification of high-risk pregnancies, routine screening for anemia and pre-eclampsia, and abdominal palpation using Leopold's maneuvers.
  • Labour and Delivery: Active management of the third stage of labour, plotting and interpreting the partograph, conducting sterile vaginal examinations, and performing immediate newborn resuscitation.
  • Postnatal Care: Monitoring uterine involution, assessing lochia, promoting exclusive breastfeeding, and recognizing early signs of postpartum hemorrhage or puerperal sepsis.

Designing the Comprehensive Skills Passport and Assessment Rubrics

The skills passport serves as a permanent, portable record of an intern's practical achievements throughout their rotation. It compiles individual check-offs for procedures ranging from simple catheterization to complex breech delivery assists. To maintain objectivity, assessment rubrics must accompany every entry in the passport.

Effective rubrics grade performance on a clear scale, usually spanning from novice to competent. Preceptors evaluate not only the final outcome of a procedure but also infection control practices, communication with the mother, and proper disposal of biomedical waste. Standardizing these rubrics across all affiliated teaching hospitals ensures that a sign-off in one facility holds the exact same weight and clinical standard as another.

Standardized Direct Observation of Procedural Skills (DOPS) in the Ward

Direct Observation of Procedural Skills, commonly known as DOPS, provides structured workplace-based assessment. During a DOPS evaluation, a preceptor watches an intern perform a real clinical procedure on a patient—such as setting up an intravenous infusion for a pre-eclamptic patient or repairing a first-degree perineal tear.

Immediately following the observation, the preceptor provides constructive feedback, highlighting strengths and correcting errors. This formative approach allows interns to refine their technique in real-time. To ensure reliability, interns must complete a set minimum number of successful DOPS assessments across different acuity levels before they can be cleared for graduation.

Managing High-Risk Scenarios and Emergency Simulations

Obstetric emergencies unfold rapidly, leaving little time for hesitation. Routine ward placements do not always guarantee that every intern will witness rare complications like shoulder dystocia, eclamptic fits, or cord prolapse. Therefore, simulation-based training is a mandatory component of competency verification.

Using high-fidelity mannequins and role-play scenarios, preceptors test an intern's ability to remain calm, delegate tasks, and execute emergency protocols under pressure. Debriefing sessions following simulations help interns analyze their decision-making and teamwork. Testing these responses in a controlled environment ensures that graduates can translate crisis management theory into decisive, life-saving action on the ward.

Inter-Professional Communication and Ward Management Competencies

A graduating midwife must function effectively as both a clinician and a junior ward manager. Clinical competence extends beyond technical tasks to include shift handover leadership, multidisciplinary communication with obstetricians and pharmacists, and accurate record-keeping.

Interns are assessed on their ability to use structured communication tools like SBAR (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation) during shift changes or when calling for emergency medical help. Ward management evaluation also covers delegating duties to junior nursing aides, prioritizing care when multiple admissions arrive simultaneously, and managing conflict calmly within the healthcare team.

Addressing Deficiencies and Remediation Strategies for Interns

Finding a clinical deficiency during assessments does not imply failure; rather, it highlights an area requiring targeted support. When an intern struggles with a specific skill—such as neonatal resuscitation or perineal repair—preceptors implement a structured remediation plan.

Remediation involves targeted coaching, additional supervised practice hours, and repeated formative evaluations. The focus remains supportive rather than punitive, ensuring the intern builds genuine confidence and competence. Clear timelines are established for re-assessment, allowing trainees to overcome their weaknesses before their final graduation clearance date.

Final Sign-Off, Certification, and Transition to Independent Practice

The culmination of the internship is the final sign-off panel review. Senior ward managers, clinical coordinators, and nursing school tutors review the completed skills passport, DOPS records, and simulation logs. Once the panel verifies that all criteria have been met, the intern receives official institutional clearance to sit for licensing exams or enter independent practice.

Stepping into independent practice brings a shift in professional accountability. Newly qualified midwives must recognize their scope of practice and understand when to consult senior obstetricians. Cultivating lifelong learning habits and maintaining professional indemnity insurance ensures a safe, successful career serving mothers and babies across Nigeria.

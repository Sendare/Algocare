NMCN Midwifery Regulations and Standards of Practice

Introduction to the Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria

The Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria (NMCN) is the statutory body established by the federal government to regulate all aspects of nursing and midwifery education, training, and practice in Nigeria. Operating under the Nursing and Midwifery (Registration, etc.) Act, the Council holds the vital responsibility of safeguarding the health of the public through the maintenance of high professional standards.

Key functions of the NMCN include:

  • Accreditation: Inspecting and approving training institutions for nursing and midwifery.
  • Examinations: Conducting professional qualifying examinations and awarding certificates.
  • Regulation: Setting and enforcing the scope of practice and professional conduct.
  • Licensing: Maintaining the official register of qualified practitioners and issuing practicing licenses.

Understanding the role of the Council is the first step for any student midwife in Nigeria, as it defines the boundaries of your future profession and protects the title of a registered midwife.

Legal Framework and Scope of Midwifery Practice

The scope of practice defines the legal boundaries of what a registered midwife is trained, authorized, and competent to do. In Nigeria, this scope is backed by statutory law, meaning stepping outside these boundaries constitutes illegal practice.

A registered midwife is legally empowered to independently manage normal pregnancy, labor, and the postnatal period, including the care of the newborn. However, the scope also demands recognizing deviations from normal and making timely referrals to medical officers when complications arise.

AntenatalMonitor normal fetal growth, provide health educationRefer to a doctor for severe pre-eclampsia or bleeding

PostnatalAssess involution, promote exclusive breastfeedingRefer for postpartum hemorrhage or severe sepsis

Attempting to perform procedures outside this defined scope, such as performing a Caesarean section or managing major surgical interventions without appropriate advanced training and authorization, violates the law and exposes patients to grave danger.

Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct

Ethics provide the moral compass for midwifery practice. The NMCN Code of Ethics outlines the duties of a midwife toward patients, colleagues, the profession, and society. At the heart of this code is the fundamental right of every childbearing woman to receive safe, respectful, and dignified care.

Key ethical pillars include:

  • Autonomy: Respecting the right of the pregnant woman to make informed choices about her care after receiving adequate explanation.
  • Beneficence: Acting in the best interest of both the mother and the newborn at all times.
  • Non-maleficence: Doing no harm, which includes avoiding negligence and keeping up-to-date with safe practices.
  • Confidentiality: Protecting patient health information and medical history from unauthorized disclosure.

Midwives must maintain professional boundaries and avoid exploitative relationships with vulnerable clients. Respecting cultural beliefs while promoting evidence-based lifesaving interventions is an everyday balancing act in Nigerian clinical settings.

Registration, Licensing, and Continuing Professional Development

Holding a midwifery certificate does not automatically grant the legal right to practice. A practitioner must be duly registered with the NMCN and hold a valid, current practicing license. Registration is permanent upon passing qualifying examinations, whereas licenses are renewed periodically.

To renew a practicing license in Nigeria, midwives must fulfill requirements for Continuing Professional Development (CPD). CPD ensures that practitioners update their knowledge, learn new evidence-based protocols, and discard outdated practices. Participating in accredited workshops, seminars, and online modules earns credit units required for license renewal.

Practicing with an expired license is illegal and carries severe professional penalties. It is the personal responsibility of every midwife to track their renewal dates and complete the necessary CPD credits well ahead of time.

Disciplinary Procedures and Professional Misconduct

When a midwife breaches the code of ethics, acts outside the legal scope of practice, or commits gross negligence, the NMCN invokes disciplinary mechanisms to investigate and penalize the offender. The overarching tribunal responsible for this is the Nursing and Midwifery Disciplinary Tribunal.

Examples of professional misconduct include:

  • Patient abandonment during active labor.
  • Falsification of clinical records or partographs.
  • Practicing while under the influence of alcohol or drugs.
  • Financial extortion or demanding illegal fees for free government maternity services.

If found guilty after a fair hearing, penalties range from formal admonition and suspension of the practicing license to complete erasure of the midwife's name from the official register, permanently stripping them of the right to practice in Nigeria.

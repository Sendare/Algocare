International Confederation of Midwives (ICM) Code of Ethics

Introduction to the International Confederation of Midwives (ICM)

The International Confederation of Midwives (ICM) serves as the global nongovernmental organization representing midwives worldwide. Founded to strengthen the profession and advocate for the health of childbearing women, newborns, and their families, the ICM sets international standards for midwifery education, regulation, and practice. Its overarching mission is to achieve safer childbirth for all women by promoting autonomous, high-quality midwifery care. Through its official policy statements and guidelines, the ICM unifies midwives across diverse cultural and healthcare systems under a shared professional identity and global vision.

Core Categories of the ICM Code of Ethics

The ICM Code of Ethics is structured around four primary categories that guide professional conduct. These sections provide a comprehensive framework for ethical decision-making in clinical, educational, and research settings:

  • Midwifery Practice: Responsibilities related to providing safe, competent, and evidence-based care.
  • The Midwife and Persons in Professional Relationship: Upholding human rights, respecting dignity, and advocating for women and newborns.
  • The Midwife's Practice Environment: Fostering supportive working conditions and participating in policy development.
  • Professional Obligations: Maintaining competence, upholding professional integrity, and advancing the profession.

Midwifery Practice and Professional Accountability

Professional accountability means a midwife must answer for her clinical decisions and actions. Midwives are morally and legally responsible for the care they provide to mothers and babies. Upholding this standard requires staying current with evidence-based practices rather than relying solely on outdated traditions. When a midwife recognizes a clinical situation beyond her scope of competence, she must seek immediate consultation or referral. Accountability also involves admitting mistakes honestly, engaging in reflective practice, and participating in continuous professional development throughout one's career.

The Midwife's Responsibility to Women and Newborns

At the heart of the ICM code is the principle of respect for human dignity and autonomy. Midwives must ensure that women receive informed consent before any procedure is performed. This means explaining risks, benefits, and alternatives in language the patient clearly understands. Care must be culturally safe, respecting the woman's beliefs, values, and traditions. Furthermore, the midwife acts as a steadfast protector of the newborn, ensuring immediate essential care, promoting skin-to-skin contact, and safeguarding the vulnerable infant from harm.

Collaboration and the Interprofessional Healthcare Team

Safe maternity care rarely happens in isolation. Midwives must collaborate respectfully with doctors, nurses, laboratory scientists, and community health workers. Effective teamwork requires clear communication, mutual respect, and a shared focus on the well-being of the mother and newborn. When interprofessional conflicts arise, the midwife must prioritize patient safety and professional diplomacy. Valuing the distinct skills of other health professionals strengthens the entire healthcare system and ensures seamless patient transfers when emergencies occur.

Advocacy for Human Rights and Gender Equality

Midwives are powerful agents of social change. The ICM code mandates that midwives advocate for gender equality and women's human rights, recognizing that maternal mortality is often rooted in poverty, discrimination, and lack of power. Midwives must actively speak out against gender-based violence, harmful traditional practices, and coercion in healthcare settings. By championing vulnerable populations, midwives help dismantle barriers that prevent women from accessing timely, respectful maternity care.

Applying Global Ethical Standards to Nigerian Practice

Translating international ethical principles into local Nigerian practice requires navigating unique cultural and resource realities. Midwives working in busy urban teaching hospitals and remote rural primary health centers alike encounter challenges such as stockouts of essential drugs, cultural pressure regarding birth positions, and traditional beliefs. Applying the ICM code in Nigeria means honoring a woman's dignity even when resources are scarce, advocating for respectful maternity care to eliminate verbal or physical abuse during childbirth, and partnering respectfully with traditional birth attendants to bridge community care gaps.

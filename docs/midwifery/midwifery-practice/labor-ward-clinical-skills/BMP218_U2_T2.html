Partograph Initiation, Monitoring, and Interpretation

Introduction to the WHO Partograph and Its Purpose

The partograph is a standardized, low-cost graphical tool used to monitor the progress of labor and record maternal and fetal well-being. Developed by the World Health Organization, its primary purpose is to provide a pictorial overview of labor to help midwives detect prolonged labor or fetal distress early, thereby preventing complications like obstructed labor, uterine rupture, and postpartum hemorrhage. In busy clinical settings across Nigeria, from primary health care centers to tertiary hospitals, this document serves as an early warning system that guides timely referrals and interventions.

Partograph Initiation: Criteria and Baseline Documentation

Midwives must not start a partograph too early, as doing so can lead to unnecessary interventions. Initiation is strictly reserved for women in the active phase of labor.

  • Cervical dilation: The cervix must be 4 centimeters or more.
  • Uterine contractions: There should be regular, painful contractions—typically at least 3 contractions in 10 minutes, each lasting 40 seconds or longer.

Once these criteria are met, baseline data is recorded on the chart. This includes the patient's name, parity, hospital number, date and time of admission, time of rupture of membranes (ROM), and the baseline fetal heart rate.

Monitoring Fetal Condition During Labor

Fetal well-being is paramount throughout childbirth. The partograph provides specific rows to track three key indicators of the fetus's state:

  1. Fetal Heart Rate (FHR): Must be auscultated every 30 minutes using a Pinard stethoscope or Doppler, and recorded with a clear dot on the graph.
  2. Membranes and Liquor: Assessed and recorded at every vaginal examination. Intact membranes are marked as (I), clear liquor as (C), meconium-stained as (M), blood-stained as (B), and absent/clear liquor as (A).
  3. Moulding: Indicates how well the fetal skull bones are overlapping as they adapt to the pelvis. Recorded as suture lines touching (+

), overlapping but reducible (++), or severely overlapping and non-reducible (+++).

Tracking Maternal Well-being and Progress of Labor

Maternal physiological parameters ensure the mother remains stable while her body undergoes the stress of labor. Pulse rate is recorded every 30 minutes, blood pressure every 4 hours, and temperature every 4 hours. Urine is tested for volume, protein, and acetone at every voiding.

Progress of labor focuses on cervical dilation, assessed by vaginal examination every 4 hours, and the descent of the fetal head, measured using abdominal fifths palpable above the pelvic brim. Contractions are counted over a 10-minute window every half-hour, noting their frequency and duration in seconds.

Understanding the Alert and Action Lines

The graphical section of the partograph features two diagonal reference lines starting at 4 cm dilation:

  • The Alert Line: Begins at 4 cm and travels upward to the right at a rate of 1 cm per hour. When the plot of cervical dilation moves to the right of the alert line, it serves as a warning that labor may be proceeding slower than normal, and the patient should be transferred to a facility with comprehensive emergency obstetric care if she is at a primary health center.
  • The Action Line: Parallels the alert line but is situated 4 hours to the right. Crossing or reaching the action line indicates that definitive management for prolonged labor must be undertaken without delay.

Interpretation and Clinical Decision-Making

Accurate interpretation of the completed partograph transforms raw data into life-saving clinical decisions. Midwives must synthesize all findings together rather than looking at a single parameter in isolation. For instance, meconium-stained liquor combined with a normal fetal heart rate requires vigilance, whereas meconium alongside fetal bradycardia demands immediate resuscitation and delivery. When the alert line is crossed, midwives in primary clinics initiate prompt transfer. When the action line is reached, senior medical staff intervene to resolve obstructed labor, safeguarding both mother and baby.

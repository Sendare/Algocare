Episiotomy Care and Perineal Repair Assistance

Understanding Perineal Trauma and Episiotomy

Perineal trauma during childbirth can occur naturally as tearing or may be intentionally created through a surgical incision known as an episiotomy. While once routine in many labor wards across Nigeria, modern evidence supports restricted use, performed mainly for fetal distress, shoulder dystocia, or instrumental delivery. Midwives must understand the classification of perineal tears to assist effectively:

  • First-degree tear: Involves vaginal mucosa and perineal skin only.
  • Second-degree tear: Involves the perineal muscles as well as skin and mucosa.
  • Third-degree tear: Extends into the anal sphincter complex.
  • Fourth-degree tear: Involves the anal sphincter and the rectal mucosa.

Preparation of the Patient and Sterile Field

Before any perineal repair begins, proper preparation ensures both comfort and infection prevention. Explain the procedure clearly to the mother using simple language, reassuring her while she remains in the lithotomy position. Ensure adequate lighting is focused on the perineum. Assemble a sterile instrument tray containing needle holders, dissecting forceps, suture scissors, and appropriate suture materials such as absorbable polyglycolic acid. Cleanse the perineal area gently with an antiseptic solution from front to back, maintaining a strict sterile field to prevent postpartum infection.

Assisting the Practitioner During Perineal Repair

A midwife assisting with perineal repair plays a critical support role. You must manage adequate lighting, hand instruments to the operator safely, and monitor the mother's vital signs and emotional state throughout the procedure. Keep track of all swabs and needles used to ensure none are retained inside the vagina or wound. Anticipate the practitioner's needs by holding sutures ready and providing gentle suction or swabbing with sterile gauze only when necessary to keep the field clear of blood.

Post-Procedure Episiotomy Care and Hygiene

Good postnatal hygiene promotes rapid wound healing and prevents infection. Instruct the mother to wash her perineum gently with clean warm water from front to back after every toilet use. Advise her to change her maternity pads frequently, usually every four hours or whenever soaked. Emphasize that she should never apply harsh chemicals or unprescribed herbal concoctions directly to the fresh wound, as this causes irritation and delays healing.

Monitoring for Complications and Pain Management

Pain is expected following perineal repair, but excessive or worsening pain requires immediate investigation. Assess the wound regularly for signs of hematoma formation, excessive swelling, or localized infection such as foul discharge and spreading redness.

  • Analgesia: Administer prescribed simple analgesics like paracetamol or ibuprofen.
  • Cold therapy: Apply a cold pack wrapped in a clean cloth to the perineum during the first twenty-four hours to reduce swelling.
  • Sitz baths: Recommend warm sitz baths after the first twenty-four to forty-eight hours to soothe the area and promote circulation.

Documentation and Patient Discharge Education

Accurate record-keeping ensures continuity of care and legal protection. Document the type of episiotomy or tear, the suture material used, the name of the practitioner who performed the repair, and how well the mother tolerated the procedure. Before discharge, provide clear education on pelvic floor exercises to improve muscle tone, signs of infection to watch out for, and when to return to the clinic for follow-up evaluation.

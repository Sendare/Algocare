Constipation

Why Pregnant Women Get Constipated: Hormonal and Anatomical Triggers

Constipation is one of the most common complaints during the antenatal period. The primary hormonal driver is elevated progesterone, which relaxes smooth muscle tissue throughout the body. Because the gastrointestinal tract is made of smooth muscle, its peristalsis slows down significantly, allowing more time for water to be absorbed from the stool. Anatomically, as the gravid uterus expands, it exerts mechanical pressure on the bowels, displacing them and obstructing the normal transit of waste through the abdominal cavity. Furthermore, routine iron supplementation prescribed during antenatal care in Nigeria often precipitates or worsens hard stools due to unabsorbed iron reacting within the gut lumen.

Recognizing Signs and Symptoms during Antenatal Visits

During routine antenatal booking and follow-up visits, midwives must actively screen for bowel habits, as many pregnant women consider mild constipation a normal burden of pregnancy and fail to mention it.

  • Infrequent bowel movements: Passing fewer than three stools per week.
  • Hard or lumpy consistency: Stool that resembles small pellets or is difficult to expel.
  • Excessive straining: Needing to push unduly hard during defecation.
  • Abdominal discomfort: Feeling bloated, crampy, or experiencing a sense of incomplete evacuation.
  • Secondary anal issues: The development of painful hemorrhoids or anal fissures due to the passage of hard stools.

Dietary and Lifestyle Management

First-line management for antenatal constipation focuses entirely on non-pharmacological adjustments. Midwives should counsel women on these practical measures before recommending medications:

  • Fluid intake: Drinking at least eight to ten glasses of clean water daily softens stool by preventing excessive fluid reabsorption in the colon.
  • Dietary fiber: Incorporating local high-fiber foods such as beans, brown rice, moimoi, and fresh fruits like pawpaw and oranges adds bulk to the stool.
  • Physical activity: Engaging in safe, moderate exercise like brisk walking stimulates natural bowel motility.
  • Toilet habits: Encouraging women not to ignore the urge to defecate and allowing adequate time without rushing.

Safe Use of Laxatives and Medical Interventions

When lifestyle modifications fail to relieve symptoms, pharmacological agents may be introduced under medical or midwifery supervision. Not all laxatives are safe during pregnancy; stimulant laxatives can potentially trigger uterine contractions if used aggressively.

  • Bulk-forming laxatives: Agents like psyllium husk absorb water in the intestine, swelling the stool and promoting peristalsis safely.
  • Osmotic laxatives: Lactulose or polyethylene glycol draw water into the bowel lumen, softening the stool without stimulating the uterine muscle directly.
  • Stool softeners: Docusate helps mix dietary fat and water into the stool, making passage easier.
  • Iron adjustment: If routine iron pills are the culprit, switching to a lower-dose or a different formulation (like iron liquid or intermittent dosing) can sometimes resolve the issue.

When Constipation Signals a Serious Complication: Red Flags for Midwives

While routine constipation is uncomfortable, certain associated symptoms indicate a more serious underlying pathology that requires prompt medical referral. Midwives must remain vigilant during clinical assessments.

  • Severe abdominal pain: Sharp, localized, or colicky pain that does not resolve after a bowel movement.
  • Persistent vomiting: Inability to keep down fluids or food, which could suggest a mechanical bowel obstruction.
  • Rectal bleeding: Bright red blood or dark tarry stools that go beyond simple surface bleeding from external hemorrhoids.
  • Fever and systemic signs: Elevated temperature or signs of infection associated with acute abdominal distress.
  • Failure to pass gas: Complete cessation of flatulence alongside abdominal distension, pointing toward a possible surgical emergency like a volvulus or obstruction.

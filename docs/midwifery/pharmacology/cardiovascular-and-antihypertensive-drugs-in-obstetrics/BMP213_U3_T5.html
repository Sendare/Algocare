Magnesium Sulfate in Severe Preeclampsia and Eclampsia: Regimens, Toxicity Signs, Antidote (Calcium Gluconate)

Why Magnesium Sulfate is the Drug of Choice

Magnesium sulfate ($ ext{MgSO}_4$) remains the gold standard for preventing and treating convulsions in patients with severe preeclampsia and eclampsia. Unlike standard anti-seizure medicines, it works primarily by blocking neuromuscular transmission and acting as a cerebral vasodilator. It stabilizes the irritable central nervous system without causing excessive sedation to the mother or depressing the newborn's breathing at therapeutic levels.

Standard Regimens: Loading and Maintenance Doses

Effective treatment requires two distinct dosing phases to build a safe therapeutic blood level and maintain it over time.

  • Loading Dose: A high initial dose, typically 4g to 5g of magnesium sulfate, mixed and given slowly intravenously, often supplemented by intramuscular injections to achieve rapid therapeutic levels.
  • Maintenance Dose: Follow-up doses of 5g given intramuscularly every four hours into alternate buttocks, or a continuous intravenous infusion of 1g to 2g per hour.

Administration Protocols: Intravenous and Intramuscular Routes

In busy Nigerian clinical settings, midwives administer magnesium sulfate using either the intravenous route, the intramuscular route, or the combined Pritchard regimen. Intravenous infusion requires an infusion pump or strict drop-rate counting to avoid dangerous overdose. Intramuscular administration involves deep injection into the upper outer quadrant of the buttock, often mixed with lignocaine to reduce the severe pain associated with the shot.

Essential Clinical Monitoring Before Every Dose

Because magnesium sulfate has a very narrow safety margin, midwives must perform mandatory clinical checks before administering any maintenance dose. The triad of safety checks includes:

  1. Patellar Reflexes: Presence of knee-jerk reflexes confirms the nervous system is not dangerously depressed.
  2. Respiratory Rate: Must be at least 16 breaths per minute to ensure the diaphragm is working properly.
  3. Urine Output: Must be at least 30 mL per hour, because the kidneys excrete the drug and failure leads to rapid accumulation.

Early and Late Toxicity Signs

Recognizing the progression of magnesium toxicity saves lives. Early warning signs appear as the serum level begins to climb above therapeutic thresholds. As toxicity worsens, vital body systems start to shut down.

Toxicity StageClinical Signs and Symptoms
Early SignsLoss of patellar reflexes, feeling of warmth, flushing, muscle weakness
Late SignsSevere respiratory depression, slurred speech, cardiac arrest, coma

Emergency Management and the Antidote (Calcium Gluconate)

If signs of magnesium toxicity appear, the midwife must act instantly. The first step is to stop the magnesium sulfate infusion immediately. Next, summon medical help and ensure airway patency and oxygen administration. The specific pharmacological antidote for magnesium toxicity is calcium gluconate (usually 10% solution, 10 mL given slowly intravenously over three minutes), which competitively blocks magnesium at the neuromuscular junction.

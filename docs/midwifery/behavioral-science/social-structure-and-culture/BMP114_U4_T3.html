Social Stratification, Class and Health Disparities

Understanding Social Stratification and Class Structures

Social stratification refers to the hierarchical arrangement of individuals into different social classes based on wealth, power, occupation, and social standing. In many societies, including Nigeria, these divisions shape how people live, work, and access resources. Class structures are rarely neutral; they determine the distribution of societal privileges and burdens. Midwives must recognize that a patient's position within this hierarchy profoundly influences their daily reality long before they ever step into a clinic.

How Socioeconomic Status Shapes Health Outcomes

Socioeconomic status (SES), measured by income, education, and occupation, is one of the strongest predictors of health and longevity. Individuals with higher SES typically enjoy better nutrition, safer housing, and superior access to quality medical care. Conversely, lower SES exposes individuals to chronic stress, environmental toxins, and nutritional deficiencies. These cumulative disadvantages mean that illness and disease do not strike all social strata equally.

Unpacking Health Disparities and Systemic Inequities

Health disparities are preventable differences in the burden of disease experienced by socially disadvantaged groups. Unlike natural biological variations, these disparities stem from systemic inequities—unfair policies, unequal resource distribution, and structural discrimination. When healthcare systems fail to protect vulnerable populations, inequality becomes embedded in morbidity and mortality statistics. Addressing these gaps requires looking beyond individual lifestyle choices to examine the underlying systems that create disadvantage.

Poverty and Maternal-Child Health Outcomes

The wealth gap exerts a devastating toll on mothers and infants. Women trapped in poverty often delay antenatal care due to user fees, lack of transportation, or the need to prioritize daily survival. Malnutrition during pregnancy increases the incidence of low birth weight, birth asphyxia, and maternal hemorrhage. Without adequate financial resources, families struggle to afford emergency obstetric interventions, turning routine birth complications into life-threatening emergencies.

Barriers to Healthcare Access Among Marginalized Classes

Marginalized classes face multiple interconnected barriers when attempting to use health services. These obstacles include direct financial costs like consultation fees and medication purchases, as well as indirect costs such as lost wages. Furthermore, geographic isolation leaves many rural and informal settlement communities miles away from the nearest comprehensive health facility. Cultural distance and stigmatizing attitudes from healthcare personnel also deter vulnerable patients from seeking timely care.

The Role of the Midwife in Mitigating Health Inequities

Midwives occupy a frontline position to challenge and alleviate health disparities. By providing respectful, non-judgmental maternity care, midwives can bridge the trust gap between marginalized communities and the formal health system. Advocacy is equally crucial; midwives must champion policies that remove user fees for vulnerable mothers and improve resource allocation in underserved clinics. Ultimately, skilled midwifery care serves as a powerful tool for social justice in healthcare delivery.

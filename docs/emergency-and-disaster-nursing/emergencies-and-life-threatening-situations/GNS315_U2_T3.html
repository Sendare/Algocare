Causes of Emergencies

Classification of Emergencies: Medical Versus Traumatic

Clinical emergencies generally divide into two broad etiological categories: medical and traumatic. **Medical emergencies** stem from internal pathophysiological failures, such as a myocardial infarction, acute severe asthma, or diabetic ketoacidosis. These crises evolve from underlying disease processes disrupting homeostatic mechanisms. **Traumatic emergencies**, conversely, result from sudden external physical forces exceeding tissue tolerance. Road traffic crashes, penetrating stab wounds, and accidental falls typify this category. Recognizing the distinction dictates immediate clinical priorities; medical events demand targeted pharmacological and physiological stabilization, whereas traumatic incidents require rapid structural assessment, hemorrhage control, and preservation of spinal alignment.

Environmental and Natural Disaster Causes

Environmental conditions and large-scale natural phenomena trigger acute health crises that overwhelm local healthcare infrastructure. Prolonged exposure to extreme heat leads to heatstroke, characterized by core temperatures exceeding 40 degrees Celsius and altered mental status. Flooding, common during rainy seasons in many tropical regions, precipitates waterborne disease outbreaks, severe diarrheal illnesses, and drowning incidents. - **Extreme Temperatures**: Hypothermia and heat exhaustion impair thermoregulation, leading to multi-organ dysfunction. - **Hydrometeorological Disasters**: Heavy storms and floods contaminate drinking water sources, causing cholera and typhoid spikes. - **Vector-Borne Proliferation**: Standing water post-disaster dramatically increases malaria transmission rates, presenting later as severe systemic infection.

Man-Made and Accidental Causes of Emergencies

Human activity generates a high volume of acute admissions through structural failures, occupational hazards, and interpersonal violence. **Road traffic crashes** remain a leading source of devastating polytrauma, head injuries, and orthopedic damage across Nigerian highways. Industrial accidents, such as chemical burns, crush injuries from heavy machinery, and electrical shocks, introduce complex toxicological and surgical challenges. Domestic settings also harbor substantial risk. Accidental ingestion of caustic agents like kerosene or bleaching solutions by toddlers requires immediate, specialized poisoning protocols rather than standard emetic measures, which can worsen esophageal mucosal destruction.

Underlying Pathophysiological Triggers of Acute Crises

Every clinical emergency shares common biological pathways that compromise cellular viability. **Hypoxia**, resulting from airway obstruction or respiratory depression, starves vital organs of oxygen, shifting cells toward anaerobic metabolism and lactic acidosis. **Circulatory collapse**, or shock, fails to maintain adequate tissue perfusion, depriving the brain and kidneys of essential nutrients. Electrolyte disturbances, particularly severe hyperkalemia, destabilize cardiac cell membrane potentials, swiftly triggering lethal ventricular arrhythmias. Identifying these core physiological breakdowns allows nurses to institute life-saving interventions even before definitive diagnostic testing confirms the root disease.

Risk Factors and Vulnerable Populations in the Nigerian Context

Vulnerability to emergencies is heavily influenced by socioeconomic and environmental determinants. In many resource-limited settings, extreme poverty restricts timely access to preventive healthcare, turning manageable chronic conditions like hypertension into acute stroke emergencies. Pediatric populations face high burdens of severe dehydration from acute gastroenteritis and severe malaria complications. Elderly individuals experience heightened susceptibility to falls, fragility fractures, and adverse drug reactions due to altered pharmacokinetics. Recognizing these demographic risk profiles helps nursing personnel anticipate patient volumes and prepare targeted triage protocols.

Rapid Assessment and Identification of Etiology

Efficient emergency nursing relies on structured evaluation to uncover the root cause of instability. The primary survey systematically checks the Airway, Breathing, Circulation, Disability, and Exposure (ABCDE), halting immediate life threats. Once stability is established, the secondary survey dives into focused history-taking using mnemonic frameworks like SAMPLE (Signs, Allergies, Medications, Past medical history, Last oral intake, Events leading up to). Discerning whether an acute confusion stems from hypoglycemia, hypoxia, or intracranial hemorrhage prevents misdirected treatment. Nurses must correlate physical observations with contextual clues gathered from relatives or bystanders at the scene.

Initial Nursing Management Based on Underlying Causes

Immediate nursing actions are entirely dictated by the identified etiology of the crisis. For hypoxic crises, high-flow oxygen delivery and airway clearing take precedence. Hemorrhagic shock demands immediate establishment of large-bore intravenous lines and rapid fluid resuscitation or blood product administration. Conversely, fluid overload in a patient experiencing acute decompensated heart failure requires diuretics and positioning adjustments rather than fluid boluses. Matching the intervention precisely to the underlying trigger prevents iatrogenic harm and stabilizes the patient for definitive medical or surgical intervention.

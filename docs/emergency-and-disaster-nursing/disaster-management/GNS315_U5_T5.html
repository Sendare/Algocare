Physical Support During Disasters

Introduction and Triage Principles

Disaster management requires rapid physical support to minimize mortality and morbidity. When a mass casualty incident occurs in resource-constrained environments like rural Nigerian communities, standard hospital workflows fail immediately. Triage becomes the foundational skill for survival. Nurses must categorize victims using rapid assessment tools to allocate scarce resources effectively. The most widely used framework is the **START** system, which stands for Simple Triage and Rapid Treatment. This method evaluates ventilation, perfusion, and mental status within sixty seconds per patient.

Immediate Life Support and First Aid

Once casualties are sorted, physical support shifts to immediate life-saving interventions. Uncontrolled hemorrhage remains the leading preventable cause of death in trauma scenarios. Applying direct pressure, wound packing, and commercial or improvised tourniquets must take precedence over airway management in massive bleeding situations. Airway clearance follows, utilizing the recovery position for unconscious breathing victims to prevent aspiration of vomitus or blood. Spinal precautions should only be enforced if a distinct mechanism of injury suggests vertebral trauma, avoiding unnecessary resource drain on stable victims.

Emergency Shelter and Infrastructure

Displaced populations require physical shelter to protect against harsh weather, vector-borne illnesses, and security threats. Temporary settlements must be strategically planned rather than built haphazardly. Key physical requirements include: - **Site Selection**: High ground avoiding flood zones, with stable soil and adequate drainage. - **Space Allocation**: Minimum surface area per person to prevent overcrowding and airborne disease transmission. - **Ventilation**: Adequate airflow within tent structures to reduce respiratory pathogen accumulation. - **Lighting**: Solar or generator-powered lighting around communal pathways and sanitation blocks to prevent gender-based violence.

WASH Interventions

Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene infrastructure prevents outbreaks of cholera, typhoid, and dysentery in displaced populations. Physical support teams must establish protected water points separated by a safe distance from latrines—at least thirty meters is standard to prevent groundwater contamination. Emergency latrines should be dug downwind from living areas and water sources, keeping a ratio of one latrine per twenty people where possible. Handwashing stations equipped with soap or chlorinated water must be placed immediately outside all communal toilet blocks to enforce behavioral compliance.

Emergency Nutrition and Feeding

Food insecurity spikes immediately following natural disasters or civil conflicts. Nutritional support begins with rapid screening using **MUAC** (Mid-Upper Arm Circumference) tapes to identify severe acute malnutrition in children under five. General food distributions must provide balanced macronutrients rather than empty calories, focusing on energy-dense grains, pulses, and fortified oils. Supplementary feeding programs target pregnant women, lactating mothers, and infants who face the highest risk of irreversible developmental deficits during crises.

Mobility and Assistive Device Support

Disasters disproportionately impact individuals with pre-existing mobility impairments or those who sustain new trauma injuries. Physical support must include the distribution of appropriate assistive devices such as crutches, walkers, and wheelchairs. Nurses play a crucial role in fitting these devices correctly and educating users on safe navigation across uneven terrain in temporary settlements. Furthermore, camp infrastructure must incorporate ramps rather than steps at clinics, food distribution points, and sanitation blocks to ensure barrier-free access.

Environmental Health and Vector Control

Stagnant water and disrupted waste management create breeding grounds for disease vectors like mosquitoes, flies, and rodents. Physical vector control involves clearing brush, filling puddles, and distributing long-lasting insecticide-treated nets (LLINs). Proper solid waste management—including regular collection and safe burial or incineration in pit burners—prevents vector proliferation. Nurses monitor displaced populations for febrile illnesses such as malaria, ensuring early diagnosis and treatment to prevent camp-wide outbreaks.

Vulnerable Populations and Special Needs

Certain groups require tailored physical support during emergencies due to heightened dependency or physical fragility. Unaccompanied minors, pregnant women, older adults, and individuals with chronic physical or mental illnesses face heightened risks. Emergency response plans must establish safe spaces for women and children to protect against violence. Outreach teams must conduct house-to-house or tent-to-tent visits to identify homebound older adults who cannot travel to central distribution points for food, water, or medical care.

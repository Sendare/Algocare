Electronic Communication in Nursing

Introduction to Electronic Communication in Nursing

Electronic communication has fundamentally transformed the delivery of healthcare and nursing education in Nigeria and globally. As healthcare facilities adopt electronic health records (EHRs), digital messaging platforms, and virtual care modalities, nurses must become proficient in utilizing these tools safely and effectively. Electronic communication in nursing encompasses all forms of digital information exchange, including interprofessional messaging, patient portals, telehealth consultations, and online academic or professional collaboration. This technological shift improves the speed and accuracy of information sharing, bridging geographical gaps between rural healthcare centers and tertiary hospitals. However, it also introduces unique challenges, such as data privacy concerns, the risk of misinterpretation due to the lack of non-verbal cues, and technical hurdles. For Nigerian nursing students and practicing professionals, mastering electronic communication is essential for meeting modern regulatory standards, enhancing patient safety, and ensuring seamless coordination of care within multidisciplinary healthcare teams. Understanding foundational digital literacy prepares nurses to adapt to rapidly evolving healthcare technologies throughout their careers.

Individual Communication

Individual communication in nursing refers to one-on-one digital exchanges between healthcare professionals, or between a nurse and a patient. This can take place via secure SMS, direct messaging within an EHR system, or private professional emails. In a clinical setting, individual electronic communication is vital for quick updates regarding a patient's condition, clarifying medication orders with a physician, or providing individualized discharge instructions to a patient via a secure portal. Because these messages often contain sensitive health information, nurses must adhere strictly to confidentiality guidelines. Unlike face-to-face interactions, individual electronic messages lack tone of voice and facial expressions, making clarity, professionalism, and conciseness paramount. Nigerian nurses must ensure that any individual communication involving patient data complies with institutional policies and national data protection acts to prevent unauthorized disclosure. Writing professional, well-documented individual messages also serves as a legal record of communication and care coordination.

Group Communication

Group communication involves the simultaneous exchange of information among three or more participants using digital platforms. In nursing practice and education, group chats, mailing lists, and collaborative workspaces are indispensable for shift handovers, committee meetings, multidisciplinary clinical rounds, and student group projects. Effective group communication ensures that all team members remain updated on critical patient status changes, facility policies, or academic schedules. However, group platforms can easily become cluttered with irrelevant chatter, leading to 'alert fatigue' or missed critical updates. Nurses must exercise digital etiquette by keeping messages relevant, concise, and professional. Additionally, when discussing patient care in group settings, strict precautions must be taken to anonymize patient data to prevent breaches of confidentiality. Establishing clear ground rules for group chats helps maintain an organized environment where essential clinical and academic information can be easily accessed and reviewed by all participants.

Video Conference

Video conferencing has revolutionized telehealth, remote nursing education, and virtual administrative meetings. By combining audio and visual feeds, video conferences restore many non-verbal communication cues that are lost in text-based messaging, such as body language, eye contact, and facial expressions. In clinical nursing, video conferencing facilitates teleconsultations, allowing nurses in remote areas of Nigeria to consult with specialists in urban teaching hospitals or monitor chronic disease patients from afar. In nursing education, it enables synchronous lectures, virtual clinical simulations, and interactive seminars. Successful video conferencing requires stable internet connectivity, adequate lighting, professional attire, and adherence to netiquette, such as muting microphones when not speaking to reduce background noise. Nurses must also ensure that the physical environment surrounding them during a video call maintains patient confidentiality and projects a professional image, especially when interacting with patients or academic evaluators.

Zoom

Zoom is a widely adopted video conferencing platform utilized extensively in nursing education, administrative meetings, and some telehealth applications. It offers features such as breakout rooms for small group discussions, screen sharing for presentations, chat functions for real-time questions, and recording capabilities for lectures that students can review later. For nursing students in Nigeria, Zoom provides a reliable way to attend virtual lectures and participate in collaborative group work despite physical distances. To use Zoom effectively and securely, hosts must implement security measures like password protection, waiting rooms, and restricted screen sharing to prevent 'zoombombing' or unauthorized access. Nurses hosting patient consultations or academic sessions must be familiar with privacy settings and encryption protocols. Knowing how to navigate features like polling and hand-raising also enhances engagement and ensures structured participation during large virtual nursing seminars or clinical debriefing sessions.

Google Meet

Google Meet is another prominent video conferencing tool integrated within the Google Workspace ecosystem, making it popular among educational institutions and healthcare organizations. Its seamless integration with Google Calendar, Gmail, and Google Drive allows nursing students and professionals to schedule meetings, share documents instantly, and collaborate on care plans or academic assignments with ease. Google Meet offers live closed captions, screen sharing, and secure encryption for data in transit. In clinical and academic settings in Nigeria, Google Meet is valued for its user-friendly interface and accessibility across various devices, including smartphones and tablets, which is beneficial when internet bandwidth fluctuates. Nurses using Google Meet must ensure they log in through verified institutional accounts and manage participant permissions carefully to maintain confidentiality and prevent uninvited guests from joining clinical briefings or sensitive academic discussions.

Microsoft Teams

Microsoft Teams is a comprehensive collaboration platform that combines video conferencing, persistent chat, file storage, and application integration into a single hub. In modern hospitals and nursing schools, Teams is frequently utilized for enterprise-level communication. It allows nursing units to create dedicated channels for specific shifts, clinical departments, or research projects, keeping discussions organized and searchable. Teams supports robust security and compliance features required in healthcare environments, making it suitable for discussing administrative policies and coordinating interprofessional care. For nursing students, Teams provides a centralized space to access course materials, submit assignments, and collaborate with peers and faculty members. Proficiency in Microsoft Teams is a valuable digital competency for Nigerian nurses entering tech-enabled healthcare facilities, as it streamlines workflow management and supports continuous interprofessional communication.

Skype

Skype is one of the pioneer Voice over IP (VoIP) and video chat platforms used globally for voice calls, video conferencing, and instant messaging. While newer platforms like Zoom and Microsoft Teams have gained dominance in enterprise and educational settings, Skype remains relevant for personal and simple point-to-point communication. In nursing, Skype can be used for individual patient follow-ups, remote consultations, or connecting with international mentors and colleagues. It supports international calling features, making it useful for diaspora nurses communicating with Nigerian healthcare institutions. However, nurses must evaluate Skype's security features against institutional compliance standards before using it for transmitting sensitive patient health information. Understanding how to use Skype expands a nurse's toolkit for digital communication, ensuring they can adapt when patients or collaborators prefer or only have access to legacy platforms.

Moodle

Moodle (Modular Object-Oriented Dynamic Learning Environment) is a widely used Learning Management System (LMS) designed to provide educators, administrators, and students with a robust system for online learning. In Nigerian nursing schools and universities, Moodle serves as the primary portal for delivering course content, hosting discussion forums, submitting assignments, and taking online quizzes. It allows nursing educators to upload lecture notes, share clinical guidelines, and track student academic progress transparently. For nursing students, Moodle encourages asynchronous learning, enabling them to review course materials at their own pace and engage in academic debates through structured forums. Proficiency in navigating Moodle is critical for nursing students to successfully complete their coursework, access clinical rotation schedules, and communicate with instructors outside of traditional classroom hours.

Best Practices and Security in Nursing Digital Communication

Best practices and security in nursing digital communication are paramount to protecting patient confidentiality and maintaining professional integrity. Nurses are legally and ethically bound by patient privacy laws, such as the Nigerian Data Protection Regulation (NDPR) and professional codes of ethics, to safeguard health information. Best practices include never sharing login credentials, always logging out of shared workstations, avoiding the transmission of identifiable patient data over unsecured personal messaging apps (like standard WhatsApp without encryption or verification), and using strong, multi-factor authentication passwords. Furthermore, nurses must maintain professional boundaries in digital spaces, avoiding the acceptance of patient friend requests on personal social media accounts. By prioritizing cybersecurity, data encryption, and professional etiquette, nurses ensure that electronic communication remains a powerful tool for improving patient outcomes without compromising safety or trust.

Application of Information System Technology to Clinical Practice

Introduction to Information Systems in Nursing

Nursing informatics merges nursing science, computer science, and information science to manage and communicate data, information, knowledge, and wisdom in nursing practice. Health information systems (HIS) streamline workflow, reduce documentation errors, and support evidence-based decisions at the bedside. In Nigerian clinical settings, transitioning from paper-based record-keeping to digital platforms improves continuity of care across various hospital departments. Nurses interact daily with these systems to input vital signs, track medication administration, and review multidisciplinary notes.

Core Components of Electronic Health Records

An Electronic Health Record (EHR) serves as the digital repository for a client's comprehensive health history. Unlike isolated departmental systems, an EHR aggregates data from laboratory results, radiology reports, nursing flowsheets, and physician orders into a unified dashboard.

  • Demographics: Basic patient identification, emergency contacts, and residential details.
  • Clinical Notes: Structured and unstructured entries detailing ongoing patient assessments.
  • Diagnostic Reports: Digital imaging scans, pathology results, and laboratory values.
  • Medication Profiles: Active prescriptions, past drug reactions, and administration logs.

Clinical Decision Support Systems in Practice

A Clinical Decision Support System (CDSS) acts as an intelligent assistant embedded within health technology. It analyzes current patient data against a knowledge base to generate targeted alerts, reminders, and clinical guidelines. For instance, when a nurse attempts to administer a prescribed drug, the system checks for known allergies and flags potential adverse interactions.

A common misconception is that a CDSS makes final medical or nursing decisions; however, it only provides recommendations while the human clinician retains ultimate responsibility for patient care.

Enhancing Patient Safety and Medication Administration

Technology significantly minimizes medication errors through automated safety checks. Bar Code Medication Administration (BCMA) requires the nurse to scan the patient's identification band and the medication barcode before delivery. This closed-loop process verifies the five rights of medication administration: right patient, right drug, right dose, right route, and right time. Additionally, Smart Infusion Pumps feature built-in dose error reduction systems that prevent accidental programming of lethal medication dosages.

Telehealth and Remote Patient Monitoring Applications

Telehealth bridges geographic gaps, allowing healthcare providers to consult, diagnose, and monitor patients remotely. Remote Patient Monitoring (RPM) utilizes digital tools like wearable blood pressure cuffs, glucometers, and pulse oximeters to transmit biometric data from patients' homes directly to clinical facilities. In rural Nigerian communities, telehealth initiatives allow nurses and community health extension workers to consult with specialists in urban teaching hospitals without requiring expensive patient travel.

Data Security, Privacy, and Ethical Considerations

Protecting patient health information is a fundamental ethical and legal duty for every nurse. Electronic systems store highly sensitive data that must be guarded against unauthorized access, breaches, and cyber threats. Measures like role-based access control ensure staff only view records relevant to their specific duties. Furthermore, nurses must never share their personal login credentials or leave active patient computer terminals unattended in public clinical areas.

Overcoming Challenges in Health Information Technology Adoption

Implementing new technology in clinical environments often encounters resistance from staff accustomed to traditional paper routines. Technical hurdles, such as unstable internet connectivity, erratic electrical power supply, and complex software interfaces, frequently disrupt workflow. Successful adoption requires comprehensive staff training, continuous technical support, and involving frontline nurses in selecting and designing the digital tools they will use every day.
